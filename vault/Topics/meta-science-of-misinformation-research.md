# Reflections on the State of Misinformation and Online Harm Research

Papers that step back from primary empirical study to critically assess the misinformation/online-harms research field itself — diagnosing biases (e.g., WEIRD-centrism, overreliance on cognitive proxies), mapping gaps in evidence (causal, longitudinal, cross-national), and proposing directions or borrowed lessons (e.g., from climate psychology) to make the field more rigorous and generalizable.
