# Generative AI and Synthetic Media Manipulation

## The Arc of Inquiry

This collection traces a single, escalating argument: generative AI has not merely added a new weapon to the arsenal of online manipulation but has restructured the economics, aesthetics, and epistemology of persuasion itself. The papers move from empirical documentation of AI-enabled harms, through theoretical reckonings with what this means for "truth" and trust, to tentative探索 of countermeasures—countermeasures that themselves increasingly rely on the very technology posing the threat.

## Industrial-Scale Persuasion: Gambling, Fraud, and the Economics of Synthetic Content

The empirical core of this topic is the discovery that generative AI functions primarily as a scale multiplier for existing manipulation tactics rather than a wholly novel persuasive logic. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents this most precisely: analyzing over 2,300 images from coordinated Facebook gambling networks, it shows an exponential, structurally-dated surge in posting volume immediately following ChatGPT's late-2022 launch, with generative AI intensifying—rather than replacing—established drivers like aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, and FOMO. Crucially, the paper locates the enabling mechanism not in content moderation failures but in *algorithmic amplification* and Meta's regulatory gap between paid and organic content. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] extends this insight across a wider typology, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category that deliberately decouples manipulation from falsity: its gambling, propaganda, and unmoderated-group cases share a common structural logic of affordance exploitation and simulated coordination regardless of underlying truth-value or motive. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] generalizes this argument into policy terms, mapping the "scam ecosystem" of platforms, telecoms, and financial intermediaries and arguing that liability must shift from individual victims to gatekeepers—a claim that resonates with Giglietto and Giada's shared diagnosis that the problem is infrastructural, not merely representational.

Complementary computational work extends detection into underexamined terrain: [[Luceri2025-tr]] adapts coordinated-inauthentic-behavior detection to TikTok's video-first architecture, Bastos2025-ol mines visual self-presentation features in troll-farm profile imagery from the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset, and [[Orlando2025-ul]] demonstrates—via generative agent-based modeling—that LLM agents can spontaneously reproduce the coordination signatures of real information operations without explicit instruction, suggesting the organizational machinery of influence operations may itself become automatable. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s organizational history of the IRA provides an essential empirical baseline against which these AI-native successors can be measured, showing that even "low-tech" troll farms were already embedded in conventional media labor markets—a reminder that synthetic manipulation inherits as much from older propaganda infrastructures as it innovates beyond them.

## Political and Cultural Manifestations

Several papers trace how this infrastructure manifests in specific political contexts. [[Beacken2026-zb]] insists on a comparative, anti-deterministic reading: GenAI propaganda's form and intensity across Bolivia, Hungary, India, Nigeria, Tunisia, and Turkey depends on structural—not technological—conditions. [[Perez-Curiel2026-ld]] and [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] examine platform-specific deployments, the latter usefully complicating alarmist narratives by showing pro-immigration content dominating Canadian TikTok even as generative AI enables "speculative worldbuilding" for xenophobic narratives. [[Crichton2026-kn]] and [[Topinka2026-hb]] push furthest into the aesthetic register: Topinka's theorization of "slopaganda" as an affective lure rather than belief-altering propaganda—AI content that is obviously fake yet "affectively real"—offers a conceptual bridge between Manovich's medium-theoretic account of generative AI ([[Manovich2026-ih]], which argues the medium is structurally conservative, privileging conventional over avant-garde logics) and the political urgency of the other papers. Crichton's "re-generative pipeline," in which commercial image models sanitize but do not neutralize extremist imagery, shows this aesthetic conservatism being weaponized rather than merely observed.

## Deepfakes, Audiences, and the Sociality of Belief

A second cluster interrogates how audiences actually process synthetic deception, consistently complicating simple "seeing is believing" assumptions. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] finds that AI-generated visual disinformation does not universally outperform text—its persuasive advantage is issue-dependent and contingent on the availability of plausible real footage, suggesting fears about generative AI's unprecedented power may be overstated relative to low-tech decontextualization. [[Wack2026-bt]]'s large Kenyan field experiment reframes deepfake impact as "collective sensemaking": a video's persuasive effect is not fixed at production but negotiated through surrounding social commentary, with partisanship moderating the direction of that negotiation. [[Choi2026-bz]] identifies a "modality-congruent carryover effect," whereby difficulty authenticating one deepfake shapes vulnerability to subsequent same-format fakes—pointing to cumulative, format-specific erosion of discernment rather than one-off deception events. Together these papers suggest that synthetic media's persuasive power is less an intrinsic property of the artifact than an emergent property of its reception context—a theme that dovetails with [[Dierickx2026-tw]]'s epistemological argument that GenAI outputs constitute a new category, "emergent facts," irreducible to evidence-based, interpretative, or rule-based factuality and demanding relational rather than binary verification.

## Synthetic Reality and the Erosion of Epistemic Ground

[[Emilio2026-ik]] offers the most totalizing synthesis, arguing that the real danger is not isolated deepfakes but "synthetic realities"—coherent, interactive, personalized fabrications spanning content, identity, interaction, and institutions—culminating in the "Generative AI Paradox": as synthetic content proliferates, rational actors may discount *all* digital evidence, producing an epistemic tax that benefits strategic deniers. [[Schroeder2026-im]] operationalizes a parallel anxiety at the level of collective agency, describing how LLM-powered "malicious AI swarms" could manufacture synthetic consensus and poison training data via "LLM grooming," concluding that symmetric pro-social countermeasures cannot reliably match adversarial ones given attention-economy incentives. [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s empirical audit of Grokipedia offers a concrete instance of this epistemic risk: an AI-generated encyclopedia that is heavily derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically more likely to cite unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial topics, illustrating how synthetic knowledge infrastructures can quietly shift the reliability baseline of public information at scale.

## LLMs as Counter-Weapon: Persuasion, Inoculation, and Transparency

Against this threat landscape, several papers test LLMs as tools of repair rather than harm. [[Costello2024-bg]]'s landmark finding—that personalized GPT-4 dialogues durably reduce conspiracy belief by roughly 20%, generalizing beyond the focal belief—anchors an emerging consensus that AI's persuasive capacity is agnostic to its moral valence. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends this logic to electoral contexts across four democracies, finding AI dialogue persuasion effects exceeding traditional advertising, driven mainly by factual argumentation rather than manipulation—though with a troubling asymmetry whereby right-leaning advocacy produces more inaccurate claims. [[Szabo2026-rd]] operationalizes a chatbot-based "conversational inoculation" that outperforms static reading/writing interventions once baseline susceptibility is controlled, while [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] demonstrates that even simple, non-conversational prebunking ads can scale cheaply and durably on Instagram. [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]] complicates the transparency solution favored elsewhere in the volume (e.g., in [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]]'s labeling discussion): disclosing mere AI identity, as mandated by the EU AI Act, barely dents persuasive power, while disclosing *persuasive intent* cuts it roughly in half—suggesting regulation must target purpose disclosure, not source disclosure. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]]'s audit of Google's AI Overviews shows this same fragility in automated correction systems: guardrails work reasonably well for entrenched conspiracies like chemtrails but can actively legitimize newer ones like "15-minute cities" by framing them as open "debates."

## The Hidden Labor Behind the Machine

Finally, two papers redirect attention from content and audiences to the human infrastructure sustaining both AI safety and AI harm. [[Gillespie2026-aa]] argues that red-teaming—the practice meant to catch generative AI's harms before release—is itself a sociotechnical system riddled with the same outsourced, psychologically taxing labor arrangements that characterized early content moderation, raising doubts about whether current safety practices can scale responsibly. [[Slack1782736288-sl09]] turns to companion chatbots' handling of emotionally vulnerable users, arguing that existing prompt-based safety audits miss the deeper "decision policies" shaping sustained, potentially harmful accommodation. [[Tang2026-gu]] closes the loop on production-side labor from a different angle, documenting Chinese "AI slop" creators who treat virality itself as an engineerable output—formalizing the same industrial logic of synthetic content production that [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] identifies in gambling networks, but now as a legitimate creative/economic practice rather than covert manipulation, underscoring how thin the line has become between platform-native content strategy and coordinated deception.
