# Synthetic Media & Generative Manipulation

## Synthetic Media & Generative Manipulation: The Arc of the Argument

### From artifact to ecosystem

The papers filed here trace a shared arc: the object of concern keeps shifting from the *synthetic artifact* (a deepfake video, an AI image) to the *ecosystem* that manufactures, distributes, and monetizes deception at scale. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] exemplifies this move explicitly, arguing that deepfake financial fraud cannot be understood as a problem of individual forged content but as a "scam ecosystem" spanning platforms, telecoms, banks, and laundering networks — and that regulation must target gatekeepers rather than victims. [[Emilio2026-ik]] generalizes this insight theoretically, proposing that GenAI risk is best modeled as a four-layer "synthetic reality" stack (content, identity, interaction, institutions) rather than a catalogue of fake artifacts, culminating in the "Generative AI Paradox": as fabrication becomes cheap and ubiquitous, societies may rationally discount *all* digital evidence, corroding the very possibility of shared truth. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] operationalizes this ecosystem view empirically, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" (DIOs) as a category defined not by falsity of content but by the convergence of deceptive intent, affordance exploitation, and coordination — showing that a pro-Putin propaganda network, a gambling promotion ring, and a poorly moderated adult-content operation share the same structural logic despite radically different actors and motives. This paper's "Zombie Internet" framing — where bot and human activity become indistinguishable and platforms' engagement architecture itself becomes the vulnerability — functions as something like the keystone concept for the whole cluster.

### Gambling as the paradigm case of industrialized deception

Several papers converge on online gambling promotion as an unusually clean empirical window onto AI-enabled manipulation at scale. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents an exponential, statistically discontinuous jump in coordinated gambling-post volume coinciding with ChatGPT's public launch, and builds a typology of visual persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localization) that generative AI intensifies and recombines rather than replaces. Critically, this paper locates the enabling mechanism not in content moderation failure per se but in *algorithmic amplification* and the regulatory grey zone between paid and organic content — a diagnosis that echoes Schiffrin's gatekeeper argument. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]'s gambling case study corroborates this from the coordination side: near-total synthetic activity, brandjacking, and a "maximize exposure before takedown" strategy. Together these two papers make gambling promotion a kind of model system for how GenAI, platform incentive structures, and coordinated inauthentic behavior interlock — a template later work (on propaganda, fraud, or extremism) can be read against.

### Coordination, agents, and the automation of influence operations

A second thread asks what happens when the "coordination" in coordinated inauthentic behavior is itself generated rather than merely amplified by AI. [[Orlando2025-ul]] demonstrates in simulation that networked LLM agents can *spontaneously* reproduce the signatures of real-world information operations — network densification, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification — and that mere mutual awareness among agents (without explicit deliberation) suffices to approximate full coordination. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a threat-model for democracy, arguing that "malicious AI swarms" constitute a qualitative leap beyond botnets because they combine persistent memory, cross-platform adaptivity, and minimal human oversight, opening pathways from manufactured consensus to "LLM grooming" of future training data. Read together, these two papers suggest the field is moving from documenting human-orchestrated campaigns that use AI tools (as in [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]) toward anticipating campaigns where AI agents themselves are the orchestrators — a shift [[Beacken2026-zb]] complicates by insisting, comparatively across six democratically weakened states, that GenAI propaganda's form and impact remain contingent on local political-economic structure rather than following one universal technological trajectory.

### Contested effects: does synthetic media actually persuade?

A skeptical counter-current runs through the audience-effects literature, cautioning against assuming synthetic content is automatically more powerful than older forms of deception. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] finds that AI-generated images do *not* uniformly outperform text or repurposed real video in credibility or engagement — decontextualized authentic footage can be more persuasive than fabrication when ample real footage exists, suggesting some anxieties about GenAI's unprecedented persuasive power are overstated relative to low-tech recontextualization. [[Wack2026-bt]], working in Kenya, shows that a deepfake's political impact is not fixed at production but socially negotiated through audience commentary — credulous comments amplify harm, skeptical ones blunt it, and partisanship moderates everything — reframing deepfakes as "flexibly deceptive cues" within participatory sensemaking rather than freestanding persuasive weapons. [[Choi2026-bz]] adds a cognitive-psychological layer, showing that difficulty authenticating one piece of fake content carries over, modality-congruently, to shape confidence and vulnerability when encountering the next. Together these three complicate the more alarmist ecosystem-level papers by insisting that synthetic media's power is downstream of audience psychology, platform commentary culture, and comparative context — not an intrinsic property of the artifact.

### Adjacent manifestations: platforms, culture, and labor

The remaining papers extend the topic's boundaries in three directions. [[Triedman2025-uy]] examines Grokipedia as a large-scale case of AI-generated "knowledge" that is derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically skewed toward unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial topics — an institutional-capture variant of synthetic manipulation distinct from fraud or propaganda but structurally continuous with this cluster's concern about GenAI degrading verification infrastructure. [[Askanius2026-de]] and [[Galip2026-ix]]/[[Tang2026-gu]] approach synthetic manipulation from its cultural and infrastructural peripheries: the former shows how far-right influencers hybridize activist and commercial-influencer logics into a cohesive alternative influence network predating heavy GenAI use, providing a template of platform-native persuasion tactics that generative tools now intensify; the latter pair theorize and empirically document "AI slop" itself — in [[Galip2026-ix]] as an ambivalent cultural gimmick under "too late capitalism," and in [[Tang2026-gu]] as a Chinese creator practice that industrializes virality into a managed production pipeline. Finally, [[Gillespie2026-aa]] turns the lens inward on the AI industry's own defenses, arguing that red-teaming — the practice meant to catch these very harms before deployment — is itself an opaque, undertheorized sociotechnical labor system inheriting content moderation's worst pathologies, suggesting that the infrastructures built to govern synthetic manipulation may reproduce the same accountability gaps this whole literature diagnoses in the manipulation itself.
