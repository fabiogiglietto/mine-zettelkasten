# Digital Methods & Computational Social Science

## The Shape of a Field in Flux

This structure note gathers work spanning three registers of a single problem: how do we know anything about mediated social life when the infrastructures producing our evidence are themselves unstable, contested, and increasingly opaque? The papers filed here move from foundational theory (sociocybernetics, hybrid media systems) through the hard-won methodological toolkit of social network analysis and survey/ad-based recruitment, to a sustained meditation on data ethics and access politics in the "post-API age." Read together, they trace an arc from confident measurement to methodological humility.

### Theoretical Foundations: Systems, Hybridity, and Second-Order Observation

The oldest conceptual layer here draws on cybernetics and systems theory to resist simplistic causal stories about media effects. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] is paradigmatic: against creator-intent-centered definitions of "fake news," it mobilizes second-order cybernetics (von Foerster, Bateson) to argue that information is observer-dependent and that truth-claims emerge from chains of propagator judgments rather than singular acts of deception. This systems sensibility recurs in [[Trilling2024-slc8]], which replaces the overused "echo chamber"/"filter bubble" metaphors with a feedback-loop framework borrowed from cybernetics—showing why self-reinforcing processes don't inevitably produce runaway radicalization. [[Chadwick2013-ns]] and [[Chadwick2015-ii]] extend this anti-dichotomous instinct to political communication at large, theorizing a "hybrid media system" in which old and new media logics interpenetrate through adaptation and interdependence rather than displacement—a frame later empirically interrogated by [[Barbera2015-fw]], whose network-based ideology estimation shows that "echo chamber" dynamics are topic- and time-contingent rather than universal. [[Slack1783508184-sld3]] (boyd) supplies the foundational vocabulary—networked publics, affordances, context collapse—that much of this corpus still works with or against; [[Chancellor2026-sld9]] explicitly retheorizes context collapse for an algorithmic, consumption-oriented platform landscape that boyd's original framework could not have anticipated. Meanwhile [[Ng2026-og]] pushes this theoretical lineage forward into the agentic-AI era, arguing that multi-agent AI systems require social theory (not just alignment engineering) as a structural prior—explicitly building on the sociological tradition this cluster represents.

### Social Network Analysis and Computational Detection

A dense cluster concerns the technical apparatus of network analysis, especially as applied to detecting coordination and manipulation. [[Bruns2025-fz]] addresses a classic SNA problem—the illegible "hairball"—with vector-embedding-based "practice mapping." A substantial sub-thread tackles coordinated inauthentic behavior (CIB): [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers the field's conceptual survey, proposing a unifying definition along axes of authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, and time-variance; [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] advance multiplex, time-aware network models for detecting coordination across modalities; [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] operationalizes this into the CooRTweet software tool; [[Yang2025-iv]] and [[Minici2024-tf]] push detection into adversarially robust and platform-general (graph foundation model) territory; and [[Luceri2025-tr]] extends the whole enterprise to TikTok's video-first ecosystem, where text-centric methods break down. Yet this cluster is punctuated by productive skepticism: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] finds that real coordinated accounts achieve influence far below strategic optima, resembling random placement; [[Pante2025-pq]] re-examines claims of inter-state coordination and finds them empirically thin without proper controls; Bastos2025-ol pivots from network signatures to visual self-presentation as an attribution signal. Together these papers model good scientific practice in a subfield prone to alarmist overclaiming—a corrective echoed at the macro level by [[Oswald2025-km]], which shows that the tiny minority of highly active social media producers systematically distorts both citizens' and researchers' inferences about public opinion, and by [[Bail2018-fk]], whose field experiment demonstrates that naive assumptions about exposure-reduces-polarization can backfire entirely.

### Access, Infrastructure, and the Post-API Condition

The largest and most urgent thread documents the collapse of the data-access regime that made the above research possible. [[Freelon2018-ao]] coins "post-API age" in direct response to Facebook's 2018 Pages API closure; [[Bruns2019-nr]] names this moment the "APIcalypse" and argues platforms weaponized Cambridge Analytica to suppress accountability research. [[Freelon2024-sc]] and [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] periodize nearly two decades of this history—from open "Wild West" APIs through a brief "Golden Age" to commercial paywalls—while Bastos2025-ya offers an elegiac retrospective specifically on Twitter studies, and [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]] quantifies the human cost bibliometrically: a 13% decline in Twitter-based publications in 2024 alone. The EU's Digital Services Act emerges as the central institutional response and battleground: [[de-Vreese2026-zx]] frames current turbulence as evidence regulation is finally working, not failing; [[Annabell2026-ev]] shows how "data" itself is a contested, undefined "floating signifier" that platforms exploit to narrow researcher access; [[Peters2026-mo]] traces how "data quality" entered DSA rulemaking only through sustained academic/NGO advocacy; [[Giglietto2025-ed60bc90]] surveys the resulting API/tool landscape. The lived texture of this regime is documented ethnographically: [[Cullen2026-cb]] theorizes "seeing like an API" through CrowdTangle users' frustrations; [[Bruns2026-pn]] extends this to the more restrictive "clean room" model (Meta Content Library), showing how it structurally excludes qualitative and non-programming researchers; [[Rieder2025-ju]] audits YouTube's search API and finds it "forgetful by design," systematically losing findable content after 20-60 days; [[Zheng2026-bi]] builds alternative infrastructure (TubeStats/TokStats) via random sampling precisely because sanctioned APIs cannot produce representative data; and [[Rieder2020-sl35]] offers the deepest theoretical reframing, arguing "transparency" itself is the wrong regulatory metaphor and should be replaced by "observability" as a continuous social relation. [[Park2026-tr]] widens the lens to show that legal risk under laws like the CFAA chills not just platform-data research but computer science research broadly—a sobering coda to this whole thread. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] demonstrates concretely how access engineering (a 100-share anonymization threshold) can itself become an artifact that contaminates findings, a methodological cautionary tale that should inform anyone using platform-provided datasets.

### Survey Recruitment and Measurement Validity

A smaller but foundational thread addresses recruitment and measurement integrity underpinning any computational social science. [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] pioneers Facebook ad-based recruitment for hard-to-reach populations (conspiracy believers), using Pixel-based tracking for genuine conversion-rate measurement—a direct methodological ancestor of [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]]'s Instagram-based prebunking field experiment, which shows inoculation effects can scale cheaply via ad infrastructure. [[Stagnaro2025-pz]] provides the most systematic treatment of opt-in sample quality, documenting trade-offs between representativeness and response validity across nine platforms. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] extends measurement-validity concerns to web-tracking methodology, showing that ex-situ scraping (not just time delay) massively distorts news-consumption measures.

### LLMs as Method: Promise, Bias, and Epistemic Risk

A rapidly growing cluster interrogates large language models as research instruments. Several papers are cautiously optimistic about augmentation: [[Carammia2024-sl90]] shows fine-tuned open-source LLMs can match proprietary models while preserving reproducibility; [[Farjam2026-zg]] and [[Ober2026-vd]] develop human-in-the-loop agentic workflows for content analysis and interview coding; [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] reframes LLMs as "analytic collaborators" that surface codebook ambiguities rather than replace human judgment; [[Meher2025-qb]] demonstrates efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) democratizing conflict-event classification; [[Alizadeh2026-es]] finds coding agents can reproduce a striking share of published social science findings, while also documenting sycophantic specification-search risks. Counterbalancing this optimism, [[Balluff2026-if]] offers a pointed critique of unreflective LLM adoption—corporate dependency, environmental cost, synthetic-data bias—and [[Brown2025-jk]] empirically tests demographic bias in LLM annotation, finding item difficulty (not demographics) is the dominant predictor of disagreement. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the annotation/synthesis literature broadly, while [[Suk2026-ai]] extends the trust literature from mass/social media to generative AI.

### Visual, Multimodal, and Platform-Specific Methods

A final thread pushes computational methods beyond text. [[Arminio2025-tw]] shows Vision-LLMs dramatically improve *connotative* (as opposed to denotative) image clustering for computational social science; [[Gardam2025-er]] and [[Bravo2026-ue]] argue for visual-first methods given Instagram's image-centric conspiracy and climate discourse; [[Arora2025-tx]] extends framing analysis multimodally to news images; [[Fan2025-ut]] addresses a subtler methodological problem—confounding in text embeddings—with linear concept erasure. [[Fan2026-af]] proposes mining fine-grained temporal "user-sequences" from donated digital trace data, arguing communication research has neglected the dynamic, processual nature of its own theories. [[Lompe2026-vm]] revives URL-link analysis for cross-platform controversy mapping, showing the same artifact (a link) carries different evidential meaning depending on platform affordances—a fitting closing note, since it insists that even our most basic analytic objects are never platform-neutral.

### Synthesis

Taken as a whole, this collection narrates a discipline maturing under duress: early systems-theoretic ambition and classical SNA techniques have given way to a methodologically self-conscious, infrastructure-aware computational social science that must simultaneously build new tools (LLM pipelines, multiplex coordination detection, random-sampling dashboards), audit the old ones for bias and distortion, and fight—often through regulatory and legal channels—for the basic conditions of empirical access that earlier research took for granted.
