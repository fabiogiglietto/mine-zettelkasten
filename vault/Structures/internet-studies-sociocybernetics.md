# Internet Studies and Sociocybernetics

## From Systems to Screens: A Sociocybernetic Arc

The papers gathered under this heading share a commitment to treating the internet not as a technical artefact but as a communicative system whose dynamics can only be grasped through the conceptual toolkit of systems theory and sociocybernetics. Read together, they trace an arc from architectural questions about how the network *should* be built, through the mid-decade preoccupation with how information *circulates* through it, to the current moment in which the very "socialness" of the medium — and the intelligence of its non-human participants — has come under suspicion.

## Designing the Social In

The earliest contribution, [[Sofia2012-802a21dc]], stakes out an ambitious interdisciplinary claim: that Internet architecture itself should be redesigned around social capital. Its central move is to align technical centrality measures (degree, betweenness, closeness) with their sociological counterparts, and to argue that the prosumer — and later the "network prosumer" of User-centric Networks — cannot be accommodated by an asymmetric supply chain built for passive consumers. The paper reads, in retrospect, as a hopeful moment in the sociocybernetic tradition: technology and sociality are treated as co-designable, and trust, reach, and reciprocity are proposed as first-class engineering parameters. It sets a baseline optimism against which the later papers can be read as increasingly critical.

## Circulation, Observers, and the Limits of Intent

[[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] shifts the register from architecture to circulation, mobilising second-order cybernetics (von Foerster, Bateson) and Luhmann to reframe "fake news" as an observer-dependent phenomenon. Where Sofia et al. want to encode social properties into infrastructure, Giglietto and colleagues insist that meaning — including truthfulness — is produced anew at each propagation event. Their three-level model (micro judgments, meso injector/propagator matrix, macro cascades) is a paradigmatic sociocybernetic gesture: emergent global patterns arise from local, recursive acts of observation and selection. The paper thus displaces the creator-centric ontology of misinformation research and replaces it with a systems-theoretic account of hybrid media dynamics, in which "societal consensus" about truth is treated as the exception rather than the norm.

## Communication Without Understanding

[[Ptz2024-sl20]] extends the Luhmannian line into the LLM era, and in doing so sharpens what a systems-theoretic account of the internet can uniquely contribute. By distinguishing generation from understanding, and by analysing ChatGPT's failures of conversational repair, the authors show that competent communication can occur without any cognitive substrate on one side of the exchange — the chatbot's apparent fluency is parasitic on human interpretive labour. This is a striking sociocybernetic result: it dissolves the "does the machine understand?" question by relocating intelligence to the communicative system as a whole, echoing Giglietto et al.'s insistence that meaning is a system-level, not creator-level, property.

## From Social Media to Parasocial Media

[[Boyd2026-op]] closes the arc with a diagnosis that resonates uncomfortably with the earlier optimism of [[Sofia2012-802a21dc]]. The prosumer imagined a decade prior has been re-engineered by algorithmic and financial pressures into an audience for professionalised influencers; reciprocal sociality has yielded to parasocial engagement. boyd's argument is not framed in explicitly systems-theoretic terms, but it maps neatly onto the concerns of the other papers: the *communicative* character of the medium has mutated, the feedback loops between users and platforms have been captured by extractive logics, and the emergent macro-pattern (scrolling over posting) can no longer be read off from the intentions of any individual actor.

## The Line of Argument

Taken as a whole, the file tells a story in which the sociocybernetic frame becomes progressively more indispensable as the internet's own contradictions deepen. [[Sofia2012-802a21dc]] proposes to build sociality into the stack; [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] shows that once information is in circulation, no design decision at the source can determine its meaning downstream; [[Ptz2024-sl20]] demonstrates that even the interlocutors themselves may lack understanding while communication proceeds successfully; and [[Boyd2026-op]] observes that the emergent macro-form of the medium has drifted far from the reciprocal sociality its architects imagined. The theoretical backbone — observer-dependence, emergence, communication as a system-level phenomenon — is what makes this drift legible rather than merely lamentable, and it is what continues to justify sociocybernetics as a foundation for internet studies.
