# Italian Electoral Communication

## From Hybrid Media to Platform Data: Tracing the MINE Line

The Italian electoral communication line begins conceptually before its central object—social media coordination—becomes fully visible. [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] establishes the foundational premise that would organise the entire research programme: Italian political communication cannot be understood as platform-native but must be read through the *hybridity* of legacy and networked media. Studying the 2013 election's talk-show/Twitter second-screen dynamics, it shows that Twitter conversation largely followed broadcast television's agenda rather than challenging it, and that participation was narrow and dominated by "opinion" rather than agenda-building. This early finding—that Italian digital politics is structurally entangled with an older, more concentrated media system—sets the interpretive frame later papers in the line inherit: Italy as a "polarized pluralist" case (Hallin and Mancini) where party-press parallelism migrates into, and is amplified by, platform architectures.

## Partisan Attention and the Architecture of Insularity

[[Giglietto2019-882f1900]] operationalises that inheritance directly for the 2018 general election, marrying Twitter-based measures of partisan attention with Facebook engagement data to show that insularity is not evenly distributed: sources favoured by the populist M5S and League are markedly more insular than those aligned with PD or LeU, and insularity itself predicts an amplification-versus-contestation split in how audiences engage (shares vs. comments). Crucially, M5S alone shows a strategic asymmetry—positive coverage is shared, negative coverage is contested—suggesting that populist "cyber party" organisation, not populism per se, converts insularity into an attentional advantage. This paper is the empirical anchor for the line's central claim that Italian political parallelism operates not merely through outlet ideology but through audience-level attention economies mediated by platform affordances.

## From Attention to Coordination: Detecting Manipulation at Scale

The attentional dynamics documented in 2018 motivate a shift toward detecting *engineered* rather than organic amplification. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] reframes disinformation research around coordinated link-sharing behaviour across the 2018 and 2019 elections, showing that networks of pages, groups, and profiles sharing URLs near-simultaneously are disproportionately linked to problematic domains and Avaaz-flagged disinformation sources—and that the ratio of openly political to deceptively "non-political" entities within a network predicts distinct sharing strategies. This behavioural, actor-agnostic logic is then operationalised into a scalable methodology in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which builds a workflow for continuously detecting and updating coordinated account lists rather than relying on static blacklists, applied to the 2022 snap election. Its three case studies—an M5S-aligned hyperpartisan echo chamber, a clickbait network exploiting religious pages, and a religious-proselytism operation—demonstrate that coordination detection surfaces qualitatively different manipulation logics (ideological, commercial, and religious) under a single behavioural signature, extending the 2018/2019 findings into a durable, methodologically self-updating infrastructure for election monitoring.

## Methodological Turn: LLMs as Analytical Infrastructure

As the corpus of Italian political content grows across 2018 and 2022, the line pivots toward validating LLM-based tools for at-scale analysis. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] benchmarks OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo model for clustering political news shared on Facebook, finding the general-purpose LLM embedding consistently superior—extending English-language findings to Italian text and licensing LLM embeddings as the default tool for subsequent clustering work. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] builds directly on this result, integrating LLMs at three pipeline stages (classification, embedding, cluster labelling) for the 2018 and 2022 datasets, and confronts the deeper methodological problem the earlier paper leaves open: how does one *validate* a pipeline where the analytic tool itself is general-purpose and variably granular? Its answer—expert rather than crowdsourced validation, task-specific rather than global evaluation—becomes a methodological commitment for the whole line's later empirical work. [[Paci2025-ag]] complicates this optimism from a different angle, testing LLMs' capacity to interpret implicit content (implicatures, presuppositions) in Italian political speech via the IMPAQTS corpus; even top models fall well short of human interpretive ceilings, indicating that while LLMs are adequate for classification and clustering (as the line's other methods papers show), they remain limited for the subtler pragmatic reading of manipulative political rhetoric that motivates work like [[Mosca2026-yh]].

## Rhetoric, Delegitimization, and the Politics of "Fake News"

[[Mosca2026-yh]] shifts the line's attention from detecting manufactured amplification to theorising the *rhetorical* use of fake-news accusations themselves. Its Strategic Delegitimization and Selective Amplification (SDSA) model reframes "fake news" talk in Italian newspapers as a weapon of negative campaigning, selectively amplified by partisan outlets—linking back to the insularity/partisanship dynamics of [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]] and anticipating the broader question of media capture explored elsewhere in the line.

## Platform Governance, Transparency, and the Limits of Access

The later papers in the line turn from studying content and behaviour to studying the *infrastructure* that makes such study possible—and its political consequences. [[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]] uses the Meta Content Library (a DSA-enabled dataset) to show that Meta's political content reduction policy suppressed Italian MPs' Facebook reach by up to 72%, beginning roughly ten months before Meta's announced rollout and disproportionately sparing extremist accounts, which compensated through posting volume. This finding gives the coordination and insularity work upstream a new causal layer: platform policy itself can reshape the very attentional asymmetries [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]] documented as emergent. [[Philipp2026-tl]] zooms out to survey the DSA's broader effect on researcher data access, cataloguing the uneven design and eligibility of new APIs and portals—effectively theorising the methodological precondition that made the empirical study in [[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]] possible while flagging the inequities and gaps that persist. Read comparatively, [[Balluff2026-bv]]—on the Austrian "Inseratenaffäre"—extends the line's media-capture lens beyond Italy, showing with a difference-in-differences design how covert advertising arrangements between a leading politician and an outlet inflate his visibility and darken his competitors' coverage without necessarily improving his own favorability. This "pay positive to go negative" pattern offers a structurally analogous, cross-national counterpoint to the domestic dynamics of partisan attention and outlet-level parallelism the Italian papers trace, suggesting that the mechanisms Italian researchers detect via coordinated behaviour and platform policy have functional equivalents in traditional media capture elsewhere in Europe's polarized-pluralist and hybrid systems.

## The Arc

Together these eleven papers trace a coherent trajectory: from establishing that Italian political communication is irreducibly hybrid ([[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]]), through mapping how partisan attention becomes structurally unequal on social platforms ([[Giglietto2019-882f1900]]), to detecting the coordinated and often inauthentic mechanisms that manufacture such asymmetries ([[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]]), to building and critically validating the LLM-based infrastructure necessary to study these phenomena at scale ([[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]], [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]], [[Paci2025-ag]]), and finally to interrogating the platforms and rhetorical practices that govern and legitimise—or delegitimise—this entire information ecology ([[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]], [[Philipp2026-tl]], [[Mosca2026-yh]], [[Balluff2026-bv]]). The line's throughline is methodological escalation in service of a constant substantive concern: how political parallelism, once a property of national press systems, is reproduced, amplified, and occasionally engineered within platform-mediated electoral communication.
