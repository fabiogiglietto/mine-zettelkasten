# LLM-Assisted Content Analysis

## The turn to LLMs as content analysts

The corpus documents a field in the middle of retooling its basic instrument — content analysis — around large language models, and it does so with unusual self-consciousness about what is gained and lost in the process. At one pole sit papers that simply *use* LLMs to do familiar coding tasks at new scale: [[Bailard2024-pj]] fine-tunes DeBERTa to classify collective-action frames in Proud Boys Telegram messages, [[Larsson2026-ro]] uses zero-shot GPT-4 to sentiment-code a decade of Norwegian Facebook posts, [[Meher2025-qb]] fine-tunes Llama 3.1 via QLoRA for conflict-event classification, and [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] shows GPT-4o outperforming purpose-built computer-vision pipelines for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery. At the other pole sit papers that interrogate the instrument itself — asking what it means to validate, what the model is actually doing when it "codes," and what gets lost when convenience substitutes for theory. [[Balluff2026-if]] is the most explicit statement of this second stance, arguing that the field's LLM adoption has been "rapid but largely unreflective" and proposing a trade-off mindset that weighs epistemic, environmental, and infrastructural costs against gains in convenience. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] radicalizes the skepticism further, using conversation analysis and Luhmann to argue that chatbot competence is parasitic on human repair work rather than evidence of understanding — a caution that sits behind much of the validation literature below.

## Validation as its own research problem

A cluster of papers converges on validation methodology as the central unsolved problem of LLM-assisted content analysis, and several of them explicitly belong to the same collaborative Italian-election line. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] together document a pipeline for Italian political Facebook content — political classification, embedding-based clustering, LLM-generated cluster labels — and argue that each stage needs its *own* validation protocol, that crowdsourced annotators are now inadequate benchmarks because LLMs often outperform them, and that cluster "narratives" vary in granularity in ways that destabilize even the definition of what is being validated. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] pushes this insight into deductive coding proper, reframing the LLM not as a classifier to be scored against ground truth but as an "analytic collaborator" whose disagreements with a codebook expose latent theoretical assumptions — a genuinely different validation logic, where divergence is data rather than error. [[Ober2026-vd]] extends the human-in-the-loop framework to qualitative interview analysis, combining topic modeling's mathematical transparency with LLM-assisted labeling and iterative codebook refinement. [[Brown2025-jk]] complicates the picture from a fairness angle, finding that LLM-human disagreement on subjective annotation tasks is driven far more by item difficulty (label entropy) than by annotator demographics — suggesting that "bias" audits of LLM annotators need to control for task difficulty before attributing disagreement to demographic proxying. [[Paci2025-ag]] and [[DeVerna2025-dl]] supply sobering ceiling estimates from adjacent tasks: LLMs remain well below expert performance at interpreting implicit/manipulative content in Italian political speech, and even reasoning- and search-augmented models fact-check political claims poorly unless fed curated retrieval context — a finding that reinforces Balluff's warning against assuming capability gains will simply resolve validity problems. Together these papers suggest an emerging consensus: LLM-in-the-loop pipelines are usable, but only if validation is redesigned task-by-task, with domain-expert human judgment retained as the arbiter, rather than imported wholesale from either classical intercoder-reliability statistics or NLP benchmark culture.

## Comparative embedding-model studies

A tightly linked sub-line treats embeddings themselves as the object of methodological scrutiny, asking which representational choices best serve unsupervised, exploratory content analysis. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] directly compares OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian BERT-based UmBERTo for clustering political news, finding the general-purpose LLM embedding consistently more semantically coherent — a finding that generalizes claims previously made mostly for English. [[Fan2025-ut]] addresses a different failure mode of embeddings: spurious confounds like source or language dominate similarity structure, and linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this contamination cheaply without degrading out-of-domain performance, with gains concentrated where the confound explains the dominant variance direction. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends the embedding-comparison logic to the visual domain, showing that routing images through a vision-LLM's textual descriptions before embedding captures *connotative* meaning that CNN-based pipelines miss, at some cost to denotative precision but with a large gain in interpretability. [[Waight2025-al]] and [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] show what these embedding advances enable substantively: distilling texts into claims/subjects for cross-lingual narrative-similarity measurement, and asking-and-averaging LLM judgments to scale political texts on policy dimensions, both explicitly framed against the estimand-estimator mismatches that plagued earlier text-as-data methods (echoing Grimmer and King). Read together, these papers argue that embedding choice is not a neutral implementation detail but a substantive methodological decision on par with codebook design.

## From classification to structural and narrative analysis

A further set of papers uses LLM-assisted annotation as an *input* to larger structural or narrative claims about political discourse, rather than as an end in itself. [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes Greimas' Actantial Model via an LLM to measure "narrative polarisation" on YouTube, finding that video content polarizes sharply while comments homogenize surface narratives yet preserve deeper motif-level partisan divergence — a finding enabled entirely by treating LLM annotation as reliable enough to support fine-grained narrative coding at scale (with inter-coder agreement checked against human coders). [[Gerard2025-br]] and [[Arora2025-tx]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] extend this logic to cross-platform discourse mapping, multimodal framing, and unsupervised frame discovery respectively, each replacing fixed category schemes with emergent, LLM- or embedding-derived structures. [[Lee2026-je]] and [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] turn LLM inference toward respondent-level measurement — inferring political alignment from nonpolitical text, and scaling open-ended survey responses via LLM-paired comparisons — both illustrating how annotation pipelines are being repurposed for latent-trait measurement beyond manifest content coding. This arc shows the throughline from the topic's methodological core (can LLMs code reliably?) to its substantive payoff (what new phenomena become visible once they can).

## Adjacent infrastructures and boundary cases

Several papers sit at the edges of the topic, either supplying infrastructure that LLM-assisted pipelines depend on or testing the outer limits of what such pipelines can responsibly claim. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] and [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Minici2024-tf]] address data-quality and detection infrastructure — scraping distortion, coordinated-behavior detection, graph-based information-operation identification — that any downstream LLM-annotation pipeline inherits as measurement error if unaddressed. [[Triedman2025-uy]] offers a cautionary empirical case of AI-generated content at encyclopedic scale (Grokipedia), documenting derivative reproduction, ideological skew, and "LLM auto-citogenesis" that content-analysis pipelines relying on such corpora would need to detect. [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Szabo2026-rd]] move from analysis to intervention, showing LLM dialogue can durably shift conspiracy beliefs and inoculate against misinformation — evidence that the same generative capacities scrutinized for coding bias can also be harnessed as treatment. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extend the meta-methodological question to the research process itself, benchmarking whether AI coding agents can reproduce published social-science findings and whether agentic review tools can catch errors in manuscripts — both a natural extension of the validation concerns raised by [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] and [[Balluff2026-if]] into the realm of scientific practice writ large. Finally, [[Tan2024-vl]], [[Gagrcin2024-dl]], [[Ng2026-og]], [[Bruns2025-fz]], and [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] mark the topic's outer boundary: surveys and position papers (LLM annotation broadly, algorithm literacy, social theory for agentic AI) and studies (practice mapping, dark personality traits on Reddit) that share methodological kinship with the LLM-content-analysis line — annotation, embedding, or agent-based measurement — without being centrally about political discourse analysis, marking productive directions this working group's collaboration may expand into next.
