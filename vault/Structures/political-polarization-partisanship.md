# Political Polarization and Partisanship

## The shape of the field: fragmentation, method, and the search for common ground

The corpus opens onto a discipline still arguing about its own object. [[Falkenberg2026-ka]] diagnoses polarization research as disciplinarily fragmented and geographically skewed toward the United States, a complaint echoed methodologically by [[Hartmann2025-px]], whose systematic review of 129 echo-chamber studies finds that disagreement over whether filter bubbles exist stems largely from incompatible operationalizations rather than incompatible realities. [[Schemer2026-mh]] demonstrates this concretely: running 504 specification variants on the same panel data, the choice of media-slant measure and polarization operationalization swings coefficient magnitude sevenfold, though never reverses direction — a sobering lesson about "researcher degrees of freedom" that resonates with [[van-Eck2026-xg]]'s finding that depolarization *recommendations* in the literature are almost never grounded in the empirical findings that precede them, resting instead on unexamined deliberative-democratic norms. [[Trilling2024-slc8]] offers a conceptual corrective to this morass, proposing that the metaphors of "echo chamber" and "filter bubble" be replaced by a feedback-loop framework capable of explaining why reinforcing dynamics are real but rarely produce runaway radicalization. Together these pieces frame a field caught between empirical modesty and metaphorical overreach — a tension [[Unknown2026-sl3d]]'s Nature editorial and [[Esau2025-tf]]'s theorization of "destructive polarization" both try to resolve into actionable, normatively explicit research agendas.

## Echo chambers revisited: exposure, curation, and the limits of the bubble metaphor

A long empirical lineage interrogates whether echo chambers are real, and at what level they operate. [[Bakshy2015-rn]], [[Flaxman2016-lm]], and [[Barbera2015-fw]] converge on a nuanced picture: ideological segregation exists but is modest, topic-dependent, and shaped as much by individual clicking as by algorithmic curation; [[Messing2014-jc]] shows social endorsement cues can override partisan source cues entirely. Later, larger-scale work complicates this reassurance. [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]] finds Facebook segregation far exceeding earlier web-browsing estimates, rising steeply along the "funnel of engagement" from potential to actual exposure, and sharply asymmetric — a homogeneous conservative corner hosting nearly all fact-checked misinformation. Yet [[Nyhan2023-gb]]'s companion field experiment finds that mechanically reducing like-minded exposure by a third changes feed composition but produces *no* measurable attitudinal effects, a precisely-estimated null that unsettles simple bubble-to-polarization causal stories. [[Green2025-ap]]'s concept of "curation bubbles" reframes the puzzle at the story level: domain-level partisan scores mask enormous heterogeneity in which specific articles reach which partisan audiences, meaning source-level echo-chamber measures may be measuring the wrong unit altogether. [[Dehghan2026-sy]] extends this skepticism to Reddit, finding r/politics functions nothing like a cross-ideological town square despite superficial diversity, while [[Del-Vicario2016-uj]]'s percolation modeling and [[Barbera2015-je]]'s ideal-point estimation both anchor the older, more confident echo-chamber claims that these newer studies now qualify. [[Schulz2026-ts]] adds a final twist: much apparent polarization is "false" — an artifact of self-selection into posting by the already-extreme, meaning visible discourse overstates division relative to underlying attitudes, a claim structurally aligned with [[Rossini2026-jn]] and [[Karlsson2026-hd]]'s findings that *perceptions* of polarization and interference can themselves become causal forces independent of ground truth.

## Elites, parties, and the "logic of connective faction"

A second cluster shifts from audiences to elites, asking how political actors exploit — and are reshaped by — partisan media ecosystems. [[Chadwick2015-ii]]'s foundational hybrid-media-system framework, in which older and newer media logics interact rather than one displacing the other, underwrites much of this work. [[Benkler2018-lw]] and [[Bennett2018-ys]] independently diagnose an asymmetric radicalization of the American right, with Benkler's "propaganda feedback loop" locating the crisis in the insulation of right-wing media from truth-correcting norms, and Bennett extending the argument in [[Bennett2025-xs]] to theorize "digital surrogate organizations" — decentralized networks (QAnon, Stop the Steal, meme communities) that pull conservative parties toward illiberalism, arguing that technocentric disinformation research has neglected this organizational dimension. [[Knupfer2025-vt]] operationalizes this dynamic empirically: Republicans who adopted the Critical Race Theory talking point were more ideologically extreme, more networked into hyper-partisan media, and rewarded with disproportionate engagement — a "logic of connective faction" that shows factional radicalization as a measurable, incentive-driven process. [[Eady2025-vm]]'s joint ideology-estimation model corroborates this at scale, finding that the most ideologically extreme politicians share vastly more news, and that electoral safety (not just ideology) predicts polarized sharing — implicating structural features like gerrymandering. [[Tai2026-qk]] and [[Gattermann2025-yx]] extend the elite lens to misinformation-sharing checks and far-right electoral performance, while [[Rodarte2026-sl16]] and [[Rodarte2026-dk]], both set in Brazil, show parliamentarians internalizing platform "media logics": congressional substance is systematically penalized with engagement while antagonism and crisis-narration are rewarded, and during the Manaus healthcare collapse, government allies and opposition members pursued rival "epistemic authority" strategies (direct authority claims versus derivative reporting) that map directly onto the broader hybrid-media contest over who gets to narrate crisis.

## Affect, identity, and the texture of partisan community

Where the elite-level literature explains supply-side incentives, a parallel body of work examines the affective and identity-based glue that binds partisan communities from within. [[Iyengar2019-jj]]'s canonical review establishes affective polarization — partisanship as social identity generating out-group animus — as conceptually distinct from issue-based polarization, a distinction load-bearing for much of this topic. [[Marino2026-slef]] tests this empirically in a Brazilian pro-Bolsonaro Facebook network, finding affective engagement strikingly *unstable* rather than the fixed echo chamber the theory predicts, with emotional register shifting sharply around the 2023 coup attempt and Lula's inauguration — evidence that hyperpartisan communities are contested, permeable territories rather than static enclaves. [[Grusauskaite2026-po]] examines "Great Reset" discourse across six platforms through the lens of symbolic boundary work, arguing conspiracy communities cohere through reclaimed stigma and affective solidarity around personal hardship narratives more than through ideological content or platform architecture per se — a claim that dovetails with [[Nangle2026-yo]]'s account of far-right Instagram as an "affective-discursive ecology" where humor and positive affect, not hostility, do the normalizing work. [[Anwar2024-34dba628]]'s review of Facebook Reactions research corroborates the underlying mechanism at scale: anger reliably drives engagement in political contexts, especially for populist and far-right actors. [[Van_Erkel2026-mk]] identifies a cognate identity-driven bias — a "hostile misinformation effect" whereby partisans believe their own side is disproportionately targeted by misinformation — extending hostile-media-effect logic into the misinformation domain, while [[Osmundsen2021-et]] and [[Vosoughi2018-at]] anchor the affective account of *sharing* behavior itself: fake news spreads via out-party hatred and novelty rather than ignorance or bot manipulation.

## Misinformation, epistemic authority, and democratic erosion in Brazil and beyond

The Brazilian case studies form a tight sub-cluster on how misinformation belief translates into democratic harm. [[Rossini2026-jn]] and [[Rossini2026-mj]], both drawing on the same three-wave panel around Brazil's 2022 election, show electoral misinformation belief predicting rising political intolerance and eroding trust in results, with messaging apps (WhatsApp) operating as an indirect amplification channel — a finding paralleled by [[Berlinski2023-rj]]'s US experimental evidence that elite fraud claims damage electoral confidence asymmetrically among co-partisans, resistant even to fact-checks. [[Ventura2026-yc]] captures a further institutional twist: Brazil's 2024 ban on X produced a partisan "sorting ratchet," with conservative users disproportionately evading the ban and the platform's news environment shifting durably rightward — a case study in how state content moderation can inadvertently entrench the very polarization it targets, a concern that reverberates with [[Slack1787858551-sl4d]]'s account of platform-lobbied defeat of Brazil's Fake News Bill and the broader "privatization of public discourse" thesis linking platform governance to data colonialism in the Global South. [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]] reframes disinformation itself as recurring cross-media *narratives* rather than discrete claims, a conceptual move mirrored by [[Elfes2026-jb]]'s "narrative polarisation" framework, which finds that while YouTube videos on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict are sharply polarized, comment sections converge on shared narrative surfaces even as deeper "narrative motifs" preserve partisan difference — suggesting audiences may be less polarized than the content engineered to reach them, a finding that chimes suggestively with [[Schulz2026-ts]]'s false-polarization thesis.

## Comparative media ecosystems: from national outlets to transnational and alternative infrastructures

A final strand maps the outlet-level architecture of partisan media across borders. [[Heft2021-ky]] finds right-wing alternative sites building interlinked national and transnational ecologies via hyperlinking, with U.S. outlets as central hubs — a pattern [[Groebner2026-pc]] confirms independently through YouTube commenter-overlap networks across four Anglophone hyperpartisan channels, again finding Fox News occupying the structural center of a transnationalizing far-right audience. [[Zhu2026-tn]] complicates the alternative/mainstream binary comparatively: audience overlap between alternative and mainstream outlets depends on national media systems and populist parties' institutional access, producing isolated "alternative enclaves" in Sweden but blurred boundaries in Austria and France. [[Yoo2026-ev]] and [[Bouchafra2026-ts]] examine how hyperpartisan and populist actors weaponize discursive and visual tools — the "fake news" label and securitizing meme templates, respectively — to redraw Hallin's spheres of consensus and deviance, while [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]] and [[Giglietto2017-4375de2f]] provide grounding Italian cases, showing populist parties (M5S) exploiting insular Twitter attention economies and Facebook amplification/contestation dynamics, and dissenting hashtag communities articulating counter-narratives against dominant solidarity framings after Charlie Hebdo. [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] extends the pattern to science communication, finding UK newspapers' citation of COVID scientists tracking outlet political orientation rather than scientific consensus. Methodologically, [[Gaisbauer2025-by]], [[Mosleh2024-op]], [[Sarmiento2025-as]], [[Zhao2025-ny]], and [[Lee2026-je]] point toward the field's computational frontier — multi-level, cross-platform, and LLM-assisted approaches to mapping news circulation, framing, hashtag contestation, and even inferring hidden partisan identity from ostensibly nonpolitical text — while [[Brady2026-ln]]'s Bluesky field experiment closes the loop back to intervention, showing that redesigned "diversified extremity" algorithms can reduce moralized/emotional content amplification and improve norm perceptions without denting platform enjoyment, offering one of the few experimentally validated levers against the dynamics this whole literature otherwise diagnoses but struggles to remedy.
