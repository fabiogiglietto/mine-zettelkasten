# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## The Arc: From Behavioral Signatures to Contested Definitions

The CIB/CLSB line of work begins from a methodological wager articulated most clearly by [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] and its intellectual predecessor [[Starbird2019-qv]]: that disinformation research should pivot away from adjudicating the truth-value of content or the identity of "bad actors," and toward detecting the *behavioral signature* of coordination itself—near-simultaneous, repeated, structurally anomalous collective action. This founding move, grounded in Bennett and Segerberg's connective-action framework and François's Actors-Behaviors-Content model, licenses the entire CooRnet/CLSB research program. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] operationalize this as an iterative "detect→monitor→reseed" workflow, demonstrated on Italian COVID-19 misinformation, while [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] formalizes the workflow for continuous list-updating, applied to the 2022 Italian election and surfacing heterogeneous actors—from M5S hyperpartisan networks to clickbait-driven religious pages to covert proselytism bots. [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]] situates this empirical program within broader shifts in political communication wrought by big data, networked publics, and micro-targeting.

A parallel genealogy runs through Twitter-based detection: [[Keller2019-nk]] establishes, via the rare ground-truth case of South Korea's NIS astroturfing campaign, that coordination traces—not bot-like individual features—are the decisive signal, and that principal-agent frictions leave detectable fingerprints even in well-resourced operations. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] extends this by reconceptualizing coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, showing on UK 2019 election data that the *most* coordinated communities are often small activist fringes, not mainstream parties, and that coordination is orthogonal to automation. This continuum-thinking, and the insistence that coordination ≠ inauthenticity ≠ harm, becomes a throughline that the later theoretical and survey papers will crystallize.

## Methodological Diversification: Multiplex, Temporal, Cross-Platform, and Causal Tools

A dense cluster of 2025–2026 papers extends detection methodology along several axes. Temporal and multiplex integration is addressed by [[Iannucci2025-eg]] (time-decayed, multi-layer collaboration networks) and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] (a systematic comparison of multimodal operationalizations, finding multiplex community detection the best-balanced approach)—both responding to the realization that single-modality, fixed-window methods are brittle and evadable, a concern echoed in [[Yang2025-iv]]'s critique of timing-dependent signals on Facebook. Graph-foundation-model approaches ([[Minici2024-tf]]) and cross-platform discourse embeddings ([[Gerard2025-br]]) push detection toward generalizability across campaigns and platforms, the latter identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed most narrative migration between Truth Social and X. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet generalizes the CooRnet lineage into a platform-agnostic R package validated against the Keller et al. NIS ground truth, while [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers a field-wide survey reconciling fragmented platform and academic definitions into a unified actors/actions/intent framework—explicitly building on Nizzoli's orthogonality thesis.

Platform expansion is a major 2025–2026 preoccupation: coordination detection is extended to TikTok's video-first ecosystem ([[Luceri2025-tr]]), Bluesky ([[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]]), WhatsApp's encrypted Brazilian political groups ([[Kansaon2025-id]]), and Korean news comment sections across two decades ([[Kim2026-br]]). A separate methodological turn moves from *detecting* coordination to *quantifying its causal influence*: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] model coordinated accounts' placement within retweet cascades, finding—provocatively—that real-world coordinated accounts achieve influence far below optimal or even greedy benchmarks, close to random placement, challenging assumptions that CIB is pivotal to diffusion. This skeptical note resonates with [[Appel2026-qr]]'s large-scale Meta-FIES study showing that most exposure to deceptive networks occurs indirectly via reshares by non-network accounts, and that correlational associations between exposure and downstream beliefs evaporate under confounder control—a sobering empirical check on inflated claims about CIB's political effects. [[Pante2025-pq]] extends this skepticism methodologically, re-litigating prior claims of inter-state IO collaboration with more rigorous controls.

## Conceptual Reckoning: What *Is* Coordination, and Does It Matter?

A cluster of theoretically ambitious papers grapples with the definitional instability that practitioners have long finessed. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposes a principal-agent, rational-choice typology for attribution under data scarcity. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] empirically demonstrates that identical coordination signatures underlie influence operations, legitimate media syndication, and grassroots meme-sharing alike, tracing how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed over time from "false amplifiers" to "adversarial threat actors"—a regulatory retreat that lets much manufactured attention persist on-platform. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] pushes further, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category untethered from misinformation per se, unifying a pro-Putin propaganda network, a gambling promotion ring, and poorly moderated adult-content groups under a shared "Zombie Internet" logic of affordance exploitation and human/synthetic blur. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents this gambling-promotion vector in detail, showing an exponential, AI-driven escalation in visual persuasion tactics after ChatGPT's launch—extending the vera.ai team's long-running interest in coordinated commercial exploitation alongside political manipulation. [[Rogers2026-cy]] complements this conceptual work with the VERA-AI-affiliated notion of "post-truth spaces," mapping Moldovan Facebook discourse around the Russia-Ukraine war to show that fact-checking leads often come from "borderline" bridge clusters rather than core post-truth nodes themselves—directly relevant to the Alert monitoring system's operational logic.

Song2025-yh and [[Song2025-yh]]... (noting CLSB's moral agnosticism) exemplifies a related strand demonstrating that CLSB is not intrinsically malicious: both pro- and anti-vaccine communities deploy coordinated sharing, with divergent credibility profiles and national framings (UK safety-skepticism vs. US freedom-rhetoric). This finding dovetails with [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]]'s methodological caution that platform-imposed anonymization thresholds (the 100-share rule in Meta's URL Shares Dataset) can themselves manufacture spurious cross-national "signals," a necessary warning for any comparative CLSB research relying on such datasets.

## State, Platform, and Infrastructural Case Studies

A substantial empirical wing applies CLSB/CIB methods to specific authoritarian and electoral contexts. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] examine Russian and Belarusian regime-aligned Telegram/Twitter coordination, the latter extending connective action theory to "connective counteraction," showing pro-Navalny networks are centralized while pro-government networks are modular (team-based astroturfing). [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] extends this to China's COVID-19 protests, finding social media functioning as dual-use infrastructure for both mobilization and repression. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] documents Serbia's SNS party deploying decentralized, mimetic CIB that evades standard detection—explicitly calling for new frameworks beyond the bot-farm paradigm, a call [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s organizational history of the IRA complicates empirically by showing the "troll factory" was itself a labor-market-embedded media company rather than a pure disinformation cell. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage, foregrounding the political-economic infrastructure of manipulation. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] traces CitizenGO's centrally coordinated, Spain-headquartered transnational anti-gender network, finding coordinated link-sharing functioning as "symbolic glue" across language communities—bridging CIB methodology with social-movement mainstreaming literature also visible in [[Rothut2026-or]]'s account of Querdenken's entanglement with far-right Telegram networks and [[Bailard2024-pj]]'s Granger-causal analysis of Proud Boys framing and offline violence.

Several papers extend coordination analysis to platform-specific vernaculars and visual/multimodal content: Bastos2025-ol analyzes visual self-presentation in troll-farm profile imagery for attribution; [[Karo2026-dn]] documents jihadist evasion tactics on TikTok as "everyday extremism"; [[Graham2026-fb]] provocatively destabilizes the human/bot binary itself through Reddit's "fungi" phenomenon, arguing authenticity is culturally negotiated rather than technically determined—a direct challenge to the detection paradigm's ontological assumptions. [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows that Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of election-audit accounts produced network-wide ripple effects *not* attributable to CIB, reinforcing that human disengagement, not bot suppression, drove discourse change. Transnational media-propaganda studies—[[Copland2025-em]], [[Askanius2026-de]], [[Groebner2026-pc]]—map how hyperpartisan outlets (Sky News Australia, Swedish AIN YouTubers, Fox News) function as nodes bridging national and international far-right ecosystems, complementing CLSB's actor-network logic with platform-specific audience analysis.

## Emerging Frontiers: Generative AI, Agentic Swarms, and Counter-Infrastructure

The newest stratum of the collection turns toward LLM-driven coordination and its detection/mitigation. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates emergent coordination among networked LLM agents, finding that mere mutual awareness—short of explicit collective deliberation—suffices to produce real-world-like coordination signatures (network density, narrative homogeneity, synchronized amplification). [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy-forum warning about "malicious AI swarms" capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning training data, proposing layered governance responses. On the counter-manipulation side, [[Jaidka2026-sl34]]'s field experiment demonstrates that structurally suppressing elite visibility—rather than targeting bad actors—reduces misinformation susceptibility, reframing the problem as one of exposure architecture. [[Renault2025-uh]] interrogates X's Community Notes as a bridging-algorithm alternative to professional fact-checking, finding persistent partisan asymmetries, while [[Miller2026-cp]] describes the infrastructural extension of Community Notes via an open AI-writer API—both bearing directly on the VERA-AI Alert system's monitoring and verification mission. Rounding out the methodological toolkit, work on hashtag hijacking ([[Zhao2025-ny]]), bot-driven agenda-building ([[Arceneaux2026-xk]]), inauthentic account use in Romanian EU elections ([[Oprea2025-lf]]), German electoral advertising ([[Slack1783507532-sl10]]), scientific-discourse coordination ([[Efstratiou2026-ij]]), firestorm dynamics ([[Slack1783507463-sl24]]), co-shared mainstream-news repurposing ([[Goel2025-iq]]), and the still-emerging "appropriation of events" thesis ([[FitzGerald2025-nv]]) collectively testify to how thoroughly the coordination-detection paradigm has diffused—while the field's own surveys ([[Mannocci2026-kc]]) and critiques ([[Thiele2025-ol]], [[Rogers2025-sl8f]]) insist that the next phase must resolve what, exactly, coordination is for, and for whom it counts as harm.
