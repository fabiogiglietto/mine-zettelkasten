# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour Detection: Tracing a Field From Tool to Theory

### Foundational methods and the CooRnet/CooRTweet lineage

The spine of this topic is a methodological program built around Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) and its descendants. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] establishes the founding move: shift disinformation research away from content-veracity or actor-blacklist detection toward an ecological, action-based account of coordination, operationalized through near-simultaneous, repeated URL sharing on Facebook during the 2018 and 2019 Italian elections. This logic is implemented as software in [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] (CooRnet), which formalizes an iterative Actors-Behaviors-Content cycle, and extended in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (the MINE-FACTS project) to support real-time fact-checking during the COVID-19 "infodemic," and again in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which converts static coordinated-account lists into a continuously updating workflow validated on the 2022 Italian election. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] generalizes this entire lineage with CooRTweet, abstracting coordination to "repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object," making the detection logic platform- and modality-agnostic and validating it against the ground-truth South Korean NIS dataset of [[Keller2019-nk]]. That paper itself is foundational to the whole topic: it introduces political astroturfing as a disinformation category distinct from bot detection, showing that *coordination signatures*—not individual account traits—are the most robust and hardest-to-hide signal, a methodological commitment that virtually every later paper inherits.

Parallel but complementary innovations push past co-sharing-of-links toward multimodal and temporal sophistication. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] reframes coordination as a continuous, community-specific spectrum rather than a binary label, demonstrating that coordination and automation are orthogonal—an insight that recurs throughout the corpus. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five multimodal operationalizations (monomodal, flattening, multiplex) on the 2019 UK election dataset, finding multiplex community detection the best-balanced strategy; [[Iannucci2025-eg]] extends this with a time-aware multiplex collaboration model validated against 26 benchmark datasets. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the vulnerability of timing-based detection directly, proposing speed/frequency statistical regularities as evasion-resistant signals, while [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on the decentralized platform Bluesky. [[Minici2024-tf]] (IOHunter) and [[Gerard2025-br]] (CANE/t-CANE) represent the field's move toward foundation-model and embedding-based generalization—detecting information operations across platforms and campaigns with minimal labeled data, with Gerard's "bridge user" findings on Truth Social/X narrative migration showing how structural, discourse-based modeling can recover cross-platform influence invisible to interaction graphs. [[Luceri2025-tr]] signals the next frontier: adapting network-similarity detection to TikTok's video-first ecosystem, where text-centric assumptions break down.

### Conceptual stock-taking: definitions, attribution, and critique

A cluster of theoretical papers works to consolidate and critique this expanding toolkit. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers the most comprehensive synthesis, reconciling fragmented platform and academic definitions into a framework of actors, synergic actions, and intent, and insisting that coordination and inauthenticity/harm are orthogonal dimensions—formalizing what Nizzoli and Keller demonstrated empirically. [[Thiele2025-ol]] tackles the "attribution problem" head-on, arguing that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing a rational-choice typology linking campaign scale/elaborateness/disguise to a principal's resources and stakes. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] both push back against equating coordination signatures with malicious intent: Rogers shows that influence operations, mainstream media groups, and political activists can produce *identical* coordination signatures on Facebook, tracing how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed over time to exclude much of what it originally targeted; Giada's "Deceptive Information Operations" framework similarly argues that misinformation is neither necessary nor sufficient for harm, and that a "Zombie Internet" condition—where engagement-driven ranking blurs human and synthetic participation—unites propaganda, gambling, and poorly-moderated-group operations under one structural logic. [[Rogers2026-cy]] situates CIB as one of five "problematic information" spaces (alongside post-truth spaces, alternative influence networks, fake-news engagement, and participatory propaganda), useful for fact-checkers mapping Moldovan Facebook ahead of elections. Undergirding all of this is [[Starbird2019-qv]], which remains the field's touchstone for insisting that information operations are *participatory*—collaborative "work" performed with and through organic crowds—making the orchestrated/organic boundary perpetually blurry, a theme [[Graham2026-fb]] revisits playfully via Reddit "botting," arguing authenticity itself is a culturally negotiated performance rather than a technical property.

### Platforms, elections, and geographies

The empirical core applies these tools across elections and platforms worldwide. On Facebook, [[Song2025-yh]] and [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] show CLSB's double edge: coordinated networks spread both vaccine misinformation and credible public-health messaging, and later, industrially-scaled AI-generated gambling promotion exploiting Meta's paid/organic regulatory gap. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces how coordinated "Alternative Influence Network" actors and legacy media jointly amplify COVID-skeptic narratives in Italy, while [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification across Romanian party pages in the 2024 EU election. [[Appel2026-qr]] offers the largest-scale corrective: analyzing Meta's own 49 deceptive networks from the 2020 US election, finding reach was concentrated in a handful of networks, driven mostly by non-network reshares, and that correlational claims about persuasive effects collapse once pre-exposure confounders are controlled—a sobering empirical check on inflated narratives of CIB's influence.

Beyond Facebook, the topic ranges into Telegram ([[Kuznetsova2025-nu]], [[Bailard2024-pj]] on Proud Boys mobilization), WhatsApp ([[Kansaon2025-id]] linking Brazilian coordinated groups to real-world Supreme Court attacks), Twitter/X ([[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] on pro-Navalny/pro-government counter-mobilization, [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] on China's COVID protests, [[Simeone2025-vo]] on deplatforming's ripple effects, [[Pante2025-pq]] skeptically re-examining inter-state coordination claims), YouTube ([[Copland2025-em]], [[Askanius2026-de]]), and Korean news comments ([[Kim2026-br]]). Several papers extend into state-level and civil-society actors: [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] documents Serbia's SNS mimicking grassroots support to evade detection; [[Righetti2025-slf9]] maps CitizenGO's centrally-coordinated transnational anti-gender network; [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage; [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] and Bastos2025-ol dig into the organizational and visual anatomy of Russian troll factories.

### Measuring impact, not just detection

A newer strand interrogates whether detected coordination actually *matters*. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] formalize post-hoc influence quantification in retweet cascades, finding real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election achieve far less influence than optimal or even random placement would predict—suggesting CIB is often resource-poor and poorly targeted rather than pivotal. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complements this by showing that deplatforming's effects operate through network-structural "ripple effects" unrelated to CIB (only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds), while [[Keller2019-nk]] already demonstrated the NIS campaign's negligible discourse-level impact despite professionalization. Together these findings counsel caution against assuming detected coordination equals outsized influence.

### Horizon: generative AI and swarms

The newest papers anticipate how LLMs reshape coordination itself. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates LLM-agent information operations, showing that mere mutual awareness among agents—without explicit deliberation—produces near-maximal coordination, network density, and narrative convergence. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance warning about "malicious AI swarms" capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning training data at democratic scale. [[Tuters2026-vo]] closes the loop conceptually, arguing that platform grammars increasingly generate conspiratorial alignment through pattern and repetition rather than coherent belief—an infrastructural account of how coordination (real or perceived) becomes politically potent regardless of underlying intent, echoing Starbird's founding insight that the hardest problem is not detecting orchestration but understanding its entanglement with organic human meaning-making.
