# Computational Text Methods & LLMs

## Validating LLMs as classifiers and annotators of political text

The methodological core of this literature is the question of whether LLMs can reliably stand in for human coders on political classification tasks, and under what conditions. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is the most direct statement of the problem: it documents an "LLMs-in-the-loop" pipeline for Italian Facebook political content (classification, embedding, cluster labeling) and argues that each stage needs its own tailored validation protocol, since crowdworkers no longer provide an adequate ground truth once LLMs outperform them. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] operationalizes one piece of that pipeline, showing text-embedding-3-large beats UmBERTo for clustering Italian political news, with GPT-4o-mini itself used as the coherence rater — an early instance of LLM-as-validator becoming inseparable from LLM-as-method. [[Ober2026-vd]] extends the human-in-the-loop logic to qualitative interview coding, using topic modeling for transparency and LLMs for label generation, explicitly arguing that mathematically grounded clustering should anchor LLM-assisted qualitative work. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] pushes validation furthest upstream, benchmarking whether AI coding agents can reproduce published social-science findings at all — a meta-validation exercise that bears directly on how much trust the field can place in LLM-mediated pipelines generally.

Domain-specific fine-tuning appears as an alternative to prompting: [[Bailard2024-pj]] fine-tunes DeBERTa to classify collective-action frames in extremist Telegram discourse at scale, and [[Meher2025-qb]] fine-tunes Llama 3.1 via QLoRA for conflict-event classification, both showing that efficient, resource-light adaptation can outperform zero-shot prompting on domain-specific coding schemes. [[Larsson2026-ro]] and [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] instead validate zero-shot LLMs directly against human coders — GPT-4 for Norwegian Facebook sentiment, GPT-4o for face recognition and person-counting in German campaign Instagram imagery — with both papers reporting that general-purpose multimodal/text LLMs meet or exceed specialized supervised baselines. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] complicates the classification-by-prompting story for fact-checking specifically: raw LLM knowledge and even web search are insufficient, and only curated retrieval-augmented context yields usable accuracy, with search-enabled models additionally showing a left-leaning citation skew. [[Brown2025-jk]] takes annotation validation in a different direction, asking not whether LLMs match human labels but whether they diverge systematically by annotator demographics, concluding that item difficulty (label entropy) dwarfs demographic bias as a predictor of LLM-human disagreement. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys this whole terrain, organizing the literature on LLM-generated annotation and synthesis around generation, assessment, and downstream use.

## Embeddings, clustering, and semantic representation

A parallel strand asks how embeddings should be constructed and cleaned before they are used for clustering or similarity. [[Fan2025-ut]] shows that pretrained sentence embeddings carry confounds (source, language) that bias document-similarity estimates, and demonstrates that linear concept erasure (LEACE) is a cheap, broadly applicable fix — a foundational corrective for any downstream clustering work in this topic cluster, including [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends embedding-based clustering into the visual domain, arguing that CNN pipelines capture only denotative image content and that vision-LLMs, by generating connotative textual descriptions prior to embedding, better capture socially meaningful (and interpretable) clusters of Instagram climate imagery. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes "practice mapping" via vector embeddings of network actions as a way past the "hairball" problem in multimodal social-network visualization, situating embedding methods as a general-purpose alternative to conventional network analysis. [[Sarmiento2025-as]] and [[Arora2025-tx]] both push against predefined frame categories — the former proposing an unsupervised ML/network/NLP pipeline for emergent frames in polarized discourse, the latter extending frame extraction across text, image, and cross-modal cues in news coverage of gun violence — signaling a shared methodological turn from fixed schemes toward emergent, LLM- or embedding-derived category structures.

## Scaling ideology, narrative, and framing at scale

Several papers use LLMs or embeddings not to classify discrete categories but to place texts or actors on continuous latent dimensions. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking instruction-tuned LLMs to locate a text on a policy dimension, and averaging many responses, recovers established ideological benchmarks across languages — a strikingly simple validation of prompt-and-average as a scaling method, echoed methodologically by [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]], which pairs LLMs with paired-comparison techniques to scale open-ended survey responses into latent traits. [[Eady2025-vm]] and [[Lai2024-to]] pursue ideology estimation through behavioral rather than prompted signals — joint sharing-based scaling of media, politicians, and the public, and Reddit-linking-based scaling of YouTube videos, respectively — offering an important non-LLM counterpoint: latent-variable estimation from revealed behavior remains a live alternative (or complement) to LLM self-report scaling, and [[Lee2026-je]] bridges the two by showing LLMs can infer political alignment directly from nonpolitical text, exploiting exactly the kind of lexical correlates these scaling models rely on.

Narrative- and frame-level measurement gets its own LLM-driven toolkit. [[Waight2025-al]] formalizes "narrative similarity" as a distinct estimand from lexical or topical overlap and builds an LLM-distillation-plus-SBERT pipeline validated against text reuse, topic modeling, and Relatio, showing each alternative catastrophically under- or over-detects narrative diffusion relative to the LLM approach. [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes "narrative polarisation" using an LLM applying Greimas' actantial model to YouTube video/comment data, explicitly validating LLM-human inter-coder agreement against a benchmark comparable to human-human agreement. [[Scalco2026-bd]] takes a non-LLM but complementary measurement approach, defining and detecting "information voids" via time-series anomaly detection on demand/supply signals — part of the same broader project of turning previously qualitative or metaphorical constructs (voids, echo chambers, narratives) into quantifiable, validated pipelines, a project [[Trilling2024-slc8]] argues should replace the vague "echo chamber"/"filter bubble" metaphors with a formal feedback-loop framework altogether.

## Detecting coordination and inauthentic behavior

A distinct methodological cluster concerns coordinated/inauthentic behavior detection, an area where validated, generalizable tools matter for downstream substantive claims. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] introduces CooRTweet, a platform-agnostic R package built on a minimal definition of coordination, validated against the Keller et al. NIS dataset. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both interrogate the operationalization choices behind coordination detection — the former adding temporal decay and multiplex layering to Newman's collaboration model, the latter systematically comparing five multimodal operationalizations to expose a trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness. [[Minici2024-tf]] moves toward foundation-model-style generalization with IOHunter, combining language and graph representations to detect IO drivers across heterogeneous campaigns and out-of-distribution settings. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] offers a skeptical counterpoint from information-cascade modeling, finding that real coordinated accounts achieve far less influence than optimal or greedy benchmarks predict — a reminder that detection methods and effect-size claims are separable questions.

## Limits, biases, and critical perspectives on LLM-based methods

Several papers interrogate what LLM-based methods get systematically wrong or whose knowledge they encode. [[Balluff2026-if]] is the most explicit methodological critique in the set, cataloguing reproducibility, environmental, and bias trade-offs of unreflective LLM adoption in communication research and arguing for a "least resource-intensive adequate method" heuristic — a frame that implicitly organizes much of this topic's other work (e.g., when fine-tuned encoder models such as [[Bailard2024-pj]] or [[Meher2025-qb]] outperform prompting). [[Paci2025-ag]] empirically demonstrates one such limit: LLMs struggle with pragmatic phenomena (implicature, presupposition) central to manipulative political discourse, well below expert human ceilings. [[Waight2026-ts]] traces a structural bias pathway, showing state media control seeps into training data and produces measurable pro-regime skew in commercial LLM outputs — a caution for any study using LLMs as neutral coders of politically contested content. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] provides a theoretical grounding for these empirical limits, arguing via conversation analysis and Luhmannian systems theory that chatbot "competence" is parasitic on human repair work rather than genuine understanding — a useful corrective to overclaiming LLM comprehension in any of the annotation or classification tasks above. [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] similarly finds a gap between validated psychological constructs and their computationally observable linguistic traces, reinforcing that surface text signals do not straightforwardly recover latent constructs even when self-report does.

## Persuasion, trust, and downstream social effects of LLMs

A related set of studies uses validated LLM pipelines to study LLMs' effects on people rather than as neutral measurement tools. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically decomposes what makes conversational AI persuasive, isolating information density as the key mechanism and documenting a persuasion–accuracy trade-off with direct relevance to how LLM-generated content might be used (or misused) in political communication. [[Costello2024-bg]] shows personalized LLM dialogue durably reduces conspiracy belief, using fact-checker validation of the model's own claims as part of its methodological apparatus. [[Gilardi2026-hw]] and [[Suk2026-ai]] turn to audience-side reception, the former experimentally showing AI-generated/assisted news is rated no differently in quality than human-written news pre-disclosure, the latter proposing a multilevel framework for trust in generative AI drawing on media-trust scholarship. [[Allen2025-ot]] reviews a platform-independent LLM-reranking experimental design for studying social media exposure effects, situating it against the Meta-partnered Facebook and Instagram Election Study as a methodological response to closing platform access.

## Data infrastructure, reproducibility, and the changing research environment

Underlying all of the above is the question of what data these methods can even be applied to. Bastos2025-ya and [[Bruns2026-pn]] chart the shift from the open Twitter-API era to today's restrictive "clean room" platform-data regimes (e.g., Meta Content Library), with the latter showing how these environments structurally disadvantage qualitative, cross-platform, and Global South research. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] and [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] address adjacent data-quality problems in web-tracking and ad-based survey recruitment, both showing that measurement infrastructure choices (scraping timing/environment, platform ad-targeting mechanics) introduce systematic, non-random biases that downstream LLM or embedding analyses would otherwise inherit silently. [[Fan2026-af]] proposes a complementary shift in analytic ambition, arguing digital trace data should be modeled as time-evolving sequences rather than aggregated cross-sections, and finds neural embedding models among the more promising (if data-hungry) tools for this. [[Triedman2025-uy]] offers a case study in what happens when an AI system itself becomes the object of this kind of large-scale comparative content analysis, finding Grokipedia derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically skewed toward unreliable sources on controversial topics. [[Ng2026-og]] and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] look forward to a next-generation validation problem: as agentic AI systems (including AI peer reviewers) become both objects and instruments of communication research, existing single-task evaluation paradigms — and social theory itself — must be adapted to multi-agent, population-level dynamics.

## Substantive applications anchored in validated pipelines

Finally, a set of papers show these methods deployed on live substantive questions, serving as demonstrations of validity-in-use as much as findings: [[Balluff2026-bv]] and [[Balluff2026-ev]] apply large-scale automated content analysis (paragraph-level actor visibility/favorability; named-entity co-occurrence networks) to media-capture and alternative-media questions in Austria and Germany respectively, both relying on the same automated-classification infrastructure discussed above to make quasi-experimental and relational claims tractable at corpus scale. Together with the earlier persuasion, framing, and coordination studies, these applications close the loop this topic traces: from validating whether LLMs and embeddings can classify, annotate, or cluster political text at all, to what happens once researchers trust them enough to build empirical claims on top.
