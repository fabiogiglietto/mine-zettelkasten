# Computational Methods & LLMs for Social Media Research

## The shape of the field

The papers gathered here trace a discipline retooling itself twice over: once around large language models as annotation and classification engines, and again around the infrastructural collapse of the platform APIs that computational social science had come to depend on. Read together, they describe a methodological moment in which LLM-in-the-loop pipelines are rapidly professionalizing (validation protocols, embedding comparisons, fine-tuning recipes) at precisely the moment when the underlying data supply — Twitter/X, Facebook, YouTube — is becoming scarcer, pricier, and more legally contested. A third throughline, detecting coordinated and inauthentic accounts, sits at the intersection of these two currents: it is simultaneously a computational methods problem and a case study in why platform data access matters.

## LLM-in-the-loop pipelines for political discourse

The most concentrated methodological contribution is a set of papers that treat LLMs not as black-box oracles but as one stage in a multi-step, human-validated pipeline. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] makes this explicit, documenting a three-stage OpenAI-based pipeline (fine-tuned classifier, embedding-based clustering, LLM-generated cluster labels) for Italian election Facebook data, and arguing that each stage needs its own validation protocol rather than a single blanket evaluation — a methodological reflexivity echoed in [[Ober2026-vd]]'s hybrid topic-modeling-plus-LLM workflow for qualitative interview coding and in [[Larsson2026-ro]]'s use of GPT-4 zero-shot sentiment classification (with human validation) on a decade of Norwegian populist Facebook posts. [[Bailard2024-pj]] shows the stakes of getting this right: a fine-tuned DeBERTa model classifying collective-action frames in over 500,000 Proud Boys Telegram messages becomes the empirical backbone for a Granger-causality argument linking online rhetoric to offline violence. [[Meher2025-qb]]'s ConflLlama extends this logic to conflict-event classification, showing that QLoRA-based fine-tuning of open-weight models can rival specialized tools like ConfliBERT on consumer hardware — a democratization argument that resonates with [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]]'s finding that prompted GPT-4o outperforms purpose-built computer-vision pipelines for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery.

Other papers push LLM classification into new measurement territory: [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] proposes "asking and averaging" — repeatedly prompting an LLM to place a text on an ideological dimension — as a lightweight alternative to Wordscores-style scaling, while [[Lai2024-to]] recovers video-level ideology for YouTube content from Reddit sharing patterns. [[Lee2026-je]] demonstrates a more unsettling capability: LLMs can infer users' political alignment even from ostensibly nonpolitical text, exploiting subtle cultural lexical cues, which the authors frame as both a methodological advance and a privacy risk. That risk calculus recurs in [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]], which use conversational AI as an experimental treatment for persuasion research rather than a text-classification tool, revealing a persuasion-accuracy trade-off and belief-shifting effects among skeptics respectively — results that sit uneasily alongside the null or fragile effects reported in the older-style megastudy [[Voelkel2026-lc]] and the scaled Instagram field experiment [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]].

Not every LLM application succeeds cleanly. [[Paci2025-ag]] finds that current LLMs cannot reliably interpret implicatures and presuppositions in Italian political speech, exposing a pragmatic competence gap that pure classification accuracy metrics would miss. [[Brown2025-jk]] complicates fears of demographic bias in LLM annotation, showing that disagreement with human annotators is driven far more by item difficulty (label entropy) than by annotator demographics — a finding in tension with the more skeptical stance of [[Balluff2026-if]], which catalogs reproducibility, bias, and environmental costs across text-analysis, synthetic-data, and simulation uses of LLMs in political communication. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys this expanding methodological space more broadly, while [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] extends LLM-assisted measurement to open-ended survey scaling via paired comparisons, and [[Alizadeh2026-es]] pushes furthest of all, benchmarking whether AI coding agents can autonomously reproduce published social-science findings — with striking success rates that nonetheless reveal sycophantic specification-search risks when prompted for confirmation.

## Embeddings, representation, and multimodal meaning

A parallel cluster interrogates what embedding models actually encode and how that shapes downstream clustering and similarity measures. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] directly compares OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, finding the general-purpose commercial embedding consistently more semantically coherent — a result feeding directly into the pipeline validated in [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]. [[Fan2025-ut]] tackles a subtler problem: embeddings encode confounders like source or language that distort document-similarity estimates, and shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this contamination cheaply and without degrading out-of-distribution performance. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends the representational question into the visual domain, arguing that vision-language models generating connotative textual descriptions of images produce more socially meaningful (and more interpretable) clusters than CNN-based pipelines — a visual-first sensibility shared by [[Gardam2025-er]]'s methodological proposal for Instagram climate-denial discourse and by [[Arora2025-tx]]'s multi-modal framing analysis of gun-violence news coverage.

Narrative-level measurement is emerging as a distinct sub-problem within this cluster. [[Waight2025-al]] argues that narrative similarity — whether two texts make the same claims about the same events — is conflated in prior work with lexical overlap or topical similarity, and proposes an LLM-based claim-distillation pipeline that outperforms text-reuse, topic-modeling, and Relatio baselines when tracking the cross-source diffusion of the Ukraine "biolabs" story. [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes Greimas's actantial model via an LLM to measure "narrative polarisation" in YouTube discourse on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, finding that comments harmonize surface narratives across partisan camps even as deeper narrative motifs preserve divergence. [[Sarmiento2025-as]] and [[Gaisbauer2025-by]] gesture toward similar ambitions — unsupervised, multi-level framing detection, and a "political cartography" of news sharing that moves beyond outlet-level partisan scores — while [[Sadler2025-vu]] offers a theoretical corrective from narratology and hermeneutics, arguing disinformation research's loose use of "narrative" and its true/false binary obscures texts that are referentially and ethically problematic without being straightforwardly false.

## Detecting coordinated and inauthentic behavior

Coordination detection is the topic's most densely populated methodological sub-literature, and it shows a clear trajectory from single-signal, single-platform approaches toward multiplex, temporal, and platform-agnostic frameworks. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both argue that coordination is inherently multimodal — across retweets, mentions, hashtags, URLs — and that naive flattening of these layers loses structurally important information; [[Iannucci2025-eg]] adds a temporal decay kernel to weight synchronicity, while [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically benchmarks five operationalizations against the 2019 UK election, finding multiplex community detection the most balanced compromise between integration and inclusiveness. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet operationalizes a minimal, object-agnostic definition of coordination (repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object) precisely so the tool survives platform API volatility, validating it against the South Korean NIS dataset also used as ground truth in [[Kim2026-br]]'s two-decade, explainable-ML analysis of state-linked trolling on Naver News — which finds moral condemnation, not overt promotion, is trolls' dominant and most effective rhetorical register. [[Yang2025-iv]] targets a specific evasion tactic, showing that manipulators can trivially alter post-timing but not the statistical regularities of sharing speed and frequency, while [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Luceri2025-tr]] push detection into foundation-model territory (graph + language models generalizing across campaigns) and into TikTok's video-first ecosystem respectively, arguing text-centric methods do not transfer straightforwardly.

Several papers complicate the assumption that coordination equals influence. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models the counterfactual influence achievable by coordinated nodes in retweet cascades and finds real-world coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve influence far below optimal or even greedy benchmarks — closer to random placement — suggesting CIB may be less pivotal than assumed. [[Thiele2025-ol]] steps back to theorize the "attribution problem" itself, proposing a rational-choice typology linking observable campaign features (scale, elaborateness, disguise) to a covert principal's resources and stakes, explicitly critiquing platform-derived concepts like Meta's "coordinated inauthentic behavior" for conflating inauthenticity with manipulative intent. [[Graham2026-fb]] pushes this critique further still, using Reddit's mushroom-emoji "Anus_Fungi" phenomenon to argue the human/bot binary itself is analytically unstable — authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance, not a technical property — while Bastos2025-ol opens a new detection modality entirely, mining the visual self-presentation of troll-farm profile images for country-of-origin signatures.

## The eroding data infrastructure

A large contingent of papers documents, and reacts to, the collapse of open platform data access that makes all of the above harder to do reproducibly. [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]] quantifies the scale of the loss bibliometrically — a "eulogy" showing 25% median annual growth in Twitter-based research through 2022 followed by a 13% decline in 2024 — while [[Freelon2024-sc]] and Bastos2025-ya periodize the broader history of platform API regimes, and [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] and [[Giglietto2025-ed60bc90]] track the shift from "Wild West" APIs through a brief Academic Golden Age to today's commercial paywalls and DSA-mediated access. [[Yang2026-tq]]'s systematic review confirms the downstream effect empirically: social-media-based social science publishing tripled over 2010–2022 before plateauing and declining, still overwhelmingly concentrated on Twitter/X and Facebook despite their declining share of public platform use. [[Pierri2025-hm]], [[Peters2026-mo]], and [[Lukito2026-nb]] focus on the EU's Digital Services Act as the emergent regulatory alternative to platform philanthropy, documenting uneven implementation, contested definitions of "data quality," and a "price, proficiency, or permission" triage that continues to exclude under-resourced and non-Western researchers. [[Heiss2026-qv]] frames the underlying problem structurally, arguing platforms' exclusive control of data creates industry-influence risks analogous to tobacco or pharma research capture.

Several papers demonstrate concretely how fragile or distorted platform-mediated data collection actually is. [[Rieder2025-ju]]'s audit of YouTube's search API finds a severe temporal-decay effect that makes the tool "forgetful by design," while [[Ulloa2024-jm]] shows that even seemingly neutral web-scraping introduces ~34% content distortion relative to in-situ browser capture, systematically biased by content category. [[Efstratiou2025-gs]]'s comparison of algorithmic versus chronological Twitter feeds and [[Bouchaud2026-lr]]'s reconstruction of X's recommender embedding space both exemplify a workaround: using data donation and reverse-engineering to study platform behavior independent of official API cooperation, a strategy [[Allen2025-ot]] explicitly frames as the necessary future of "platform-independent" experimentation given closing API access. Older infrastructural work — [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]] on big data's reshaping of political communication practice, [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] on Facebook-ad-based survey recruitment for hard-to-reach populations, and [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]]'s discovery that Meta's URL Shares Dataset threshold artifact masquerades as cross-national social signal — collectively establish that data-quality skepticism long predates the current API crisis.

## Reflexivity and validation of the computational toolkit

Running alongside the tool-building papers is a steady current of methodological self-scrutiny. [[Balluff2026-if]] and [[Matias2025-px]] bookend a debate about whether LLM adoption and AI evaluation more broadly are being pursued with adequate epistemic humility — the former cataloguing trade-offs researchers routinely ignore, the latter arguing that public and lived-experience participation can materially improve (not just legitimate) AI science. [[Hartmann2025-px]]'s systematic review of 129 echo-chamber studies and [[Luhring2025-od]]'s audit of the NewsGuard trustworthiness database make a shared argument: much apparent empirical disagreement in the field is an artifact of inconsistent conceptualization and operationalization rather than of the underlying phenomena, a diagnosis [[Schemer2026-mh]] confirms directly via specification-curve analysis, showing that 504 different ways of measuring media partisan slant and polarization substantially change coefficient magnitudes (though rarely direction). [[Trilling2024-slc8]] and [[Oswald2025-km]] offer conceptual correctives in the same spirit — replacing "echo chamber" with feedback-loop dynamics, and reframing visible online discourse as an unrepresentative "tip of the iceberg" relative to a silent majority — while [[Frischlich2025-vn]] argues that misinformation's dominant analogies (virus, warfare, pollution) each obscure part of a genuinely multilevel complex system. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] and [[Ng2026-og]] push reflexivity toward theory: the former uses Luhmann and conversation analysis to argue ChatGPT's dialogic competence is parasitic on human repair work rather than understanding, and the latter argues agentic multi-agent AI needs social theory as a structural prior rather than being modeled as isolated task-completing agents — a claim empirically illustrated with data from an AI-only social network. [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] closes this cluster with a cautionary validation case: linking validated dark-personality questionnaires to real Reddit text, the authors find self-reports of toxic behavior track observable language far more reliably than personality traits themselves do — a sobering note on the gap between psychological constructs and their textual traces.

## Substantive extensions: platforms, narratives, and ideology in the wild

Finally, a broad set of papers puts these computational tools to work on live substantive questions, often serving as demonstrations of method as much as contributions to theory. Several study the platform layer itself: [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues the "social media" paradigm is dissolving into algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private spheres, and AI-mediated communication; [[Rieder2026-pp]] traces how deplatforming fails to contain Andrew Tate's "ambient ideology" on YouTube; [[Knupfer2025-vt]] shows how the Critical Race Theory issue activated a "logic of connective faction" linking Republican elites to hyper-partisan media; and [[Green2025-ap]] demonstrates that source-level partisan-audience scores mask substantial story-level heterogeneity, a point echoed in [[Goel2025-iq]]'s finding that mainstream outlets are routinely repurposed to support misleading narratives. [[Gerard2025-br]]'s cross-platform embedding framework (CANE/t-CANE) identifies a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed most narrative migration between Truth Social and X, while [[Smith2025-kc]] proposes a causal mechanism ("attention brokerage") for triad-transitivity formation, and [[Bruns2025-fz]] offers "practice mapping" as a general alternative to hairball network visualization. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] and [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] extend computational methods to causal and mixed-methods studies of antivaccine Twitter content's epidemiological impact and influencer-driven toxicity respectively, while [[Fan2026-af]] proposes treating digital trace data as temporal user-sequences rather than cross-sectional aggregates. Media- and discourse-level studies of AI itself — [[Nguyen2026-vm]] on agentic framing of LLMs in news, [[Dodds2026-df]] on AI hype as journalistic infrastructure, [[Richter2026-bt]] on shifting AI-discourse stakeholders on Twitter — and studies more tangential to the political-discourse core, such as [[Volpe2026-um]] on micro-influencer platform labor and [[Ferrara2026-io]]'s general-purpose ECHO community-detection architecture, round out a corpus whose center of gravity is nonetheless clear: computational and LLM-based methods are being built, stress-tested, and reflexively questioned in near-real time, against the backdrop of a data-access regime that can no longer be taken for granted.
