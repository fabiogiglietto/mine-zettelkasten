# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The Line of Argument: From CLSB to a General Theory of Coordination

The corpus filed here traces a two-decade arc from a single methodological insight—that *coordination itself*, independent of content veracity, is a tractable and diagnostic signal of manipulation—to an increasingly contested and generalized field. The foundational move is made in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which argues that disinformation research should pivot from content- and actor-detection toward detecting *coordinated collective action*: networks of pages, groups, and profiles sharing identical URLs near-simultaneously. This paper, alongside [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]]'s broader diagnosis of how big data has blurred organizational boundaries and enabled fringe amplification, establishes Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) as both a concept and an operational method, later formalized in the CooRnet tool ([[Slack1783507719-slc1]]) and demonstrated at scale during the COVID-19 infodemic in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and its Italian election follow-up [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which introduces a continuously-updating workflow to counter the problem of static, quickly-obsolete actor lists.

### Extending and Generalizing the Method

A second wave of work generalizes CLSB beyond Facebook link-sharing into a platform-agnostic science of coordination detection. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet reconceives coordination as the repeated near-synchronous sharing of *any* uniquely identifiable object, enabling cross-platform and multi-modal analysis and validating itself against the South Korean NIS troll dataset. This abstraction is pushed further methodologically by [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]], both of which argue that coordination is inherently multiplex—spanning hashtags, retweets, mentions, and URLs simultaneously—and that naive flattening of these modalities loses structurally important signal; Iannucci additionally shows that *temporal* decay weighting improves detection robustness against dilution tactics. [[Yang2025-iv]] contributes a timing-independent signal (speed/frequency regularities) precisely to counter manipulators' growing awareness that post-timing is an evadable tell. Meanwhile, graph-foundation-model approaches ([[Minici2024-tf]]) and platform-agnostic discourse-embedding methods ([[Gerard2025-br]]) push detection toward greater generalizability across campaigns and platforms, the latter identifying a tiny population of "bridge users" who seed cross-platform narrative migration between Truth Social and X. Together these papers mark CLSB's evolution from a bespoke Facebook technique into a family of formally specified, benchmarked, platform-independent methodologies—a trajectory that culminates in [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey, which synthesizes fragmented industry and academic definitions into a unified conceptual scheme built on actors, synergic actions, and intent, and insists that authenticity and harmfulness are orthogonal axes requiring separate treatment.

### The Platform Frontier: TikTok, WhatsApp, Bluesky, Telegram

A cluster of papers extends coordination detection into ecosystems the original CLSB method was never built for. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first architecture of TikTok, where user-similarity networks must be reimagined around video content rather than text or links; [[Karo2026-dn]] complements this from a qualitative angle, cataloguing five vernacular evasion tactics (audio camouflage, meme infiltration, emoji codes) that jihadist propagandists use to exploit TikTok's recommendation system—underscoring that detection and moderation face an adversarial, creatively evolving opponent regardless of ideology. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts rapid-spread-network detection to the encrypted, closed architecture of WhatsApp in Brazil, showing coordinated messaging translating directly into doxxing and street mobilization around January 8, 2023. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on the decentralized platform Bluesky, while [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] apply coordination detection to authoritarian Telegram/Twitter ecosystems in Russia, Belarus, and China, revealing dual-use infrastructures where the same tools serve both protest mobilization and state-aligned repression—a theme extended by [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]]'s explicit extension of Bennett and Segerberg's connective-action framework to "connective counteraction," contrasting Navalny protesters' centralized networks with pro-government astroturfing's more modular, team-based structure.

### Contesting What Coordination *Means*—and What It's *For*

A crucial strand of the corpus turns reflexively on the CIB/CLSB paradigm itself, questioning whether coordination signatures are sufficient markers of inauthenticity or influence. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] is pivotal here: applying CooRnet-style detection broadly, it finds that influence operations and legitimate media outlets can produce *identical* coordination signatures, arguing that Meta's operational definition of CIB has narrowed opportunistically from "false amplifiers" to "adversarial threat actors," letting much manufactured attention persist unchallenged—a typology of gambling networks, hijacked groups, political activists, and media operations that is echoed and deepened in [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]'s "Deceptive Information Operations" framework and its diagnosis of a "Zombie Internet" where human and synthetic activity become indistinguishable. [[Song2025-yh]] makes the normative point explicit: CLSB is ideologically agnostic, capable of powering both anti-vaccine misinformation and legitimate NHS/CDC public-health messaging, so actor alignment and context—not coordination per se—determine harm. [[Thiele2025-ol]] goes furthest in critiquing the field's core vocabulary, arguing "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing an attribution-focused rational-choice typology centered on principals' scale, elaborateness, and disguise decisions instead. This skepticism is empirically radicalized by [[Di-Marco2025-aa]], which models coordinated accounts' influence within retweet cascades and finds real-world CIB actors achieve influence far below optimal placement—resembling random rather than strategic positioning—suggesting coordination may be less pivotal to diffusion than the field assumes. [[Pante2025-pq]] similarly re-audits prior claims of inter-state operation collaboration, finding that rigorous control-dataset benchmarking undercuts earlier conclusions. Together, this cluster constitutes an internal corrective movement: coordination detection is necessary but insufficient, and must be paired with actor/content analysis, formal null models, and causal caution—concerns that also motivate [[Appel2026-qr]]/[[noauthor_undated-bm]]'s finding that most of Meta's 2020 "deceptive networks'" reach came from *organic* reshares by unaffiliated users, and that correlational links to downstream beliefs evaporate under proper controls.

### Novel Actors, Modalities, and Escalating Threats

Several papers probe the human and organizational substrate of coordination. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT reconstruction of the St Petersburg IRA's labor structure and [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s reframing of Philippine influence operations as political *brokerage* both insist that coordination is fundamentally an organizational and economic phenomenon, not merely a network signature—complementing [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]]'s account of Serbia's SNS party, whose decentralized, mimetic CIB evades detection precisely by avoiding the centralized bot-farm signatures the CooRnet-style literature was built to catch. Bastos2025-ol extends detection into the visual domain, mining troll-farm profile imagery for attributable signatures, while [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents how generative AI has driven an exponential, ChatGPT-era escalation in coordinated gambling-promotion networks, exploiting regulatory gaps between paid and organic content. This escalatory logic reaches its speculative extreme in [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]], which respectively warn about and simulate "malicious AI swarms"—LLM-driven multi-agent systems that autonomously reproduce coordination mechanisms (network density, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification) without human orchestration, suggesting that mere mutual awareness among agents is sufficient for emergent coordination nearly indistinguishable from deliberate collusion. [[Graham2026-fb]] provides a valuable counterweight from participatory-culture theory, showing that apparent "bot invasions" (Reddit's mushroom-emoji spam) can be human-driven imitative play rather than malicious automation—reinforcing the volume's recurring caution against collapsing "coordinated," "inauthentic," and "harmful" into a single category.

### Coordination as Infrastructure for Mainstreaming and Cross-Platform Flow

A final thematic strand connects coordinated network structures to the broader mainstreaming and transnationalization of radical politics, extending CLSB-adjacent network analysis to movements rather than campaigns. [[Rothut2026-or]] traces how Querdenken's Telegram infrastructure structurally embedded protest sympathizers in far-right and conspiracist networks; [[Righetti2025-slf9]] finds centralized coordination and cross-language "symbolic glue" behind CitizenGO's anti-gender network; and [[Askanius2026-de]], [[Kakavand2026-kt]], [[Groebner2026-pc]], and [[Copland2025-em]] map how far-right actors in Sweden, Germany, and the Anglophone world exploit platform-specific affordances to build alternative influence networks and transnational audiences—Sky News Australia's YouTube/Facebook dual strategy and the Fox News-centered Anglophone hyperpartisan ecosystem both echoing the volume's central insight that coordination signatures are inseparable from the affordances of the platform on which they are measured. [[Bailard2024-pj]] and [[Simeone2025-vo]] bookend this concern with the online–offline nexus: framing dynamics predicting Proud Boys violence, and deplatforming's demonstrated capacity to collapse an entire community's authority structure—reminding that the ultimate stakes of coordination research are not networks for their own sake, but their downstream relationship to real-world mobilization, repression, and harm. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]], [[Zhao2025-ny]], [[Arceneaux2026-xk]], and [[Kim2026-br]] extend the coordination lens to science communication, hashtag contests, agenda-building, and cross-national troll rhetoric respectively, while [[De_Leon2025-qn]] and [[Rogers2026-cy]] gesture toward the next conceptual frontier—"networked exposure" and "post-truth spaces"—arguing that even ordinary, non-fabricated content becomes politically weaponized once embedded in coordinated circulatory infrastructures, a fitting endpoint for a research program that began by asking not *what* is being said, but *how* it moves.
