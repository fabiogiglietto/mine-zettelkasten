# Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour

## From CLSB to a general theory of coordination

The line begins with [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research away from content- and actor-level detection toward an ecological, behaviour-based diagnostic: coordinated link sharing behaviour (CLSB), operationalised through near-simultaneous, repeated URL sharing across pages, groups, and profiles. This single methodological move—detect the *pattern of collective action* rather than the veracity of any given post—becomes the throughline for everything that follows. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalises CLSB into the CooRnet package and tests it directly against professional fact-checkers during COVID-19, showing that a content-agnostic coordination signal surfaces problematic material at a higher hit rate than routine manual verification, while also documenting the tactical repertoire (link laundering, first-comment link hiding, religious-group cover) that coordinated actors use to evade enforcement. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then solves the staleness problem inherent to any list-based approach, proposing an iterative workflow that seeds from known coordinated accounts and continuously discovers new ones, applied to the 2022 Italian election to reveal not only political hyperpartisan networks but click-economy and religious-proselytism operations that a purely political framing would miss.

This lineage matures into two complementary generalisations. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] abstracts CLSB's core insight—repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object by a stable set of accounts—into CooRTweet, a platform- and modality-agnostic tool validated against the South Korean NIS ground-truth dataset. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] instead turns the CooRnet method reflexively on itself, showing that coordination *signatures* alone (full-scale, evenly distributed link sharing) cannot distinguish influence operations from legitimate media outlets or grassroots activists, and building a typology of "manufactured attention" that tracks how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed from broad "false amplifiers" to a much stingier "adversarial threat actors" category—leaving most coordinated attention-manufacture legally on-platform.

## VERA-AI and continuous, applied monitoring

The vera.ai-era papers show the methodology moving from retrospective case study to a live alerting infrastructure. [[Rogers2026-cy]] situates "post-truth spaces" within a taxonomy of five problematic-information formations (alongside alternative influence networks, fake-news engagement spaces, CIB campaigns, and participatory propaganda) and proposes an influence metric based on a cluster's proximity to mainstream discourse, demonstrated on Moldovan Facebook ahead of the 2024 elections. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] pushes further, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category that deliberately loosens the requirement of falsity or automation, showing via three Facebook case studies (pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, hijacked unmoderated groups) that structurally identical coordination mechanics underlie epistemic, economic, and social-normative harms alike—diagnosing a "Zombie Internet" condition in which engagement-driven ranking makes human and synthetic participation indistinguishable. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] extends this monitoring apparatus to visual content, tracing an exponential, ChatGPT-triggered surge in coordinated gambling promotion and documenting how generative AI recombines established persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, cultural localisation) into industrial-scale synthetic imagery that exploits Meta's asymmetric governance of paid versus organic content.

## Contesting and hardening the paradigm

A second cluster interrogates the CLSB/CIB paradigm's assumptions and technical limits. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "inauthenticity" is the wrong axis entirely—manipulative *intent*, not identity deception, is what matters normatively—and proposes a principal-agent, rational-choice typology for attributing campaigns from observable scale, elaborateness, and disguise. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] performs the field-wide reconciliation this critique calls for, surveying industry and academic definitions to isolate three components (actors, synergic actions, intent) and four dimensions (authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance), explicitly decoupling authenticity from harm. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] tackle a shared technical weakness: single-modality, time-agnostic detection. Mannocci systematically compares five ways of integrating multiple co-action layers (retweets, replies, hashtags, URLs), finding a fundamental trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness, with multiplex community detection as the most balanced compromise; Iannucci independently converges on a similar multiplex-plus-temporal-decay architecture, validated against 26 labelled datasets. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the timing-dependence problem from another angle, showing that the statistical regularities of sharing *speed and frequency* survive manipulation of raw timestamps. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] delivers the most skeptical result in this cluster: modelling coordinated accounts' influence within real retweet cascades from the 2019 UK election, it finds their actual placement resembles random assignment far more than any strategically optimal configuration, suggesting CIB's causal weight in diffusion may be overstated. [[Pante2025-pq]] applies comparable rigor to reported inter-state collaboration, arguing that claims of coordination between state actors need re-testing against proper control datasets. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] push detection toward foundation-model and cross-platform generalisation—IOHunter combining language models with graph neural networks for few-shot IO-driver detection, and CANE/t-CANE reconstructing discourse networks from shared narrative-cluster membership rather than platform-specific interaction traces, identifying a tiny population of "bridge users" who seed most narratives migrating between Truth Social and X. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] and [[Luceri2025-tr]] extend the whole apparatus into new platform territories—real-time bot coordination on Bluesky and video-first coordination on TikTok—arguing each requires non-trivial methodological adaptation rather than direct transplantation of Twitter/Facebook-era techniques.

## Empirical applications: platforms, regimes, and issue arenas

A large empirical wing applies coordination-detection logic to specific political and health contexts, often confirming CLSB's founding intuition that coordination is agnostic to ideology or motive. [[Song2025-yh]] compares pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB in the UK and US, finding coordination itself is not inherently malicious but takes on different credibility profiles depending on which actors deploy it. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts coordination detection to WhatsApp's closed ecosystem in Brazil, linking coordinated messaging directly to real-world mobilisation including the January 8 attacks. [[Oprea2025-lf]] and [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] examine European elections (Romania, Germany) through hyperactive-user amplification and paid/coordinated advertising respectively. Authoritarian and hybrid-regime contexts recur throughout: [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] maps pro-government Telegram coordination in Russia and Belarus; [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] contrasts centralized pro-Navalny mobilisation with modular, team-based pro-government astroturfing on Russian Twitter; [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] extends this to China's COVID-19 protests; [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS exemplifies a subtler, mimicry-based digital authoritarianism that evades standard bot-farm detection; and [[Kim2026-br]] builds an explainable troll-detection pipeline across two decades of Korean news comments, finding condemnation rhetoric—not overt boosting—drives suspected foreign-influence engagement. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] steps behind the curtain organisationally, using leaked CVs to reconstruct the IRA's labour-market structure and timeline, complicating the standard "2016 election" origin story. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] and [[Copland2025-em]] trace transnational amplification networks—CitizenGO's anti-gender coordination and Sky News Australia's dual-track propaganda pipeline—while [[Askanius2026-de]] and [[Kakavand2026-kt]] map how German and Swedish far-right actors adapt coordinated/networked practice to each platform's distinct affordances. [[Graham2025-gp]], Bastos2025-ol, [[Zhao2025-ny]], and [[Gaw2025-ru]] round out this wing with, respectively, an infrastructural reading of #IStandWithPutin, visual-identity attribution of troll-farm accounts, an analysis of hashtag hijacking as attention warfare, and a political-brokerage reframing of Philippine influence operations as intermediary labour rather than pure propaganda. [[Simeone2025-vo]] offers a valuable counter-note: examining Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of election-audit accounts, it finds negligible CIB in the network yet dramatic authority-structure collapse, underscoring that not all consequential network interventions are about coordinated inauthenticity at all. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]] use Meta-provided exposure data from the US 2020 FIES study to show that deceptive-network reach is highly concentrated, driven mainly by reshares from unaffiliated accounts, and that its measurable political effects mostly evaporate once pre-exposure user characteristics are controlled—a sobering corrective to assumptions about coordinated networks' persuasive power. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] finds a comparable dynamic in COVID-19 science communication: a small, non-automated, ideologically homogeneous coordinated network disproportionately amplifies contrarian credentialed experts, with news coverage following rather than leading this Twitter-based amplification.

## The AI frontier and adjacent discourse-level extensions

Two forward-looking papers reorient the field toward the coordination threats of tomorrow. [[Orlando2025-ul]] shows, via generative agent-based modelling, that LLM agents given nothing more than shared awareness of teammates spontaneously reproduce the coordination signatures characteristic of real IO campaigns—network densification, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification—without explicit deliberation. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance argument, warning that agentic AI "swarms" could fabricate consensus and evade synchrony-based detectors at a scale and adaptivity beyond anything the CLSB tradition was built to catch, and calling for provenance infrastructure and international monitoring to match.

Finally, a handful of papers sit at the topic's edge, using coordination or networked-discourse concepts to explain offline consequences rather than to detect coordination itself: [[Bailard2024-pj]] traces how online framing dynamics among the Proud Boys predict offline violence; [[Rothut2026-or]] shows how the Querdenken protest movement's Telegram network structurally embedded moderate publics within far-right and conspiracist milieus; and [[Karo2026-dn]] catalogues the adversarial creative tactics jihadist supporters use to evade TikTok moderation. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] gestures, more thinly, toward the broader claim uniting all of these threads—that informational manipulation campaigns persist by continually appropriating new events—confirming that whatever detection methodology this research programme perfects, it is chasing a target that keeps reinventing itself.
