# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The methodological core: from CLSB to generalized coordination detection

The topic's foundation is a sequence of methodological papers that built, tested, and generalized a specific behavioral signature of manipulation: coordinated link sharing behaviour (CLSB). [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] is the originating move — it argues that disinformation research had over-invested in content veracity and bad-actor detection, and proposes instead an ecological, action-based approach: look for networks of pages, groups and profiles sharing identical URLs near-simultaneously and repeatedly. Applied to the 2018 and 2019 Italian elections, this yields the first empirical grounding for what Facebook would later call "coordinated inauthentic behavior" (CIB), showing coordinated networks are markedly more likely to spread problematic domains. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalizes this into a working tool (CooRnet) for a live use-case — Italian COVID-19 fact-checking — introducing the crucial insight that static lists of bad actors decay quickly, since malicious operators mutate, get banned, or launder links through reputable outlets. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] pushes this into a full monitoring *workflow*: starting from a seed list, it iteratively surfaces new coordinated accounts across the 2022 Italian election, uncovering three structurally distinct case studies (a partisan echo chamber, a clickbait-for-profit religious network, and a proselytizing bot operation) that demonstrate the method's actor-and-content agnosticism.

This lineage culminates in two generalization papers. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] introduces CoorTweet, reframing coordination as a minimal, abstract pattern — repeated near-synchronous sharing of *any* uniquely identifiable object — freeing detection from platform- or modality-specific constraints and validating it against the South Korean NIS dataset. Contemporary network-science work extends this generalizing impulse further: [[Iannucci2025-eg]] adds temporal decay and multiplex layering to improve robustness against dilution tactics, while [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically maps the trade-offs among monomodal, flattened, and multiplex operationalizations of multimodality, finding multiplex community detection the most balanced. Together these papers mark a shift from a single Italian-election tool to a mature subfield asking *how* coordination should be formally represented — a question also taken up by newly emerging platform-specific extensions: [[Luceri2025-tr]] for TikTok's video-first ecosystem, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] for real-time detection on Bluesky, [[Yang2025-iv]] for timing-evasion-resistant Facebook detection, and [[Minici2024-tf]]'s graph-foundation-model approach (IOHunter) for cross-campaign generalization.

## Contesting the boundaries of "coordination" and "inauthenticity"

A second cluster interrogates what coordination signatures actually mean, pushing back against treating detection as equivalent to unmasking malice. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] is the pivotal intervention: applying CooRnet broadly, it finds that influence operations and legitimate media outlets can produce *identical* coordination signatures, proposing a typology of "manufactured attention" and tracking how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed from "false amplifiers" to "adversarial threat actors" — leaving much coordinated activity unaddressed. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] extends this with the concept of Deceptive Information Operations, arguing that misinformation is neither necessary nor sufficient for harm; what matters is the convergence of deceptive intent, affordance exploitation, and coordination, producing a "Zombie Internet" where genuine and synthetic engagement become indistinguishable. [[Thiele2025-ol]] takes the critique furthest theoretically, arguing that "CIB" as a category conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing instead a rational-choice attribution framework centered on scale, elaborateness, and disguise as signals of a hidden principal's resources and stakes. [[Song2025-yh]] complicates the moral valence empirically, showing CLSB techniques applied to UK/US vaccine discourse capture both misinformation-spreading and credible public-health amplification — coordination is ideologically agnostic. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] offers a further corrective from information-cascade modeling: observed coordinated accounts in UK election cascades achieve influence far below optimal, resembling random placement, suggesting CIB may be structurally less pivotal than assumed. [[Graham2026-fb]]'s Reddit "botting" study and [[Rogers2026-cy]]'s post-truth-spaces framework (positioning CIB as one of five overlapping "problematic information" spaces) both further destabilize a clean authentic/inauthentic binary, arguing authenticity is a negotiated, contextual, or continuum property rather than a binary technical fact.

## Case studies: elections, protest, and authoritarian information environments

A large empirical wing applies coordination detection to specific national and platform contexts, building comparative texture. Election-focused studies include [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] and [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s German case, [[Oprea2025-lf]]'s Romanian European-election analysis of hyperactive Facebook users, [[Appel2026-qr]]/[[noauthor_undated-bm]]'s large-scale Meta-collaboration study of US 2020 deceptive networks (distinguishing CIB from financially-motivated operations and showing reshares by unaffiliated accounts drive most reach), [[Kansaon2025-id]]'s WhatsApp-based account of Brazilian coordination linking to real-world protest and violence, and [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s reconceptualization of Philippine influence operations as political brokerage. Authoritarian and protest contexts form another strand: [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] examine Russian and Chinese protest/repression dynamics respectively, both extending connective-action theory to "connective counteraction"; [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] maps pro-government Telegram coordination in Russia/Belarus; [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] documents Serbia's SNS party mimicking grassroots support to evade detection; and [[Rothut2026-or]] traces how Germany's Querdenken movement became structurally embedded in far-right and conspiracist Telegram networks — a mainstreaming dynamic echoed in [[Askanius2026-de]]'s Swedish far-right YouTube network and [[Righetti2025-slf9]]'s account of CitizenGO's centrally-coordinated transnational anti-gender campaign. State-level attribution work includes [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s organizational history of the IRA, Bastos2025-ol's visual-identity analysis of troll farms, [[Kim2026-br]]'s two-decade study of Korean troll rhetoric, and [[Pante2025-pq]]'s methodologically skeptical re-examination of claimed inter-state coordination.

## Adjacent phenomena: bots, platforms, and emerging AI threats

Several papers extend the topic toward algorithmic amplification, financially-motivated manipulation, and generative AI. [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examine social bots as agenda-builders and hashtag hijackers respectively; [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces coordinated contrarian amplification of anti-consensus science experts and its downstream effect on news coverage; [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Graham2025-gp]] examine how legacy and state media exploit platform infrastructure for transnational propaganda. Commercial exploitation appears in [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s study of AI-generated gambling promotion, which — alongside [[Orlando2025-ul]]'s simulation of emergent LLM-agent coordination and [[Schroeder2026-im]]'s speculative treatment of "malicious AI swarms" and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]]'s note on manipulation campaigns' adaptive persistence — signals where this line of inquiry is heading: toward a future in which generative AI intensifies, rather than replaces, the coordination dynamics this whole research program was built to detect. Finally, [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE framework, modeling cross-platform narrative diffusion via shared discursive clusters rather than direct interaction ties, points to a next methodological frontier — reconstructing coordination and influence in a fragmented, API-poor platform ecosystem where the original CLSB toolkit's assumptions no longer hold.
