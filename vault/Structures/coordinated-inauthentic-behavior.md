# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## Methodological origins: from CLSB to CooRnet

The line of work gathered here begins with a deceptively simple empirical move: instead of chasing content or actors, track the *behavior* of near-simultaneous link sharing. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] lays the methodological and theoretical foundation, arguing that disinformation research should shift from content-veracity and bad-actor detection toward an ecological, action-based account of coordinated collective behavior, and demonstrating on the 2018/2019 Italian elections that coordinated networks disproportionately amplify problematic domains. This becomes an operational tool in [[Slack1783507719-slc1]], which formalizes CooRnet as an iterative implementation of François's Actors-Behaviors-Content cycle, and in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], which extends the same logic to COVID-19 "infodemic" monitoring for Italian fact-checkers, documenting how coordinated networks evolve tactics (first-comment link placement, link laundering, religious-group cover) to evade enforcement. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] pushes the workflow toward continuous, self-updating list maintenance, showing how a small seed of known coordinated accounts can be used to surface hundreds of new ones during the 2022 Italian election, uncovering ideologically, economically, and religiously motivated operations side by side. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Slack1783507463-sl24]] apply the CLSB-derived dataset to specific interpretive puzzles — the role of "Intellectual Dark Web" amplifiers in Italian vaccine-skeptic discourse, and the multiple sociological logics (marketing crisis vs. political protest) that can underlie superficially similar online firestorms.

## Generalizing, refining, and benchmarking detection

A second wave of the research line abstracts away from Facebook-specific link-sharing toward general-purpose coordination detection. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] is pivotal in reframing coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, showing on the 2019 UK election that automation and coordination are largely orthogonal and that the most tightly coordinated clusters are often small activist groups rather than mainstream partisans. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] generalizes the CooRnet logic into CooRTweet, a platform- and modality-agnostic tool validated against the ground-truth NIS dataset from [[Keller2019-nk]]. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five ways of operationalizing *multimodal* coordination (monomodal, flattening, multiplex), establishing that multiplex community detection best balances integration and inclusiveness, while [[Iannucci2025-eg]] adds a temporal-decay kernel to multiplex models and benchmarks it against 26 labeled datasets, and [[Yang2025-iv]] proposes evasion-resistant detection based on the statistical regularities of sharing speed rather than manipulable timing signals. [[Minici2024-tf]] brings graph foundation models (IOHunter) to bear on cross-campaign generalization, and [[Mannocci2026-kc]] caps this methodological arc with a survey that reconciles fragmented platform and academic definitions into a unified actors–actions–intent framework, explicitly decomposing the field into detection and characterization tasks. Attempts to extend detection to new platforms recur throughout: [[Luceri2025-tr]] for TikTok's video-first ecosystem, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] for real-time bot coordination on Bluesky, and [[Gerard2025-br]] for cross-platform discourse bridging via latent narrative clusters (CANE/t-CANE) between Truth Social and X.

## Defining coordination: authenticity, intent, and the limits of "CIB"

Running alongside the technical refinements is a persistent conceptual argument about what coordination *means* and whether "coordinated inauthentic behavior" is even the right unit of analysis. [[Starbird2019-qv]] opens this strand by recasting information operations as participatory, collaborative "work" that blends orchestrated and organic action, a framing echoed and complicated by [[Rogers2025-sl8f]], which shows that identical coordination signatures can characterize influence operations, mainstream media groups, and legitimate political activists alike, and documents how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed from "false amplifiers" to "adversarial threat actors" over time. [[Thiele2025-ol]] formalizes this critique theoretically, arguing that CIB conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing a rational-choice typology for attributing campaigns to principals based on scale, elaborateness, and disguise. [[Graham2026-fb]] extends the authenticity question to the vernacular edge case of Reddit "botting," arguing authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance rather than a technical property, while [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a broader category unifying propaganda, gambling promotion, and poorly-moderated group exploitation under shared structural mechanisms rather than shared motive. [[Rogers2026-cy]] (a vera.ai output) situates CIB as one of several adjacent "problematic information" spaces — alongside alternative influence networks, fake-news engagement spaces, and participatory propaganda — and proposes influence-by-proximity-to-mainstream as a practical metric for fact-checkers.

## Attribution, organization, and the people behind the networks

A smaller but important set of papers steps back from network signatures to the organizational substrate of manipulation. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] uses leaked CVs to reconstruct the Internet Research Agency as a conventional media-labor organization rather than a purely ideological unit, complicating assumptions baked into much of the detection literature. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reconceives influence operations in the 2022 Philippine elections as political brokerage, foregrounding the political-economic infrastructures that commission and sustain campaigns, a theme also present in [[Kansaon2025-id]]'s account of coordinated WhatsApp networks escalating into real-world mobilization in Brazil. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] documents a more elusive case — Serbia's SNS party — where CIB mimics authentic grassroots support precisely to evade the detection paradigms built for centralized bot farms, a concern structurally related to [[Keller2019-nk]]'s foundational demonstration that coordination traces, not bot-like individual behavior, are what expose astroturfing, even when overall persuasive impact is modest.

## Does coordination matter? Impact and skepticism

A recurring countercurrent in this literature asks whether detected coordination actually moves opinion or behavior. [[Eady2023-xg]] and [[Appel2026-qr]], both drawing on the Facebook/Twitter election-study collaborations, find that exposure to IRA content and to 49 deceptive networks in the 2020 US election was heavily concentrated among already-committed partisans and largely swamped by domestic political communication, with most correlational effects vanishing after controlling for pre-exposure characteristics. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] formalize this skepticism graph-theoretically, showing that real coordinated accounts in UK election cascades achieve influence far below optimal or even randomly-matched benchmarks — a sobering counterweight to alarmist readings of CIB's power. [[Kim2026-wg]] complicates this picture by showing that *average* null effects can mask large, real harms concentrated in specific targeted subpopulations (geo-racially targeted voter-suppression ads), reinforcing the methodological lesson that aggregate exposure metrics are an unreliable guide to consequence.

## Domains of application: elections, health, and manufactured attention

The methodology's portability across substantive domains is one of its defining features. Electoral applications span Italy ([[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]]), Germany ([[Klinger2023-qu]], [[Slack1783507532-sl10]]), Romania ([[Oprea2025-lf]]), the UK ([[Nizzoli2020-cf]], [[Mannocci2025-ig]], [[Di-Marco2025-aa]]), Brazil ([[Kansaon2025-id]]), the Philippines ([[Gaw2025-ru]]), South Korea ([[Keller2019-nk]], [[Kim2026-br]]), and the US ([[Appel2026-qr]], [[Eady2023-xg]], [[Kim2026-wg]]). Health communication forms a second major cluster: [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Song2025-yh]] trace coordinated vaccine discourse in Italy, the UK, and US, finding that CLSB is ideologically agnostic — equally capable of scaling misinformation and credible public-health messaging — while [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] extends this to the broader science-communication ecosystem, showing a coordinated, non-automated network amplifying contrarian credentialed experts ahead of news coverage. A third cluster, visible especially in the newest vera.ai-affiliated work, examines "manufactured attention" for financial rather than ideological ends: [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] document how coordinated Facebook gambling networks exploit regulatory gaps between paid and organic content, with generative AI driving an exponential, structurally-dated surge in volume after ChatGPT's release.

## Far-right and authoritarian networks, transnational and mainstreaming dynamics

A substantial subset examines how coordination intersects with extremist and authoritarian mobilization. [[Klinger2023-qu]] shows intermediary networks "laundering" Identitarian content into mainstream AfD politics; [[Askanius2026-de]] and [[Copland2025-em]] map alternative influence networks and transnational propaganda pipelines (Swedish far-right YouTube; Sky News Australia) that hybridize influencer commerce with political messaging; [[Righetti2025-slf9]] traces CitizenGO's centrally coordinated, multilingual anti-gender network functioning as transnational "symbolic glue"; and [[Rothut2026-or]] and [[Bailard2024-pj]] examine how protest communication itself (Querdenken, Proud Boys) can mainstream radical framing and predict offline violence. State-aligned coordination in authoritarian contexts is covered by [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] (Russia/Belarus Telegram), [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] (Russian protest/counter-protest and Chinese COVID-19 protest coordination), and [[Graham2025-gp]], which frames #IStandWithPutin propaganda as exploiting rather than evading the platform "infrastructure of truth." [[Pante2025-pq]] and Bastos2025-ol push attribution further, respectively re-testing claims of inter-state IO collaboration against control datasets and mining visual self-presentation signatures across the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium archive for country-of-origin attribution. [[Simeone2025-vo]] offers a complementary moderation-side perspective, showing that targeted deplatforming of a handful of accounts produced network-wide "ripple effects" on the Arizona Election Review community independent of any CIB — reinforcing the broader lesson that structural network position, not just coordination per se, drives discursive power.

## Platform-specific evasion and the AI frontier

The most recent arc turns to how manipulation tactics adapt to platform affordances and generative AI. [[Karo2026-dn]] catalogs five TikTok-specific evasion strategies used by jihadist propagandists, echoing the adversarial-creativity concerns raised for gambling networks in [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] and for hashtag-based contests in [[Zhao2025-ny]]. [[Crichton2026-kn]] documents a "re-generative pipeline" whereby commercial image models sanitize but do not eliminate far-right extremist imagery, while [[Orlando2025-ul]] uses generative agent-based modeling to show that LLM agents can autonomously reproduce real-world IO coordination signatures — network densification, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification — once given even minimal mutual awareness, without human orchestration. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this trajectory into a policy register, arguing that LLM-agentic "malicious AI swarms" constitute a qualitatively new democratic threat requiring layered governance (provenance tools, an international AI Influence Observatory, commercial-incentive levers) rather than reliance on detection alone.

## Governance, transparency, and the Alert infrastructure

Threaded through the corpus — and converging most visibly in the vera.ai-affiliated papers ([[Rogers2025-sl8f]], [[Rogers2026-cy]], [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]], [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], [[Jurg2025-ur]]) — is a shift from pure detection science toward monitoring infrastructure for fact-checkers, platforms, and regulators. [[Jurg2025-ur]] audits YouTube's moderation and "authoritative source" labeling during the 2024 EU elections, finding inconsistent application of publisher-context labels and opaque removal communications, and calls for expanded researcher API access. Together with the CooRnet-to-CooRTweet-to-Alert methodological lineage, these papers mark the field's maturation from ad hoc case studies toward a standing, cross-platform observatory logic — one explicitly designed, as [[Mannocci2026-kc]] argues at the survey level, to reconcile academic rigor with the operational, definitionally unstable categories platforms themselves have used since Facebook first coined "coordinated inauthentic behavior" in 2018.
