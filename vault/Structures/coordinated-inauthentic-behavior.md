# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The Line of Argument: From Coordination-as-Symptom to Coordination-as-Infrastructure

This body of work traces a two-decade arc from establishing that coordination is a detectable, meaningful signal of manipulation, to interrogating what that signal actually means, to building generalized tools for capturing it across an increasingly fragmented and AI-saturated platform ecosystem. Read together, the papers under this topic chart the maturation of coordinated inauthentic behavior (CIB) research from a forensic curiosity into a full-fledged subfield with its own methodological canon, theoretical disputes, and now a successor infrastructure in VERA-AI.

### Foundations: Coordination as Method

The intellectual lineage begins with two foundational moves. [[Keller2019-nk]] uses rare ground-truth data from the South Korean NIS scandal to show that *coordination patterns*, not individual bot-like features, are the most robust signature of astroturfing — a claim that reoriented the field away from bot-detection toward network-behavioral analysis. [[Starbird2019-qv]] complements this with a CSCW lens, arguing that disinformation is participatory "work" performed by hybrid orchestrated/organic crowds, making the boundary between authentic and inauthentic activity intrinsically blurry. This tension — coordination as clean signal versus coordination as messy, graded phenomenon — becomes the field's animating problem. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] formalizes this by showing coordination exists on a continuum, is orthogonal to automation, and varies by community, previewing debates that recur throughout the corpus.

Out of this lineage emerges the CooRnet methodology itself: [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] establishes coordinated link-sharing behavior (CLSB) as an ecological, actor-agnostic alternative to content- or bot-centric detection, validated against Italian elections. [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] and [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalize this via the Actors-Behaviors-Content (A-B-C) cycle, iteratively surfacing new coordinated actors around COVID-19 misinformation in Italy — work extended into a living, continuously updated monitoring workflow in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]]. These papers collectively demonstrate CLSB's versatility: it surfaces not only political disinformation but click-economy operations and religious proselytism networks, establishing coordination-detection as a general-purpose diagnostic for "problematic information" rather than a narrowly political tool.

### Generalizing and Formalizing the Method

A second wave pushes toward abstraction and rigor. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] generalizes CLSB into CooRTweet, an R package built on a minimal, platform-agnostic definition of coordination (repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object), validated against the NIS ground-truth dataset from [[Keller2019-nk]]. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] tackle the multimodality problem head-on, showing that different co-action layers (retweets, hashtags, URLs, mentions) capture partly complementary, partly redundant coordination signals, and that naive flattening loses structurally important actors — a methodological caution echoed by [[Yang2025-iv]], which shows that timing-based signals are trivially gameable and proposes speed/frequency regularities as more robust alternatives. [[Minici2024-tf]] pushes detection into the foundation-model era with IOHunter, combining language and graph models for cross-campaign generalization. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] caps this methodological arc with a systematic survey that reconciles fragmented platform and academic definitions into a unified framework organized around actors, synergic actions, and intent — explicitly decoupling coordination from both harmfulness and inauthenticity, a distinction long implicit in [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] and now made axiomatic.

### The Attribution and Authenticity Problem

A recurring second-order question is not just *whether* coordination is present but *what it means* and *who is behind it*. [[Thiele2025-ol]] offers a rational-choice typology for attributing campaigns to hidden principals, critiquing "coordinated inauthentic behavior" as conceptually muddled because it conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] empirically demonstrates this muddle: identical coordination signatures on Facebook can belong to influence operations, legitimate media outlets, or grassroots activists, and traces how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed over time to exclude much of what it once flagged. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] responds with "Deceptive Information Operations" as a broader category built on deceptive intent, affordance exploitation, and coordination jointly — deliberately loosened from strict falsity requirements — while diagnosing a "Zombie Internet" condition where engagement-driven ranking blurs human and synthetic participation. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] extends this authenticity problem to a hard case: Serbia's SNS party, whose networks mimic grassroots support so convincingly that they evade standard detection, calling for new frameworks attuned to "mimetic" rather than centralized digital authoritarianism. [[Graham2026-fb]] radicalizes the point furthest, arguing via a Reddit "botting" case study that the human/bot binary itself is analytically unstable and that authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance, not a technical property — a direct challenge to the premises of CIB detection as such.

Complementing this conceptual work, [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and [[Simeone2025-vo]] push back empirically on assumptions about coordination's causal power: the former shows coordinated accounts in UK election cascades exert influence far below strategic optimum, resembling random placement; the latter shows that Arizona election-audit deplatforming effects were *not* attributable to CIB, implying real human disengagement rather than bot suppression. Together these complicate any easy equation of "coordinated" with "consequential."

### Empirical Applications Across Platforms and Contexts

A large cluster of papers applies CLSB/CIB detection to specific national and thematic cases, mapping the method onto new political and platform terrains. Italian election and pandemic contexts recur as a proving ground: [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces how "Intellectual Dark Web" influencers and legacy media jointly amplify COVID-skeptic narratives via remediation, while [[Righetti2025-slf9]] shows CitizenGO's centrally coordinated multilingual network mainstreaming anti-gender ideology transnationally. [[Song2025-yh]] applies CLSB comparatively to UK/US vaccine discourse, showing the method's ideological agnosticism — coordination amplifies both misinformation and credible public health messaging depending on context. Authoritarian contexts extend the method further: [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] on Russian/Belarusian pro-government Telegram channels, [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] on Chinese and Russian protest/counter-protest dynamics (the latter explicitly extending Bennett and Segerberg's connective action framework to "connective counteraction"), and [[Kim2026-br]] on two decades of suspected state-linked trolling in Korean news comments. Electoral applications proliferate across geographies: [[Oprea2025-lf]] on Romanian EP elections, [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] on German federal elections, [[Kansaon2025-id]] on WhatsApp-driven mobilization in Brazil, [[Gaw2025-ru]] reconceptualizing Philippine influence operations as political brokerage, and [[Pante2025-pq]] critically re-examining claims of inter-state coordination with more rigorous controls. [[Appel2026-qr]] scales this up with platform-level Meta data on 49 deceptive networks reaching millions during the 2020 US election, showing reach is concentrated and reshare-driven rather than directly authored — a finding that complicates simple exposure narratives. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] and Bastos2025-ol turn to organizational and visual dimensions of troll infrastructure (the IRA's labor-market structure; visual self-presentation as an attribution signal), broadening the empirical toolkit beyond text and network topology.

### New Terrains: Platforms, Modalities, and Synthetic Media

As platforms diversify, so does the method's reach. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first challenge of TikTok, while [[Karo2026-dn]] documents jihadist actors' adversarial creativity in evading TikTok moderation through audio, meme, and emoji camouflage — both signaling that text-centric coordination detection cannot simply transfer to new modalities. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot detection on decentralized Bluesky. [[Gerard2025-br]] reframes cross-platform influence structurally, via narrative-cluster embeddings (CANE/t-CANE) that identify "bridge users" seeding migration between Truth Social and X, sidestepping the loss of interaction data caused by API restrictions — a methodological response to the same data-access crisis motivating [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s platform-agnostic design. [[Rogers2026-cy]] and its companion mapping techniques (developed under vera.ai) generalize further still, situating "post-truth spaces" within a five-part typology of problematic information spaces (alongside AINs, CIB, participatory propaganda, fake-news engagement spaces), explicitly designed as an operational tool for fact-checkers rather than a purely academic contribution — marking the transition from CooRnet-as-research-method to VERA-AI-as-monitoring-infrastructure. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] exemplifies this new phase concretely, documenting how coordinated gambling-promotion networks exploded exponentially after ChatGPT's release, with generative AI intensifying rather than replacing established persuasion drivers — direct evidence that the detection infrastructure must now contend with synthetic content at industrial scale.

### The Horizon: Agentic AI and the Future of Coordination

The corpus's newest strand confronts a paradigm shift: coordination increasingly may not require human orchestration at all. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates LLM agents that spontaneously reproduce IO coordination signatures — network density, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification — merely from mutual awareness, without explicit deliberation. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy-forum warning about "malicious AI swarms" capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning training data at a scale and adaptivity beyond anything IRA-style botnets achieved, proposing layered governance responses. Read against [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s account of the IRA's very human, labor-market-embedded organization, this trajectory suggests the field's next challenge is theorizing coordination when the "coordinators" are themselves generative models — a problem for which the actor-behavior-content and multiplex-detection apparatus built across this topic may need fundamental rearchitecting.

### Adjacent Currents

Several papers sit at the topic's periphery, testing where coordination-adjacent phenomena bleed into broader questions of platform manipulation, protest, and discourse: [[Rothut2026-or]] on protest-facilitated mainstreaming via Telegram network embedding; [[Askanius2026-de]] on Swedish far-right YouTube hybridization; [[Copland2025-em]] on Sky News Australia as a node in transnational network propaganda; [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] on coordinated amplification of contrarian science communicators; [[Zhao2025-ny]] on hashtag hijacking; [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] on influencer-driven toxicity; [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] on bots as agenda-builders; [[Renault2025-uh]] and [[Goel2025-iq]] on adjacent misinformation-measurement problems (Community Notes partisanship, mainstream-content repurposing); [[Graham2025-gp]] on propaganda exploiting "infrastructures of truth"; and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] on the persistence and adaptability of manipulation campaigns over time. Together these confirm that coordination detection has become a load-bearing methodology across nearly every corner of platform and political communication research — no longer a niche technique but the connective tissue of the field itself.
