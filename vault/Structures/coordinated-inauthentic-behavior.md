# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## Overview

This body of work traces the maturation of Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior (CIB) research from an ad-hoc detection heuristic into a full methodological subfield — one whose founding contribution, the CooRnet/Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) framework, sits at the center of the collection and radiates outward into theory, tooling, cross-platform extension, and critique. The papers can be read along four interlocking threads: (1) the original CLSB methodology and its direct descendants, (2) generalization and formalization of coordination detection as a computational problem, (3) empirical applications that stress-test and complicate the concept across platforms and political contexts, and (4) a growing meta-level conversation questioning what "coordination" and "inauthenticity" even mean.

## The CLSB/CooRnet lineage

The foundational move is made in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation detection away from content- or actor-veracity judgments toward an ecological, behavior-centered signal: near-simultaneous, repeated link-sharing among Facebook entities. This paper establishes the empirical signature (problematic domains disproportionately shared by coordinated networks) and the structural typology (centralized vs. clustered networks) that recur throughout the corpus. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalizes this into an applied fact-checking tool during COVID-19, demonstrating that CLSB-derived leads outperform baseline fact-checking hit rates and documenting adversarial adaptations (link laundering, first-comment link-hiding, religious-group hijacking) that anticipate later evasion literature. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] closes the loop methodologically, turning static detection into a continuously updating *workflow* — addressing the field's Achilles' heel, that lists of "bad actors" decay as networks adapt. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends the same CLSB-derived Italian dataset to trace how Alternative Influence Networks and legacy media co-produce COVID misinformation through remediation, showing the sociological payoff of the network once actors are identified.

The line reaches its most recent, most policy-relevant instantiation in the VERA-AI ecosystem: [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a unifying category that need not involve falsity at all, applying the CooRnet-style detection pipeline to a "Zombie Internet" of pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, and hijacked groups; [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] uses the same Vera AI-identified gambling networks to show how generative AI has industrialized visual persuasion since ChatGPT's launch; and [[Rogers2026-cy]] situates "post-truth spaces" as one of five adjacent categories of problematic information that CIB-style network mapping can help fact-checkers monitor. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] provides a critical coda to the entire lineage, arguing from fresh CooRnet-based data that coordination *signatures* alone cannot distinguish influence operations from legitimate media syndication or grassroots activism — a typology of "manufactured attention" that directly interrogates the assumptions built into the earlier papers.

## Generalizing and formalizing detection

A second thread pushes CLSB-style detection toward theoretical and technical generality. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet reformulates coordination as the repeated near-synchronous sharing of *any* uniquely identifiable object, deliberately decoupling the method from Facebook/link-sharing specifics and validating it against the South Korean NIS dataset. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] independently tackle the multimodality problem inherent in this generalization — the former systematically comparing five ways of integrating action types (retweets, replies, hashtags, URLs), the latter adding a temporal-decay layer to multiplex detection — both converging on multiplex/multi-layer representations as the most robust compromise between integration and inclusiveness. [[Minici2024-tf]] takes generalization further into graph foundation models, combining language and structural signals for out-of-distribution IO detection, while [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE framework abandons interaction-based signals altogether in favor of latent narrative-cluster co-membership, enabling detection of cross-platform "bridge users" even where API access is degraded. [[Yang2025-iv]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] address a shared evasion concern — that timing-based signals are trivially gameable — by proposing frequency/speed-based statistical regularities (Facebook) and real-time detection (Bluesky) as more resistant alternatives. [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey is the field's self-portrait at this stage: it catalogues the definitional fragmentation across Meta, Twitter/X, and academic work, and proposes actors–actions–intent and authenticity–harmfulness–orchestration–time-variance as the organizing axes that much of this collection, implicitly, already uses.

## Testing the concept against harder cases

A third cluster applies and complicates coordination detection in specific empirical settings, often finding that the CIB paradigm strains at its edges. [[Song2025-yh]] shows CLSB is ideologically agnostic, powering both anti-vaccine misinformation and credible NHS/CDC amplification — a finding that anticipates Rogers's critique. [[Kansaon2025-id]] and [[Luceri2025-tr]] extend coordination detection into new infrastructures (encrypted WhatsApp, video-first TikTok) where existing network signals must be substantially adapted. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]], [[Kulichkina2026-zk]], and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] apply coordination analysis to authoritarian contexts (Russia, Belarus, China), with the latter explicitly extending Bennett and Segerberg's connective-action framework to "connective counteraction," showing regime-aligned coordination is structurally distinct (modular, team-based) from protest coordination (centralized, synchronized). [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] pushes further, arguing Serbia's SNS-linked networks are designed specifically to mimic authenticity and thus evade the very detection paradigms this literature has built. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] applies coordination detection transnationally to CitizenGO's anti-gender network, finding centralized coordination dressed in the aesthetics of grassroots advocacy. Skeptical counterpoints matter too: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and [[Pante2025-pq]] use rigorous benchmarking (optimal-influence modeling; control datasets) to argue that observed coordinated accounts are often less pivotal, or less genuinely inter-state coordinated, than headline claims suggest — a methodological corrective in the same spirit as Rogers's critique but from a network-influence angle rather than a definitional one. Large-scale platform-collaboration studies — [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]] — extend the empirical stakes to national elections, showing that reach from Meta-identified deceptive networks is highly concentrated and substantially reshare-driven, while causal claims about downstream effects mostly evaporate under rigorous controls.

## Adjacent and future-facing concerns

The collection's outer ring addresses phenomena coordination detection must eventually reckon with. Some papers document coordination-adjacent behaviors without formal network detection — troll-factory labor organization ([[Poliakoff2026-fa]]), visual self-presentation of fake accounts (Bastos2025-ol), platform-specific evasion vernaculars ([[Karo2026-dn]]), hashtag hijacking ([[Zhao2025-ny]]), bot-driven agenda-building ([[Arceneaux2026-xk]]), long-horizon troll rhetoric in Korea ([[Kim2026-br]]), and hyperactive-user amplification in Romanian elections ([[Oprea2025-lf]]) — extending the empirical palette beyond link-sharing into labor, visuals, and rhetoric. Others theorize the attribution and definitional problem directly: [[Thiele2025-ol]] critiques "CIB" as conflating inauthenticity with intent and proposes a principal-attribution typology; [[De_Leon2025-qn]] argues exposure research must move beyond direct content encounters to "networked exposure," where even ordinary content is weaponized through circulation. Case studies of mainstreaming and network propaganda — [[Rothut2026-or]], [[Copland2025-em]], [[Askanius2026-de]], [[Bailard2024-pj]], [[Graham2025-gp]] — show how coordination-adjacent dynamics (protest ecosystems, alternative influence networks, transnational media pipelines) blur into the territory this literature maps, without always deploying formal coordination detection. Finally, a forward-looking pair — [[Orlando2025-ul]] and Schroeder2026-ir/[[Schroeder2026-im]] — models emergent LLM-agent coordination and warns of "malicious AI swarms," suggesting that the entire CLSB-to-CooRTweet-to-VERA-AI trajectory this note documents will soon confront adversaries that coordinate at machine speed and adapt around exactly the synchronicity signals the field's methods currently exploit — closing the arc from detecting human coordination to anticipating synthetic coordination as the next frontier.
