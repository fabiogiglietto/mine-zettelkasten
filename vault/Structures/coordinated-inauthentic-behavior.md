# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The arc of coordination research

Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior (CIB) is the name Meta gave to a phenomenon that Giglietto's own research had already begun to formalize as "coordinated link sharing behavior" (CLSB): networks of pages, groups, and profiles that share the same content within improbably short windows to game distribution algorithms. The line of argument running through this corpus begins with that founding methodological move — [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] shows that shifting disinformation research from content- and actor-detection toward *behavioral* detection of coordinated collective action reliably surfaces problematic domains around Italian elections — and then follows two decades of elaboration, generalization, critique, and, increasingly, unease about what coordination detection can and cannot tell us.

## From CooRnet to continuous monitoring

The CooRnet lineage is the methodological backbone of this topic. [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] formalizes CLSB detection as an iterative application of François's Actors-Behaviors-Content cycle; [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (MINE-FACTS) operationalizes it as a content-agnostic triage tool for fact-checkers, tracing how an Italian covid-skeptic network grew out of pre-existing political clusters and adapted its evasion tactics over time; and [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] closes the loop by turning static lists of coordinated accounts into a self-updating workflow, demonstrated on the 2022 Italian election through case studies spanning partisan hyperpartisanship, click-economy religious pages, and covert religious proselytism via Messenger bots. This same actor-identification logic is redeployed substantively in [[Marino2023-9137f448]], which traces how an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster and legacy media jointly remediate expert statements into covid-skeptic narratives, and in [[Song2025-yh]], which shows CLSB operating symmetrically for anti- and pro-vaccine communities in the UK and US — an early signal that coordination itself is ideologically agnostic. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] extends the approach transnationally to anti-gender advocacy (CitizenGO), finding centrally administered multilingual pages and a coordinated amplification network that functions as "symbolic glue" across languages.

The most recent work under vera.ai pushes this program toward monitoring-as-infrastructure and toward newer harms. [[Rogers2026-cy]] frames "post-truth spaces" as one of several problematic-information formations fact-checkers need mapped and ranked by influence rather than merely flagged; [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] turns the CooRnet toolkit reflexively on Meta's own shifting definition of CIB, showing that identical coordination *signatures* can belong to influence operations, mainstream media, or grassroots activists alike, and arguing that Meta's policy language has quietly narrowed from "false amplifiers" to "adversarial threat actors," leaving much manufactured attention unaddressed. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] generalizes this insight into "Deceptive Information Operations," a category built from three orthogonal dimensions — deceptive intent, affordance exploitation, and coordination — that captures a pro-Putin propaganda network, a synthetic gambling-promotion operation, and poorly moderated adult-content groups under one "Zombie Internet" logic of engagement-driven governance failure. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] specializes this further into visual persuasion, documenting how coordinated gambling networks' post volume exploded exponentially after ChatGPT's release, with generative AI intensifying (not replacing) existing persuasion drivers inside Meta's organic/paid regulatory grey zone.

## Generalizing and stress-testing detection

A parallel strand treats CLSB/CIB detection as a *methods problem* to be generalized beyond Facebook links and single time-window heuristics. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] (CooRTweet) abstracts coordination to "repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object," validating recovery of 85–92% of known accounts in the South Korean NIS dataset and explicitly citing Giglietto's operational definitions as its starting point. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] independently argue that single-modality, time-agnostic detection is insufficient, proposing temporal-decay and multiplex-network representations that better resist dilution tactics; [[Yang2025-iv]] proposes speed/frequency regularities as signals more robust to evasion than timing alone. [[Minici2024-tf]] brings graph foundation models to bear on cross-campaign generalization, while [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extend detection into video-first (TikTok) and decentralized (Bluesky) ecosystems where existing text-centric assumptions break down. [[Gerard2025-br]] reframes cross-platform coordination altogether as shared narrative-cluster affiliation rather than direct interaction, identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed most content migrating between Truth Social and X. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] surveys this entire terrain, proposing a unifying definition around actors, synergic actions, and intent, and insisting — echoing Giglietto's own emphasis on ecological, actor-agnostic detection — that authenticity and harmfulness are orthogonal axes, so that coordination can be authentic-but-harmful or inauthentic-but-harmless.

## Conceptual reckonings: what does coordination actually mean?

A growing subset of this literature turns critical attention back on the CIB concept itself. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposes a rational-choice attribution typology built around scale, elaborateness, and disguise instead. [[Graham2026-fb]], studying Reddit's "Anus_Fungi" mushroom-emoji phenomenon, dissolves the human/bot binary altogether, treating authenticity as a culturally negotiated performance rather than a technical property — a direct counterpoint to detection frameworks premised on a clean inauthentic/authentic divide. This conceptual unsettling is reinforced empirically by a cluster of papers questioning how much coordinated activity actually *matters*: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] finds that real coordinated accounts in UK election retweet cascades achieve influence far below optimal or even random-placement benchmarks; [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows that Arizona "audit" mobilization collapsed after deplatforming even though bots were never a meaningful driver, implicating authentic human structure rather than CIB; and the companion studies [[Appel2026-qr]] / [[noauthor_undated-bm]], analyzing Meta's own FIES data on 49 deceptive networks around the 2020 US election, find that most exposure to these networks occurred through reshares by ordinary, unaffiliated accounts, and that troubling correlations with factual discernment or partisan behavior evaporate once pre-exposure user characteristics are controlled. Read together, these papers push back against a purely detection-centric literature by asking whether coordination detection, however methodologically refined, is chasing an effect whose downstream causal footprint may be smaller — or differently distributed — than assumed.

## Geographies of coordination: elections, protest, and authoritarian ecosystems

A large empirical bloc applies coordination detection across national and regime contexts. Electoral studies include [[Oprea2025-lf]] on hyperactive-user amplification in Romania's 2024 EP campaign, [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] on advertising and coordination ahead of the 2021 German election, and [[Kansaon2025-id]] on WhatsApp-based coordination fueling Brazilian post-election mobilization and the January 8 attacks. Authoritarian and hybrid-regime contexts feature prominently: [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] documents Serbia's SNS deploying decentralized, mimetic CIB that evades standard bot-farm detection; [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] traces coordinated Telegram amplification of regime narratives in Russia and Belarus; [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] extends Bennett and Segerberg's connective-action framework to "connective counteraction," contrasting centralized pro-Navalny mobilization with modular, astroturf-like pro-government coordination; and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] examines dual-use coordination amid China's 2022 COVID protests. [[Pante2025-pq]] revisits claims of inter-state IO collaboration with more rigorous controls, while [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as a form of political brokerage embedded in campaign economies rather than free-floating disinformation.

## Mainstreaming, extremism, and the limits of coordination as a lens

Several papers examine radicalization and mainstreaming processes that coordination detection alone cannot fully explain, because organic and coordinated amplification blur together. [[Rothut2026-or]] proposes "protest-facilitated mainstreaming" to describe how Germany's Querdenken movement became entangled with far-right and conspiracist Telegram networks through shared anti-elite framing rather than explicit coordination. [[Askanius2026-de]] and [[Kakavand2026-kt]] map how Swedish and German far-right actors build cohesive cross-platform "alternative influence networks" through guest appearances, hyperlinks, and platform-specific affordances, while [[Copland2025-em]] shows Sky News Australia operating a dual strategy — native content for domestic audiences, viral conspiracist content for the US pipeline — that exemplifies network propaganda without necessarily requiring covert coordination. [[Bailard2024-pj]] and [[Karo2026-dn]] extend the lens to violent extremism, respectively linking Proud Boys' online framing to offline violence via Granger causality, and cataloguing jihadist evasion tactics ("everyday extremism") on TikTok — both cases where the object of study is discursive infrastructure rather than a discrete coordinated network.

## Troll labor, synthetic swarms, and the adjacent misinformation ecosystem

A final set of papers situates CIB within its human and technological infrastructure and its neighboring phenomena. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] and Bastos2025-ol look inside troll-farm operations — the former reconstructing the IRA's organizational hierarchy and labor-market embeddedness from leaked CVs, the latter probing visual self-presentation signatures in the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset — while [[Kim2026-br]] applies explainable ML to two decades of Korean news comments to show that state-linked trolls rely on morally condemning rhetoric rather than overt propaganda. Looking forward, [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] anticipate a coming shift from human-coordinated networks to autonomous "malicious AI swarms," with generative-agent simulations already reproducing narrative convergence and synchronized amplification without human orchestration — a trajectory that dovetails with the AI-driven gambling imagery documented empirically in [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]. Surrounding this core, a set of adjacent studies extends the coordination lens into cognate misinformation dynamics: coordinated amplification of contrarian science experts ([[Efstratiou2026-ij]]), narrative repurposing of true mainstream content ([[Goel2025-iq]]), bot-driven hashtag hijacking and agenda-building ([[Zhao2025-ny]], [[Arceneaux2026-xk]]), propaganda's exploitation of "truth infrastructure" ([[Graham2025-gp]]), the notion of networked rather than direct exposure ([[De_Leon2025-qn]]), and the still-open question of how such manipulation campaigns persist by appropriating emergent events ([[FitzGerald2025-nv]]). Together these papers mark the outer boundary of the topic: phenomena that share CIB's logic of algorithmic exploitation and networked amplification but do not always require, or yield to, the coordination-detection apparatus that anchors Giglietto's own program.
