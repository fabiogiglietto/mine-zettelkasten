# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## Methodological Foundations: From Content to Coordinated Action

The intellectual anchor of this collection is a methodological wager made early and refined repeatedly: that disinformation is better detected as a *pattern of coordinated behavior* than as a property of individual pieces of content. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] lays this foundation, showing that Facebook pages, groups, and profiles sharing the same URL in rapid, repeated succession are disproportionately linked to problematic domains—operationalizing "coordinated inauthentic behavior" against existing scholarship on authenticity and connective action. This behavioral turn echoes [[Starbird2019-qv]]'s argument that information operations are better understood as *collaborative work*, blurring bots, trolls, and organic crowds, and [[Keller2019-nk]]'s ground-truthed demonstration, from the South Korean NIS case, that coordination signatures—not individual bot-like traits—are the most reliable fingerprint of astroturfing. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] pushes this further by reframing coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, showing that automation and coordination are empirically orthogonal. The CooRnet/CLSB research program matures through [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Slack1783507719-slc1]], which operationalize François's Actors-Behaviors-Content cycle as an iterative detection workflow, and through [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which tackles the problem of list staleness by continuously updating coordinated-account registries against the 2022 Italian election, surfacing not just political networks but click-economy and religious-proselytism operations that blur neat ideological categories.

## Generalizing and Stress-Testing the Toolkit

A second wave of papers interrogates and extends the detection apparatus itself. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet generalizes coordination detection to any uniquely identifiable shared object, any platform, and any modality, explicitly responding to the brittleness of single-platform tools under API volatility. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] independently converge on the same insight—that coordination is inherently multimodal and time-sensitive—proposing multiplex and temporally-weighted architectures that outperform monomodal or naively flattened baselines, while [[Mannocci2026-kc]] synthesizes this entire research program into a survey that reconciles fragmented platform and academic definitions around three components (actors, synergic actions, intent) and insists authenticity and harmfulness are orthogonal axes. [[Yang2025-iv]] addresses evasion directly, proposing speed-and-frequency statistical regularities as detection signals more robust than manipulable timing windows. [[Thiele2025-ol]] takes a step back to interrogate the attribution problem itself, arguing that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with intent and proposing a rational-choice typology linking campaign scale, elaborateness, and disguise to a principal's resources and stakes. The toolkit is also ported to new terrains: [[Luceri2025-tr]] adapts network-based detection to TikTok's video-first affordances, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on Bluesky, [[Minici2024-tf]] builds a graph foundation model (IOHunter) for cross-campaign generalization, [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE framework reconstructs discourse networks via shared narrative clusters rather than interaction traces to trace cross-platform migration between Truth Social and X, [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] applies coordination detection to multilingual protest/repression dynamics around China's COVID-19 protests, and [[Pante2025-pq]] uses modern multi-signal coordination methods with control datasets to critically re-examine prior claims of inter-state collaboration.

## Does Coordination Actually Matter? Quantifying Influence

A notable skeptical thread asks whether detected coordination translates into real-world influence. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] formalize post-hoc influence evaluation on retweet cascades, finding that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve influence far below optimal or even random-matched benchmarks—suggesting CIB may be less pivotal to diffusion than assumed. [[Appel2026-qr]] offers a complementary, large-scale corrective from the platform side: across 49 Meta-identified deceptive networks in the 2020 US election, most exposure arrived indirectly via reshares by non-network accounts, and correlations between exposure and downstream beliefs evaporate once pre-exposure characteristics are controlled. [[Simeone2025-vo]] adds a network-intervention angle, showing that targeted deplatforming of a handful of hub accounts produced effects far exceeding the mechanical loss of those nodes—even though the affected community showed minimal evidence of CIB, implying organic authority structures, not bots, were destabilized.

## The VERA-AI Alert System and Applied Monitoring

A cluster of papers reports directly from the vera.ai project's applied monitoring infrastructure, extending CooRnet-style detection into operational "alert" systems for fact-checkers and researchers. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] develops a typology of "manufactured attention," showing that identical coordination signatures can mark influence operations, legitimate media syndication, or grassroots meme-sharing alike—and tracks how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed over time, leaving much coordinated amplification on-platform. [[Rogers2026-cy]] situates "post-truth spaces" within a broader taxonomy of problematic-information clusters and proposes an influence metric based on proximity to mainstream networks, demonstrated on Moldovan Facebook discourse around the Russia-Ukraine war. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] generalizes further still, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category untethered from truth/falsity and unifying three structurally similar but motivationally distinct cases—pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, and poorly moderated adult-content groups—under a "Zombie Internet" diagnosis of engagement-driven governance failure. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] zooms into one such case, documenting an exponential, ChatGPT-coincident rise in AI-generated visual persuasion within coordinated gambling networks. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces how "Alternative Influence Networks" and an Intellectual-Dark-Web cluster of covid-skeptic micro-celebrities achieved outsized share-driven engagement on Italian Facebook, while [[Slack1783507463-sl24]] and [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] extend this monitoring lens to online firestorms-as-protest and to political advertising during the 2021 German federal election.

## Case Studies: State, Party, and Platform-Specific Operations

A substantial empirical arc documents coordination within specific national and institutional contexts. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] reconstructs the organizational life of the St Petersburg IRA via OSINT, revealing a young, Crimea-triggered workforce embedded in Russia's ordinary media labor market rather than a 2016-election-specific operation. Authoritarian and semi-authoritarian contexts recur throughout: [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] on Serbia's SNS-linked networks that mimic grassroots support more convincingly than centralized bot farms, [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] on pro-government Telegram amplification in Russia and Belarus, and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] on the "connective counteraction" dynamics between pro-Navalny mobilization and pro-government astroturfing. [[Kim2026-br]] offers a two-decade, explainable-ML analysis of suspected foreign trolls in Korean news comments, finding moral condemnation—not direct boosterism—as the dominant and most engaging rhetorical strategy. Electoral contexts beyond Europe appear in [[Kansaon2025-id]]'s tracing of coordinated WhatsApp activity into real-world mobilization around Brazil's January 8 events, [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s reframing of Philippine influence operations as political brokerage, and [[Oprea2025-lf]]'s manual audit of hyperactive-user amplification during Romania's 2024 EP campaign. Bastos2025-ol and [[Graham2025-gp]] round out this cluster by examining, respectively, the visual self-presentation of troll-farm profile images and how the #IStandWithPutin campaign exploited the platform infrastructures of truth rather than merely evading them.

## Health, Science, and Narrative Repurposing

A smaller but important strand turns coordination analysis onto health and science communication. [[Song2025-yh]] applies CLSB methodology comparatively to pro- and anti-vaccine Facebook networks in the UK and US, finding that coordination itself is ideologically agnostic—serving both misinformation and credible public-health messaging depending on context. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces how a predominantly contrarian coordinated retweet network amplifies a narrow set of credentialed anti-consensus experts, who precede rather than follow mainstream and fringe news coverage of COVID-19 science. [[Goel2025-iq]] complicates source-level misinformation metrics entirely, showing that factually accurate mainstream articles are disproportionately co-shared with fake news to support misleading narratives—suggesting coordination research must track narrative repurposing, not just source reliability.

## Far-Right and Extremist Ecosystems

A dense cluster examines how far-right, extremist, and movement-adjacent actors exploit platform architectures to achieve reach without necessarily triggering classic coordination signatures. [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Groebner2026-pc]] both document the transnationalization of hyperpartisan broadcasters (Sky News Australia, Fox News, Rebel News, GB News) into a shared Anglophone far-right audience, while [[Askanius2026-de]] maps a Swedish "alternative influence network" hybridizing activism with influencer monetization during the pandemic. [[Rieder2026-pp]] shows how Andrew Tate's content persists as "ambient ideology" across a diffuse YouTube ecosystem that outpaces moderation, and [[Karo2026-dn]] documents parallel adversarial-creativity tactics—audio camouflage, emoji codes, bait-and-switch—used by jihadist supporters on TikTok, underscoring that such evasion strategies are not ideology-specific. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] extends this to transnational anti-gender advocacy (CitizenGO), finding centrally coordinated multilingual amplification beneath an apparently grassroots structure. [[Bailard2024-pj]] and [[Rothut2026-or]] connect online discursive dynamics to offline consequences—respectively, Proud Boys collective-action framing predicting violence, and Querdenken protest channels serving as conduits mainstreaming far-right and conspiracist narratives. [[Tuters2026-vo]] theorizes this entire dynamic infrastructurally, arguing that the Great Reset's "conspiricization" emerges from platform-grammar-driven repetition and adjacency rather than coherent narrative—a fitting capstone to the collection's recurring insight that coordination and ideology are separable phenomena.

## Automation, Authenticity, and the Agentic Frontier

The final arc interrogates the ontological status of "inauthenticity" itself and previews where coordinated manipulation is heading. [[Graham2026-fb]] challenges the human/bot binary directly through Reddit's "fungi" phenomenon, arguing authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance rather than a technical property—a conceptual counterweight to the detection-oriented papers above. [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examine bots as active discourse participants—agenda-builders shaping campaign sentiment and hijackers redirecting hashtag narratives—reinforcing that automation studies and coordination studies, while related, answer different questions. Looking forward, [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] signal the field's next challenge: LLM-driven "malicious AI swarms" and generative-agent simulations that autonomously reproduce the coordination mechanisms this literature has spent a decade learning to detect, with [[Orlando2025-ul]]'s finding that mere mutual awareness among agents—without explicit deliberation—suffices to generate dense, synchronized coordination. Finally, [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]], [[FitzGerald2025-nv]], and [[De_Leon2025-qn]] gesture toward adjacent concerns—source-driven toxicity, the adaptive persistence of manipulation campaigns, and "networked exposure" to weaponized ordinary content—that the coordination-detection paradigm will need to absorb as the object of study keeps mutating faster than the methods built to catch it.
