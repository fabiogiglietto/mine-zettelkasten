# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The CooRnet program: origins, method, and generalization

The backbone of this topic is a single methodological lineage running from [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] through to the present. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] lays the theoretical groundwork by arguing, via second-order cybernetics and Chadwick's hybrid media system, that "fake news" scholarship's fixation on creator intent is analytically bankrupt — attention should shift to *propagators* and the cascades they generate. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] operationalizes this shift, introducing Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) as a content-agnostic, action-based signal detected via near-simultaneous, repeated URL sharing across Facebook pages, groups, and profiles, validated against the 2018/2019 Italian elections. [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] and [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] extend this into the CooRnet tool proper, embedding it within François's Actors-Behaviors-Content framework as an *iterative* detection cycle (content seeds actor detection, actors seed new content) and demonstrating it against Italian COVID-19 disinformation. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] pushes the workflow toward continuous monitoring — the crucial move from static lists to near-real-time updating — surfacing hyperpartisan, click-economy, and religious-proselytism networks in the 2022 Italian election. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] is the methodological culmination of this arc: CooRTweet reconceives coordination in maximally abstract terms (repeated near-synchronous sharing of *any* uniquely identifiable object) to become platform- and modality-agnostic, validated against the South Korean NIS dataset. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] then steps back to synthesize this entire academic-plus-industry literature into a unifying conceptual framework (actors, synergic actions, intent; authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance), functioning as the encyclopedia/handbook-style capstone of the whole detection paradigm.

## VERA-AI, Alert monitoring, and manufactured attention

A second cluster represents the CooRnet method's institutional successor, the VERA-AI project, and its diversification into an "alert" monitoring posture aimed at fact-checkers and platform accountability rather than election-cycle case studies alone. [[Rogers2026-cy]] frames this shift conceptually, situating "post-truth spaces" among four sibling categories of problematic information (alternative influence networks, fake-news engagement spaces, CIB campaigns, participatory propaganda) and demonstrating a mapping technique on Moldovan Facebook ahead of the 2024 elections. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] then interrogates the CIB category itself, showing that identical CooRnet-style coordination signatures characterize influence operations, legitimate media outlets, political meme communities, and ad networks alike — arguing that Meta's operational definition of CIB has narrowed from "false amplifiers" to "adversarial actors," leaving much manufactured attention on-platform. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] builds on this critique with the "Deceptive Information Operations" (DIO) framework and the evocative "Zombie Internet" diagnosis: three structurally similar Facebook operations (pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, poorly moderated adult-content groups) reveal that engagement-driven ranking plus inconsistent moderation blur the line between human and automated participation. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] applies the same detection infrastructure to document how generative AI has intensified — not replaced — established visual-persuasion drivers in coordinated gambling promotion, with an exponential, statistically dated inflection point at ChatGPT's launch. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] extends the same coordination-detection lens to a non-Facebook-native domain, showing CitizenGO's transnational anti-gender network relies on centrally administered multilingual accounts and a tightly coordinated 148-account amplification subnetwork — evidence that CLSB-style detection travels productively beyond disinformation into advocacy astroturfing.

## Contesting and refining the detection paradigm

A parallel, more computer-science-oriented cluster treats coordination detection as an open technical problem, often explicitly benchmarking against or complementing the CooRnet tradition. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five multimodal operationalizations of coordination and identifies a fundamental trade-off between integration strength and inclusiveness, cautioning against the "union flattening" that dominates practice (implicitly including CooRnet-style single-modality approaches). [[Iannucci2025-eg]] proposes a time-aware, multiplex extension of Newman's collaboration model, benchmarked against 26 labeled datasets, again arguing that neither timing nor modality alone suffices. [[Yang2025-iv]] targets a specific vulnerability — timing-based signals are trivially evadable — and proposes speed/frequency regularities as a more robust alternative for Facebook link-sharing detection. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] push toward foundation-model and narrative-embedding approaches (IOHunter; CANE/t-CANE) that generalize across campaigns and platforms with less labeled data, the latter identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed cross-platform narrative migration between Truth Social and X. [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extend detection into under-served terrains — TikTok's video-first ecosystem and Bluesky's decentralized architecture, respectively — arguing that text-centric coordination signatures do not transfer cleanly. [[Thiele2025-ol]] steps back from detection entirely to attribution, arguing that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing a rational-choice typology (scale, elaborateness, disguise) for inferring covert principals — a pointed theoretical counterpoint to the largely empirical CooRnet program.

## Does coordination matter? Skepticism, evasion, and impact

A skeptical thread within the topic questions how much causal work coordinated accounts actually do. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models the theoretical influence ceiling coordinated nodes could achieve in real cascades and finds observed CIB accounts perform far below optimal or even resource-matched benchmarks, resembling random placement — a sobering counterpoint to alarmist framings. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complements this from the deplatforming side: the Arizona Election Review network's collapse after Twitter's 2021 suspensions was *not* attributable to bot-like CIB (only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds), suggesting network interventions work through authentic human disengagement rather than removing manipulative infrastructure. [[Pante2025-pq]] directly re-audits prior claims of inter-state coordination on Twitter/X using modern detection signals and control datasets, questioning whether previously reported collaboration holds up. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] and [[Graham2025-gp]] address adjacent conceptual problems — the persistence and event-appropriation of manipulation campaigns over time, and how propaganda (via #IStandWithPutin) exploits rather than evades the platform "infrastructure of truth." Together with Bastos2025-ol, which probes visual self-presentation in the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset for attribution signals, and [[Poliakoff2026-fa]], whose OSINT reconstruction of the IRA's organizational hierarchy shows it as an ordinary media-labor-market actor rather than a covert monolith, this cluster collectively pressures the field toward more modest, structurally grounded claims about coordination's actual causal footprint.

## Case studies across platforms and polities

The bulk of empirical application demonstrates CLSB/CIB-style detection (or close cousins) across an increasingly diverse set of platforms, regimes, and issue-publics. On Facebook, [[Song2025-yh]] compares pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB networks across the UK and US, showing coordination is ideologically agnostic even as content credibility diverges sharply; [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces how Alternative Influence Networks and legacy-media remediation amplified Italian COVID-19 problematic information; [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification in Romanian EU election pages. Authoritarian and electoral-authoritarian contexts feature heavily: [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] examine pro-regime Telegram coordination in Russia/Belarus and SNS's mimetic, harder-to-detect Serbian networks; [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] extend Bennett and Segerberg's connective-action framework to "connective counteraction" and digital repression around Russian protest and Chinese COVID-19 protest Twitter activity. Elsewhere, [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts Rapid-Spread-Network detection to WhatsApp's closed ecosystem for Brazilian political mobilization; [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage via interview-based fieldwork; [[Askanius2026-de]] and [[Copland2025-em]] map far-right and hyper-partisan alternative influence networks on YouTube (Sweden) and cross-platform (Sky News Australia); [[Bailard2024-pj]] and [[Rothut2026-or]] trace the online-offline discursive dynamics of Proud Boys Telegram messaging and Querdenken's mainstreaming of extremist frames; and [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] examines political advertising alongside coordinated behavior in the 2021 German elections.

## New frontiers: generative AI, swarms, and adjacent manipulation phenomena

The topic's most forward-looking cluster anticipates where coordination detection must go next as generative AI reshapes the threat landscape — directly continuing the trajectory that motivates VERA-AI's alert systems. [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] theorize and simulate "malicious AI swarms": the former as a governance forum piece mapping capability advances and policy responses, the latter as the first systematic generative-agent-based-modeling study showing that mere teammate awareness among LLM agents produces coordination nearly as dense as explicit collective deliberation — an empirical proof-of-concept for exactly the threat [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents already unfolding in gambling promotion. [[Karo2026-dn]] and [[Kim2026-br]] extend the coordination lens to adversarial creativity (TikTok jihadist evasion tactics) and two decades of suspected state-linked troll rhetoric in Korean news comments, respectively, both using explainable/behavioral signals reminiscent of the CooRnet approach's actor-behavior focus. Finally, a set of adjacent studies — [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] on coordinated contrarian amplification in COVID-19 science communication, [[Zhao2025-ny]] on hashtag hijacking, [[Goel2025-iq]] on the co-sharing of true-but-repurposed mainstream content, [[Marwick2025-vx]] on processual "redpilling" narratives, and [[Appel2026-qr]] (duplicated in [[noauthor_undated-bm]]) on the FIES/Meta collaboration measuring actual exposure to 49 deceptive networks in the 2020 US election — collectively push the field beyond narrow bot/CIB detection toward a richer account of how coordination, authentic reshare behavior, and narrative content jointly determine real-world reach and influence, the very question the CooRnet-to-VERA-AI trajectory was built to answer at scale.
