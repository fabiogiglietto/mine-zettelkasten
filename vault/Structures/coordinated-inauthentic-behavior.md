# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior: Detection, Theory, and the Widening Field

### From content to behavior: the CooRnet lineage

The conceptual backbone of this collection runs through Giglietto's own trajectory, beginning with the foundational argument in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] that disinformation research should pivot from adjudicating content truthfulness or blacklisting "bad actors" to detecting *coordinated collective action* — operationalizing Facebook's own "coordinated inauthentic behavior" (CIB) category through the measurable signature of near-simultaneous, repeated link-sharing. This behavioral turn, formalized in CooRnet, is extended methodologically in [[Slack1783507719-slc1]], which embeds the tool within François's Actors-Behaviors-Content (A-B-C) cycle, and operationally in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which solves the staleness problem of static actor lists by iteratively discovering new coordinated accounts from known ones — a workflow whose case studies (a hyperpartisan M5S network, a clickbait operation laundering political content through religious pages, and a religious-proselytism bot network) prefigure the taxonomic diversity later systematized in [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] applies the same logic to COVID-19, showing CLSB-based detection outperforms routine fact-checking and documenting tactics (link laundering, first-comment link-hiding, AdSense-linked blogspot networks) that adversarial actors use to evade enforcement. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this empirical program to show how an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster, amplified via legacy-media remediation, became a vector for pandemic misinformation independent of formal coordination — linking CLSB work to alternative-influence-network (AIN) scholarship that recurs in [[Askanius2026-de]] and [[Groebner2026-pc]].

The newest layer of this lineage, [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], marks the field's shift toward *generative AI-era* manipulation and a more capacious analytic vocabulary. The gambling-promotion study documents an exponential, ChatGPT-linked explosion in coordinated visual persuasion exploiting Meta's organic/paid regulatory gap, while the "Zombie Internet" paper proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" (DIOs) as a category broader than CIB or misinformation — unifying propaganda, gambling, and adult-content networks under shared structural logics of affordance exploitation and simulated coordination, explicitly diagnosing where Meta's own enforcement taxonomy fails to capture harm. This conceptual expansion is echoed in [[Rogers2026-cy]], which situates "post-truth spaces" within a five-part typology of problematic-information spaces for fact-checkers (part of the same vera.ai ecosystem that grounds the Alert monitoring system), and in [[Thiele2025-ol]], which critiques CIB itself as analytically inadequate for conflating inauthenticity with intent, proposing instead an attribution-focused typology grounded in rational-choice modeling of campaign principals.

### Methodological frontiers: generalization, multimodality, and quantifying impact

A substantial cluster advances the technical detection apparatus itself. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] generalizes CooRnet-style detection into CooRTweet, an platform-agnostic R package validated against the ground-truth NIS dataset, explicitly responding to API volatility and the need for cross-platform, cross-modality tools. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] both tackle multimodality, demonstrating that monomodal or naively flattened multi-layer networks lose structurally important coordination signals, and that multiplex, temporally-weighted representations better balance integration against inclusiveness — directly answering the evasion concern (dilution across modalities) raised by [[Yang2025-iv]], which shows that speed/frequency regularities in link-sharing resist the trivial timing manipulation that undermines older detection signals. [[Minici2024-tf]] pushes detection toward foundation-model generalization, combining language and graph models to detect IO drivers across heterogeneous state campaigns, while [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers the field's most systematic stock-taking: a survey reconciling fragmented platform and academic definitions into a unified framework built on actors, synergic actions, and intent, crossed with authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, and time-variance as independent axes — formally decoupling, as [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] first demonstrated empirically for the 2019 UK election, coordination from both automation and inauthenticity.

A second frontier interrogates not whether coordination can be detected but whether it *matters*. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] develop graph-theoretic frameworks for post-hoc evaluation of coordinated-account influence within retweet cascades, finding that real-world coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve influence far below optimal or even greedy benchmarks — a skeptical finding that resonates with [[Appel2026-qr]]'s large-scale FIES analysis showing that most exposure to deceptive networks during the 2020 US election came from organic reshares rather than network accounts themselves, and that correlational effects on beliefs mostly vanish under proper controls. Together these works caution against assuming that detected coordination equals outsized causal impact, a tension this strand holds productively alongside the more alarmed framings elsewhere in the collection.

### Platform-specific extensions and new terrains

Beyond Facebook and Twitter/X, the strand is rapidly colonizing new platforms and modalities. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts TikTok's video-first ecosystem, arguing text-centric detection methods need fundamental adaptation; [[Bosch2024-hj]] complements this by identifying *sound* itself as an underexamined propaganda vector in TikTok's Ukraine-war content; [[Karo2026-dn]] documents jihadist "everyday extremism" exploiting TikTok's vernacular and moderation blind spots through audio camouflage and emoji codes. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] opens the decentralized frontier with real-time bot detection on Bluesky. Encrypted and closed platforms also enter the frame: [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts Rapid Spread Network detection to 13 million Brazilian WhatsApp messages, linking coordinated misinformation directly to the January 8 insurrection, while [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] examine Twitter-based coordination amid Chinese COVID protests and Russian pro-/anti-Navalny mobilization respectively — the latter explicitly extending Bennett and Segerberg's connective action framework to "connective counteraction," a conceptual move paralleled in [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]]'s account of Serbia's SNS party mimicking authentic grassroots support more successfully than centralized bot farms, and in [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]]'s mapping of pro-government Telegram amplification in Russia and Belarus.

Visual and multimodal dimensions of inauthenticity receive dedicated treatment: Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium's troll-farm imagery for nationally distinctive visual self-presentation signatures, while [[Nangle2026-yo]] examines how Instagram far-right content uses positive affect and memetic softening — adjacent to [[Graham2026-fb]]'s playful reconceptualization of Reddit "botting" as participatory culture that destabilizes the human/bot binary altogether, arguing authenticity is culturally negotiated rather than technically verifiable.

### Case studies in influence operations and disinformation infrastructure

A rich empirical seam documents specific operations and their organizational substrates. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] offers an unusually granular OSINT reconstruction of the IRA's internal labor structure via leaked CVs, revealing it as an "outsourced troll" operation integrated into Russia's ordinary media labor market rather than a state-run bot farm — a finding that complicates simplistic bot-centric narratives and resonates with [[Keller2019-nk]] and [[Starbird2019-qv]], two foundational papers establishing that coordination traces (not individual bot signatures) are the most robust evidence of astroturfing, and that information operations are best understood as participatory, collaborative "work" blending orchestrated and organic elements. [[Gaw2025-ru]] extends this organizational lens to the Philippines, reconceiving influence operations as political brokerage sustained by political-economic infrastructure rather than ad hoc propaganda. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] and [[Graham2025-gp]] (on #IStandWithPutin) both emphasize the adaptive persistence of manipulation campaigns, the latter arguing propaganda increasingly *exploits* rather than evades the platform infrastructures built to signal trustworthiness. [[Pante2025-pq]] provides a methodological corrective within this space, showing that prior claims of inter-state IO collaboration may not survive more rigorous, control-benchmarked coordination analysis — a caution that pairs naturally with [[Di-Marco2025-aa]]'s skepticism about overstated CIB influence.

### Mainstreaming, transnationalization, and the far right

A distinct but overlapping thread tracks how coordinated and connective dynamics enable the mainstreaming of extremist and populist politics. [[Knupfer2025-vt]]'s "logic of connective faction" shows how US Republican elites' adoption of Critical Race Theory as a talking point was rewarded by hyper-partisan media engagement, radicalizing intra-party dynamics; [[Rothut2026-or]] documents similar mainstreaming mechanics in the German Querdenken movement's Telegram entanglement with far-right networks; and [[Righetti2025-slf9]] traces CitizenGO's centrally coordinated, Change.org-mimicking multilingual network spreading anti-gender "symbolic glue" transnationally. Transnational far-right media infrastructure is mapped empirically in [[Copland2025-em]] (Sky News Australia's dual Facebook/YouTube strategy bridging Australian and US audiences), [[Heft2021-ky]] (hyperlink networks among six countries' alternative news sites, with the US as hub), [[Groebner2026-pc]] (sustained transnational commenter overlap across Anglophone hyperpartisan YouTube channels), and [[Askanius2026-de]] (the hybridized, monetized Swedish far-right YouTube "alternative influence network"). [[Tuters2026-vo]] offers a theoretically ambitious coda to this cluster, reframing Great Reset conspiracism not as belief or narrative but as an infrastructural effect of platform grammars — patterned adjacency preceding coherent story — proposing "conspiricization" and the "Memeplex" as concepts that could reorient how coordination-adjacent mainstreaming is studied.

### Emergent and automated coordination: bots, agentic AI, and algorithmic governance

Several papers push the strand toward its likely future: coordination increasingly generated or amplified by algorithmic and AI systems rather than human operators alone. [[Orlando2025-ul]] offers a striking proof of concept — LLM agents in simulated information operations spontaneously reproduce real-world coordination signatures, with mere teammate awareness nearly matching explicit collective deliberation — while [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy-forum warning about "malicious AI swarms" capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning training data at democratic scale. [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] documents bots functioning as agenda-builders against human campaigns via Granger-causal attribute-salience transfer, and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines bot-assisted hashtag hijacking as a deliberate public-opinion tactic. Algorithmic governance itself comes under scrutiny: [[Efstratiou2025-gs]] shows how X's post-Musk algorithm systematically privileged agitating, Musk-adjacent accounts over institutional ones; [[Bouchaud2026-np]] finds X's Community Notes bridging algorithm structurally undermoderates polarizing (and electorally risky) content by design; and [[Simeone2025-vo]] demonstrates that targeted deplatforming of a small hub cluster can flip an entire community's authority structure — notably finding CIB was *not* the operative mechanism, underscoring that human disengagement, not bot removal, drove the effect. [[Bruns2026-yv]] closes this thread with a broader obituary for Twitter as a once-prosocial public sphere undone by weaponized disinformation and inconsistent governance, questioning whether successor platforms (Bluesky, Mastodon, Threads) can avoid repeating these failures.

### Adjacent and exploratory cases

Several papers extend the strand's reach into less expected empirical territory, testing the boundaries of what "coordination" analysis can explain. [[Bailard2024-pj]] applies collective-action framing and Granger causality to Proud Boys Telegram discourse and offline violence, implicitly asking whether online coordination signatures translate into real-world mobilization — a question also central to [[Kansaon2025-id]] and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]]. [[Song2025-yh]] and [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] both show CLSB/coordination detection applied to health communication, finding — contra a purely malicious framing — that coordination is ideologically agnostic, amplifying credible public-health messaging as readily as anti-vaccine narratives, and that even a near-entirely contrarian coordinated retweet network driving COVID science discourse was not primarily bot-driven. [[Slack1783507463-sl24]] and [[Oprea2025-lf]] round out the collection with smaller-scale case studies — a pro-Vietnam Facebook firestorm reframed as coordinated political protest, and manual forensic analysis of Romanian election-page "hyperactive users" — while [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] and [[Kim2026-br]] gesture toward further national contexts (German federal elections; two decades of Korean state-linked trolling) where the CooRnet-descended toolkit and its conceptual vocabulary continue to be tested, extended, and occasionally challenged.
