# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The Line of Argument: From Detection to Governance

This body of work traces an arc from methodological invention to conceptual reckoning: it begins by asking *how do we detect coordination?*, moves through *what does coordination actually explain?*, and arrives at *what should we do about a media ecosystem where coordination is structurally invited?* The throughline is the researcher's own CLSB/CooRnet lineage, which functions as both a technical toolkit and an argumentative anchor that later papers test, extend, contest, or reframe.

### The founding move: coordination as behavior, not content

The foundational papers establish the field's central methodological pivot. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] makes the case that disinformation research should shift from adjudicating truth/falsity or flagging "bad actors" to detecting *coordinated collective action*—operationalized as near-simultaneous, repeated URL sharing across accounts. This content-agnostic, behavior-first logic is then operationalized at scale in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (the MINE-FACTS/CooRnet pipeline applied to Italian COVID misinformation) and formalized as a continuously-updating workflow in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which shows how static blocklists become obsolete and proposes iterative re-detection from a seed list of known coordinated accounts. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] pushes the abstraction further with CooRTweet, generalizing "coordination" to *any* uniquely identifiable shared object across any platform or modality—explicitly designed for a post-API, multi-platform world. Together these four papers constitute the technical spine of the topic: a progressively more general, more portable, more evasion-resistant definition of coordination.

### Case studies that stress-test the method

A dense cluster of applied studies pressure-tests CLSB/CooRnet-style detection against real political and commercial phenomena, each complicating the notion that "coordination = malice." [[Song2025-yh]] finds that both pro- and anti-vaccine communities use CLSB, with credibility and density diverging by ideology and country—coordination is ideologically agnostic. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] uses coordination analysis to expose CitizenGO's centrally-administered, transnational anti-gender network, showing coordination as a *scaffold for legitimate-looking advocacy* rather than covert deception. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] go furthest in destabilizing the assumption that coordination signatures are diagnostic of inauthenticity at all: identical coordination signatures appear behind an influence operation, a tabloid newsroom, and a gambling ad network alike, forcing a typology of "manufactured attention" and "Deceptive Information Operations" that separates structural coordination from actor intent. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] extends this into the generative-AI era, showing how ChatGPT-era visual content industrializes gambling promotion within the same coordinated-group infrastructure, exploiting Meta's paid/organic regulatory asymmetry. [[Rogers2026-cy]] situates all of this within a broader cartography of "post-truth spaces," positioning CIB as one of several overlapping categories (alongside alternative influence networks, fake-news engagement spaces, participatory propaganda) useful to working fact-checkers—an explicitly applied, VERA-AI-adjacent framing. [[Oprea2025-lf]] and [[Kansaon2025-id]] extend the empirical reach geographically and platform-wise (Romanian Facebook elections; Brazilian WhatsApp), the latter adapting Twitter-style rapid-spread-network detection to an encrypted, closed messaging environment and linking coordinated messaging causally to real-world mobilization (Supreme Court doxxing, January 8 protests).

### Platform expansion: beyond text-based Twitter/Facebook

A second cluster explicitly addresses the field's platform bias toward text-centric Twitter/Facebook data. [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Karo2026-dn]] tackle TikTok's video-first, vernacular culture—the former adapting network-similarity detection to video interactions, the latter cataloguing five inductive evasion strategies (audio camouflage, meme infiltration, emoji codes) that show adversarial creativity is platform-specific but ideology-agnostic. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes into Bluesky, a decentralized platform, arguing for real-time bot-coordination detection as viral misinformation increasingly rides on non-organic amplification. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] apply coordination detection to Telegram and multilingual Twitter data in authoritarian contexts (Russia, Belarus, China), while [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues that Serbia's SNS-linked networks represent an even subtler, harder-to-detect species of digital authoritarianism—mimicking grassroots support rather than running centralized bot farms, and thus evading exactly the kind of signatures CLSB/CooRnet were built to catch. This raises an implicit methodological challenge back to the founding papers: as coordination becomes more "authentic-looking," what signal remains detectable?

### Methodological refinement: multiplex, temporal, and cross-platform architectures

A distinct sub-line addresses the internal engineering of coordination detection itself, largely in explicit dialogue with (and extension of) the CLSB tradition. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares monomodal, flattened, and multiplex operationalizations of multimodal coordination, finding multiplex community detection the most balanced—an argument that single-signal methods (like pure co-URL-sharing) miss complementary modalities. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] builds on this by adding temporal decay kernels to multiplex layers, benchmarking against 26 labeled datasets and outperforming existing baselines including CooRnet-style approaches. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the timing-manipulability weakness directly, proposing statistical regularities in sharing speed/frequency as a more evasion-resistant signal for Facebook link-sharing detection. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] represent a further leap toward foundation-model and embedding-based architectures: IOHunter combines language models with graph neural networks for cross-campaign generalization, while CANE/t-CANE models users as distributions over latent narrative clusters to reconstruct discourse networks *without* relying on interaction metadata at all—directly responding to the post-API scarcity that motivated CooRTweet's redesign. [[Pante2025-pq]] applies these more rigorous, multi-signal detection standards retrospectively, questioning whether prior claims of inter-state coordination survive proper control-dataset benchmarking—an explicit corrective gesture within the methodological literature.

### Does coordination matter? Causal skepticism and reassessment

A striking countercurrent within the topic questions whether detected coordination is causally consequential at all. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] models coordinated accounts' influence within retweet cascades and finds their real-world placement resembles *random* assignment far more than strategic optimization—CIB may be much less pivotal to diffusion than commonly assumed. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]] (both analyzing Meta's 49 deceptive networks around the 2020 US election) similarly puncture inflated causal narratives: most reach comes from *unaffiliated* resharers rather than network accounts themselves, and correlations between exposure and downstream political outcomes evaporate after controlling for pre-exposure characteristics. [[Simeone2025-vo]] finds that a Twitter deplatforming's dramatic network effects on the Arizona Election Review community were *not* attributable to CIB (only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds)—real human disengagement, not bot removal, drove the change. Together these papers form a methodologically sober counterweight to alarmist framings, insisting that coordination detection must be paired with causal and comparative rigor before claims of "influence" are warranted. [[Kim2026-wg]], by contrast, offers a rare case where direct exposure measurement *does* support a causal turnout effect from targeted (IRA-linked) suppression advertising—illustrating that skepticism should be evidentiary, not blanket.

### Conceptual reckoning: what is coordination, actually?

A cluster of more theoretical papers works to sharpen or critique the conceptual foundations that the empirical/methodological lines have been operationalizing. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" is analytically inadequate because it conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent, proposing instead a rational-choice attribution model centered on scale, elaborateness, and disguise as signals of a hidden principal's strategy. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes influence operations as a form of political *brokerage*, emphasizing political-economic infrastructure over discrete campaigns—resonant with [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT excavation of the IRA as an ordinary media-labor-market organization rather than an ideologically pure troll factory. [[Rogers2026-cy]] and [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] (same author line) converge on the idea that authenticity is a continuum, not a binary, echoing Thiele's critique from a different angle. [[Graham2025-gp]] contributes the evocative idea that propaganda succeeds not by evading verification infrastructure but by *parasitizing* it—a conceptual cousin to the "manufactured attention" and "Zombie Internet" framings.

### The frontier: generative AI and swarm coordination

The most forward-looking papers extend coordination research into a near-future where LLM agents themselves coordinate. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates emergent coordination among generative agents, finding that mere mutual awareness—short of explicit deliberation—suffices to produce dense, synchronized, narrative-homogeneous IO-like behavior. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance-oriented warning about "malicious AI swarms" that could manufacture consensus at civilizational scale, arguing existing detection paradigms (including synchrony-based methods central to this whole topic) will be systematically evaded by adaptive, human-mimicking agents. These papers effectively project the topic's methodological arms race—each detection advance met by adversarial adaptation—into an AI-native future, closing the loop opened by the founding CLSB papers: coordination detection is not a solved problem but a permanently contested one, whose targets evolve as fast as the tools built to catch them.

### Adjacent applications and outliers

Several papers sit at the topic's edges, applying coordination-adjacent lenses to phenomena (extremist mobilization, agenda-building, science communication, visual attribution, hashtag hijacking, cross-national trolling, Sky News propaganda networks, Proud Boys framing, Querdenken mainstreaming, Swedish far-right YouTube) that draw on or parallel CIB methodology without centering the CooRnet/CLSB toolkit itself—among them [[Bailard2024-pj]], [[Rothut2026-or]], [[Arceneaux2026-xk]], [[Efstratiou2026-ij]], [[Zhao2025-ny]], Bastos2025-ol, [[Kim2026-br]], [[Copland2025-em]], [[Askanius2026-de]], [[Knupfer2025-vt]], [[Smith2025-kc]], [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]], [[Goel2025-iq]], [[FitzGerald2025-nv]], and [[Slack1783507532-sl10]]. These extend the topic's reach into political communication, platform propaganda, and network-structural theory more broadly, confirming coordination detection's status as a general-purpose diagnostic lens now applied well beyond its original election-integrity use case.
