# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## From Anomaly to Ecosystem: How the Concept Travels

The papers filed here trace an arc from the initial framing of coordinated inauthentic behavior as a discrete, detectable anomaly to a more diffuse understanding of coordination as an ecosystem property. The foundational move is [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframed disinformation research away from content veracity and bad-actor detection toward *coordinated collective action*, operationalizing Facebook's own "coordinated inauthentic behavior" vocabulary through the CLSB algorithm on Italian elections. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] extends this into a living workflow — recognizing that static lists decay quickly as actors adapt, get suspended, or rebrand — while [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] applies the same behavioural, content-agnostic logic to COVID-19 fact-checking, showing that CLSB surfaces problematic content at roughly 40% versus a 28% baseline. Together these establish the PI's central methodological wager: behaviour is a more durable signal than content or identity.

## Contesting and Refining the Concept

A second cluster interrogates the concept itself. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "CIB" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and is tailored to real-name platforms, proposing "coordinated social media manipulation" grounded in a rational-choice model of principals trading off influence against detectability. [[Hurcombe2025-cs]] shows how Meta's Newsroom has strategically deployed "coordinated inauthentic behaviour" as a floating signifier that externalises problems from platform design — a discourse-analytic complement to Thiele's critique. [[Graham2026-fb]] pushes further, arguing from the Reddit "Anus_Fungi" case that the human/bot binary is analytically unstable and that authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance. [[Song2025-yh]]'s comparison of pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB networks empirically demonstrates the concept's ideological agnosticism: coordination can serve credible public-health messaging as readily as anti-vaccine narratives.

## Detection Methods and their Limits

A dense methodological conversation surrounds how coordination should actually be identified. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares monomodal, flattened, and multiplex operationalisations, showing that flattening — the field's dominant practice — discards structurally central nodes; [[Iannucci2025-eg]] extends this with time-aware multiplex modelling that adds an exponential-decay temporal kernel. [[Yang2025-iv]] responds to a specific vulnerability — timing signals are trivially manipulable — by exploiting speed and frequency regularities in link sharing. [[Minici2024-tf]] moves toward a graph foundation model that generalises across campaigns and few-shot settings, while [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the transferability limits of these methods on video-first TikTok, and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extends detection to decentralised Bluesky. Bastos2025-ol opens a further modality — visual identity signatures of troll-farm profiles. Yet [[Pante2025-pq]] warns that without proper control datasets and multi-signal analysis, prior findings (including claims of inter-state IO collaboration) may not survive scrutiny.

## Platform-Specific Ecologies

Extensions across platforms trace how coordination refracts through affordances. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts Rapid Spread Networks to encrypted WhatsApp in Brazil, showing coordinated messaging feeding into real-world mobilisation around the January 8 events. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] identifies message-forwarding clusters among pro-regime Russian and Belarusian Telegram channels; [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as an attention-and-narrative contest; [[Askanius2026-de]] maps the Swedish far-right YouTube alternative influence network fused with pandemic-era wellness and anti-vaccine communities. [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification during Romania's 2024 EP election, and [[Kim2026-br]] provides a two-decade view of suspected troll behaviour in Korean news comments, finding that morally condemning rhetoric — not overt foreign promotion — drives engagement.

## Actors, Organisations, Economies

Where detection work often abstracts away from principals, a set of papers rehumanises the operations. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] reconstructs the Prigozhin-era IRA as a medium-sized media/PR company embedded in Russia's labour market, integrating disinformation-for-hire with mainstream promotional culture. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reconceptualises Philippine influence operations as *political brokerage* undergirded by political-economic infrastructures. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] shows how Serbia's SNS mimics grassroots authenticity to evade detection tuned to centralised bot farms. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] tracks the industrial-scale expansion of coordinated Facebook gambling promotion after ChatGPT's launch, arguing that algorithmic amplification — not just moderation — is the operative regulatory site. [[Graham2025-gp]] and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] both stress that propaganda persists by *parasitising* rather than evading the trust and event infrastructures of the information environment.

## Contested Effects and Scale

A striking counter-current questions whether coordination actually matters as much as its detectability suggests. The Meta-collaborative [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]] show that 49 deceptive networks reached tens of millions of Americans in 2020, but that reach was dominated by resharing from *non-network* accounts, exposure was concentrated in a small elderly-conservative slice of users, network content averaged 0.3% of political content views, and associations with attitudes largely dissolved under adjustment. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]]'s post-hoc cascade analysis reaches a compatible verdict: observed coordinated placements in retweet trees resemble random more than strategic assignment, suggesting CIB is less pivotal to diffusion than assumed. [[Goel2025-iq]] complicates the picture from the other side — much narrative harm flows through mainstream articles repurposed by users, invisible to source-level or CIB-based metrics.

## The Emerging LLM/Swarm Horizon

The most recent papers pivot toward generative AI. [[Schroeder2026-im]] articulates the "malicious AI swarm" threat model — persistent, adaptive, cross-platform agentic operations capable of LLM grooming and synthetic consensus. [[Orlando2025-ul]] provides the first process-level simulation, showing that even minimal *teammate awareness* among LLM agents produces coordination signatures nearly indistinguishable from explicit collective decision-making, spontaneously reproducing amplification, narrative convergence, and hashtag diffusion. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] already documents an analogous empirical pattern in COVID-19 science communication, where a coordinated retweet network — not bot-driven — asymmetrically amplifies a small set of credentialed contrarian experts and precedes low-trust news coverage. Together with [[Kulichkina2026-zk]]'s work on dual-use coordination during China's COVID protests, these push the field toward a view of coordination as an emergent, ambient property of hybrid human–algorithmic ecosystems rather than a discrete adversarial artefact — precisely the horizon on which the next generation of detection, attribution, and governance work will have to operate.
