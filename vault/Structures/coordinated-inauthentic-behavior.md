# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The methodological core: from coordinated link sharing to a general grammar of coordination

The spine of this topic is the researcher's own line of tool-building, which begins with a conceptual argument rather than a piece of software: [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] insists that "fake news" scholarship has fixated on the intent of content-creators when it should attend to propagators and the cycles of judgment that make information circulate. That reorientation—from content/actor detection to coordinated collective *action*—is operationalized in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which introduces coordinated link sharing behavior (CLSB) as a content-agnostic signal of manipulation during the 2018/2019 Italian elections, and is generalized into the CooRnet toolkit used throughout the corpus. [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]] situates this methodological turn within broader shifts wrought by "big data" on political communication—blurred organizational boundaries, microtargeting, and attention manipulation—explaining why a behavior-first detection strategy was needed in the first place.

From there the lineage forks into applied demonstrations and infrastructural refinements. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] extends CLSB into a workflow for real-time fact-checking support during COVID-19, showing that coordinated networks evolve tactically (link-laundering, first-comment link-hiding, exploitation of religious groups) to evade detection. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] formalizes this into an iterative "detect–monitor–update" workflow for the 2022 Italian election, arguing that static lists of bad actors quickly go stale and that behavior-based seeding can surface previously unknown coordinated assets—ideological, commercial, and even religious-proselytizing. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Righetti2025-slf9]] apply the same lens to two different "alternative influence networks"—Italian COVID-skeptic Intellectual-Dark-Web figures and the transnational anti-gender organization CitizenGO—showing how legacy media remediation and centrally administered multilingual page networks respectively sustain coordinated amplification of fringe narratives. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] then abstracts the whole CLSB paradigm into CooRTweet, a platform-agnostic tool built on a minimal definition of coordination (repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object), validated against the South Korean NIS dataset and explicitly positioned as a successor to CooRnet's platform-specific constraints. Two very recent papers push this tradition into new empirical and conceptual territory: [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] shows that identical CooRnet-detected coordination signatures can belong to influence operations, legitimate media groups, or gambling ad networks alike, arguing that Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed opportunistically over time; and [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] extend the workflow to visual/generative-AI persuasion in gambling promotion and to a broader "Deceptive Information Operations" typology spanning propaganda, gambling, and poorly moderated adult-content groups—diagnosing a "Zombie Internet" condition in which coordinated seeding and platform affordance-exploitation blur the line between human and automated activity.

## Generalizing and stress-testing detection beyond CLSB

A second cluster treats coordination detection as a computational-methods problem in its own right, often explicitly benchmarking against or extending the CLSB tradition. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both argue that coordination is inherently multimodal and temporal: the former proposes a time-decayed multiplex network model validated on 26 labeled datasets, the latter systematically compares five ways of operationalizing multimodality on 2019 UK election data, finding multiplex community detection the most balanced. [[Yang2025-iv]] returns to Facebook link-sharing specifically to replace easily-gamed timing signals with statistical regularities in sharing speed and frequency. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] move toward foundation-model and embedding-based approaches—IOHunter fuses language models with graph neural networks for cross-campaign generalization, while CANE/t-CANE models users as distributions over latent narrative clusters to reconstruct cross-platform discourse networks, identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed roughly 70% of narratives migrating between Truth Social and X. [[Thiele2025-ol]] steps back to argue that the underlying concept itself—Meta's "coordinated inauthentic behavior"—conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent, proposing instead a rational-choice typology for attributing campaigns to covert principals based on scale, elaborateness, and disguise.

## Elections, voter manipulation, and information operations

A large share of the corpus applies coordination detection—or critiques its findings—to electoral contexts. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion entry [[noauthor_undated-bm]] report the Meta-collaborative FIES study of 49 deceptive networks in the 2020 US election, showing reach is concentrated in a few networks and driven mostly by reshares from non-network accounts, while post-hoc causal analysis finds little robust evidence that exposure shaped political outcomes—an important corrective note within the topic. [[Kim2026-wg]] instead finds a measurable causal effect: geo-racially targeted voter-suppression ads, including IRA-linked ones, plausibly depressed turnout among their targeted subpopulations. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] turns from platform data to labor-market OSINT, reconstructing the IRA's organizational structure via employee CVs and showing recruitment spiked around Crimea's annexation rather than the 2016 election—reframing troll factories as ordinary media-labor organizations. Other studies scrutinize coordination in non-US and non-Western elections and regimes: [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] on Serbia's SNS party mimicking grassroots support to evade detection, [[Gaw2025-ru]] reconceptualizing Philippine influence operations as political brokerage, [[Kim2026-br]] tracing two decades of suspected state-linked trolling in Korean news comments, [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] mapping pro-government Telegram coordination in Russia/Belarus, and [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] analyzing pro-Navalny/pro-government and COVID-protest coordination dynamics on Russian and Chinese Twitter, respectively extending connective-action theory to "connective counteraction" and digital repression. [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows that deplatforming a handful of central accounts around the Arizona "audit" produced network-wide ripple effects unrelated to CIB per se, while [[Pante2025-pq]] explicitly re-examines prior claims of inter-state coordination on X and finds them methodologically overstated absent proper controls—part of a broader skeptical throughline. [[Kansaon2025-id]] extends coordination detection into WhatsApp, documenting how coordinated Brazilian groups fed real-world mobilization including the January 8 attacks, and [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] examines advertising and coordination around the 2021 German election.

## Health communication and epidemic-adjacent applications

Coordination detection also anchors a cluster of health-communication studies. [[Song2025-yh]] applies CLSB/CooRnet comparatively to pro- and anti-vaccine Facebook networks in the UK and US, finding CLSB is ideologically agnostic—amplifying both misinformation and credible public-health messaging depending on context. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces a predominantly contrarian coordinated retweet network amplifying anti-consensus scientific experts and shows news coverage typically follows rather than leads superspreader activity. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] pushes furthest toward causal inference, embedding geolocated antivaccine tweets in a compartmental epidemic model to estimate a lower-bound body count attributable to online antivaccine speech. These sit alongside [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]'s COVID-fact-checking workflow and [[Marino2023-9137f448]]'s AIN analysis as evidence that the CLSB paradigm travels well from elections into pandemic-era information ecosystems.

## Platform-specific extensions and the limits of transferability

As the object of study diversifies beyond Facebook and Twitter, several papers ask whether coordination signatures transfer. [[Luceri2025-tr]] adapts network-similarity detection to TikTok's video-first affordances; [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] proposes real-time bot-coordination detection for the decentralized platform Bluesky; [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts a Twitter-derived "Rapid Spread Network" method to WhatsApp's closed, encrypted architecture; and Bastos2025-ol turns to computer vision on the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset to ask whether troll farms carry identifiable *visual* signatures. [[Graham2026-fb]], by contrast, complicates the whole enterprise from within: its Reddit "botting" case study shows that repetitive, bot-like posting can be an authentic participatory-culture practice rather than manipulation, arguing that authenticity is a negotiated cultural performance rather than a stable technical property—an important counterweight to detection-first framings elsewhere in the corpus.

## Far-right mainstreaming and alternative influence networks

A parallel strand uses coordination and network analysis to trace how extremist and radical content moves into mainstream discourse. [[Rothut2026-or]] introduces "protest-facilitated mainstreaming" via the German Querdenken movement's Telegram entanglement with far-right networks; [[Copland2025-em]] shows Sky News Australia operating a dual native/YouTube strategy that inserts Australian content into the US right-wing propaganda pipeline; [[Askanius2026-de]] maps a cohesive Swedish far-right "alternative influence network" on YouTube hybridizing activism with influencer monetization; [[Knupfer2025-vt]] traces a "logic of connective faction" linking Republican elites to hyper-partisan media via the Critical Race Theory issue; and [[Marwick2025-vx]] complements these structural accounts with a qualitative study of "redpilling" narratives, arguing radicalization is processual socialization rather than sudden exposure-driven conversion—useful ballast against overly mechanistic coordination-equals-effect assumptions. [[Bailard2024-pj]] and [[Righetti2025-slf9]] extend this arc to collective-action framing (Proud Boys) and transnational anti-gender advocacy (CitizenGO), respectively.

## Skepticism, scale, and the frontier of AI-driven coordination

The topic closes on papers that interrogate coordination's actual causal weight and anticipate its next mutation. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models influence in retweet cascades and finds real coordinated accounts perform far below optimal or even random-matched benchmarks, suggesting CIB may be less pivotal than assumed. [[Dahlke2026-sl34]], [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]], and [[Goel2025-iq]] each complicate simple exposure/reach narratives—showing platforms' indirect referral effects are underestimated, that misinformation diffusion is concentrated among few viral posts, and that "true" mainstream content is routinely repurposed to serve misleading narratives, escaping domain-list detection entirely. [[Donovan2025-ws]] historicizes the whole enterprise, arguing "misinformation-at-scale" and the CIB framework itself emerged as platforms' preferred, engagement-preserving alternative to adjudicating truth. Finally, [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] look forward: the former warns that LLM-agent swarms constitute a qualitatively new coordination threat beyond botnets, while the latter demonstrates in simulation that mere mutual awareness among generative agents—without central control—is sufficient to reproduce the dense, synchronized coordination signatures this entire topic has spent a decade learning to detect, suggesting the CLSB/CooRnet paradigm will need to evolve once more as the coordinating "actors" themselves become artificial.
