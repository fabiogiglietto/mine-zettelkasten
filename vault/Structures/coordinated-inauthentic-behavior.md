# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The CooRnet/CLSB lineage: a methodological signature

This corpus is anchored by a continuous methodological program running from [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] and [[Giglietto2019-0bd8b768]] through [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], and into the VERA-AI-adjacent case studies of [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] and [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]. The founding move—reframing disinformation research away from content-veracity or actor-blacklisting and toward *coordinated collective action*—is first operationalized as Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) on Facebook during the 2018/2019 Italian elections, then formalized as an iterative Actors-Behaviors-Content cycle in [[Slack1783507719-slc1]], and extended into a continuously-updating monitoring workflow in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]]. The CooRnet tool itself is generalized across platforms and content modalities in [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet, which explicitly builds on and tests against the South Korean NIS ground-truth dataset established by [[Keller2019-nk]]. This lineage culminates empirically in studies that mine the same behavioral signature for strikingly different phenomena—pandemic disinformation and covid-skeptic networks ([[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]), online firestorms as political protest ([[Slack1783507463-sl24]]), AI-generated gambling promotion ([[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]), and the multi-actor "Zombie Internet" condition of [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], which generalizes CLSB findings into a typology of Deceptive Information Operations spanning state propaganda, synthetic gambling ads, and poorly-moderated adult-content groups.

## Foundational theorization: coordination, authenticity, and the attribution problem

A parallel foundational strand establishes the conceptual vocabulary the empirical work depends on. [[Starbird2019-qv]] reframes information operations as *participatory, collaborative work*, insisting that bots-and-trolls framings obscure the blending of orchestrated and organic activity—a point echoed throughout the corpus whenever researchers find "real" users entangled with coordinated cores (e.g., [[Simeone2025-vo]], [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]). [[Keller2019-nk]] supplies the discipline's rare ground-truth case (the NIS astroturfing campaign) and the influential insight that coordination, not automation, is the more robust signature of managed disinformation—a claim structurally validated decades later by [[Nizzoli2020-cf]], which shows coordination and automation are orthogonal and that coordination itself is a continuum rather than a binary. These insights are synthesized and systematized in [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey, which proposes actors/synergic-actions/intent as the irreducible components of coordination and argues authenticity and harmfulness are themselves orthogonal axes. [[Thiele2025-ol]] pushes further into the normative register, arguing that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing a rational-choice typology oriented toward the harder problem of *attribution*—identifying the hidden principals behind campaigns. [[Rogers2025-sl8f]] operationalizes a version of this critique empirically, showing that identical CLSB signatures can belong to influence operations, legitimate media outlets, or political activists alike, and tracing how Meta's own definition of CIB has narrowed from broad "false amplifiers" to narrow "adversarial actors."

## Detection methods: multiplex, temporal, graph-native, and generalizable

A dense methods-development cluster works on the technical edges of coordination detection. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five operationalizations of multimodal coordination and finds multiplex community detection the best-balanced approach; [[Iannucci2025-eg]] extends this with a time-aware multiplex model validated against 26 benchmark datasets. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the vulnerability of timing-based signals directly, proposing speed/frequency regularities as harder-to-evade indicators, while [[Minici2024-tf]]'s IOHunter and [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE/t-CANE push toward graph-foundation-model and narrative-embedding approaches that generalize across campaigns and even across platforms (Truth Social/X). A notable sub-thread interrogates *influence*, not just detection: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] formally bound how much cascade influence coordinated accounts could plausibly exert, finding real UK-election coordinated accounts far below optimal placement—a sobering corrective to assumptions of CIB's outsized impact that resonates with [[Keller2019-nk]]'s finding of "limited measurable impact" for the well-resourced NIS campaign, and with [[Pante2025-pq]]'s re-scrutiny of claimed inter-state coordination using control datasets.

## Platform expansion beyond Facebook and Twitter

As the methodology matures, researchers extend it to platforms resistant to the original CooRnet logic. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts TikTok's video-first, non-textual affordances; [[Karo2026-dn]] documents jihadist "everyday extremism" exploiting TikTok's vernacular culture and keyword-filter blind spots, echoing adversarial-evasion themes found across the corpus (e.g., brandjacking and name-cycling in [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]). [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] targets real-time bot coordination on the decentralized Bluesky; [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts Rapid Spread Network detection to the encrypted, closed WhatsApp ecosystem in Brazil, linking coordinated messaging directly to the January 8 insurrection; and [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] bring the method to Telegram- and Twitter-based authoritarian information control in Russia/Belarus and China respectively.

## Case studies: states, parties, and manufactured consensus

A geographically wide-ranging empirical layer applies these tools to specific political contexts. State-linked operations are examined historically and organizationally in [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT reconstruction of the IRA's labor-market structure and in Bastos2025-ol's visual-identity analysis of troll farms; [[Appel2026-qr]] quantifies the reach of 49 Meta-identified deceptive networks in the 2020 US election, emphasizing that financially motivated operations deserve equal scrutiny to politically motivated ones and that most exposure occurs via organic reshares rather than direct network posting. Electoral contexts recur throughout: Italy ([[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2019-0bd8b768]], [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]]), Germany ([[Righetti2025-sl2a]], [[Slack1783507532-sl10]], [[Klinger2023-qu]]), Romania ([[Oprea2025-lf]]), Serbia ([[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]]), South Korea ([[Kim2026-br]], [[Keller2019-nk]]), the Philippines ([[Gaw2025-ru]]), Russia ([[Kulichkina2025-sl09]]), and the UK ([[Nizzoli2020-cf]], [[Mannocci2025-ig]], [[Di-Marco2025-aa]]). Several of these studies converge on a key theoretical point: coordination is not inherently malicious. [[Song2025-yh]] finds both pro- and anti-vaccine communities coordinating, with credibility rather than coordination itself distinguishing them; [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] documents a decentralized, mimetic form of digital authoritarianism (Serbia's SNS) explicitly designed to resemble authentic grassroots support, extending the detection challenge raised by [[Thiele2025-ol]] and [[Rogers2025-sl8f]].

## Alternative influence networks, mainstreaming, and ideological laundering

A related cluster examines how coordination enables ideas to migrate from fringe to mainstream without necessarily looking like conventional CIB. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces an Italian "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster amplifying covid-skeptic narratives via legacy-media remediation; [[Askanius2026-de]] maps a Swedish far-right "alternative influence network" on YouTube that fuses influencer monetization with propaganda; [[Righetti2025-slf9]] shows CitizenGO's centrally coordinated multilingual network functioning as transnational "symbolic glue" for anti-gender politics; and [[Klinger2023-qu]] details how AfD "laundered" extremist Identitarian content through intermediary networks to avoid visible coordination with a surveilled movement. [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Rieder2026-pp]] extend this logic to propaganda infrastructures (Sky News Australia's dual Facebook/YouTube strategy) and ambient ideological ecosystems that survive deplatforming (the "Tate-space"), while [[Tuters2026-vo]] offers a more infrastructural theory of conspiratorial alignment—"the Memeplex"—arguing that patterned circulation, not coherent belief, produces apparent coordination around the Great Reset. [[Rothut2026-or]] closes this loop by showing how the Querdenken protest movement itself became a conduit connecting moderate publics to organized far-right and conspiracist networks, a case of "protest-facilitated mainstreaming" synthesized at a meta-level in [[Rothut2026-wt]]'s 25-year scoping review of online radicalization research.

## Consequences, countermeasures, and the coming AI-era

Several papers push the topic toward its edges: offline consequences and moderation efficacy. [[Bailard2024-pj]] links Proud Boys framing dynamics to offline violence; [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows targeted deplatforming producing network-wide "ripple effects" exceeding simple node removal, explicitly ruling out CIB as the driving mechanism; [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces how a genuinely coordinated (but non-automated) contrarian network shapes COVID science communication pathways into news media; and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as an opinion-contest tactic. Finally, a forward-looking cluster anticipates how generative AI reshapes the whole problem: [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates LLM agents spontaneously reproducing IO coordination mechanics under mere "teammate awareness," [[Schroeder2026-im]] theorizes "malicious AI swarms" as a qualitative escalation beyond botnets, [[Crichton2026-kn]] documents a "re-generative pipeline" sanitizing and recirculating far-right imagery through commercial image models, and [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] empirically documents an exponential, ChatGPT-coincident surge in coordinated gambling-promotion volume—together suggesting that the CLSB/CIB methodology's next major challenge is detecting coordination whose very authors may no longer be human.
