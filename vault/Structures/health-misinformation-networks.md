# Health Misinformation Networks

## The methodological core: coordinated link-sharing as a signal

The empirical backbone of this cluster is a single behavioral signal — Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) — and the tool built to detect it. [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] introduces CooRnet, operationalizing François's Actors-Behaviors-Content framework as an iterative cycle: start from flagged harmful content, use timing anomalies in shares to surface coordinated pages/groups/profiles, then feed the resulting actors' output back into the next round. The key methodological wager is that *behavior* (unusually synchronized sharing) is a more durable and content-agnostic tell than either debunking specific claims or maintaining static blacklists of "bad actors," precisely because malicious networks constantly rotate domains and accounts. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalizes this into a working prototype for the Italian fact-checking outlet Facta.news, demonstrating in a live one-month test that CLSB-based surfacing outperforms routine fact-checking's baseline hit rate — the clearest evidence in the corpus that coordination-detection can genuinely *accelerate* fact-checking rather than merely describing misinformation after the fact. Both papers document the same empirical object across pandemic waves: an Italian covid-skeptic network that emerges as an offshoot of pre-existing political (League/Five Star) coordinated clusters, and that adapts tactics — link-laundering through reputable outlets, first-comment link placement, exploitation of religious/community groups — specifically to evade platform enforcement. This lineage effectively defines "coordinated networks" for the rest of the topic: not simply clusters of anti-vaccine believers, but structurally organized amplification apparatuses with their own life cycle, discoverable through timing signatures rather than semantic content.

## What coordinated networks actually circulate

Once the network is mapped, two companion papers ask what it circulates and why it is effective. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] follows the same Italian covid-skeptic accounts identified via CLSB and shows that an "Alternative Influence Network," anchored by an Intellectual-Dark-Web-style cluster of contrarian intellectuals, achieves outsized engagement relative to volume — driven overwhelmingly by shares rather than comments, a signature of hyperpartisan, low-friction amplification. Crucially, legacy media and mainstream journalists remain the central nodes recirculating these voices: the network's power depends on *remediation*, recycling expert soundbites from TV and newspapers into shareable Facebook artifacts that then evade the scrutiny those original venues might invite. This dovetails with [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]]'s pre-pandemic study of Google News Italia as a health-information gatekeeper, which shows that emotionally charged, "popular" health topics (celebrity illness, healthcare-system malfunction) generate disproportionate engagement compared to substantive scientific or clinical content — establishing, before COVID-19 even arrived, the structural asymmetry in attention that coordinated actors would later exploit. Together these three Italy-focused papers trace a single argument: coordination doesn't create demand for problematic health content out of nothing, it channels a pre-existing hybrid-media appetite for emotionally resonant, share-friendly narratives, using legitimate journalistic and institutional content as raw material.

## Coordination beyond Italy: comparative and platform-native accounts

The corpus's non-Italian coordination studies extend the same logic to different platforms and countries, testing whether coordinated amplification is inherently partisan-loaded or ideologically neutral. [[Song2025-yh]] applies CLSB analysis (via the same CooRnet lineage) to UK and US Facebook vaccine discourse and finds a genuinely two-sided picture: anti-vaccine networks are denser and more engaged in the UK, while pro-vaccine coordination dominates in the US, with national political culture (safety skepticism vs. individual-freedom framing) shaping content even when the coordination *mechanism* is identical — an explicit argument that CLSB itself is ideologically agnostic and can scaffold credible public-health messaging as easily as misinformation. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] shifts to Twitter and shows an asymmetric picture: a coordinated retweet network is almost entirely contrarian, deliberately amplifying a narrow set of credentialed anti-consensus experts who are not otherwise the platform's most popular voices, while organically influential "superspreaders" skew pro-consensus. It further shows that news coverage of scientific papers tends to *follow* superspreader activity rather than precede it, and that high- versus low-trust outlets align respectively with conformist versus contrarian superspreaders — extending the Italian "remediation" finding into an explicit information-pathway model linking platform actors to the broader news ecosystem.

## From network structure to belief and behavior

A second arc pushes past network mapping to ask what coordinated and diffuse misinformation exposure actually does to beliefs and outcomes. [[Adam2026-tz]] combines panel survey and web-tracking data to show that conspiracy belief formation is a "marriage" of predispositions and media diet: populists and the politically distrustful selectively engage with conspiracy-supporting alternative media and avoid mainstream debunking, and when they do encounter debunking, it can backfire rather than correct — a micro-level mechanism that helps explain why the coordinated contrarian clusters documented by [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] and [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] find such a durable audience. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] takes the causal question furthest, building a compartmental SIRVA epidemic model fit to geolocated antivaccine tweets to estimate that Twitter antivaccine content causally suppressed vaccination uptake, translating online coordination-adjacent speech into a lower-bound body count of excess cases and deaths — the clearest attempt in the corpus to close the loop from network-level content to offline epidemiological harm. [[Lyons2026-ca]] complicates the picture from the demand side: linking survey and passive browsing data, it shows that exposure to low-credibility health content is rare in the aggregate but extremely concentrated (top 10% of users account for 77% of exposure), skews sharply toward older adults, and travels through habitual cross-site referral chains among low-credibility domains rather than through search or social media referrals — a reminder that not all problematic health information exposure is coordination-driven or platform-native in the way the Italian and Twitter studies emphasize.

## Diagnosing the ecosystem: voids as the demand-side complement

[[Scalco2026-bd]] supplies a structural, ecosystem-level diagnostic that complements the actor-centered work above: rather than mapping who coordinates supply, it quantifies "information voids" — anomalous mismatches between public demand (Wikipedia views, search trends) and reliable supply (news, social posts) — around COVID-19 vaccine events across six European countries. Its finding that voids are empirically associated with reduced high-credibility content and increased misinformation prevalence offers a mechanistic bridge between the demand-side vulnerability documented in [[Lyons2026-ca]] and [[Adam2026-tz]] and the supply-side coordinated amplification documented in the Italian CooRnet lineage and [[Song2025-yh]]/[[Efstratiou2026-ij]]: coordinated contrarian networks and alternative influence clusters appear to be especially effective precisely during the windows when legitimate information supply lags public demand, suggesting that fact-checking acceleration tools like CooRnet and void-detection methods like this one are naturally complementary early-warning instruments for the same underlying infodemic dynamic.
