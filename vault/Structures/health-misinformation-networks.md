# Health Misinformation Networks

## The coordination paradigm: CooRnet and the Italian laboratory

A substantial cluster of papers filed here shares a common methodological lineage: the detection of Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) on Facebook via the CooRnet toolkit, developed and refined against the backdrop of the Italian COVID-19 infodemic. [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] lays out the foundational method, operationalizing Camille François's Actors-Behaviors-Content framework as an iterative detection loop: start from debunked claims, find the networks that coordinate to share them, then use those networks' own output to seed the next round. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (the MINE-FACTS project proper) takes this infrastructure and turns it into an operational tool for Italian fact-checkers, tracing how a covid-skeptic coordinated network grew out of pre-existing political clusters (League, Five Star Movement) and cataloguing the evasive tactics these networks adopted — first-comment link placement, "link laundering" through reputable outlets, and infiltration of unrelated religious or civic groups. Crucially, the paper demonstrates that a purely behavioral, content-agnostic signal can surface problematic content at a substantially higher hit rate than routine manual fact-checking, directly answering the "speed-up" aspiration in the project's name.

Two companion papers mine this same Italian CLSB infrastructure for what the coordinated actors actually say and who amplifies them. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] zooms in on the "Alternative Influence Network" that rides atop these coordinated clusters, showing that a small, high-engagement "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of pundits and that legacy journalists and right-leaning newspapers remain surprisingly central nodes — problematic content is less a fringe phenomenon than one sustained by remediation of mainstream statements. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] complements this by examining the gatekeeping role of Google News Italia upstream of Facebook circulation, finding that algorithmic aggregation foregrounds technical/scientific health topics while user engagement downstream is disproportionately driven by emotionally charged "popular" stories (celebrity illness, healthcare malfunction) — a structural mismatch between what gatekeepers prioritize and what audiences amplify that helps explain why coordinated networks find such fertile ground. Read together, these three papers trace a pipeline from gatekeeping, through coordinated amplification, to influencer-driven virality, all within a single national case.

[[Song2025-yh]] extends the CLSB paradigm cross-nationally (UK and US) and, importantly, de-moralizes it: coordination is shown to be an ideologically neutral *tactic* rather than an inherently malicious signature, used as effectively by pro-vaccine institutional networks (NHS, CDC) as by anti-vaccine ones. Its finding that UK anti-vaccine coordination clusters around safety skepticism while US anti-vaccine coordination clusters around individual-liberty framing echoes the religious/ethnic and political inflection that the broader Gates-funded program (India/Nigeria) was designed to probe, reinforcing that coordinated sharing behavior is inseparable from local political culture.

## From detecting coordination to measuring its consequences

A second thread pushes past network detection to ask whether coordinated or low-quality content actually changes beliefs or outcomes — a question CooRnet-style studies can map but not answer on their own. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] offers the most ambitious causal claim in the set, building a compartmental SIRVA epidemic model fit to geolocated antivaccine tweets, attributing roughly 14,000 vaccine refusals and measurable excess cases and deaths to Twitter exposure — a rare attempt to close the loop from online speech to offline epidemiological harm. [[Allen2024-av]] arrives at a strikingly different emphasis using survey experiments and large-scale exposure data: fact-check-flagged misinformation is more persuasive per view but reaches almost nobody, while unflagged, "vaccine-skeptical" content — often from mainstream outlets — drives roughly 46 times more aggregate harm simply by virtue of scale. This directly complicates the coordination-centric framing of the CooRnet cluster: if mainstream, non-coordinated content is the larger driver of hesitancy, then interventions targeting coordinated inauthentic networks may be necessary but insufficient.

[[Lyons2026-ca]] reinforces this exposure-centered skepticism with passive web-tracking data, showing that low-credibility health content exposure is rare in the aggregate but extremely concentrated — a small, older, more conspiracist-minded population absorbs the overwhelming majority of it, reached not via social media or search but through habitual cross-site referral chains among low-credibility domains themselves. This finding sits productively against the coordination literature: it suggests that beyond organized sharing networks, there exists a self-reinforcing "low-credibility web" that operates largely outside platform recommendation systems, posing a distinct intervention challenge. [[Adam2026-tz]] supplies the belief-formation mechanism linking exposure to outcome, using web-tracking plus panel data to show that conspiracy belief is jointly produced by alternative-media contagion and mainstream-media mitigation, filtered through populist predispositions that drive selective engagement, selective avoidance, and even backfire effects when debunking is encountered. Together, [[Lyons2026-ca]], [[Allen2024-av]], and [[Adam2026-tz]] suggest that the "who coordinates with whom" question answered by the CooRnet studies needs to be paired with "who is exposed, how much, and with what psychological predisposition" to fully explain downstream harm.

## Ecosystem-level diagnostics: voids, pathways, and superspreaders

A final pair of papers steps back from both coordination networks and individual exposure to characterize the broader information ecosystem's structure and failure modes. [[Scalco2026-bd]] formalizes the notion of "information voids" — periods where public demand for reliable content outstrips supply — showing empirically that such voids, triggered by events like vaccine authorizations, are associated with reduced high-credibility content and increased misinformation share, and persist asymmetrically longer than periods of informational overabundance. This offers a demand-side complement to the supply-side focus of the coordination literature: coordinated networks may be especially effective precisely during these void periods, when legitimate supply has not yet caught up.

[[Efstratiou2026-ij]] closes the loop between platform coordination and news media by tracing the pathways through which COVID-19 scientific papers circulate: a small, overwhelmingly contrarian coordinated network disproportionately amplifies a handful of credentialed anti-consensus experts, while conformist "superspreaders" (mostly verified physicians and scientists) dominate everyday discourse; critically, news coverage is shown to typically *follow* rather than precede superspreader activity on Twitter, with high- and low-trust outlets each aligning with their respective superspreader camp. This mirrors, at the level of scientific discourse rather than vaccine URLs, the remediation dynamic documented in [[Marino2023-9137f448]]: legacy media do not stand outside the misinformation ecosystem but are drawn into its circulation pathways, often following rather than correcting platform-level coordination.

## Synthesis

Across this set, a consistent arc emerges: methodologically, the field moved from building better detectors of coordinated inauthentic behavior ([[Slack1783507719-slc1]], [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]) to asking what such networks actually say and who listens ([[Marino2023-9137f448]], [[Song2025-yh]], [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]]), and finally to interrogating whether detected coordination is even the right unit of harm, given that exposure is concentrated, persuasion often flows from unflagged mainstream content, and ecosystem-level supply/demand imbalances shape when misinformation takes hold ([[Lyons2026-ca]], [[Allen2024-av]], [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]], [[Scalco2026-bd]], [[Adam2026-tz]], [[Efstratiou2026-ij]]). The throughline connecting the Italian CLSB case studies to the broader cross-country ambitions of this research program (India, Nigeria) is the recurring finding that coordination tactics are portable and ideologically agnostic, but their content, framing, and civic consequence are always refracted through local political, religious, and media context — meaning no single network signature or exposure metric travels cleanly across settings without that contextual calibration.
