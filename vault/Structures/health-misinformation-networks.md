# Health Misinformation Networks

## The Structure of Coordination: From Detection to Consequence

The papers filed here trace an arc from *how to see* coordinated health misinformation, to *what it looks like* in specific national contexts, to *whether it actually matters* for beliefs and behavior. Read together they form something like a methodological genealogy: a family of network-detection tools built around Facebook's CooRnet infrastructure, applied to increasingly detailed country and topic case studies, set against a second cluster of papers that step back and ask the harder causal question — does any of this coordinated activity move the needle on vaccination, belief, or death counts?

### Building the detection infrastructure

The technical backbone of this topic is the CooRnet methodology for identifying Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) on Facebook. [[Slack1783507719-slc1]] lays out the core tool, operationalizing Camille François's Actors-Behaviors-Content framework as an iterative cycle — content seeds detection of coordinated behavior, which surfaces malicious actors, whose subsequent output reseeds the next round. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] takes this infrastructure into production, building the MINE-FACTS prototype for Italian fact-checkers and demonstrating, in a live one-month test with Facta.news, that content-agnostic coordination signals surface problematic content at nearly double the hit rate of routine manual fact-checking. Both papers converge on a key methodological insight: purely content-based (debunking) or purely actor-based (blocklist) approaches are brittle against adversaries who dynamically launder links, hide URLs in first comments, or route through image macros and religious groups — coordination behavior itself is a more durable signal than any specific claim or actor identity.

This infrastructure then radiates into substantive case studies of the Italian COVID information ecosystem. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] uses the coordinated-actor dataset to trace an "Alternative Influence Network," showing that an Intellectual Dark Web cluster of contrarian intellectuals generates disproportionately shareable content, and — crucially — that legacy media and mainstream journalists remain the central conduits through which this content is *remediated* into circulation, rather than being displaced by fringe actors. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] examines an adjacent gatekeeping layer, Google News Italia, finding that algorithmic aggregation privileges technical/scientific health topics while user engagement on Facebook skews toward emotionally charged, non-technical stories — a structural mismatch between what institutions promote and what audiences amplify that helps explain why coordinated networks find fertile ground downstream. Together, these three papers characterize a full pipeline: gatekeepers select, engagement patterns filter, and coordinated networks exploit the resulting attentional imbalance.

### From single-platform coordination to cross-national and cross-platform comparison

[[Song2025-yh]] extends the CLSB paradigm comparatively, contrasting UK and US Facebook vaccine discourse and complicating the assumption that coordination is inherently malicious: pro-vaccine communities coordinate too, circulating credible institutional content (NHS, CDC), while anti-vaccine coordination diverges nationally — UK networks fixate on safety and scientific skepticism, US networks on individual freedom — showing that coordination is a *tactic* shaped by political culture rather than a fixed signature of bad actors. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] pushes the same logic onto Twitter/X and into the news ecosystem, identifying an almost entirely contrarian coordinated retweet network that amplifies a narrow set of credentialed anti-consensus experts, and tracing how this Twitter-based coordination *precedes* rather than follows news coverage, with low-trust outlets aligning with contrarian superspreaders and high-trust outlets aligning with mainstream ones. This paper effectively bridges the network-mapping tradition with the media-effects question that dominates the second half of this topic.

[[Scalco2026-bd]] offers a complementary infrastructural contribution at a more macro, demand-side level: rather than mapping who coordinates, it quantifies *information voids* — moments when public demand for vaccine information outstrips credible supply — and shows these voids are empirically associated with drops in high-credibility content and rises in misinformation share, giving the coordination literature a structural, event-triggered explanation for *when* windows of vulnerability open.

### Does coordination and exposure translate into belief and behavior?

A second thread interrogates causal consequence more directly, and results are notably mixed. [[Adam2026-tz]] combines panel survey and web-tracking data in Germany/Switzerland to show that conspiracy belief formation is a genuine interaction of media exposure and political predispositions: alternative media produce contagion, mainstream media produce mitigation, but populists selectively engage/avoid and even counterargue against debunking — a nuanced rebuttal to any simple "exposure causes belief" story. [[Lyons2026-ca]] complements this with fine-grained behavioral tracking showing low-credibility health content exposure is rare in the aggregate but extremely concentrated among older, more conspiracist, politically dubious-news-consuming users, and crucially that this exposure flows through cross-domain low-credibility referral chains rather than search or social media — suggesting coordinated network effects may matter less than habitual, individually-driven consumption ecologies.

The most direct causal claims come from [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] and [[Allen2024-av]], and they point in tension with each other. Bollenbacher et al. build a novel SIRVA epidemic model fit to geolocated antivaccine tweets and estimate a genuine, if modest, causal chain from Twitter antivaccine content to ~14,000 vaccine refusals, ~545 cases, and 8 deaths. Allen et al., working at Facebook scale with experimental persuasion estimates, argue the opposite emphasis: flagged misinformation was more persuasive per view but reached so few people that its aggregate societal impact was dwarfed 46-fold by *unflagged*, factually accurate but "vaccine-skeptical" mainstream content — directly challenging the infodemic framing that motivates much of the coordination-detection literature. Read against [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]], which show legacy media as central amplification nodes for problematic Italian content, Allen et al.'s finding that mainstream outlets (not fringe coordinated actors) drove the largest US vaccine-hesitancy impact suggests a convergent, uncomfortable conclusion: coordinated inauthentic networks may be easier to detect and study than the mainstream-media pathways that matter more.

[[Lieu2025-nl]] rounds out this causal thread from an adjacent domain (climate misinformation), showing that content *category* (e.g., attacks on climate solutions) rather than rhetorical fallacy type drives perceived veracity and political polarization — reinforcing, across topics, the broader pattern in this collection that *what* is said and *who* says it (mainstream vs. fringe, credentialed vs. not) matters more for downstream belief effects than surface-level markers of coordination or logical fallacy.

### The arc

Taken as a whole, this set of papers moves from infrastructure (CooRnet, MINE-FACTS) to characterization (Italian AIN/IDW networks, UK/US CLSB comparison, Twitter coordination and news pathways, information voids) to consequence (panel/tracking studies of belief formation, exposure concentration among vulnerable populations, and competing causal estimates of platform impact on vaccination). The throughline is a productive tension: tools optimized to detect *coordination* are very good at finding fringe, adversarial, algorithm-gaming networks, but the causal-impact papers increasingly suggest that mainstream media and habitual, individually-selected consumption patterns — not necessarily coordinated inauthentic behavior — carry the larger burden of real-world harm. Future work in this space (including forthcoming Gates-funded India and Nigeria country studies) will need to reconcile these two findings: coordinated networks are detectable and dramatic, but may not be where the epidemiological damage is actually done.
