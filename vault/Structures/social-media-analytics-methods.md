# Social Media Analytics and Digital Methods

## From Golden Age to Walled Garden: A Field Renegotiating Its Empirical Ground

The papers collected here trace an arc from the exuberant expansion of computational social science on open APIs to its present precarity, and forward to a landscape being remade by regulation, platform strategy, and methodological improvisation. The two most sweeping historical accounts — [[Freelon2024-sc]] and [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] — periodize this arc: a laissez-faire prehistory, a brief "Golden Age" of Twitter's Academic API, and a post-2023 era of pay-to-play access and "independence by permission." [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]] provides the bibliometric obituary, documenting 33,306 Twitter studies and a 13% decline in output after the API closure, while Bastos2025-ya eulogizes the platform's distinctive research affordances. [[Yang2026-tq]]'s systematic review over 2010–2024 shows the same pattern at the scale of the whole social sciences: Twitter/X and Facebook dominated empirical work, and the plateau since 2022 coincides precisely with the API restrictions others chronicle.

## The Post-API Toolkit: Access Regimes and Their Discontents

A cluster of papers examines the regulatory and infrastructural response. [[Ohme2026-nv]], [[Peters2026-mo]], [[Giglietto2025-ed60bc90]], and [[Lukito2026-nb]] all read Article 40 of the DSA as a paradigm shift from platform philanthropy to legal obligation — but a fragile one. [[Peters2026-mo]] shows that data *quality* was almost absent from official EU discourse and had to be inserted by academic and NGO stakeholders during consultation; [[Lukito2026-nb]] recasts access barriers as "price, proficiency, or permission," warning of WEIRD-centric infrastructure and the ephemerality of platform-led regimes; [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] documents uneven early DSA compliance and preliminary findings against TikTok and Meta. [[Heiss2026-qv]] and [[Bak-Coleman2026-mk]] add a critical edge: exclusive data control produces industry influence analogous to tobacco or pharma, with roughly half of high-profile papers carrying undisclosed industry ties and an estimated 80% "industrial saturation" once editors and reviewers are counted.

## Audit and Critique of the Data That Remains

Even where access exists, the data itself is compromised in patterned ways. [[Rieder2025-ju]] audits YouTube's search API as "forgetful by design," with a temporal decay that erases historical content for retrospective research. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] shows that Meta's 100-share threshold in the URL Shares Dataset introduces platform-wide algorithmic artifacts that mimic social signal. [[Luhring2025-od]] audits NewsGuard, finding that binary trustworthy/untrustworthy cutoffs distort findings while continuous scores are stable. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] demonstrates that ex-situ web scraping introduces ~34% content disparity from in-situ browsing — a distortion driven not by time delay but by paywalls, logins, and cookie walls invisible to scrapers. Together these audits argue that platform data is not a neutral trace but an artifact co-produced by ranking, thresholding, and access architecture.

## Improvised Methods for a Closing Ecosystem

Against this backdrop, several papers develop workarounds. [[Allen2025-ot]] frames browser-extension experiments as "platform-independent" tools filling the gap left by vanished collaborations. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] uses a data-donation program to reverse-engineer X's recommender embeddings; [[Fan2026-af]] uses donated cross-platform traces to model temporal user-sequences; [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] pioneers Facebook-ad-based survey recruitment as an early workaround to post–Cambridge Analytica API closure. [[Schulte2026-df]] and [[Dahlke2026-sl34]] make the multi-platform turn explicit — the former arguing that platform observability should itself be treated as reusable research infrastructure, the latter showing via counterfactual simulation that direct-referrer studies underestimate misinformation effects by factors of 1.5–15×. [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] links self-report questionnaires to Reddit activity, exemplifying the growing hybrid-data approach.

## Coordination Detection and Cross-Platform Structure

A sustained methodological conversation runs through papers on coordinated inauthentic behavior. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] independently push CooRnet-style detection toward multiplex, time-aware networks, showing that single-modality or flattened multimodal approaches miss important structure. [[Yang2025-iv]] proposes evasion-resistant signals based on link-sharing statistical regularities; [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] and [[Luceri2025-tr]] extend the toolkit to Bluesky and TikTok respectively. [[Gerard2025-br]] reframes cross-platform influence as latent narrative alignment rather than direct interaction, identifying "bridge users" who seed narratives migrating between Truth Social and X. [[Smith2025-kc]] complements this with a causal account of triadic closure via "attention brokers."

## Beyond Text: Visual, Multimodal, and Algorithmic Turns

As platforms have become more visual and algorithmic, methods have followed. [[Gardam2025-er]] argues for a visual-first methodology on Instagram climate denial; [[Arminio2025-tw]] shows that VLLM-generated descriptions of images produce more connotatively meaningful — and interpretable — clusters than CNN pipelines. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes "practice mapping" via vector embeddings as an alternative to network-visualization "hairballs." [[McNally2025-dn]] treats the News Feed algorithm not as a black box but as a sociotechnical system whose ranking updates leave detectable, lagged traces in engagement — an approach directly aligned with DSA-style systemic auditing. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]]'s embedding audit sits in the same register.

## What the Data Sees, and What It Misses

Several papers reflect on the epistemic gap between social media traces and the publics they purport to represent. [[Oswald2025-km]] articulates the "production–consumption gap": a tiny minority produces most political content, so visible discourse is the tip of an iceberg. [[Green2025-ap]] shows that source-level partisanship scores conceal substantial story-level heterogeneity — networked curation "unbundles" stories into new partisan audiences. [[Gaisbauer2025-by]] similarly argues for multi-level "political cartography" across story, outlet, and content. [[Scalco2026-bd]] operationalizes "information voids" as demand–supply imbalances, linking them empirically to increased misinformation prevalence. [[Zhu2026-tn]] and [[Lai2024-to]] extend audience-overlap and latent-ideology methods to comparative media systems and YouTube video content. [[F2020-6278a4aa]] provides an earlier framing of why traditional political-communication methodology strains under these conditions.

## The Emerging Consensus

Taken together, these papers converge on several claims. First, that platform data access is a political and economic settlement, not a natural resource — and that the current settlement is precarious, uneven, and geographically inequitable. Second, that data quality, coverage, and representativeness must be treated as first-order methodological problems, not background concerns; audits of NewsGuard [[Luhring2025-od]], YouTube [[Rieder2025-ju]], the URL Shares Dataset [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]], and web scraping [[Ulloa2024-jm]] all sound this note. Third, that methodological innovation is moving simultaneously toward *multi-platform* [[Schulte2026-df]] [[Dahlke2026-sl34]] [[Gerard2025-br]], *multimodal* [[Gardam2025-er]] [[Arminio2025-tw]] [[Iannucci2025-eg]], and *user-centric or donated-data* designs [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] [[Fan2026-af]] [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]]. Fourth, that the field must reckon with its own dependencies — on industry funding [[Bak-Coleman2026-mk]] [[Heiss2026-qv]], on ephemeral platform generosity [[Freelon2024-sc]], and on visible-but-unrepresentative traces [[Oswald2025-km]]. The through-line is a discipline learning, sometimes reluctantly, that its empirical infrastructure was never neutral and cannot be taken for granted.
