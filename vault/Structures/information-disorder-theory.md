# Information Disorder & Hybrid Media Theory

## The Taxonomic Core: From "Fake News" to "Information Disorder"

The structure note's foundation lies in the conceptual labour of naming the problem. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] stakes out the pivotal move: 'fake news' is a creator-centric category that collapses under the weight of hybrid-system circulation, and attention must shift to propagators and their chains of judgment. This sits in direct lineage with [[Chadwick2013-ns]] and [[Chadwick2015-ii]], whose hybrid media system framework—old and new media logics bound by adaptation and interdependence rather than displacement—supplies the structural vocabulary that much of this corpus inherits, explicitly or not. [[Freelon2020-yp]] does the parallel definitional work for disinformation itself, anchoring it to the EC High Level Expert Group's triad of deception, harm, and intent, while [[Bennett2018-ys]] scales the diagnosis up to a macro "disinformation order"—a systemic erosion of institutional trust rather than a cluster of isolated incidents. [[Lazer2018-mm]] and [[Allcott2017-yz]] represent the foundational empirical-skeptical pole, each insisting that definitional rigor (fabrication mimicking journalistic form; market-equilibrium analysis of why falsehood is cheap to produce) must precede alarm. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] and [[Thiele2025-ol]] extend this taxonomic project to the adjacent construct of *coordination*, both arguing that platform-native categories (CIB) conflate inauthenticity with intent and need reconstruction around orthogonal axes (authenticity/harmfulness; scale/elaborateness/disguise). [[Frischlich2025-vn]] closes this cluster with a meta-critique: the field's own governing metaphors (virus, warfare, pollution) are themselves taxonomic choices that foreclose certain interventions—a complexity framing echoed in [[Mahl2026-hc]]'s Delphi synthesis, which finds expert consensus converging on multi-level, no-silver-bullet governance.

## Narrative and Cultural Turns Against Infocentrism

A second, increasingly dominant strand pushes back against what [[Sadler2025-vu]] calls the field's "infocentrism"—its reduction of disinformation to binary true/false claims. [[Marwick2026-qd]] is the clearest statement: disinformation is cross-platform, identity-affirming, cathartic *narrative*, recognizable through synecdochic catchphrases rather than discrete facts. [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]] operationalizes this empirically, showing cumulative narrative exposure—not single-claim encounters—predicts belief. [[Starbird2025-jj]] and [[Prochaska2025-ef]] develop complementary CSCW accounts of election-rumor "deep stories," where evidence and partisan frame interact collaboratively across quote-tweets and comments, and where *how* a story is told (style, allusion, cueing) is as constitutive as its content. [[Sadler2025-vu]] supplies the philosophical grounding via Ricoeur and Phelan, arguing content can be ethically and referentially problematic without being falsifiable in the narrow sense. [[Marwick2025-vx]] extends the narrative lens to radicalization itself, reframing "redpilling" as processual socialization through disinformation-as-evidence rather than sudden conversion. [[Goel2025-iq]] gives this cluster its sharpest methodological rebuke to source-list approaches: mainstream, factually accurate articles are routinely repurposed to support misleading narratives, meaning domain-level classification systematically undercounts the phenomenon. [[Rohrbach2026-rc]] adds that journalistic strategies can themselves inadvertently *form* new conspiracy beliefs, not merely diffuse existing ones.

## The Empirical Baseline: Prevalence, Diffusion, Sharing

Underpinning both taxonomic and narrative turns is a body of now-classic empirical work establishing basic facts about scale and diffusion. [[Vosoughi2018-at]] and [[Del-Vicario2016-uj]] established that falsehood outperforms truth in depth, breadth, and velocity, and that echo-chamber homogeneity drives cascade size. [[Allen2020-nj]] and [[Allcott2019-gn]] complicate the alarm: fake news is a vanishingly small fraction of the whole media diet and its relative weight on Facebook declined post-2016, even as Twitter's held steady—an early signal of the *platform-specificity* that recurs throughout this set. [[Guess2019-ym]] and [[Osmundsen2021-et]] narrow in on who shares, converging on partisan motivation (specifically out-party animus) and age over ignorance or trolling as explanatory variables—directly setting up [[Pennycook2021-jq]]'s influential reframing of truth discernment as a function of analytic reasoning and inattention rather than motivated partisan cognition. [[Lewandowsky2012-vn]] supplies the cognitive-correction baseline (continued influence effect, backfire risks) that much applied intervention work, including [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]]'s Instagram prebunking field trial and [[Ramos2026-qo]]'s argument-checking proposal, builds upon or tries to move beyond.

## Comparative and Cross-National Correctives

A recurring self-critique within the corpus is its own WEIRD-centrism. [[Humprecht2020-gd]] operationalizes resilience as a macro-structural, cross-national variable, building on [[Hallin2004-kg]]'s foundational three-models typology to show the US is a uniquely vulnerable outlier rather than representative. [[Humprecht2025-ml]] frames an entire special issue around this corrective, and [[Nenno2025-xa]] empirically tests whether Western news-values typologies even travel, finding the WEIRD/non-WEIRD distinction itself variably salient depending on the value in question. [[Tornberg2025-ir]]'s 26-country parliamentary Twitter analysis exemplifies the payoff of this comparative turn: neither populism nor right-wing ideology alone predicts elite misinformation sharing, but their *interaction*—radical-right populism specifically—does, refining [[Benkler2018-lw]]'s asymmetric-polarization thesis into a more precise causal claim. [[Van_Erkel2026-mk]]'s three-country "hostile misinformation effect" study extends hostile-media-effect theory into this domain, finding in-group/out-group perceptual bias replicates robustly across Germany, the Netherlands, and Poland.

## Platform Governance, Infrastructure, and the Shrinking Research Commons

Running throughout is a parallel story about the infrastructural conditions of studying disinformation at all. [[Bruns2019-nr]] names the founding trauma—the post-Cambridge-Analytica "APIcalypse"—and [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] extends this into the DSA era, showing data access has evolved non-linearly from philanthropy to regulatory right, but remains fragile and unevenly enforced. [[Rogers2026-zz]] demonstrates the stakes empirically: Meta's successive redefinitions of the problem (fake news → false news → misinformation → borderline) have tracked its narrowing data-access regime, each terminological shift simultaneously depoliticizing and obscuring the persistently right-skewed character of high-engagement borderline content. [[Gillespie2022-jx]] identifies *reduction*—algorithmic demotion without removal—as the under-theorized complement to visible moderation, while [[Tonneau2025-bv]] documents how this invisible governance is itself linguistically and geographically unequal, with Global South languages starved of moderator attention. [[Holt2026-zq]] closes the loop empirically on Meta's 2025 pivot to Community Notes, showing user-flagged "false news" tracks political disagreement more than falsity—a finding that resonates with [[Hourigan2026-oc]]'s diary-study evidence that lay misinformation perception is driven by clickbait and distrust of mainstream/alternative news brands rather than fringe sources.

## Coordinated Actors, State Operations, and Elite-Driven Dynamics

A distinct empirical register addresses deliberate, organized manipulation. [[Keller2019-nk]] and [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] use rare ground-truth leaks (NIS, IRA) to show astroturfing operates as *coordinated labor*—detectable through behavioral synchrony rather than individual bot signatures, and organizationally embedded in national media labor markets rather than purely covert. [[Starbird2019-qv]] reframes disinformation itself as *collaborative work* performed with, not merely upon, online crowds, complicating any bots-versus-trolls binary. [[Kim2026-wg]] and [[Berlinski2023-rj]] turn to consequences: targeted voter-suppression advertising measurably depresses turnout among geo-racially targeted groups, and elite fraud claims erode election confidence in ways fact-checks fail to reverse. [[Bennett2025-xs]] synthesizes much of this register into an institutionalist corrective to technocentric bias, arguing digital "surrogate organizations" (QAnon, Stop the Steal) have reorganized party structures themselves—a claim given sharper causal purchase by [[Jaidka2026-sl34]]'s elite-blocking field experiment, which shows reducing exposure to political elites (not content moderation broadly) reduces misinformation susceptibility, reframing the problem as one of exposure architecture.

## Frontier Problems: Generative AI and the Dissolution of "Social Media"

The newest layer of the corpus confronts generative AI's destabilization of prior categories. [[Emilio2026-ik]] theorizes "synthetic realities" as a four-layer stack culminating in a paradox—ubiquitous fakery licenses blanket discounting of all digital evidence. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] proposes "emergent facts" as a needed fourth epistemic category beyond evidence-based, interpretative, and rule-based truth, while [[Slack1783075716-sl1c]] and [[Slack1783073271-slbb]] (Esposito) push back against AI-as-cognition framings altogether, recasting LLMs as communication partners whose outputs derive meaning from human interpretive labor. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] empirically tempers alarm about AI-generated visual disinformation, finding low-tech video decontextualization often outperforms AI stills in credibility—contingent on footage availability—while [[Choi2026-bz]] identifies modality-congruent carryover effects on self-confidence after deepfake exposure. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] delivers the most sweeping claim: the "social media" paradigm itself is dissolving into algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private spheres, and AI-mediated communication, demanding wholesale conceptual reorientation—a note that [[Baym2026-tr]] answers in a different register, arguing a decade of critique has failed to blunt worsening platform harms and calling for engaged, cross-sector collaboration rather than further diagnosis. [[Spampatti2026-kx]] offers a comparable call to arms, importing climate psychology's lessons on real-world behavioral outcomes, systems thinking, and collective scientific organization as a corrective to misinformation research's insularity.

## Residual Threads

Several papers extend the hybrid-system argument into specific rhetorical and infrastructural niches: [[Yoo2026-ev]] and [[Mosca2026-yh]] trace how "fake news"/accusations are weaponized as delegitimizing labels rather than descriptive terms, reshaping Hallin's spheres of legitimacy; [[Gaber2022-bk]] locates "strategic lying" as spin's digital-era intensification; [[Rogers2026-cy]] and [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] map infrastructural "post-truth spaces" and search-engine conspiratorial affordances; [[Graham2025-gp]] and [[Tuters2026-vo]] examine how propaganda and conspiracism exploit, rather than evade, the very infrastructures of institutional truth-telling; [[Scalco2026-bd]] formalizes "information voids" as measurable supply-demand anomalies; [[Tsfati2020-uo]] traces mainstream media's paradoxical role as fake news's chief amplifier; [[Trilling2024-slc8]] proposes feedback loops as a successor to the exhausted echo-chamber/filter-bubble metaphors; and [[Rothut2026-wt]], [[De_Leon2025-qn]], [[Ventura2025-sw]], [[Aharoni2026-sl6f]], [[Esau2025-tf]], [[Guo2026-fi]], and [[Lewandowsky2026-ob]] variously extend the hybrid-system argument into radicalization research, networked exposure, encrypted messaging, lay definitions of news, deliberative theory, the political/nonpolitical boundary, and platform accountability for democratic backsliding—together indicating a field whose centre of gravity is shifting from *verifying claims* toward *understanding systems*.
