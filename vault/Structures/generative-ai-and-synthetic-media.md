# Generative AI & Synthetic Media

## The Persuasion Machine: Mechanisms Over Magic

A first cluster of papers asks not *whether* generative AI persuades but *how*, and converges on a surprisingly consistent answer: information throughput, not psychological sophistication, drives AI persuasiveness. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] runs the largest systematic audit of this question — 77,000 conversations, 19 models, 707 issues — and isolates information density (the sheer volume of fact-checkable claims) as the dominant lever, ahead of model scale or personalization, while showing that the same levers that boost persuasion degrade factual accuracy. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] pushes this further by pitting AI against *expert* humans — elite debaters, coached canvassers — and finds AI wins even against professionals, with the advantage collapsing only when AI is artificially throttled to human typing speed and message length, again implicating fact density rather than rapport or perceived humanness. [[Lin2025-xp]] corroborates this across four real elections, showing AI dialogue moves voters more than traditional ads chiefly through relevant facts and evidence rather than manipulative tactics, though it surfaces an asymmetry: right-leaning AI advocacy is measurably less accurate than left-leaning advocacy. Together these three papers reframe "AI manipulation" away from speculative fears of hyper-personalized microtargeting (explicitly downweighted in [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] and [[Lin2025-xp]]) and toward a more mundane but consequential mechanism: AI can simply out-argue people by volume, at the cost of truth.

## Synthetic Visual Persuasion and Coordinated Promotion

A second strand shifts from text-based dialogue to the *visual* register, and from individual persuasion to networked, coordinated dissemination. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] is the anchor case for this topic: analyzing over 2,300 images from 223 coordinated Facebook gambling groups, it documents an exponential, structurally-broken surge in synthetic promotional imagery after ChatGPT's launch, arguing that generative AI does not invent new persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, celebrity endorsement, cultural localization) so much as intensify and recombine them into hyper-real, immersive aesthetics deployed at industrial scale through Meta's organic/paid regulatory grey zone. This foregrounds *amplification infrastructure* rather than content moderation as the crux of harm — a framing echoed structurally by [[Orlando2025-ul]], whose generative agent-based simulation shows that LLM agents autonomously reproduce real-world information-operation coordination (denser networks, homogenized narratives, synchronized amplification) once given mere *awareness* of teammates, without any explicit strategy. Read together, these two papers suggest that both human-orchestrated networks (Giglietto) and fully autonomous agent swarms (Orlando) converge on the same coordination signatures, lending empirical weight to the more speculative warnings in [[Schroeder2026-im]] about "malicious AI swarms" that fabricate consensus and poison training data at scale. [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] offers a partial counterpoint from a different platform and issue domain: on TikTok immigration discourse, generative AI appears only marginally, mostly as a tool for "speculative worldbuilding" in anti-immigration content, and ideologically opposed users deploy the platform's affordances in strikingly symmetrical ways — a reminder that synthetic-media harms are not evenly distributed across platforms, issues, or genres, and that gambling-style industrial exploitation (Giglietto) is not yet the norm everywhere.

## Iterative Deception and the Erosion of Verification

A third group of papers tracks how deception *strategies* evolve and how audiences cope, forming the topic's throughline on "iterative" manipulation. [[Emilio2026-ik]] provides the most totalizing framing, arguing that the real risk is not discrete deepfakes but coherent "synthetic realities" — a four-layer stack of content, identity, interaction, and institutions — that produce a paradox whereby ubiquitous synthetic media leads societies to rationally discount *all* digital evidence, raising the cost of truth itself. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] approaches the same epistemic rupture from fact-checking theory, arguing that traditional categories of fact (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) cannot capture GenAI outputs and proposing "emergent facts" as a new, relationally-assessed category — a conceptual complement to Ferrara's empirical case bank. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] grounds this erosion in a concrete harm domain, mapping the deepfake financial-fraud "scam ecosystem" and arguing, in a move that parallels Giglietto's amplification-infrastructure critique, that liability should shift from victims to platform and financial gatekeepers. [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s forensic comparison of Grokipedia against Wikipedia shows what institutional-layer erosion looks like in practice: an AI-generated encyclopedia that is derivative yet systematically skews toward unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial topics, illustrating Ferrara's "institutions" layer of synthetic reality empirically.

Audience-facing studies then ask how people actually cope with iterative exposure to this degraded information environment. [[Choi2026-bz]] finds a "modality-congruent carryover effect," where difficulty authenticating one deepfake shapes confidence and vulnerability toward *subsequent* fakes in the same format — direct evidence that deception operates iteratively at the level of individual cognition, not just content production. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] complicates assumptions about visual primacy, showing that AI-generated images and decontextualized real video only outperform text when an issue is highly salient and ample authentic footage exists for plausible recontextualization (Ukraine vs. MH370) — suggesting fears about generative AI's unique persuasive power may sometimes be overstated relative to older, low-tech decontextualization tactics, and that fact-checks work roughly equally across modalities. [[Wack2026-bt]]'s large Kenyan field experiment extends this social-negotiation logic: a deepfake's persuasive damage is not fixed at production but is renegotiated through surrounding social commentary, with credulous comments amplifying harm and skeptical ones — especially from cross-partisans — partially restoring it, framing deepfake impact as a matter of "collective sensemaking" rather than individual credibility judgment.

## Structural, Labor, and Governance Dimensions

Finally, two papers pull back from content and cognition to the institutional scaffolding around GenAI deception. [[Gillespie2026-aa]] reframes AI red-teaming — the practice meant to catch these harms before deployment — as a sociotechnical labor system inheriting content moderation's worst features: opaque internally-defined values, outsourced and precarious labor, and psychological harm to workers who must adversarially imagine the very deception strategies catalogued elsewhere in this topic. [[Beacken2026-zb]] insists on comparative specificity against techno-deterministic narratives, showing across six democratically weakened states that GenAI propaganda adoption is not a uniform trajectory but is shaped by local political, economic, and social structure — a necessary corrective to the topic's more universalizing claims (e.g., Ferrara's synthetic-reality stack, Schroeder's swarm threat) and a bridge back to Wack's Kenya-specific findings on low-resource vulnerability. Read as a whole, the papers filed here trace an arc from mechanism (why AI persuades) through manifestation (visual, networked, coordinated deception) to consequence (eroded verification, contested sensemaking) and finally to the human and institutional infrastructures — red-teamers, regulators, platform gatekeepers, political elites — that will determine whether these dynamics are contained or entrenched.
