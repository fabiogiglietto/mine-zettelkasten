# Generative AI & Synthetic Media in Disinformation

## Generative AI & Synthetic Media in Disinformation

### From artifact to environment: theorizing what generative AI changes

A first cluster of papers under this topic asks a foundational question: what, epistemically and structurally, is actually new about generative AI's role in deception? [[Dierickx2026-tw]] tackles this at the level of truth-claims themselves, arguing that classical fact-checking categories (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) cannot classify GenAI outputs, which are probabilistic recombinations rather than retrievals — proposing "emergent facts" as a new epistemic category requiring relational, not binary, assessment. [[Emilio2026-ik]] scales this concern up from the artifact to the environment, arguing that the real risk is not isolated deepfakes but "synthetic realities" — coherent, interactive, personalized information ecosystems spanning content, identity, interaction, and institutions — culminating in a paradox whereby ubiquitous synthetic media leads societies to rationally discount *all* digital evidence, raising the cost of truth itself. [[Galip2026-ix]] provides a countervailing cultural-theoretic register, reframing "AI slop" and "brainrot" through Sianne Ngai's theory of the gimmick: content that works simultaneously "too little" and "too hard," exposing contradictions of production under platform capitalism rather than simply representing degraded content. Read together, these three papers move from epistemology (what is a fact) to macro-diagnosis (what is a synthetic reality) to cultural critique (what does degraded abundance mean), setting the conceptual vocabulary the rest of the topic operationalizes empirically.

### Persuasion mechanics: how and why conversational AI moves attitudes

The largest and most tightly interlinked cluster empirically dissects AI persuasion. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] establishes the mechanism-level baseline: post-training and prompting strategies outperform scale and personalization, with *information density* — the sheer volume of fact-checkable claims — as the central driver, alongside a persuasion–accuracy trade-off. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] escalates the claim, showing frontier AI out-persuades even elite human debaters and professional canvassers, with coaching unable to close the gap and only artificially throttling AI to human throughput eliminating its edge — reinforcing information throughput as the operative mechanism. [[Lin2025-xp]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]] extend these findings into real electoral and contested-policy contexts: brief AI dialogues shift candidate preference and policy support across multiple democracies and issue domains, driven again by facts and evidence rather than manipulation, with effects concentrated among skeptics and out-partisans — directly complicating narratives of AI-enabled microtargeting or purely tribal reasoning. [[Costello2024-bg]] shows the same evidentially-grounded, personalized dialogue can durably erode conspiracy beliefs, reinforcing an analytic-reasoning account of belief change over motivational-resistance accounts. [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]] then turns this literature toward governance: AI-identity disclosure (as mandated by the EU AI Act) barely dents persuasion, but disclosing *persuasive intent* cuts it roughly in half — suggesting regulation should target concealed purpose, not just synthetic origin, echoing the political-advertising disclosure model. Together this cluster builds a coherent, cumulative argument: AI persuades mainly through volume and density of (often accurate) argumentation, this power generalizes across issues and contexts, existing human counter-measures and naive transparency mandates are largely inadequate, but intent-transparency and throughput-limiting interventions show promise. [[Szabo2026-rd]] and [[Choi2026-bz]] sit adjacent to this cluster from the defensive side, examining, respectively, how conversational inoculation chatbots build resistance to misinformation and how difficulty in identifying (deep)fakes creates modality-congruent carryover effects on subsequent susceptibility — both foregrounding the cognitive/interactional texture of resistance rather than pure information content.

### Coordinated and state-shaped uses: from swarms to state media laundering

Where the persuasion cluster studies dyadic human–AI exchange, a second group examines multi-actor and infrastructural manipulation. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates the mechanics directly: generative LLM agents in agent-based models spontaneously reproduce real-world information-operation coordination — network density, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification — with mere mutual awareness among agents nearly matching explicit collective deliberation, suggesting coordination is an emergent affordance of LLM agency rather than something requiring centralized command. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy-forum argument about "malicious AI swarms," mapping pathways (manufactured consensus, LLM grooming of training data, epistemic vertigo, institutional erosion) through which agentic AI could threaten democratic deliberation, and arguing symmetric "pro-social" swarms cannot counter them due to attention-economy asymmetries. [[Waight2026-ts]] provides an empirical, state-level instantiation of the "LLM grooming" concern this cluster gestures toward: showing that state-coordinated Chinese media is memorized in training corpora and measurably shifts commercial LLM outputs toward pro-regime valence when queried in low-media-freedom languages — evidence that institutional media control launders into ostensibly neutral AI outputs. [[Beacken2026-zb]] complements this with comparative political-communication framing, arguing across six democratically weakened states (Bolivia, Hungary, India, Nigeria, Tunisia, Turkey) that GenAI propaganda adoption is not a uniform inevitability but is shaped by local structural conditions — a useful corrective to techno-deterministic readings of the swarm and state-laundering papers. [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s empirical audit of Grokipedia offers a concrete, single-actor case of ideologically inflected AI content production at scale, showing how an AI-generated knowledge platform diverges from Wikipedia precisely on controversial topics and elected officials, citing markedly more unreliable sources — a bridge between the state-control and coordinated-operation literatures and the visual/commercial persuasion studies below.

### Visual and commercial synthetic media: gambling, fraud, and deepfake credibility

A distinct empirical strand turns to visual and multimodal synthetic content in commercial and everyday persuasion contexts. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents an "exponential" post-ChatGPT surge in AI-generated gambling promotion imagery across coordinated Facebook networks, showing generative AI intensifies and recombines pre-existing persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localization) rather than replacing them, while exploiting Meta's asymmetric governance of paid versus organic content — a direct empirical anchor for the topic's interest in AI-generated promotional imagery. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] extends the commercial-harms lens to deepfake financial fraud, mapping a cross-border "scam ecosystem" of platforms, telecoms, and financial intermediaries, and arguing (in a stance that resonates with Giglietto's emphasis on platform/amplification infrastructure) that regulation must target gatekeepers rather than individual victims. Against these accounts of AI's escalating persuasive/fraudulent power, [[Hameleers2026-mc]] offers an important empirical check: in controlled disinformation experiments, AI-generated still images do not universally outperform text or low-tech decontextualized video — visual disinformation's power is issue-dependent and contingent on the availability of plausible authentic footage, suggesting some GenAI alarm may be overstated relative to older decontextualization tactics. [[Wack2026-bt]]'s Kenyan deepfake experiment complicates the picture further by showing that a deepfake's impact is not fixed at production but socially negotiated through surrounding commentary and partisanship — audience "collective sensemaking" mediates whether synthetic media damages or fails to damage political support, particularly in low-resource information ecosystems.

### Disclosure, trust, and everyday production: journalism and creative labor

A final set of papers examines how synthetic media operates in more mundane, disclosed contexts — journalism and content creation — testing whether transparency and awareness of AI involvement change trust and engagement, a theme adjacent to the disclosure findings in [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]]. [[Gilardi2026-hw]] finds that undisclosed AI-generated and AI-assisted news is rated no differently from human-written news, and disclosure even boosts short-term willingness to continue reading, though this doesn't translate into durable acceptance. [[Mattis2026-gu]] refines this with task-specific disclosures, showing trust penalties vary by journalistic task (fact-checking incurs the largest penalty) and by reader profile, challenging simplistic binary AI/human disclosure models. [[Tang2026-gu]] shifts from disclosure to production practice, documenting how Chinese "AI slop" creators industrialize virality itself — benchmarking, testing, and formalizing content production via matrix account systems — reframing platform creative labor as a managed, engineered process rather than a spontaneous cultural output, a fitting empirical counterpart to Galip's theoretical treatment of slop as gimmick. Rounding out the topic's methodological self-reflection, [[Unknown2025-qj]] examines red-teaming as an emergent, sociotechnical safety practice for surfacing generative-AI harms in the public interest, underscoring that even the *evaluation* infrastructure for these systems is contested terrain — a fitting bookend to a topic that spans theorizing harms, engineering coordinated deception, empirically testing persuasion, and interrogating how transparency and governance might respond.
