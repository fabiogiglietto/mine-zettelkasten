# Polarization and Partisan Media Ecosystems

## The Arc: From Insularity to Ecosystems to Algorithmic Governance

What began as a question about whether partisans read different news has grown, across the papers filed here, into a much broader inquiry into how *entire ideological ecosystems* are assembled, sustained, and amplified — across platforms, national borders, and increasingly through the mediating layer of recommendation algorithms and LLMs. The earliest anchor in this collection, [[Bakshy2015-rn]], set a template still contested today: partisan sorting on Facebook is real but modest, and driven more by network composition and user choice than by algorithmic curation. A decade on, the papers here interrogate every one of those premises — pushing back on the "user choice" framing, complicating the "platform-as-conduit" framing, and expanding the geographic and platform range beyond the US Facebook–Twitter axis where the field cut its teeth.

## Rethinking Echo Chambers and Cross-Cutting Exposure

Several papers challenge the coherence of "echo chamber" as a construct. [[Hartmann2025-px]] argues systematically that much disagreement in the literature stems from heterogeneous conceptualizations and operationalizations rather than empirical reality. [[Green2025-ap]] shows that domain-level audience measures — the field's workhorse — mistake heterogeneity for moderation: stories from moderate outlets circulate through "partisan curation bubbles" that unbundle and re-bundle content, so aggregate source scores obscure the partisan valence of individual URLs. [[Dehghan2026-sy]] extends the critique in a different direction, showing on Reddit that even discursively resonant subreddits barely overlap, and that r/politics is not the cross-ideological town square it is often taken to be — polarization there is "sedimented" as a *precondition* through the entanglement of platform design, moderation, and discourse.

The normative flipside is equally troubled. [[van-Eck2026-xg]] finds that most depolarization recommendations in the literature are ungrounded in the studies' own evidence and rest instead on implicit deliberative-democratic ideals, while [[Esau2025-tf]] tries to rebuild that normative footing through connective democracy and inclusive listening. [[Unknown2026-sl3d]] represents the more optimistic strand — that proximate framings can soften ideological reflexes — though as [[van-Eck2026-xg]] warns, the evidence base for such interventions remains thin.

## Alternative Media, Influence Networks, and Mainstreaming

A large cluster of papers traces how alternative and hyper-partisan media weave themselves into — and out of — mainstream discourse. [[Zhu2026-tn]] provides comparative structure: alternative–mainstream audience overlap is generally low across six European countries, but the boundary blurs precisely where far-right populist parties enjoy institutionalized access, while Sweden shows a tightly knit *enclave* under conditions of strong professional journalism plus political marginalization. [[Askanius2026-de]] documents the Swedish enclave from within, mapping a dense far-right YouTube "alternative influence network" that hybridizes propaganda with influencer commerce during COVID-19. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]] locate similar dynamics in the Italian case, where legacy journalists remain central amplifiers of AIN content and where populist-aligned sources exhibit the highest insularity and most share-heavy engagement patterns.

Transnational reach is now central. [[Copland2025-em]] shows Sky News Australia operating a dual strategy — Facebook for domestic audiences, YouTube for the US right-wing propaganda pipeline. [[Rothut2026-or]] argues that the German Querdenken movement functioned as a *meso-level conduit* mainstreaming radical ideas through anti-elite framing and shifts in outlinking behavior, with far-right actors — not the protesters themselves — occupying the network's brokerage core. [[Iris2026-pg]] finds this structural rightward pull in mainstream coverage itself: the Radical Right received disproportionate visibility in the 2024 European elections across five countries, including in outlets with left-leaning editorial orientation. [[Knupfer2025-vt]] provides a mechanism through the "logic of connective faction": intra-party factions build connective ties to hyper-partisan surrogates, and are rewarded with engagement — an argument [[Bennett2025-xs]] generalizes into a call to fuse technocentric and institutionalist accounts of democratic backsliding through the concept of "digital surrogate organizations."

## Deplatforming, Moderation, and Their Discontents

The papers on platform governance converge on a sobering picture: moderation is neither neutral nor sufficient. [[Rieder2026-pp]] documents how Andrew Tate became *more* visible on YouTube after deplatforming, sustained by an ambient ecology of interviews, remixes, and Shorts that outpaces removal. [[Bailard2024-pj]] pushes past discrete-content moderation approaches by showing that Proud Boys' online *discursive ecosystems* — particularly diagnostic and motivational framing over multi-week cycles — predict offline violence. [[Marwick2025-vx]] and [[Marwick2026-qd]] together argue that treating disinformation as discrete false claims misses its function as processual socialization and as cross-platform *narrative* — findings that make platform-bound, fact-check-oriented interventions structurally inadequate. [[Nangle2026-yo]] adds the affective register, showing how positive affect and humor on Instagram normalize far-right adjacency. [[Ventura2026-yc]] demonstrates that even outright state bans produce a "sorting ratchet": Brazil's X ban shifted the platform durably rightward as conservatives circumvented while liberals complied.

## Algorithms as Political Actors

The most contested empirical terrain is algorithmic curation itself. [[Efstratiou2025-gs]] and [[Gauthier2026-iq]] both find that X's algorithm amplifies conservative and provocateur content, with [[Gauthier2026-iq]] identifying an asymmetric persistence mechanism — the algorithm alters *who users follow*, producing effects that survive switching it off. [[Brown2026-br]] complicates the picture from YouTube, distinguishing echo chambers (weak, driven by user choice), rabbit holes (strong, Markov-like), and radicalization pathways (unsupported), while still finding a platform-wide drift toward moderately conservative content. [[Giglietto2026-632ef967]] shows Facebook actively dampening partisan reach and rewarding journalistic quality — with these effects intensifying sharply around the 2020 election, evidence of *active* rather than structural gatekeeping. [[Brady2026-ln]] pushes into prosocial design, showing that engagement-based feeds amplify intergroup, moralized, emotional content and distort perceived norms, and that a "diversified extremity" algorithm can meaningfully reduce these effects. [[Bouchaud2026-np]] identifies a structural blind spot in bridging-based moderation: by requiring cross-partisan agreement, Community Notes systematically undermoderates the most polarizing content.

## New Frontiers: LLMs, Narrative, and Multi-Modal Framing

Method and object are coevolving. [[Lee2026-je]] shows that LLMs can infer political alignment even from ostensibly nonpolitical discourse — a finding with implications for micro-targeting and for the argument [[Marwick2026-qd]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] make about the *cultural* saturation of partisanship. [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes "narrative polarisation" via actantial analysis on YouTube, finding that comments harmonize surface narratives even while deeper motifs encode partisan differences. [[Arora2025-tx]], [[Sarmiento2025-as]], [[Hollingshead2026-vx]], and [[Yoo2026-ev]] extend framing analysis into multi-modal, unsupervised, and TikTok/alternative-media contexts. [[Rodarte2026-dk]], [[Balluff2026-bv]], [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]], and [[Lai2024-to]] round out the methodological horizon — from narrative contests among Brazilian parliamentarians to media capture in Austria, politicization of scientists in the UK, and latent ideology estimation for individual videos. Gaisbauer2025-as and [[Mosleh2024-op]] warn that outlet-level and single-platform designs miss the story-level, cross-platform reality of contemporary news circulation — a methodological argument that connects [[Green2025-ap]] back to [[Bakshy2015-rn]] and forward to the entire ecosystem-oriented turn this Zettel documents.

## Convergent Themes

Three claims emerge repeatedly across the arc. First, *insularity is asymmetric and structural* — populist-aligned ecosystems (M5S in [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]], MAGA in [[Knupfer2025-vt]] and [[Bennett2025-xs]], Querdenken in [[Rothut2026-or]], Swedish far-right YouTube in [[Askanius2026-de]]) are consistently denser, more amplification-oriented, and better integrated across platforms than their left counterparts. Second, *mainstreaming is a boundary-shifting process*, operating through legacy media citation ([[Iris2026-pg]], [[Marino2023-9137f448]]), transnational content flows ([[Copland2025-em]]), ambient affective ecologies ([[Rieder2026-pp]], [[Nangle2026-yo]]), and disproportionate visibility ([[Iris2026-pg]]). Third, *algorithmic and moderation interventions are inseparable from the political ecosystems they act on* — bans, bridging algorithms, deplatforming, and feed changes each produce asymmetric, often durable political consequences ([[Ventura2026-yc]], [[Bouchaud2026-np]], [[Gauthier2026-iq]], [[Giglietto2026-632ef967]]). The field has, in short, moved from asking whether echo chambers exist to asking how the entire assemblage of platforms, media, elites, algorithms, and audiences co-produces polarization as an ongoing achievement.
