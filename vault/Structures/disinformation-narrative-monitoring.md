# Disinformation Narrative Monitoring

## Reframing the Object: From False Claims to Narratives

The papers filed here converge on a foundational move: disinformation is increasingly theorized not as a set of discrete false claims but as *narrative*. [[Marwick2026-qd]] makes this argument most forcefully, showing through three US case studies that disinformation succeeds because it functions as identity-affirming, cathartic story — signalled by "catchphrases" (visual and discursive) that evoke entire plots without needing to restate them. This has direct methodological consequences for PROMPT-style monitoring: if disinformation travels cross-platform and cross-genre (memes, satire, aggregation, merchandise), then domain-blacklisting and claim-level fact-checking will systematically undercount its spread. [[Sadler2025-vu]] pushes the theoretical work further, arguing that the field has used "narrative" too loosely, and proposes a hermeneutic-realist framework (via Ricoeur and Phelan) for judging when a story is referentially or ethically problematic even absent verifiably false statements — a challenge to any monitoring system built purely on a true/false axis. [[Marwick2025-ov]] extends the narrative lens to TikTok's "ConspiracyTok," showing how populist knowledge production and a "generous epistemology" allow conspiratorial narratives to serve simultaneously as marginalized standpoint-claiming and as disinformation vectors — complicating any simple narrative-detection heuristic that assumes bad-faith actors are a fixed, identifiable demographic.

## Building the Monitoring Infrastructure

Several papers directly address the technical and operational challenge of *tracking* narratives at scale, which is the empirical core of PROMPT-adjacent tool-building. [[Waight2025-al]] is the most methodologically ambitious: it formalizes "narrative similarity" as an estimand distinct from lexical, topical, or semantic similarity, and builds an LLM-based pipeline (claim/subject distillation plus SBERT-guided pairwise comparison) that dramatically outperforms text-reuse and topic-modeling baselines — validated on the Ukrainian "biolabs" narrative's diffusion from Russian state media into low-quality and even mainstream US outlets. [[Rogers2026-cy]], from the sister vera.ai project, takes a network-mapping approach instead, defining "post-truth spaces" as clusters of mutually reinforcing but hard-to-verify actors and demonstrating on Moldovan Facebook data (ahead of the 2024 elections) how such clusters bridge into more centrally influential "borderline" content — producing not detection but *leads* for fact-checkers to monitor. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] complements these narrative- and network-level tools with a claim-level evaluation of LLMs as fact-checking engines, showing that curated retrieval context (not raw model scale or web search) is what makes automated verification usable, while also flagging an ideological skew in which sources search-enabled models cite — a caution for any dashboard that outsources triage to LLMs. Together these three papers sketch the technical stack a narrative-monitoring dashboard needs: similarity/diffusion measurement, network/cluster mapping, and LLM-assisted verification with curated evidence.

## From Detection to Response: Prebunking and Argument-Checking

Two papers push beyond monitoring into intervention design, directly relevant to the educational-resource strand of PROMPT. [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] provides field-experimental evidence that a 19-second inoculation ad on Instagram can durably improve users' ability to spot emotional manipulation, demonstrating that prebunking can be scaled cheaply within existing ad infrastructure rather than confined to lab studies. [[Ramos2026-qo]], written from inside the fact-checking organization Maldita.es, argues from the Valencia DANA-floods case that claim-by-claim fact-checking cannot keep pace with a narrative that mutates faster than corrections can be issued, proposing "narrative monitoring" (tracking clustered claims in real time) and "argument-checking" (targeting the underlying fallacies — false authority, post hoc, slippery slope) as necessary complements. Read together with [[Sadler2025-vu]]'s ethical-narratological framework, these pieces suggest that the PROMPT toolkit needs to operate at three levels simultaneously — claim, argument, and narrative — since narratives regenerate even after their constituent claims are debunked.

## Platform Ecologies and the War in Ukraine

A cluster of papers grounds these theoretical and infrastructural concerns in specific platform ecologies and the Ukraine conflict, one of PROMPT's core case domains. [[Bosch2024-hj]] identifies sound as an under-examined, platform-specific vector of TikTok disinformation about the invasion, while [[Vincent_undated-re]]'s repeated-measurement brief shows TikTok's mis/disinformation prevalence rising across successive monitoring rounds — empirical confirmation that the platform most resistant to text-centric tools is also the one where the problem is worsening. [[Makeev2026-ma]] supplies the geopolitical framing, theorizing how the Russian state runs dual-track propaganda — domestically legitimating and internationally demoralizing — that produces exactly the kind of divergent, audience-tailored narrative variants that [[Waight2025-al]]'s similarity-measurement pipeline is built to trace across borders. [[Copland2025-em]] extends the transnational-propaganda lens beyond Ukraine to Sky News Australia, showing an analogous dual-audience strategy (parochial domestic content vs. globally viral conspiracist content) that inserts a niche outlet into the US right-wing media pipeline — evidence that the network-propaganda dynamics visible in Ukraine coverage are a general feature of contemporary right-wing media infrastructure, not a war-specific anomaly.

## Beyond Ukraine: Gendered and Climate Narratives

The topic's coverage of LGBTQI+ narratives and adjacent culture-war content is anchored by [[Righetti2025-slf9]], which maps a decade of CitizenGO activity and finds a centrally coordinated, multilingual amplification network in which anti-gender narratives act as transnational "symbolic glue" — a finding structurally similar to the post-truth clusters in [[Rogers2026-cy]] and the dual-audience propaganda logics elsewhere in this set, suggesting shared network signatures across issue domains. [[Gardam2025-er]] widens the methodological toolkit further by insisting that visual-first analysis is necessary for image-driven platforms like Instagram, using climate denial and conspiracy discourse as its case — a methodological point that dovetails with [[Marwick2026-qd]]'s emphasis on visual catchphrases and with [[Marwick2025-ov]]'s attention to TikTok's visual evidentiary style, reinforcing that any PROMPT-style narrative dashboard restricted to text will miss much of the phenomenon.

## The Fact-Checkers Themselves: Institutions Under Strain

Finally, several papers turn the analytic lens onto fact-checking as an institution, which matters directly for tool adoption and trust. [[Farkas2026-lr]] documents how European fact-checkers rhetorically defend their financial dependence on platform companies, revealing an emergent and contested professional boundary-work that any collaborative dashboard or verification partnership must navigate. [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]] examines the aftermath of Meta's 2025 retreat from third-party fact-checking, showing that removal was always rare and that fact-checkers themselves favor labeling and counter-speech over deletion while distrusting Community Notes as a substitute — a cautionary data point for policy debates PROMPT-adjacent tools are meant to inform. [[Xue2025-bp]] adds a complicating wrinkle by showing that fact-checking content itself is often emotionally charged, undermining the normative self-image of dispassionate correction that underlies much of the debunking literature and echoing, from the opposite institutional position, [[Marwick2026-qd]]'s and [[Ramos2026-qo]]'s arguments that emotion and narrative — not neutral information delivery — are what actually drive engagement and belief change.
