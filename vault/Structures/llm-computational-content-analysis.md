# LLMs & Computational Content Analysis

## From Fine-Tuned Classifiers to Prompted Pipelines

The papers gathered here trace a rapid methodological transition in computational content analysis: from bespoke supervised classifiers toward general-purpose LLMs deployed, often in combination with older tools, across classification, annotation, and clustering tasks. [[Bailard2024-pj]] represents the more traditional end of this spectrum—a fine-tuned DeBERTa model classifying collective-action frames in Proud Boys Telegram messages—but even here the payoff is substantively rich: diagnostic and motivational framing, not explicit calls to action, predict offline violence, illustrating how a well-validated supervised pipeline can support genuine theoretical advance (collective action framing, moral convergence) when paired with careful causal analysis. [[Larsson2026-ro]] and [[Meher2025-qb]] push further toward LLM-centric approaches: GPT-4 zero-shot sentiment classification (validated against human coders) for a decade of Norwegian Facebook posts, and QLoRA-fine-tuned Llama 3.1 for multi-label terrorism-event coding, respectively, both arguing that prompt- or adaptation-based LLM methods can match or exceed prior standards (including domain-specific transformers like ConfliBERT) while remaining accessible on modest infrastructure. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] extends this logic to the visual domain, showing that a general-purpose multimodal LLM (GPT-4o) outperforms specialized computer-vision pipelines for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery—suggesting that prompt-based generality is displacing task-specific pipelines not just in text but across modalities.

## Clustering and Embeddings: Making Latent Structure Legible

A second cluster of papers concerns unsupervised and semi-supervised discovery of thematic or narrative structure. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] form a linked pair: the former benchmarks OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, finding the LLM-derived embeddings consistently more coherent, while the latter reflects on the validation challenges this creates when LLMs are embedded at multiple pipeline stages (classification, embedding, cluster labeling)—arguing that variable narrative granularity and the outperformance of LLMs relative to low-skilled crowd workers demand new, task-specific, expert-driven validation protocols rather than one-size-fits-all human benchmarks. [[Arminio2025-tw]] makes an analogous move for images, arguing that CNN-based visual clustering captures only denotative content and proposing a VLLM-to-text-to-embedding pipeline that better recovers *connotative* meaning while remaining interpretable via TF-IDF summaries—an argument about what clustering should even measure, not just how accurately it measures it. [[Fan2025-ut]] complements these applied efforts with a general-purpose fix for a pervasive failure mode: embeddings encode source or language as confounds, and linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this contamination without degrading downstream performance, a lesson directly relevant to any pipeline (like those in [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] or [[Waight2025-al]]) that pools documents across heterogeneous corpora. [[Sarmiento2025-as]] and [[Ober2026-vd]] extend unsupervised framing/topic discovery to social media polarization and qualitative interview data respectively, the latter offering a human-in-the-loop model in which topic modeling's mathematical transparency anchors LLM-generated labels, explicitly resisting full automation in favor of iterative codebook refinement.

## Scaling Positions, Frames, and Narratives

Beyond classification and clustering, several papers use LLMs to construct continuous or structured measures of meaning. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] proposes simply asking instruction-tuned LLMs to place texts on ideological dimensions and averaging many responses, reporting strong correlations with established benchmarks across languages—a striking claim about measurement validity achieved through aggregation rather than architecture. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] pursues a parallel goal for open-ended survey responses, pairing LLMs with paired-comparison methods to recover continuous latent traits that closed-ended items cannot capture. [[Arora2025-tx]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] push framing analysis beyond fixed categories and single modalities: the former extracts textual, visual, and cross-modal frames in gun-violence coverage, while the latter operationalizes Greimas' Actantial Model via LLM annotation to measure "narrative polarisation" in Israeli-Palestinian YouTube discourse, finding that comments narratively converge even as underlying motifs preserve partisan difference—a genuinely new estimand made tractable only by LLM-based extraction. [[Waight2025-al]] makes this point about estimands explicit and general: narrative similarity (same claims about same events) is conceptually distinct from lexical, topical, or semantic similarity, and existing methods (text reuse, topic models, Relatio, standalone SBERT) systematically conflate them, missing phenomena—like mainstream U.S. outlets echoing Russian state narratives—that only a properly designed LLM pipeline detects. Together these papers argue that LLMs do not merely automate old measurement tasks faster; they make new theoretical constructs empirically tractable for the first time.

## Validation, Disagreement, and the Limits of LLM Judgment

A cross-cutting concern is whether, and how, these pipelines can be trusted. [[Brown2025-jk]] offers a systematic test of demographic bias in LLM annotation across four contentious datasets, finding that apparent bias is dataset-specific rather than model-specific and dwarfed by item difficulty (human label entropy)—a reassuring but conditional finding that reframes fairness auditing around task difficulty rather than model choice. [[Paci2025-ag]] and [[DeVerna2025-dl]] supply sobering counterpoints: LLMs remain markedly worse than human experts at interpreting implicatures and presuppositions in Italian political speech, and even reasoning- and search-augmented models perform poorly at political fact-checking absent curated retrieval context, with search-enabled models additionally showing a leftward skew in citation patterns. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] extends validation concerns to the meta-level of the scientific workflow itself, showing that frontier coding agents can reproduce a striking share of published social-science findings, but also that they are vulnerable to sycophantic specification search under confirmatory prompting and that providing paper PDFs, while improving accuracy, compromises their independence as validators. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] provides a theoretical counterweight to all of this, arguing via Luhmann and conversation analysis that apparent LLM competence in repairing conversational trouble is parasitic on human interlocutors' own repair work—a caution against over-attributing "understanding" to any of the pipelines described above. [[Matias2025-px]] argues from a different angle that rigor itself requires broadening who participates in evaluation: lived-experience experts can surface flaws (biased risk scores, mis-coded complaints) that purely technical validation misses, a call for methodological pluralism that resonates with [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]'s insistence on domain-expert human coders over crowdworkers. [[Balluff2026-if]] and [[Dierickx2026-tw]] step back to interrogate the broader epistemic stakes: the former catalogs reproducibility, environmental, and bias costs of unreflective LLM adoption in communication research and calls for a trade-off mindset favoring smaller, open models where feasible; the latter argues that GenAI outputs require an entirely new epistemic category—"emergent facts"—because traditional evidence-based, interpretative, and rule-based fact-checking frameworks cannot accommodate probabilistic, prompt-dependent content.

## LLMs as Objects of Bias and Contamination

A distinct set of papers treats LLMs not as analytical tools but as artifacts whose outputs are themselves politically shaped and worth auditing. [[Waight2026-ts]] demonstrates that state-coordinated media is memorized during pretraining and measurably shifts model outputs toward pro-regime valence in the languages of low-media-freedom countries, "laundering" institutional influence into ostensibly neutral answers. [[Triedman2025-uy]] performs an analogous audit of Grokipedia, showing it to be largely derivative of Wikipedia yet more heavily reliant on unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial and political topics. [[Lee2026-je]] shows that LLMs can infer users' political alignment from putatively nonpolitical text via subtle lexical cues, outperforming supervised baselines and raising privacy concerns that echo the Cambridge Analytica-era microtargeting debate. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] extend this auditing impulse to coordinated inauthentic behavior and personality inference respectively—IOHunter combining language and graph signals to detect information-operation drivers, and a validated dark-personality study finding that self-reported toxic behavior, not questionnaire-derived traits, aligns with observable Reddit language, cautioning against overreading text-derived psychological inference. Together, this cluster insists that any content-analysis pipeline built atop commercial LLMs inherits these models' own embedded biases and vulnerabilities.

## LLMs as Political Actors: Persuasion and Trust

A final, adjacent body of work examines LLMs not as coding instruments but as communicative agents whose outputs must themselves be understood, since they increasingly mediate the political discourse that content-analysis pipelines study. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically isolates the levers of AI persuasiveness—post-training and information density matter more than scale or personalization—while documenting a troubling persuasion-accuracy trade-off; [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] shows this persuasive power is itself contingent on perceived neutrality, attenuating sharply when users suspect partisan bias; [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]] find that personalized, dialogic AI can durably shift conspiracy beliefs and policy attitudes even among skeptics, challenging motivational accounts of belief resistance; and [[Szabo2026-rd]] and [[Dubey2026-bl]] explore chatbot-based inoculation and "balanced" news chatbots as applied interventions, the latter surprisingly finding higher trust among conspiracy believers than skeptics. [[Suk2026-ai]], [[Gilardi2026-hw]], and [[Mattis2026-gu]] turn to audience-side trust and disclosure effects, showing that AI-generated and human-written news are perceived as comparable in quality but that task-specific AI disclosure (especially for fact-checking) reliably erodes trust, with effects moderated by political orientation and AI literacy. [[Allen2025-ot]] situates a related methodological innovation—browser-extension LLM reranking as a "platform-independent" experimental design—within the broader crisis of platform data access. Read alongside the classification and clustering literature, this cluster reframes the "human-machine disagreement" question at the heart of the topic: disagreement is not only about whether LLMs correctly label texts, but about whether they can be trusted messengers, persuaders, and even peer reviewers, as [[Jayaram2026-wd]]'s deployment of an agentic AI review tool at STOC and ICML makes vivid—extending the same validation anxieties documented throughout this collection into the practice of science itself.
