# Generative AI & LLMs in Content Analysis

## From pipelines to protocols: validating LLM-in-the-loop content analysis

The methodological core of this collection is a line of applied work that treats LLM integration into social-media content analysis not as a black-box convenience but as a design problem requiring its own validation logic. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is the anchor text: it documents a three-stage pipeline for Italian political Facebook content (fine-tuned classification, embedding-based clustering, LLM-generated cluster labels) and argues that each stage needs task-specific validation, that crowdsourced raters are inadequate once LLMs outperform them, and that "narrative" itself is a moving target whose granularity varies unpredictably across clusters. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] follows up on one link in that chain, empirically comparing OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, and finding the general-purpose commercial embedding consistently produces more coherent, interpretable clusters — extending English-language findings about LLM embeddings to a non-English, domain-specific setting. [[Larsson2026-ro]] applies the same hybrid logic (GPT-4 zero-shot classification with human validation) to a substantive question — negative campaigning and Facebook engagement in Norway — showing that the validated pipeline approach travels across languages and party systems. [[Marino2026-slef]] extends the toolkit further, pairing LLM-based actor classification with time-series and regression methods to track affective engagement in a Brazilian hyperpartisan network, demonstrating how LLM classification can be embedded within longitudinal, event-driven designs rather than static snapshots. Together these four papers trace an arc from methodological reflection to increasingly ambitious application, each carrying forward the validation ethic articulated in [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]].

## Embeddings, clustering, and the semantics of political content

A second cluster interrogates what embedding-based representations actually capture and how to make them more trustworthy or interpretable. [[Fan2025-ut]] shows that pretrained sentence embeddings encode confounders like source or language, and that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can be a cheap, principled preprocessing step — directly relevant to the multi-source, multilingual corpora typical of the pipelines above. [[Arminio2025-tw]] pushes embedding-based clustering into the visual domain, arguing that CNN pipelines capture only denotative image content and proposing a VLLM-to-text-to-embedding route that recovers connotative, culturally-laden meaning (with TF-IDF summaries restoring interpretability that neural clustering otherwise lacks) — a concern that echoes [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]'s worry about opaque, variable-granularity clusters. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes "practice mapping," using vector embeddings of network actions rather than content to escape the "hairball" problem of conventional network visualization, suggesting embeddings can represent behavioral as well as semantic structure. Read together, these papers treat the embedding space itself as an object of methodological scrutiny — something to be audited, debiased, and validated before being trusted as a proxy for meaning.

## Measuring positions, narratives, and hidden traits

A parallel set of papers uses LLMs not to cluster but to *scale* or *infer* — positioning texts and actors along latent dimensions. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking instruction-tuned LLMs to place a text on a policy dimension and averaging many such judgments reproduces established scaling benchmarks across languages, offering a lightweight alternative to Wordscores/Wordfish-style methods. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] pursues a related goal for survey research, pairing LLMs with paired-comparison methods to scale open-ended responses into continuous latent measures. [[Lee2026-je]] complicates the picture by showing LLMs can infer political alignment even from ostensibly nonpolitical text, exploiting subtle lexical and cultural cues — a capability that is analytically powerful but also, as the paper stresses, a privacy risk resonant with [[Balluff2026-if]]'s concerns about unreflective adoption. [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes a structuralist narrative theory (Greimas' Actantial Model) via LLM annotation to measure "narrative polarisation," finding that videos are sharply polarized while comments unexpectedly converge — a finding only visible because the LLM pipeline could scale a labor-intensive coding scheme. [[Sarmiento2025-as]] and [[Arora2025-tx]] extend this scaling ambition to framing analysis, arguing that pre-defined frame categories and text-only analysis miss emergent, multimodal framing patterns, though both remain more programmatic than the fully worked pipelines elsewhere in the collection.

## LLMs as persuaders and interlocutors

A distinct but related strand treats conversational LLMs not as analytic instruments but as objects of study — testing what they do to belief and behavior when deployed as interlocutors. [[Costello2024-bg]] establishes the paradigm: personalized, evidence-dense AI dialogue durably reduces conspiracy belief, challenging motivational accounts of belief resistance. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematizes the mechanisms behind such effects at scale, showing that post-training and prompting strategy (especially information density) matter more than model scale or personalization — and crucially, that persuasiveness and factual accuracy trade off against each other. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends this into live electoral contexts across four countries, finding conversational persuasion exceeds traditional advertising effects while noting an asymmetry in factual accuracy that disadvantages right-leaning claims. [[Kotz2026-lk]] generalizes the finding across issue domains (climate, vaccines, inequality), showing effects concentrate among initial skeptics and are moderated by trust in science. [[Szabo2026-rd]] narrows the lens to inoculation theory, showing a chatbot-delivered "conversational inoculation" can outperform static reading/writing interventions when adaptivity and trust-building are present. Collectively, these papers make the case — echoed methodologically in [[DeVerna2025-dl]]'s fact-checking results — that LLM output quality is a function of *design choices* (prompting, post-training, curated context) as much as raw capability, a theme that bridges persuasion research and the validation concerns of the content-analysis pipelines above.

## The limits of LLM competence: bias, pragmatics, and factuality

Running underneath both the classification pipelines and the persuasion studies is a skeptical countercurrent about what LLMs can reliably do and what they inherit from their training data. [[Balluff2026-if]] offers the most direct methodological critique, arguing that communication researchers have adopted LLMs unreflectively, ignoring reproducibility, environmental, and bias costs, and urging a trade-off mindset that prefers smaller, open, task-appropriate models — a caution that speaks directly to the design choices made in [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]]. [[Waight2026-ts]] documents a specific, geopolitically loaded bias: state media control shapes LLM training data and output valence, an institutional-influence pathway distinct from direct model regulation. [[Paci2025-ag]] shows LLMs struggle with the pragmatics of Italian political discourse — implicatures and presuppositions — falling well short of expert human interpretation even with chain-of-thought prompting, a finding with obvious implications for any pipeline that asks LLMs to interpret political meaning rather than surface content. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] shows political fact-checking fails without curated retrieval context, regardless of reasoning or web search capability, while [[Dierickx2026-tw]] argues theoretically that GenAI outputs require an entirely new epistemic category ("emergent facts") because they are neither evidence-based, interpretative, nor rule-based in the traditional sense. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] extends this skepticism to AI-augmented search, showing Google's AI Overviews inconsistently and sometimes counterproductively handle conspiratorial queries. [[Brown2025-jk]], somewhat more reassuringly, finds LLM annotation bias is dataset-specific and modest relative to item difficulty (label entropy) — a nuance that complicates blanket claims of demographic bias and offers a methodological corrective to overly broad critique.

## Beyond text: multimodal and structural extensions

Several papers push LLM-based content analysis beyond text classification into adjacent modalities and structures. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] validates GPT-4o against specialized computer-vision pipelines for political image analysis, finding the multimodal LLM outperforms bespoke face-recognition and person-counting models — a visual analogue to the text-embedding comparisons in [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]]. [[Meher2025-qb]] demonstrates that efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) of open-source LLMs can achieve strong performance on conflict-event classification on consumer hardware, offering a resource-conscious alternative that speaks to [[Balluff2026-if]]'s trade-off concerns. [[Minici2024-tf]] combines language models with graph neural networks to detect coordinated information operations, illustrating how LLM-derived textual signal can be fused with network structure. [[Fan2026-af]] surveys computational approaches (including language-based embedding models) for analyzing temporal, cross-platform digital trace data, arguing embeddings are promising for hyper-longitudinal analysis but currently data-starved. [[Ober2026-vd]] applies a human-in-the-loop LLM workflow to qualitative interview coding, showing the same "human-oversight-plus-LLM-labeling" logic from social media pipelines transfers to small-N qualitative research. [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] tests the limits of surface-level linguistic signal for personality inference, finding validated dark-trait questionnaires correlate weakly with computationally extracted toxicity features — a cautionary data point for any pipeline inferring latent traits from text.

## The wider frame: media theory, reproducibility, and teaching

Finally, a set of papers situates this methodological program within larger disciplinary and pedagogical questions relevant to the *IA Generativa e Media* course. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues that the "social media" paradigm itself is dissolving under algorithmic recommendation, generative synthetic content, and platform retreat — suggesting that the object of content analysis is shifting beneath the field's feet, with direct implications for what pipelines like [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] should even be measuring. [[Manovich2026-ih]] offers a complementary media-theoretic reading of generative AI as a creative medium with structural properties (probabilistic generation, entangled style/content, "media cognition") rather than primarily a social or ethical problem. [[Gilardi2026-hw]] speaks to the reception side of AI-generated content, finding audiences rate AI-authored and human-authored news as equivalent in quality, with disclosure producing only short-term curiosity effects. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extend the reflexive, meta-scientific thread of this topic into research infrastructure itself — the former showing AI coding agents can reproduce a striking share of published social science findings (while flagging sycophancy risks under confirmatory prompting), the latter describing an agentic AI system for automating manuscript review. [[Tan2024-vl]] rounds out the collection with a survey-level orientation to LLM-based annotation and synthesis, framing the entire topic's methodological questions — how to generate, assess, and use LLM-produced labels — as a coherent emerging subfield rather than a scattered set of ad hoc practices.
