# LLMs in Computational Content Analysis

## From ad-hoc classifiers to a validation agenda

The papers gathered here trace the maturation of LLMs from a curiosity applied opportunistically to political text into an object of sustained methodological scrutiny. Early moves are straightforwardly instrumental: a fine-tuned DeBERTa model codes collective-action frames in Proud Boys Telegram messages [[Bailard2024-pj]], a BERT classifier flags conspiracy-related browsing content in web-tracking data [[Adam2026-tz]], and GPT-4 is used zero-shot, with human validation, to code a decade of Norwegian Facebook posts for sentiment [[Larsson2026-ro]]. What distinguishes this literature from ordinary applied NLP is that these applications are increasingly staged as validation exercises in their own right—demonstrations of what a given architecture, prompting strategy, or fine-tuning regime can and cannot be trusted to do. [[Meher2025-qb]] makes this explicit, showing that QLoRA-adapted Llama 3.1 dramatically outperforms zero-shot prompting on conflict-event classification, especially for rare categories, while remaining tractable on consumer hardware—reframing LLM adoption as a question of efficient domain adaptation rather than brute scale. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] performs an analogous service for the visual domain, showing GPT-4o outperforming specialized computer-vision pipelines (RetinaFace, FaceNet512, Google Cloud Vision) for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery, while noting that specialized models replicate known gender biases that the multimodal LLM appears to avoid.

## Building and validating pipelines, not just models

A second cluster treats validation not as a single accuracy score but as a pipeline problem, since real studies chain classification, embedding, clustering, and labeling steps together. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is the clearest statement of this shift, articulating a three-phase, LLMs-in-the-loop validation protocol for Italian political Facebook data—fine-tuned classification, embedding-based clustering, and LLM-generated narrative labels—and confronting the awkward fact that crowdsourced annotators are no longer an adequate ground truth once LLMs surpass them at nuanced political judgment. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] supplies the underlying embedding benchmark this pipeline depends on, showing that OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large yields more semantically coherent clusters of Italian news than the language-specific UmBERTo model, while also warning that HDBSCAN's apparent quality gains can be an artifact of discarding data as noise. [[Fan2025-ut]] addresses a deeper confound in any embedding-based clustering or retrieval exercise: pretrained embeddings encode source, language, and style as spurious signal, and linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this confounding cheaply and near-universally, with gains concentrated exactly where the confound dominates the leading principal component. [[Ober2026-vd]] extends validation logic to qualitative coding, combining topic modeling's mathematical transparency with LLM-generated labels and iterative human codebook refinement—an explicit hedge against the opacity that worries other authors in this set.

## Measurement beyond classification: scaling, narrative, and framing

Several papers push LLM-based measurement past discrete labels toward continuous or structural representations of political meaning. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking an instruction-tuned LLM to place a text on a policy dimension, and averaging many such judgments, recovers established scaling benchmarks across languages and text types—an appealingly simple alternative to Wordscores/Wordfish-style supervised scaling. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] pursues a parallel logic for open-ended survey responses, pairing LLMs with paired-comparison methods to build continuous latent trait scales without the labor of manual coding. [[Lee2026-je]] complicates the picture from the inference side, showing LLMs can recover users' political alignment even from nonpolitical text via subtle lexical and cultural cues—an analytical capability that doubles as a privacy risk. Narrative-level measurement is its own emerging subfield: [[Waight2025-al]] formalizes "narrative similarity" as distinct from lexical, topical, or semantic similarity, using LLM-distilled claims to trace the diffusion of Russian disinformation narratives with far higher recall than text-reuse or topic-modeling baselines; [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes Greimas' actantial model via an LLM to measure "narrative polarisation" in Israeli-Palestinian YouTube discourse, finding that comments blunt surface-level divergence even as deeper narrative motifs preserve partisan asymmetries. Framing analysis is likewise being pried open from fixed category schemes: [[Sarmiento2025-as]] proposes unsupervised frame detection combining ML, network analysis, and NLP, while [[Arora2025-tx]] insists framing is inherently multimodal and extracts cross-modal frames from gun-violence coverage. [[Lai2024-to]] shows that ideology-scaling logic extends even to video content, using cross-subreddit sharing patterns—an instructive non-LLM counterpoint showing that not all scaling problems require generative models.

## Stress-testing the instrument: bias, pragmatics, and epistemic limits

A substantial counter-current interrogates what these tools get wrong. [[Brown2025-jk]] finds that LLM annotation bias on contentious labeling tasks is real but small, dataset-specific rather than model-specific, and dwarfed by item difficulty (human label entropy)—a finding that reframes fairness debates around task design rather than model choice. [[Paci2025-ag]] documents a sharper limitation: even the best multilingual LLMs fall well short of expert human interpretation of implicatures and presuppositions in Italian political speech, with Chain-of-Thought prompting helping only modestly. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] shows that political fact-checking is similarly brittle—reasoning and web search alone yield weak performance, and only curated retrieval-augmented generation closes the gap, while search-enabled models exhibit a citation skew toward left-leaning outlets. [[Waight2026-ts]] extends the bias question institutionally, showing that state media control is baked into training corpora and measurably shifts model outputs toward pro-regime valence in lower-media-freedom languages—an argument that LLMs can "launder" propaganda into ostensibly neutral answers. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] offers the most theoretically radical challenge, arguing via Luhmann and conversation analysis that chatbot competence is parasitic on human repair work rather than genuine understanding—a caution relevant to any content-analysis pipeline that treats LLM outputs as if they reflected comprehension. [[Balluff2026-if]] synthesizes these worries into an explicit methodological critique of the field's "unreflective" LLM adoption, cataloguing reproducibility, environmental, and bias costs, and arguing for a trade-off mindset favoring smaller, open, task-appropriate models—effectively the conscience of this whole topic cluster.

## The infrastructure content analysis depends on

A less glamorous but structurally important set of papers examines the data and measurement infrastructure that any LLM-based pipeline inherits. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] shows that web-scraping method, not time delay, is the dominant source of distortion in news-exposure measurement, with errors concentrated non-randomly across content categories. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] uncovers a platform-wide artifact in Meta's URL Shares Dataset—the 100-share anonymization threshold—that can masquerade as substantive cross-country or temporal patterns. [[Luhring2025-od]] audits the NewsGuard trustworthiness database itself, showing that binary thresholding (rather than continuous scores) can flip substantive conclusions when a handful of major outlets cross the cutoff. [[Nenno2025-xa]] and [[Schemer2026-mh]] make comparable points about operationalization: news-value coding of misinformation varies by WEIRD/non-WEIRD context, and specification-curve analysis across 504 measurement choices shows that partisan-media-slant metrics substantially—though not directionally—alter polarization findings. [[Hartmann2025-px]] generalizes this diagnosis across an entire subfield, arguing that echo-chamber research disagreement reflects conceptual and operational heterogeneity more than empirical reality. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] and [[Bruns2025-fz]] extend the infrastructural lens to recommender systems and network visualization respectively, showing that embedding spaces can inadvertently encode political ideology and that vector-based "practice mapping" can supersede hairball network graphs. [[Fan2026-af]] rounds out this cluster by arguing that digital trace data's temporal fine-grain is systematically discarded by cross-sectional methods, with language-based embedding models flagged as a promising but data-hungry corrective. [[Allen2025-ot]] situates platform-independent, LLM-mediated experimental designs (e.g., browser-extension feed reranking) as a necessary response to closing API access—an infrastructural argument about how content-analysis research itself must be re-engineered.

## LLMs as both instrument and object: persuasion, moderation, and agentic futures

A final arc treats conversational LLMs not merely as annotation tools but as interventions to be measured with the same rigor. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically decomposes what makes conversational AI persuasive—post-training and information density matter more than scale or personalization—while exposing a persuasion-accuracy trade-off; [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] shows this persuasive power is itself contingent on perceived neutrality, since out-party bias warnings suppress belief correction. [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]] extend the paradigm to durable, generalizable belief change across conspiracy theories and contested societal issues, while [[Szabo2026-rd]] adapts inoculation theory into a conversational chatbot format and [[Ramos2026-qo]] argues that narrative- and argument-level monitoring must supplement claim-level fact-checking during crises. Detection and classification pipelines applied to adversarial online behavior—coordinated troll rhetoric in Korean comments [[Kim2026-br]], dark personality traits and Reddit toxicity [[Cerulli2026-sl75]], and affective volatility in a hyperpartisan Bolsonaro network via LLM-based actor classification [[Marino2026-slef]]—show these methods migrating into applied moderation and polarization research. At the frontier, [[Alizadeh2026-es]] turns the evaluative lens on AI itself, benchmarking coding agents' ability to reproduce social-science findings and documenting sycophantic specification search under confirmatory prompting; [[Jayaram2026-wd]] does the same for automated peer review; and [[Ng2026-og]] argues that agentic AI operating in social environments needs social theory as a structural prior rather than single-task assumptions. Read together with the field-level survey of LLM annotation and synthesis methods [[Tan2024-vl]], this arc suggests the topic's center of gravity is shifting from "can LLMs code text as well as humans" toward "how do we validate, govern, and theorize systems that are simultaneously the instrument, the subject, and increasingly the collaborator in computational content analysis."
