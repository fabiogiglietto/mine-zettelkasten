# Generative AI for Content Analysis and Research Methods

## The Methodological Turn: LLMs as Instruments of Communication Research

This cluster of papers documents a field in the middle of rebuilding its own toolkit. Rather than treating generative AI solely as an object of study, a substantial share of this literature treats LLMs as *instruments*—for classification, clustering, scaling, annotation, and even replication—and spends considerable energy asking whether these instruments are trustworthy. The anchor texts here are the paired methodological case studies from the Italian political news project: [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] compares `text-embedding-3-large` against the Italian-specific UmBERTo model for clustering political news shared on Facebook, finding the proprietary embedding model consistently more semantically coherent, while [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] reflects on the broader challenge of validating a full "LLMs-in-the-loop" pipeline—classification, embedding, and cluster-labeling all performed by the same family of models—arguing that each stage needs its own validation protocol and that expert (not crowdsourced) human judgment remains indispensable. This pairing exemplifies the course's methodological core: embedding models are not interchangeable, and pipeline validity cannot be taken on faith.

Several papers extend this scrutiny to other measurement tasks. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking instruction-tuned LLMs to place texts on policy dimensions and averaging responses yields scores that track established scaling benchmarks. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] proposes LLM-paired comparisons for scaling open-ended survey text into latent measures. [[Meher2025-qb]] and [[Carammia2024-sl90]] both demonstrate that small, fine-tuned open-source models (QLoRA-tuned Llama variants) can match or beat large proprietary models on domain classification tasks—terrorism event coding and CAP-style policy categorization, respectively—while being cheaper, more transparent, and more reproducible, directly engaging the corporate-dependency critique later articulated by [[Balluff2026-if]]. [[Fan2025-ut]] addresses a subtler measurement problem: pretrained embeddings encode spurious source/language confounds that bias downstream similarity analyses, and linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this contamination cheaply, a technique directly relevant to any Giglietto-style clustering pipeline. [[Waight2025-al]] tackles another estimand problem—distinguishing "narrative similarity" from lexical or topical similarity—using an LLM distillation-and-comparison pipeline to track the cross-lingual diffusion of the Ukraine "biolabs" story, explicitly building on Grimmer and King's call for estimand-estimator alignment.

Validation extends into multimodal and qualitative territory. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] finds GPT-4o outperforming specialized computer-vision pipelines for face recognition and person-counting in Instagram political content; [[Arminio2025-tw]] shows vision-LLMs generate connotatively richer, more interpretable image clusters than CNN pipelines by routing through text descriptions; [[Arora2025-tx]] and [[Gardam2025-er]] both push framing and discourse analysis toward genuinely multimodal operationalization. On the qualitative side, [[Ober2026-vd]] and [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] both develop human-in-the-loop workflows—topic modeling plus LLM labeling, and LLM-as-analytic-collaborator for codebook refinement—that position the model not as a classifier to be benchmarked but as a dialogic partner that surfaces ambiguities a theory-only codebook would miss. [[Brown2025-jk]] complicates the bias-detection agenda often assumed in this literature, finding that LLM-human annotation disagreement is overwhelmingly explained by item difficulty (entropy) rather than systematic demographic skew—a useful corrective to assumptions embedded elsewhere in the topic.

A skeptical counter-current runs through this methodological cluster. [[Balluff2026-if]] offers the most direct critique: unreflective LLM adoption carries reproducibility, environmental, and epistemic costs that are rarely weighed against simpler alternatives. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] goes further, arguing via Luhmann and conversation analysis that chatbot competence is parasitic on human repair work rather than evidence of understanding—a conceptual challenge to any pipeline that treats LLM outputs as autonomous interpretation. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] proposes "emergent facts" as a new epistemic category needed because GenAI outputs are plausible recombinations rather than verified retrievals, directly relevant to [[DeVerna2025-dl]]'s empirical finding that LLMs fact-check poorly without curated retrieval context, and to [[Paci2025-ag]]'s demonstration that even strong models struggle to interpret implicit, pragmatically loaded political speech. Together these position LLM "understanding" as a persistently open question even as adoption accelerates. At the frontier of self-referential validation, [[Alizadeh2026-es]] tests whether AI coding agents can reproduce published social-science findings (largely yes, with important caveats about PDF-induced bias and sycophantic prompt-following), while [[King2026-av]] and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extend this reflexivity to the scholarly apparatus itself, reporting AI reviews rivaling or outperforming expert peer review and describing deployed tools for pre-submission error detection—raising the question of whether generative AI is now validating not just data but science.

## Persuasion, Belief Change, and the Limits of AI Influence

A second major thread moves from measurement to effects, asking what generative AI can actually do to beliefs and votes. [[Costello2024-bg]] established the template: personalized, evidence-dense LLM dialogue durably reduces conspiracy belief, challenging motivational accounts of belief rigidity. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically decomposes *why* conversational AI persuades, isolating information density and post-training as the dominant levers—while revealing a persuasion-accuracy trade-off that should trouble anyone treating AI persuasion as benign. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends this to real elections across four countries, finding brief AI dialogues shift candidate preference more than traditional ads, driven by facts rather than manipulation, though with a troubling asymmetry: right-leaning AI advocacy is systematically less accurate. [[Kotz2026-lk]] and [[Szabo2026-rd]] generalize the dialogic-persuasion finding to policy domains (climate, vaccination, inequality) and to inoculation against misinformation, respectively, both emphasizing that adaptive, responsive conversation—not static messaging—is the active ingredient, and that effects concentrate among skeptics otherwise hard to reach. Against this optimistic arc, [[Hameleers2026-mc]] offers an important corrective: visual and AI-generated disinformation is not universally more persuasive than text, with effects strongly issue-dependent, suggesting the "deepfake panic" needs empirical tempering just as the LLM-persuasion panic does. [[Choi2026-bz]] adds a psychological wrinkle, showing that difficulty authenticating one deepfake modality carries over to affect confidence and susceptibility to later same-modality content.

## Generative AI as Political and Informational Infrastructure

A third strand examines how generative AI is being woven into the infrastructure of political communication, propaganda, and information ecosystems at scale. [[Waight2026-ts]] provides striking evidence that state media control shapes LLM training data and output valence cross-nationally—an institutional-influence finding that complicates any assumption of model neutrality. [[Triedman2025-uy]] documents a parallel phenomenon in Grokipedia, an AI-generated Wikipedia-fork that is derivative yet diverges toward unreliable sourcing and ideological slant on controversial topics, raising similar concerns about encoded political influence. [[Beacken2026-zb]] and [[Perez-Curiel2026-ld]] examine GenAI propaganda comparatively (across democratically weakened states) and in far-right European campaigning respectively, both arguing against techno-deterministic narratives in favor of context-dependent adoption patterns. [[Crichton2026-kn]] traces a disturbing "re-generative pipeline" by which commercial image models sanitize but do not eliminate the substance of extremist imagery, while [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents an exponential, ChatGPT-coincident surge in AI-generated gambling promotion exploiting platform governance gaps—both papers showing generative AI intensifying, rather than replacing, established persuasive and propagandistic strategies. [[Tang2026-gu]] reframes this from the production side, describing Chinese "AI slop" creators who industrialize virality itself. [[Orlando2025-ul]] and [[Minici2024-tf]] move to simulation and detection: the former shows LLM agents spontaneously reproducing coordination signatures of real influence operations under mere mutual awareness, the latter proposes a graph-foundation-model approach to detecting such operations empirically—together bracketing the problem from generative and detective sides. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]] contributes a platform-agnostic coordination-detection tool relevant to this same arms race, while [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] shows that AI Overview search features inconsistently moderate conspiratorial querying, sometimes legitimizing newer conspiracies. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] grounds these concerns in policy, mapping the gatekeeper ecosystem enabling deepfake financial fraud and arguing for liability-based regulation. [[Lee2026-je]] rounds out this section with a privacy-oriented finding: LLMs can infer political alignment even from nonpolitical text, exploiting culturally politicized vocabulary—a capability with clear implications for the micro-targeting concerns running through the persuasion literature above.

## Reframing the Field: Post-Social Platforms and AI as Medium

Three more conceptual interventions push the topic beyond immediate methodology toward field-level reorientation. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues that generative AI, alongside algorithmic recommendation and user retreat into private spaces, is dissolving the "social media" paradigm itself, with AI-mediated communication constituting one of three emergent "post-social" formations—a provocation that reframes much of the empirical work above as studying symptoms of a paradigm already in transition. [[Manovich2026-ih]] takes the opposite tack, insisting generative AI be studied as a creative *medium* with intrinsic cognitive and aesthetic properties rather than primarily a social or ethical problem, offering a vocabulary (media cognition, bounded creativity, style-content entanglement) largely absent from the social-scientific papers elsewhere in this set. [[Matias2025-px]] closes the loop by arguing that public and lived-experience participation should be understood not as a political concession but as a way of improving the *scientific* quality of AI evaluation itself—a claim that resonates with the methodological reflexivity seen throughout this topic, from validation protocols to participatory data labeling. [[Elfes2026-jb]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] exemplify this same reflexive impulse applied to polarization research, using LLM-operationalized narrative and framing analysis to surface dynamics—such as comment-level narrative convergence beneath video-level divergence—invisible to conventional measures, demonstrating that the methodological innovations catalogued above are already generating genuinely new substantive findings, not just faster versions of old ones. Finally, [[Fan2026-af]] and [[Miller2026-cp]] gesture toward where this agenda is heading: toward hyper-longitudinal trace-data analysis and community-governed AI writing tools, respectively—signaling that the methodological frontier opened by this topic is still expanding rather than consolidating.
