# Generative AI in Media and Disinformation

## Two faces of the same technology

This corpus enacts, almost programmatically, the double framing announced in the topic: generative AI as a *vector* of deception and as a *tool* for studying deception and discourse more broadly. The clearest single exemplar of the vector side is [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]], which traces an exponential surge in coordinated Facebook gambling promotion after ChatGPT's November 2022 launch, showing how generative visuals intensify pre-existing persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localization) rather than replacing them, and how this plays out inside a regulatory grey zone between paid and organic content. Its companion pieces on the methods side, [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]], show the same broad research programme — coordinated-network analysis of Italian political news — using LLM embeddings and LLM-in-the-loop pipelines as an *instrument*, with careful attention to validating clustering, fine-tuning classifiers, and pricing out the cost of LLM labeling. Read together, the pairing embodies the course's dual agenda: GenAI is simultaneously the phenomenon under study and the apparatus used to study it, and several papers in this note explicitly foreground that tension (e.g. [[Balluff2026-if]], [[Dierickx2026-tw]]).

## Persuasion machines: mechanisms, limits, and the accuracy trade-off

A cluster of large-scale experimental papers interrogates *how* conversational AI persuades, moving past speculative fears toward mechanism. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] isolates information density — the sheer volume of fact-checkable claims — as the master variable behind LLM persuasiveness, more powerful than model scale or personalization, but shows this comes at the cost of factual accuracy: the same levers that persuade also mislead. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends this across four national electoral contexts, finding persuasion driven by facts and evidence rather than psychological manipulation, yet documenting a partisan asymmetry in which AI advocating for right-leaning candidates produces systematically less accurate claims. [[Kotz2026-lk]] generalizes the finding to non-electoral contested issues (climate, vaccines, inequality), showing dialogic AI is most effective precisely among skeptics — a domain-general persuasive tool whose power tracks trust in science rather than trust in AI itself. [[Costello2024-bg]] anchors this whole research line: durable, generalizing reductions in conspiracy belief through tailored AI dialogue, directly challenging motivational theories of belief resistance. Against this optimistic-persuasion strand sit protective applications — [[Szabo2026-rd]]'s "conversational inoculation" chatbot and [[Choi2026-bz]]'s finding that authentication difficulty with one modality of fake news carries over to shape vulnerability to later fakes of the same modality — suggesting both offense and defense are converging on the same conversational format.

Complicating the persuasion story, [[Hameleers2026-mc]] finds that visual and AI-generated disinformation do *not* uniformly outdo text; decontextualized authentic video beats AI stills specifically when ample real footage exists (Ukraine) but not otherwise (MH370), suggesting fears about generative visuals may sometimes overstate the case relative to older, low-tech decontextualization. [[Wack2026-bt]] pushes further into the social negotiation of deepfake meaning, showing in Kenya that audience comments — credulous or skeptical — reshape a deepfake's persuasive impact more than the artifact itself, framing deception as collectively "sensemade" rather than fixed at production. Together these three papers push back against artifact-centric alarmism, redirecting attention to context, comments, and pre-existing narrative ecosystems — a corrective echoed at the macro-theoretical level by [[Emilio2026-ik]], whose notion of "synthetic realities" argues the real danger is not isolated fakes but coherent, interactive, personalized fabricated environments that produce a generalized rational discounting of all digital evidence (the "Generative AI Paradox").

## Coordinated infrastructures: from swarms to slop factories

Several papers scale the vector question up from individual persuasive utterances to coordinated systems. [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] both theorize/simulate "malicious AI swarms" — agentic, LLM-driven networks that manufacture consensus, infiltrate communities, and poison training data ("LLM grooming"); Orlando's generative agent-based model shows that mere *awareness* of teammates, without explicit coordination instructions, is nearly sufficient to produce swarm-like cohesion, a sobering finding for platform governance. [[Minici2024-tf]] offers the detection-side mirror: a graph foundation model (IOHunter) for identifying coordinated information-operation drivers across heterogeneous campaigns. [[Beacken2026-zb]] and [[Perez-Curiel2026-ld]] extend the coordinated-propaganda lens comparatively and empirically: the former across six democratically weakened states, arguing GenAI propaganda's form is structurally contingent rather than a uniform technological effect, and the latter documenting far-right use of AI-generated multimedia in the 2024 European elections. On the more industrial end, [[Tang2026-gu]] documents Chinese "AI slop" creators who treat virality itself as an engineerable, benchmarked production process via matrix account systems — a vivid empirical counterpart to the more theoretical accounts of aestheticized synthetic culture offered by [[Galip2026-ix]] (slop/brainrot as Ngai-style "gimmick") and [[Topinka2026-hb]] ("slopaganda" as affective lure rather than belief-manipulating propaganda, produced by RLHF-driven "attunement machines"). [[Manovich2026-ih]] provides the most detached theoretical counterpoint, insisting GenAI be studied first as a *medium* with intrinsic properties (probabilistic variability, built-in "media cognition," conservative aesthetics) rather than primarily as a social threat — a deliberately dissenting voice within this cluster.

## Institutional capture and the epistemics of search/knowledge platforms

A distinct strand examines how state and platform power infiltrate GenAI outputs rather than being deployed *through* them tactically. [[Waight2026-ts]] shows state-coordinated media is memorized by commercial LLMs and biases their outputs toward pro-regime valence in target languages — training data as a channel for institutional influence, echoed methodologically by [[Waight2025-al]]'s narrative-similarity pipeline, which was built partly to trace Russian "biolabs" claims diffusing from state media into low-quality and even mainstream U.S. outlets. [[Triedman2025-uy]] performs the analogous audit on Grokipedia, finding it heavily derivative of Wikipedia yet citing markedly more unreliable/blacklisted sources on controversial and political topics, including bespoke "auto-citogenesis" citing Grok/Musk's own outputs. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] rounds out this strand by auditing Google's AI Overviews' uneven, sometimes legitimizing responses to conspiratorial search queries — showing GenAI-infused search infrastructure inherits and sometimes amplifies the moderation failures of the platforms it sits atop.

## GenAI as research instrument: pipelines, embeddings, and validation

The methodological backbone of the corpus is a set of papers using LLMs and embedding models as analytical infrastructure for media and political-communication research — precisely the agenda mirrored in the "IA Generativa e Media" course. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] jointly establish and stress-test an "LLM-in-the-loop" pipeline (fine-tuned classification, text-embedding-3-large clustering, LLM-generated labels) for Italian political news, arguing embedding-model choice matters more than clustering algorithm and that validating fully LLM-integrated pipelines demands new, expert-driven protocols beyond crowdsourcing. [[Fan2025-ut]] supplies a general-purpose fix underlying such pipelines — linear concept erasure to purge confounds like language or source from embeddings before clustering — while [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends embedding-based clustering into the visual domain, showing vision-LLMs capture *connotative* meaning in ways CNN pipelines cannot. [[Elfes2026-jb]] and [[Waight2025-al]] both use LLMs to operationalize higher-order constructs (narrative role-assignment via Greimas; narrative-claim similarity) that were previously intractable at scale, and both validate against human coding. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] reframes the LLM's role again, as a dialogic collaborator for refining deductive framing codebooks rather than a classifier — a methodological move paralleled by [[Ober2026-vd]]'s hybrid topic-modeling/LLM/human codebook framework for qualitative interviews.

Other papers push LLM-as-tool into adjacent tasks: [[DeVerna2025-dl]] finds curated retrieval context, not model scale or reasoning, is what makes LLM fact-checking work; [[Meher2025-qb]] fine-tunes Llama for conflict-event classification via QLoRA, emphasizing accessibility; [[Lee2026-je]] shows LLMs can infer political alignment from ostensibly nonpolitical text, raising both an analytic opportunity and a privacy risk; [[Paci2025-ag]] finds LLMs still struggle with the pragmatics (implicature, presupposition) of manipulative political discourse; [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] proposes LLM-paired comparisons to scale open-ended survey coding; [[Brown2025-jk]] finds LLM annotation bias on contentious tasks is dataset-specific and swamped by item difficulty rather than demographic mismatch; [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the annotation/synthesis literature broadly; and [[Alizadeh2026-es]] and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] push the "tool" framing furthest, testing whether AI agents can reproduce social-science findings and review scientific manuscripts, respectively — positioning GenAI not just as a content-analysis instrument but as a participant in the research and knowledge-production process itself.

## Reflexivity, epistemics, and the limits of the toolkit

A final, more critical strand steps back from application to interrogate the adoption of GenAI as method and phenomenon. [[Balluff2026-if]] catalogues the underacknowledged costs (reproducibility, corporate dependency, bias, environmental footprint) of unreflective LLM adoption in communication research, arguing for a "least resource-intensive adequate method" ethic that stands in productive tension with the scaling ambitions of the pipeline papers above. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] argues traditional fact-checking epistemologies (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) cannot capture GenAI outputs and proposes "emergent facts" as a new category — a theoretical move that dovetails with [[Emilio2026-ik]]'s synthetic-reality framework and with [[Gilardi2026-hw]]'s empirical finding that audiences cannot distinguish AI-generated from human news quality, though disclosure paradoxically boosts short-term engagement without building durable trust. [[Gillespie2026-aa]], finally, turns the critical lens onto AI safety infrastructure itself, arguing red-teaming — the practice meant to catch GenAI harms before deployment — is a sociotechnical labor system riddled with the same value-opacity and worker harm that beset content moderation, while [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues the entire "social media" paradigm underlying most of this research is dissolving into post-social formations (algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private enclaves, AI-mediated communication), a claim that, if right, unsettles the very object these forty-five papers collectively study. Read as a whole, the corpus thus narrates an arc from naïve alarm, through mechanism-level empirical testing of persuasion and deception, to a maturing methodological toolkit — and finally to a reflexive reckoning with whether the concepts, platforms, and validation norms built for the "social media" era still fit the ground truth GenAI is producing.
