# Generative AI and Media Manipulation

## Mapping the Terrain: From Persuasion Mechanics to Synthetic Realities

The papers filed here trace an arc from *how* generative AI persuades, to *how* it is weaponized in coordinated campaigns, to *what happens to truth itself* once synthetic content saturates the information ecosystem. Read together, they suggest the field is converging on a shared diagnosis: the danger of generative AI is less any single fake artifact than the systemic conditions—economic, infrastructural, cognitive—that make fakery cheap, scalable, and increasingly indistinguishable from authentic discourse.

### The mechanics of AI persuasion

A cluster of large-scale experimental papers interrogates *why* conversational AI persuades, rather than simply asserting that it does. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] establishes information density—the sheer volume of fact-checkable claims—as the central lever of AI persuasiveness, more powerful than model scale or personalization, but reveals a troubling persuasion–accuracy trade-off: the same techniques that make models more convincing make them less truthful. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] escalates the stakes by pitting AI against elite human persuaders—professional canvassers, championship debaters—and finding that no human, even after coaching, can close the gap; constraining AI to human writing speed neutralizes its advantage, confirming throughput (not rapport or humanness) as the mechanism. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends this into live electoral contexts across four countries, showing persuasion is driven by facts rather than manipulation, that personalization is not decisive, and that right-leaning AI advocacy is systematically less accurate—an asymmetry mirroring the broader information environment. [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]] complicates the policy response to this capability: disclosing that an interlocutor is *AI* (as mandated by the EU AI Act) does little to blunt persuasion, but disclosing *persuasive intent* cuts effects roughly in half—suggesting regulation aimed at identity rather than purpose is misdirected. [[Costello2024-bg]] rounds out this strand by showing the same dialogic capacities can be turned toward epistemic repair, durably reducing conspiracy beliief through tailored, evidence-rich AI conversation—evidence that the persuasion machinery is dual-use rather than intrinsically corrosive.

### Coordinated deception and industrial-scale manipulation

A second cluster documents how these persuasive capacities are operationalized in organized campaigns. [[Orlando2025-ul]] models this in silico, showing that LLM agents given mere awareness of teammates—short of explicit coordination—spontaneously reproduce the network signatures of real information operations, suggesting minimal architectural conditions suffice for emergent manipulation at scale. [[Schroeder2026-im]] theorizes the resulting threat as "malicious AI swarms," mapping pathways from manufactured consensus to training-data poisoning ("LLM grooming") and proposing layered governance responses. Empirically, [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] ground these dynamics in real platform data: the former documents an exponential, ChatGPT-triggered surge in AI-generated gambling promotion on Facebook exploiting the grey zone between paid and organic content moderation, while the latter proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a unifying framework spanning state propaganda, gambling networks, and poorly moderated adult-content groups—arguing that shared structural logics (affordance exploitation, simulated coordination) matter more than differing motives, producing a "Zombie Internet" where human and automated activity become indistinguishable. [[Tang2026-gu]] shows the same industrial logic from the production side, describing Chinese creators who reverse-engineer virality into a managed manufacturing process. [[Beacken2026-zb]] insists this is not a uniform global story: GenAI propaganda in democratically weakened states is shaped by local political-economic structure rather than following one technological trajectory. And [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] extends the manipulation frame to financial fraud, arguing that deepfake scams are best countered not through victim vigilance but through gatekeeper liability across platforms, telecoms, and banks.

### Audience reception and the limits of individual resilience

A parallel strand asks how audiences actually respond to synthetic content, often complicating alarmist assumptions. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] finds visual and AI-generated disinformation does *not* uniformly outperform text—context and the availability of plausible real footage matter more than modality per se. [[Choi2026-bz]] identifies a "modality-congruent carryover effect," where difficulty authenticating one deepfake shapes vulnerability to subsequent same-format fakes. [[Wack2026-bt]], working in Kenya, shows deepfake impact is not fixed at production but socially negotiated through surrounding commentary—credulous comments deepen harm, skeptical ones partially restore it, moderated by partisanship. [[Gilardi2026-hw]] finds audiences rate AI-generated and human-written news as equally credible absent disclosure, and disclosure itself boosts short-term engagement without denting quality perceptions—suggesting the "trust erosion" narrative may be overstated at the level of individual reception even as it holds at a systemic level. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] shows search engines' AI Overviews inconsistently police conspiratorial query framings, debunking established theories like chemtrails but sometimes legitimizing newer ones like "15-minute cities" as open "debates."

### Toward synthetic realities and epistemic security

Several papers attempt to synthesize these threads into a higher-order diagnosis. [[Emilio2026-ik]] is the most totalizing: it argues the risk is not discrete deepfakes but "synthetic realities"—a four-layer stack of content, identity, interaction, and institutions—that produce a paradox whereby ubiquitous fakery leads societies to rationally discount all digital evidence, taxing truth itself. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] approaches the same erosion epistemologically, arguing traditional fact-categories (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) cannot capture AI outputs and proposing "emergent facts" as a new category requiring relational, indicator-based evaluation. [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s empirical audit of Grokipedia offers a concrete case of this epistemic slippage: an AI encyclopedia derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically skewed toward unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial topics, exemplifying how "AI-generated knowledge" can launder ideological bias as neutral reference. [[Waight2026-ts]] extends the laundering metaphor geopolitically, showing state media control is absorbed into LLM training data and surfaces as pro-regime bias correlated with national media freedom—an involuntary channel of institutional influence distinct from deliberate model design.

### Cultural registers of slop and the politics of infrastructure

A more culturally inflected pair of papers refuses to treat synthetic content solely as instrumental deception. [[Galip2026-ix]] and [[Topinka2026-hb]] both theorize "slop" as an aesthetic-affective category rather than a truth-claim: Galip applies Ngai's theory of the gimmick to read slop as simultaneously effortless and strained, potentially harboring frictive resistance to platform enshittification; Topinka's "slopaganda" argues such content works not by deceiving viewers into belief but by affectively hooking attention regardless of perceived authenticity, with AI alignment (RLHF) itself functioning as an "attunement machine" that intensifies existing ideological formations rather than disrupting them. Together they push back against purely cognitive/verification-based framings dominant elsewhere in this set.

### Infrastructure, labor, and governance

Underlying all of the above are questions of who builds the guardrails and at what human cost. [[Gillespie2026-aa]] reframes AI red-teaming as a sociotechnical practice inheriting content moderation's labor pathologies—outsourcing, psychological toll, opaque value judgments—cautioning against treating safety evaluation as a purely technical fix. [[Slack1782736288-sl09]] raises parallel concerns about companion chatbots' implicit accommodation policies in emotionally vulnerable interactions. [[Goldberg2026-eb]] takes the most constructive institutional turn, surveying how LLMs might *rebuild* digital public squares through collective dialogue systems, bridging algorithms, and community moderation—provided synthetic participation remains transparent and human deliberation is augmented rather than replaced. [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] offers a case study in the ambivalence this implies: TikTok's affordances enable both pro-immigration solidarity and AI-assisted racist "speculative worldbuilding," deployed near-symmetrically across ideological lines. Finally, [[Dodds2026-df]] steps back to interrogate the meta-narrative itself, arguing that "AI hype" in journalism is not just discourse but an infrastructural force redistributing legitimacy and resources—a useful corrective lens for reading the urgency (and sourcing patterns) of the very corpus assembled here.
