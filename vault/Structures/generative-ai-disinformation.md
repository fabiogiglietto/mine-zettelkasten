# Generative AI & Synthetic Disinformation

## The Mechanics of AI Persuasion

A cluster of large-scale experimental papers now converges on a surprisingly consistent mechanism: conversational AI persuades primarily through *information density*, not personalization or rapport. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] establishes this across 19 LLMs and 707 issues, showing that post-training and prompting strategies matter far more than scale, and that the same fact-dense conversation that persuades also tends to be less accurate — a persuasion–accuracy trade-off that recurs as a structural worry throughout the topic. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] pushes the claim further, pitting AI against elite debaters, professional canvassers, and coached humans, and finding that no human — even after being coached on the very AI that beat them — can close the gap unless AI is artificially throttled to human typing speed, implicating raw throughput of fact-checkable claims as the causal lever. [[Lin2025-xp]] corroborates this in real electoral contexts across four countries, finding AI persuades mainly through "relevant facts and evidence" rather than manipulation, though with a disquieting asymmetry: AI advocating right-leaning candidates makes systematically more inaccurate claims. [[Kotz2026-lk]] extends the paradigm to policy attitudes on climate, vaccines, and inequality, showing effects are domain-general and strongest precisely among skeptics — the audience most resistant to conventional persuasion — while [[Costello2024-bg]] demonstrates the same dialogic mechanism can durably dismantle conspiracy beliefs when evidence is tailored to the individual's specific claims. Read together, these papers suggest generative AI has effectively solved a decades-old bottleneck in persuasion research: the capacity to generate personalized, high-volume, evidentially dense argumentation at conversational speed. [[Szabo2026-rd]] pivots this same mechanism toward inoculation rather than persuasion per se, showing that a chatbot's adaptive, trust-building dialogue can outperform static "prebunking" texts — the defensive mirror image of the offensive capability documented elsewhere in this section.

## Coordinated Campaigns and Synthetic Influence Operations

Where the persuasion papers isolate mechanism, a second group examines what happens when that mechanism is deployed at scale, adversarially, and in coordination. [[Orlando2025-ul]] provides a foundational simulation result: LLM agents given nothing more than awareness of their teammates spontaneously reproduce the coordination signatures of real information operations — narrative convergence, synchronized amplification, rapid hashtag diffusion — without any explicit collusion script, suggesting that coordinated inauthentic behavior may become an emergent property of multi-agent systems rather than something that must be engineered. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] offers the empirical, real-world counterpart: an exponential, structurally-dated surge in AI-generated gambling promotion on Facebook precisely after ChatGPT's launch, in which generative AI does not invent new persuasive tactics so much as intensify and recombine existing ones (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, cultural localization) at industrial scale, exploiting the platform's regulatory blind spot between paid and organic content. [[Schroeder2026-im]] theorizes the ceiling toward which such dynamics point — "malicious AI swarms" that fuse LLM reasoning with persistent multi-agent architectures to manufacture consensus, poison training data, and erode institutional trust — arguing that symmetric "pro-social" countermeasures cannot match adversarial ones because the attention economy structurally rewards the latter. [[Beacken2026-zb]] complicates any assumption of uniform diffusion, showing across six democratically weakened states that GenAI propaganda's form is contingent on local political-economic structure rather than technological inevitability. [[Wack2026-bt]] closes this section at the audience end of the pipeline: in Kenya, a deepfake's persuasive damage is not fixed at production but is renegotiated through surrounding social commentary, with credulous comments deepening harm and skeptical ones partially restoring it — reframing disinformation impact as a matter of collective sensemaking rather than individual credulity.

## Audience Reception: Modality, Confidence, and Disclosure

A smaller set of papers interrogates the audience side more granularly, testing assumptions that visual or synthetic modalities are inherently more dangerous. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] directly challenges the "seeing is believing" heuristic, finding that AI-generated images and decontextualized video are not universally more credible than text — video disinformation only outperforms text when abundant authentic footage exists to lend it plausibility, suggesting low-tech decontextualization may remain as potent a threat as generative synthesis. [[Choi2026-bz]] identifies a subtler psychological residue of exposure: difficulty authenticating one deepfake carries over to shape confidence and vulnerability toward *subsequent* fake content, but only within the same modality, pointing to modality-specific erosion of discernment rather than a generalized deepfake fatigue. [[Mattis2026-gu]] shifts from disinformation to legitimate disclosure, showing that transparently labeling AI's role in journalism reliably reduces trust, but unevenly by task — fact-checking disclosures hurt most, image generation least — with audiences sorting into distinct preference profiles that suggest no single disclosure policy will serve all readers.

## The Long Arc: Epistemic Erosion and Institutional Capture

Underneath the tactical literature runs a more diagnostic strand asking what all this does to public discourse's infrastructure over the long run. [[Emilio2026-ik]] supplies the master frame: a "Generative AI Paradox" in which the proliferation of synthetic content does not just enable individual deceptions but induces societies to rationally discount *all* digital evidence, raising the "cost of truth" itself — a dynamic he formalizes through a four-layer stack (content, identity, interaction, institutions). [[Dierickx2026-tw]] approaches the same erosion epistemologically, arguing that GenAI outputs fit none of fact-checking's traditional categories (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) and proposing "emergent facts" as a new, relationally-verified category suited to probabilistic, context-dependent AI content. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues the underlying media ecology itself has already shifted beneath these debates: platforms have moved from social-graph circulation to algorithmic broadcasting, users have retreated into semi-private enclaves, and AI-mediated chat now rivals posting as a communicative activity — dissolving the "social media" paradigm within which most disinformation research, including much of this topic, is still implicitly framed. [[Waight2026-ts]] and [[Triedman2025-uy]] each document concrete instances of institutional capture of the knowledge infrastructure itself: Waight shows state-coordinated media is memorized by commercial LLMs and measurably skews their political valence by query language, while Triedman's forensic comparison of Grokipedia to Wikipedia finds a supposedly independent AI encyclopedia is both derivative of and ideologically diverging from its source, disproportionately citing blacklisted domains on controversial topics. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] grounds this arc in policy, mapping how deepfake-enabled financial fraud exploits exactly the intermediary infrastructures (platforms, telecoms, banks) that the epistemic-erosion papers treat abstractly, and arguing that liability must shift from individual victims to these gatekeepers — a governance conclusion that echoes Schroeder's call for incentive-based, provenance-oriented intervention rather than reliance on detection or voluntary compliance.

Taken as a whole, the collection traces an arc from mechanism (why AI persuades) to deployment (how that mechanism is weaponized in coordinated, culturally-specific campaigns) to reception (how audiences metabolize synthetic content in real time) to diagnosis (what sustained exposure does to the shared epistemic and institutional ground beneath public discourse) — with the persuasion-accuracy trade-off identified early in the "Mechanics" cluster recurring, in different guises, as the through-line connecting nearly every subsequent paper.
