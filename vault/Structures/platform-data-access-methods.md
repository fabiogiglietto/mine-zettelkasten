# Platform Data Access & Research Methods

## The arc of a closing then contested infrastructure

This body of work traces a single historical arc: platforms opened data taps to researchers in the 2010s, then closed them, and regulators are now trying to force them back open under new legal terms. [[Freelon2024-sc]] supplies the master periodization — API prehistory, laissez-faire, authentication, limited options, academic cooperation, and now a fractured "post-API age" of four official regimes plus unofficial workarounds — and nearly every other paper in this collection can be located somewhere along that timeline. Bastos2025-ya and [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] narrate the paradigmatic case, Twitter/X, as it moved from "Wild West" openness through a brief "Golden Age" Academic API to a post-Musk commercial paywall, while [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]] quantifies the cost of that closure directly: a 25%-a-year growth trajectory in Twitter-based scholarship that stalled in 2023 and fell 13% in 2024. [[Yang2026-tq]] generalizes this from a single platform to the whole discipline, showing that the share of social science papers using any social media data tripled from 2010 to the early 2020s before plateauing and declining in 2022–2024 — a decline concentrated exactly in the platforms (Twitter/X, Facebook) that pulled back access. [[Oswald2025-km]] adds a substantive coda to this infrastructural story: even where data remains available, the visible "tip of the iceberg" of vocal posters systematically misrepresents the silent majority, so access loss compounds a pre-existing representational bias rather than merely narrowing an otherwise sound window onto public opinion.

## Regulation as the new terrain: DSA Article 40

The second act is regulatory. A cluster of papers examines the EU Digital Services Act, and specifically Article 40, as an attempt to convert platform data-sharing from discretionary "philanthropy" into a legal entitlement. [[Ohme2026-nv]], [[Philipp2026-tl]], and [[de-Vreese2026-zx]] together stake out the optimistic-but-vigilant reading: access is genuinely changing in practice, election research specifically stands to benefit from newly mandated APIs and portals, and the visible friction — platform resistance, ambiguous eligibility rules, slow implementation — should be read as evidence the law is finally biting entrenched power rather than as proof of its failure. [[Pierri2025-hm]], reporting from inside an ERC-DG CONNECT pilot, is more circumspect, cataloguing resource asymmetries, a "circular" funding problem that disadvantages early-career and Global South researchers, and a looming regulatory blind spot around LLMs. [[Peters2026-mo]] shifts the lens from access to quality, showing through analysis of consultation feedback that "data quality" is a contested political construct: academics and NGOs pushed it into the Delegated Regulation's recitals over platform resistance (Meta notably inverted the argument to claim access was futile because platform data is inherently low-quality). [[Lukito2026-nb]] and [[Giglietto2025-ed60bc90]] map the resulting landscape empirically — 72 tools sorted by a "price, proficiency, or permission" logic, with production-side data vastly outnumbering consumption-side data and access still skewed toward WEIRD researchers — arguing for a plurality of methods rather than reliance on any single permission regime.

## Auditing what access actually delivers

A third, more forensic cluster asks whether the tools created under this new regime actually work. The answer is consistently sobering. [[Rieder2025-ju]]'s audit of YouTube's search API finds it "forgetful by design," with a three-phase temporal decay that makes historical retrieval unreliable and repeat queries non-reproducible — a serious problem for any DSA-mandated systemic-risk research. [[Entrena-Serrano2025-gw]] reaches a parallel verdict for TikTok's expanded Research API: persistent limitations survive its DSA-driven expansion. [[Tonneau2025-bv]] uses DSA transparency disclosures themselves to expose disparities in human moderation coverage across languages, particularly disadvantaging Global South languages, while [[Jurg2025-ur]] audits YouTube's election-period moderation more qualitatively, finding inconsistent application of authority-signaling labels and vague removal communication, and calling explicitly for expanded API access to enable such audits at scale. [[Bruns2026-pn]] and [[Cullen2026-cb]] focus on the *architecture* of access itself: the former dissects the Meta Content Library's "clean room" model, arguing it privileges code-fluent quantitative researchers, forecloses cross-platform and longitudinal work, and reproduces Global-North/English-language advantage; the latter, via interviews with CrowdTangle users, develops a "seeing like an API" framework showing how a tool's technical design and governance rules actively shape — rather than neutrally transmit — what can be known about platform activity. [[Zheng2026-bi]] responds to this same problem constructively, building TubeStats and TokStats as independent infrastructure for genuinely random (rather than popularity-biased) sampling of YouTube and TikTok, explicitly framed as a workaround to post-API constraints.

## Industry entanglement and the limits of platform-mediated knowledge

A related strand asks not whether access exists but whether research built on it can be trusted. [[Heiss2026-qv]] and [[Bak-Coleman2025-pm]] draw the tobacco/pharma/food analogy explicitly: platforms' monopoly on the data needed to study them creates opportunities for industry influence unmatched in other fields, and existing safeguards (preregistration, IRB norms) are structurally insufficient. [[Bak-Coleman2026-mk]] operationalizes this concern bibliometrically, finding roughly half of high-profile social media papers in top journals carry undisclosed industry ties, concentrated among a small repeatedly-engaged group of authors, editors, and reviewers, with an estimated 80% "industrial saturation" of the field. [[Munger2025-cz]] turns this critique onto the single most prominent industry-academic collaboration, the Meta 2020 Election Study, arguing that its numerical estimates lack "temporal validity" on a rapidly changing platform and that its underpowered, heavily pre-registered design conveniently biases toward null findings aligned with Meta's interests — a claim [[noauthor_undated-bm]]'s companion study of coordinated and financially-motivated deceptive networks partly illustrates in practice, showing reach concentrated in a handful of networks but null causal effects on outcomes once confounding is addressed. [[Allen2025-ot]] proposes a way out of this dependency: platform-independent, browser-extension-based experiments (as in Piccardi et al.) that sidestep the need for platform cooperation entirely, though at the cost of ecological validity trade-offs relative to FIES-style collaborations.

## Forensics of the data itself: quality, thresholds, and distortion

Even where data access exists, several papers show that the data's own construction introduces artifacts researchers must model rather than ignore. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] identifies how Meta's 100-public-shares anonymization threshold in the URL Shares Dataset produces spuriously convergent cross-country trends driven by algorithmic ranking changes rather than genuine local dynamics — a cautionary finding [[Rossi2023-847d5a9f]] builds on directly in extending Guess et al.'s misinformation-exposure methodology to Germany, France, and Italy, explicitly citing the threshold's US-skew as a reason to avoid transatlantic comparison. [[Luhring2025-od]] performs an analogous audit of NewsGuard, showing its continuous scores are stable since 2022 but that binary trustworthy/untrustworthy cutoffs can be swung by single major outlets crossing threshold, with concrete consequences for reproducing published findings. [[McNally2025-dn]] uses CrowdTangle-era Facebook engagement data (before its 2024 shutdown) to demonstrate that the News Feed is not an unknowable "black box" but a system whose interventions leave detectable, lagged statistical signatures — an argument marshalled explicitly in support of DSA-based algorithm auditing. [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] uses billion-post diffusion trees from the same platform-industry collaboration to characterize the genuinely skewed, concentrated nature of misinformation virality. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] turns the lens on researchers' own scraping practices, showing the collection environment itself (not time delay, as prior work assumed) is the dominant source of measurement error in web-tracking studies of news consumption, with systematic, non-random bias toward certain content categories. [[Dahlke2026-sl34]] challenges a foundational measurement convention directly — the "direct referrer paradigm" — arguing that counterfactual, embedded-referrer estimates of platform effects on misinformation exposure are 1.5–15× larger than conventional approaches suggest, with sharply partisan-asymmetric and platform-specific effects.

## Methodological adaptation under constraint

A final cluster responds to the access crisis not through critique or audit but through methodological innovation designed to extract more from whatever access remains. [[Stagnaro2025-pz]] systematically benchmarks nine opt-in survey samples, offering practical guidance now that platform-native recruitment has become harder. [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] demonstrates a Facebook-ads-based recruitment pipeline for hard-to-reach populations, developed precisely in the shadow of tightening post-Cambridge-Analytica API access. [[Eady2025-vm]] and [[Gaisbauer2025-by]] develop new measurement frameworks for ideology and news circulation from sharing behavior, addressing prior over-simplification of the outlet-level, single-dimension approach. [[Fan2025-ut]] offers a general-purpose fix for a subtler problem — spurious source/language confounds in text embeddings — via linear concept erasure. [[Fan2026-af]] and [[Bruns2025-fz]] push toward richer representations of platform behavior: the former mines timestamped, donated cross-platform trace data to recover temporal dynamics lost in cross-sectional aggregation, the latter proposes "practice mapping" via vector embeddings to escape the interpretive limits of conventional network "hairball" visualizations. [[Scalco2026-bd]] operationalizes "information voids" as a quantifiable supply-demand imbalance using longitudinal social-media and search data, directly answering calls (Golebiewski & boyd) for measurement tools the field previously lacked only qualitatively. [[Schulte2026-df]] ties several of these threads together in a case study of the 2025 German election, arguing that the central bottleneck is not any single platform's API but the absence of a reusable, standardized *research infrastructure* for multi-platform observability — a framing that could serve as an epilogue to the whole topic.

## Detecting coordination when the ground truth disappears

A final, more specialized thread concerns coordinated inauthentic behavior (CIB) detection, which is especially exposed to the access crisis because ground-truth datasets have historically come almost exclusively from platform-controlled releases (chiefly Twitter's). [[Thiele2025-ol]] critiques the platform-derived "CIB" concept itself as analytically inadequate, proposing a principal–agent typology built from observable campaign features rather than platform-labeled ground truth. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet responds to platform volatility with a deliberately generalized, platform-agnostic detection architecture, validated against the South Korean NIS dataset. [[Luceri2025-tr]] extends coordination detection to TikTok's video-first ecosystem, where text-centric methods do not straightforwardly transfer, while [[Pante2025-pq]] reassesses prior claims of inter-state coordination using more rigorous, control-dataset-based methods — a corrective move that echoes the broader collection's recurring insistence that platform-provided categories, datasets, and access mechanisms must be interrogated, not taken at face value. [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]], written earlier and more discursively, anticipates much of this agenda, identifying blurred organizational boundaries, opaque micro-targeting, and unregulatable attention-amplification strategies as the structural conditions that make all of this downstream methodological struggle necessary in the first place.
