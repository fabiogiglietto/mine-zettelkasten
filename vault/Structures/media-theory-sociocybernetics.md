# Media Theory & Sociocybernetics

## The cybernetic turn against intentionalism

The anchor of this cluster is [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]], which makes explicit the theoretical commitments that recur, more obliquely, across the other two papers: a refusal to explain media phenomena by appeal to the intentions of individual actors, and a corresponding insistence on describing emergent, system-level dynamics instead. Giglietto and colleagues build their critique of "fake news" scholarship directly on second-order cybernetics (von Foerster, Bateson) and Luhmann's systems theory, arguing that the field's obsession with the *creator's* intent — did they mean to deceive? — is analytically a dead end once content enters a hybrid media system. What matters is not the generative act but the cascade of observer-dependent judgments that propagators make as information moves through the system. Truth, in this framing, is not a property of a message but "a difference that makes a difference" relative to each observer — a formulation lifted straight from Bateson and typical of the sociocybernetic vocabulary that underlies Giglietto's broader intellectual formation (ISA Sociocybernetics committee work, second-screen/social-TV studies) even where it is not foregrounded.

This move — relocating explanatory weight from the originating actor to the system's own self-organizing dynamics — is the connective tissue for the whole topic, even though the other two items were not written from within sociocybernetics proper.

## Media as autonomous systems with their own logic

[[Manovich2026-ih]] performs a structurally similar gesture in a very different register. Manovich insists that generative AI be studied as a *medium* with its own properties, affordances, and constraints rather than as a social or ethical problem to be solved — explicitly bracketing the intentions of users and the moral framing of outputs in favor of describing what the medium itself does: its probabilistic generation, its "media cognition," its bias toward conventional rather than avant-garde logic. This is a McLuhan-inflected echo of the cybernetic move in [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]]: both papers want to displace the human agent (creator/injector or user/prompter) as the locus of explanation and instead describe the emergent behavior of a system that has its own internal logic irreducible to any single actor's intent. Where Giglietto's system is a social-communicative one (propagation cascades across a hybrid news ecology), Manovich's is a computational-representational one (a model's internalized, statistically encoded "knowledge" of media history). Read together, they suggest a more general sociocybernetic sensibility running through this body of work: media systems — whether human-social or machine-generative — behave as autonomous wholes whose outputs cannot be read off the intentions of those who set them in motion.

## Structural affordances and the shape of propagation

[[Slack1783508184-sld3]] supplies the missing middle term between these two system-level accounts: a theory of the *structural affordances* that make such emergent, non-intentional dynamics possible in networked media in the first place. boyd's account of networked publics — persistence, replicability, scalability, searchability, and the resulting dynamics of invisible audiences and context collapse — describes exactly the kind of structural substrate that Giglietto's micro/meso/macro model presupposes but does not itself theorize in detail. The four-cell injector/propagator matrix in [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] and its claim that cascades are "emergent phenomena that can be observed as autonomous" only make sense given a networked environment whose affordances allow content to be endlessly replicated, resurfaced, and recontextualized beyond any single speaker's control. In that sense, boyd's chapter functions here less as a sociocybernetic text in its own right than as the architectural account of the networked publics within which Giglietto's cybernetic dynamics play out — and within which the earlier social-TV/second-screen research that forms Giglietto's intellectual background was itself conducted.

## An intellectual throughline, loosely held

Taken together, this small cluster does not constitute a unified theory so much as a recurring disposition: treat media not as neutral conduits for pre-formed intentions but as systems — social, computational, architectural — whose own structure and dynamics generate outcomes no participant fully controls. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] states this commitment most explicitly and traces it to its sociocybernetic sources; [[Slack1783508184-sld3]] supplies the structural vocabulary (affordances, dynamics) that makes such emergence legible in networked settings; [[Manovich2026-ih]] extends the same anti-intentionalist, systems-level sensibility into the newest terrain of generative media. The grouping is best read as the residue of a theoretical foundation Giglietto revisits only occasionally in his later empirical work, but one that continues to shape how propagation, platforms, and now AI-generated media are each approached as self-organizing systems rather than as aggregates of individual choices.
