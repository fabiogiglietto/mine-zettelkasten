# Information Disorder & Health Misinformation

## Mapping the Terrain: From "Fake News" to Information Disorder

The corpus traces a conceptual arc away from "fake news" as a discrete, binary category toward richer models of disinformation as narrative, infrastructure, and process. Early foundational work ([[Allcott2017-yz]], [[Lazer2018-mm]], [[Vosoughi2018-at]], [[Grinberg2019-ua]], [[Guess2019-ym]], [[Allen2020-nj]], [[Guess2021-ym]]) established the now-canonical empirical baseline: exposure to outright fake news is small, concentrated among older and more conservative users, and dwarfed by ordinary news consumption. [[Benkler2018-lw]] and [[Bennett2018-ys]] reframed the problem institutionally, arguing that an asymmetric, radicalized right-wing media ecosystem—not technology or foreign interference per se—drives a "disinformation order." This institutional, non-content-centric turn is carried forward by [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]], who argues "fake news" obscures more than it reveals and proposes shifting attention from creators' intent to propagators' judgments within the hybrid news system.

A parallel and increasingly dominant strand reconceives disinformation as *narrative* rather than discrete falsehood. [[Marwick2026-qd]], [[Sadler2025-vu]], [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]], [[Goel2025-iq]], [[Waight2025-al]], [[Prochaska2025-ef]], [[Starbird2025-jj]], and [[Prochaska2026-pr]] converge on the claim that stories—not facts—are the unit that matters: narratives recur across platforms and genres, acquire meaning through framing and "deep story" cues rather than veracity, and can even be built from true content (co-shared mainstream articles, accurate evidence wrapped in distorted frames). This narrative turn has generative methodological offshoots, from LLM-based narrative-similarity measurement ([[Waight2025-al]]) to grammars of recurring tropes ([[Prochaska2026-pr]]) to real-time narrative-virality triage for newsrooms ([[Altoe2026-wu]]). [[Frischlich2025-vn]] and [[Spampatti2026-kx]] push this complexity-aware sensibility further, critiquing single-metaphor framings (virus, warfare, pollution) and climate-psychology parallels respectively, both calling for multilevel, portfolio-style interventions rather than silver bullets—a conclusion echoed in the large expert Delphi study of [[Mahl2026-hc]] and the special-issue framing of [[Humprecht2025-ml]] and [[Donovan2025-ws]]'s historical account of "misinformation-at-scale" as structurally built into platform business models.

## Platforms, Coordination, and Infrastructure

A substantial cluster investigates how platform affordances and coordinated behavior—rather than individual belief—structure the spread of problematic information. The CooRnet/CLSB research program ([[Slack1783507719-slc1]], [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Marino2023-9137f448]], [[Song2025-yh]]), closely tied to the MINE-FACTS project, develops content-agnostic detection of Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour as a faster alternative to manual fact-checking, tracing Italian COVID-skeptic networks, Alternative Influence Networks, and comparative UK/US vaccine discourse. Related coordination-detection work extends this logic globally: [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] (pro-government Telegram in Russia/Belarus), [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] (China protest Twitter), [[Kansaon2025-id]] (Brazilian WhatsApp), [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] (Bluesky bots), [[Zhao2025-ny]] (hashtag hijacking), and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], which proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a unifying category transcending misinformation, gambling fraud, and poorly moderated groups alike. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] excavates the organizational substrate of one such operation via OSINT analysis of the IRA's labor-market integration, while [[Rogers2026-cy]] offers a methodological framework for mapping "post-truth spaces" for fact-checkers (also vera.ai-adjacent), and [[Rogers2026-zz]] tracks the shifting terminology and data-access politics of Meta's own moderation discourse across three US elections.

A second infrastructural concern is platform-specific affordances for evasion and amplification: TikTok's algorithmic and audiovisual vernacular facilitates jihadist propaganda ([[Karo2026-dn]]), far-right normalization ([[Nangle2026-yo]], [[Askanius2026-de]] on YouTube, [[Rieder2026-pp]] on persistent Tate visibility), sonic propaganda about Ukraine ([[Bosch2024-hj]]), and even reframed immigration discourse that cuts across ideology ([[Hollingshead2026-vx]]). [[Tuters2026-vo]] theorizes this most ambitiously, arguing conspiratorial alignment around the "Great Reset" emerges from platform grammars of repetition and adjacency rather than coherent belief—a "Memeplex" logic that resonates with [[Marwick2025-ov]]'s account of ConspiracyTok as populist, identity-inflected "generous epistemology," and with [[Marwick2025-vx]]'s processual (rather than sudden) model of far-right "redpilling." Scale and visibility studies—[[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]], [[Yang2023-cg]] on visual misinformation, [[Allen2024-av]] and [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] on causal impact of vaccine-skeptical content, [[Dahlke2026-sl34]]'s "embedded referrer paradigm," [[Bergeron-Boutin2026-yh]], and [[Vincent_undated-re]]'s cross-European platform monitoring—collectively insist on rigorous, often null or modest, estimates of platform-level causal effects, pushing back against inflated "infodemic" narratives while still identifying concentrated risk pockets (e.g., [[Lyons2026-ca]] on older adults' health misinformation exposure).

## Political Elites, Polarization, and Democratic Erosion

Another throughline examines misinformation as elite-driven political strategy rather than mass pathology. [[Tornberg2025-ir]] shows radical-right populism (the ideology-populism interaction, not either alone) best predicts parliamentary misinformation sharing; [[Tai2026-qk]] and [[Gaber2022-bk]] examine institutional/ideological checks and "strategic lying" as intensified spin. [[Renault2025-uh]] uses Community Notes data to show persistent partisan asymmetry in flagged misinformation, complicating platform claims that crowdsourcing eliminates fact-checker bias—a theme extended institutionally by [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]], [[Farkas2026-lr]], and [[Holt2026-zq]] on the post-2025 collapse of Meta's third-party fact-checking regime. Electoral consequences are traced in Brazil ([[Rossini2026-jn]], [[Rossini2026-mj]], [[Ventura2025-sw]]) and the US ([[Berlinski2023-rj]], [[Osmundsen2021-et]]), converging on the finding that belief in fraud/misinformation narratives erodes institutional trust and tolerance independent of simple exposure. [[Van_Erkel2026-mk]]'s "hostile misinformation effect" and [[Gattermann2025-yx]] extend this to perception: citizens believe their own side is unfairly targeted, a bias amplified by partisanship and right-wing orientation. [[Lewandowsky2026-ob]] and [[Adam2026-tz]] (on conspiracy belief formation via contagion/mitigation media pathways) bookend this cluster by linking platform accountability to democratic backsliding.

Radicalization and mainstreaming research—Rothut2026-tz/[[Rothut2026-wt]], [[Righetti2025-slf9]] on anti-gender transnationalization, [[Copland2025-em]] on Sky News Australia as network propaganda, [[Gardam2025-er]], [[Lieu2025-nl]] on climate contrarian taxonomies, [[Beacken2026-zb]] and [[Makeev2026-ma]] on state/GenAI propaganda, and war-specific studies ([[Graham2025-gp]], [[Wack2026-bt]] on Kenyan deepfake sensemaking, [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] on polarized UK scientist coverage)—extends the "mainstreaming" and "hybridization" vocabulary into global and wartime contexts, reinforcing that radicalization is gradual, meso-level, and infrastructurally enabled rather than a single exposure event.

## Correction, Literacy, and the Generative AI Horizon

A substantial applied cluster addresses interventions. Classic debunking psychology ([[Lewandowsky2012-vn]], [[Pennycook2021-jq]], [[Tsfati2020-uo]]) establishes the cognitive baseline—continued influence effects, truth discernment vs. inattention—against which newer tools are tested: inoculation/prebunking at scale ([[van-der-Linden2026-jt]]), conversational and AI-dialogue debunking ([[Szabo2026-rd]], [[Costello2024-bg]]), and argument-level rather than fact-level correction ([[Ramos2026-qo]]). [[Humprecht2020-gd]] supplies the comparative, cross-national resilience framework underlying much of this work, while [[Humprecht2025-ml]], [[Hourigan2026-oc]], and [[Nenno2025-xa]] push explicitly against WEIRD-centric bias, surfacing everyday, non-Western, and non-political misinformation experiences (India/Nigeria contexts figure centrally in [[Beacken2026-zb]]).

Generative AI reshapes both threat and remedy. On the threat side, [[Emilio2026-ik]], [[Dierickx2026-tw]], [[Choi2026-bz]], [[Hameleers2026-mc]], and [[Crichton2026-kn]] theorize "synthetic realities," "emergent facts," and AI-laundered extremist imagery as epistemic challenges beyond deepfake detection. On the remedial/infrastructural side, [[DeVerna2025-dl]] demonstrates LLMs need curated retrieval to fact-check reliably; [[Miller2026-cp]] and [[Jaidka2026-sl34]] explore structural interventions (Community Notes AI writers; elite-blocking field experiments) that reduce misinformation susceptibility by reshaping exposure architecture rather than correcting belief; and [[Kuznetsova2026-hw]], [[Tonneau2025-bv]], and [[Scalco2026-bd]] interrogate the governance gaps—discursive, linguistic, and informational—that leave climate misinformation, non-English speakers, and "information voids" under-addressed. [[Xue2025-bp]], [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]], [[Yoo2026-ev]], [[Cabbuag2024-me]], [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]], [[De_Leon2025-qn]], [[Kasianenko2026-tn]], [[Kim2026-br]], [[FitzGerald2025-nv]], and [[Del-Vicario2016-uj]] round out the picture with studies of affect, incivility, gatekeeping, and cross-national troll behavior—together reaffirming the collection's central trajectory: from flagging false content toward understanding the narrative, infrastructural, and political economies that make information disorder durable.
