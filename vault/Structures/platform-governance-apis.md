# Platform Governance, APIs and the DSA Era

## From Data Philanthropy to Regulatory Right

The most sustained line of argument threading these papers concerns the collapse and partial reconstruction of researcher access to platform data. [[Freelon2024-sc]] provides the definitive periodisation — from the "laissez-faire" API era through Cambridge Analytica to today's "post-API age" — while Bastos2025-ya and [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]] eulogise Twitter's APIs, the latter quantifying a 13% drop in Twitter-based publications in 2024 following the $42,000/month paywall. [[Yang2026-tq]] confirms this bibliometrically across five disciplines, showing that empirical social media research plateaued and declined in 2022–2024 precisely as APIs closed. [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] traces the same "Wild West to walled garden" arc for Twitter/X specifically, arguing progress is fragile and easily reversed by ownership changes.

Against this backdrop, the EU's Digital Services Act — especially Article 40 — emerges as the pivotal counter-force. [[Ohme2026-nv]], [[Pierri2025-hm]], and [[de-Vreese2026-zx]] frame the DSA as a paradigm shift from platform philanthropy to legal right, though all three catalogue implementation friction: opaque procedures, narrow eligibility, under-resourced regulators. [[Philipp2026-tl]] and [[Lukito2026-nb]] extend this assessment to election research specifically, with Lukito's "price, proficiency, or permission" framework crystallising how access barriers reproduce WEIRD-centric inequities. [[Peters2026-mo]] adds a critical layer: data *quality*, not merely access, is politically contested, with academics and NGOs having to force the concept into DSA delegated regulations against platform resistance. [[Entrena-Serrano2025-gw]] and [[Rieder2025-ju]] provide granular audits — of TikTok's and YouTube's research APIs respectively — showing that compliance gestures fall short of substantive transparency, with YouTube's search being "forgetful by design." [[Giglietto2025-ed60bc90]] surveys this whole tooling landscape.

## Industry Capture and the Politics of Platform-Cooperative Research

A parallel argument, sharper in tone, concerns how platform dependence corrupts the research base itself. [[Bak-Coleman2025-pm]] and [[Bak-Coleman2026-mk]] draw the tobacco/pharma analogy explicitly: roughly half of high-profile social media papers have disclosable industry ties, mostly undisclosed, with citations and policy uptake doubling for tied work. [[Heiss2026-qv]] makes the same case as commentary. [[Munger2025-cz]] turns this critique on the Meta2020 collaboration itself, arguing its null findings reflect design compromises and low temporal validity rather than genuine absence of effects — a critique that [[Allen2025-ot]] engages more sympathetically while endorsing platform-independent browser-based experiments as the way forward. [[Gauthier2026-iq]] and [[Brady2026-ln]] operationalise exactly this: independent field experiments (on X and Bluesky respectively) that recover the political and normative effects Meta2020 largely missed, in part because they intervene at the content-level rather than at platform-wide affordances.

## Algorithmic Curation, Amplification, and Visibility

Empirical audits of what algorithms actually do form a third strand. [[Bakshy2015-rn]] anchors the historical baseline — friend networks and user clicks matter more than the algorithm — but recent work complicates this. [[Gauthier2026-iq]] and [[Efstratiou2025-gs]] show X's "For You" feed asymmetrically boosts conservative, agitating, and Musk-proximate content, with [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] demonstrating that the recommender inadvertently learns users' Left-Right positions as a linear embedding direction. [[Brown2026-br]] finds YouTube produces content rabbit holes but not echo chambers or radicalisation pathways, while nudging all users toward moderately conservative content. [[McNally2025-dn]] uses time-series methods to show Facebook's News Feed algorithm is *not* a black box: hard-news engagement responds to documented ranking changes with a ~19-day lag. [[Giglietto2026-632ef967]] extends this longitudinally, finding partisan penalties and journalistic-quality rewards fluctuate in step with Facebook's "break-the-glass" interventions, and [[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]] quantifies Meta's political-content-reduction policy: a 72% reach drop for Italian MPs, undisclosed in DSA compliance reports. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] warns that even Meta's flagship research dataset is shaped by algorithmic changes distorting the 100-share threshold.

## Moderation: Escalation, Retreat, and Its Discontents

A fourth arc traces content moderation's expansion and rollback. [[Donovan2025-ws]] and [[Katzenbach2026-sl2e]] provide the historical spine: an escalation of "civic integrity" commitments from 2018–2022 followed by post-2022 backlash, with [[Moran2025-qn]] documenting the human toll on the trust-and-safety profession. [[Hurcombe2025-cs]] and [[De2026-ld]] reveal the discursive strategies — Meta's Newsroom framings, the "changecraft" through which platforms narrate transformation — while [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]] and [[Farkas2026-lr]] examine fact-checkers caught between Meta's January 2025 termination of the Third-Party Program and rhetorical accusations of censorship. [[Renault2025-uh]] shows that even under X's community-based Community Notes, Republicans are flagged 2.3× more often than Democrats — undermining the "biased fact-checker" rationale — while [[Bouchaud2026-np]] argues the bridging algorithm structurally undermoderates polarising content by design. Complementary audits reveal moderation's blind spots: [[Karo2026-dn]] on jihadist evasion tactics on TikTok, [[Rieder2026-pp]] on the "Tate-space" that survives deplatforming through recommendation-driven remix, [[Jurg2025-ur]] on YouTube's inconsistent publisher labels, and [[Tonneau2025-bv]] on stark linguistic disparities in moderator allocation (Global South languages receiving 7.5–55% of English's per-content ratio). [[Simeone2025-vo]] provides a rare positive case: targeted Twitter deplatforming did dismantle mobilisation networks around the Arizona election audit.

## The Bigger Picture: What Social Media Has Become

A more speculative strand argues the "social media" object itself is dissolving. [[Tornberg2026-lc]], [[Boyd2026-op]], [[Gerbaudo2026-fo]], and [[Helmond2026-ll]] — all anniversary essays — variously reframe platforms as "parasocial media," "clustered publics," or AI-driven "post-social" formations. [[Baym2026-tr]] and [[Bruns2026-yv]] take stock of the darkening picture, while [[Swartz2026-zb]] centres scams as a defining logic, and [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] catalogues the deepfake fraud ecosystem. [[Weinbrand2026-sf]] extends the analysis to Google's AI Overviews as the next infrastructural shift. [[Ahuja2025-ku]] connects HCI's dark-patterns scholarship to DSA Article 25, and [[Crosset2026-mq]] provides the widest-angle view: EU, UK and US legislation converges on "security bureaucratisation" — governing information flows through transparency and audit rather than direct control.

## Effects, Consequences, and Political Stakes

Finally, several papers document the political consequences that make all this matter. [[Ventura2026-yc]] shows Brazil's X ban produced a durable partisan sorting ratchet. [[Vincent_undated-re]] finds misinformation rising on TikTok in Europe. [[Dehghan2026-sy]] documents "sedimented polarisation" on Reddit that no bridging space repairs. [[Bakshy2015-rn]], [[Gauthier2026-iq]], and [[Brady2026-ln]] together trace a decade-long shift from "user choice dominates" to "algorithmic reranking causally shifts attitudes and perceived norms." [[Inacio-da-Silva2026-zf]] and [[Votta2025-xz]] examine political advertising's opacity, and [[Kristensen2025-ni]] asks whether alternative platforms drive their own polarisation. [[Lewandowsky2026-ob]] and [[Mahl2026-hc]] close the loop, calling — like most contributors here — for platform accountability as a democratic prerequisite, while [[Bechmann2026-dr]] reframes the research agenda itself: from causal effects on individuals to platforms as democratic infrastructure to be governed accordingly.
