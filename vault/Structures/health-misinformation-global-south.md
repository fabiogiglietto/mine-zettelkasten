# Health Misinformation in Global Contexts

## The arc: from gatekeepers to coordinated networks to causal harm

This cluster traces an arc that begins with classical questions about who controls the flow of health information, moves through the discovery that "who controls the flow" increasingly means *coordinated networks* rather than single gatekeepers, and ends by asking what all this circulation actually does to beliefs, vaccination behavior, and mortality. Read together, the papers form a methodological lineage — CooRnet-style coordination detection, supply/demand anomaly detection, panel-plus-tracking designs, and causal epidemic modeling — applied to an evolving object of study, from Italian Facebook health news in 2019 to COVID-19 vaccine discourse across multiple countries.

## The pre-pandemic baseline: gatekeeping as the inherited problem

[[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] is the chronological outlier and functions as prologue. Working with nine months of pre-COVID data on Google News Italia, it reframes classical gatekeeping theory (Shoemaker & Vos) for an algorithmic aggregator era, showing that engagement on Facebook is driven less by source credibility than by topic affect — celebrity health stories generate outsized comments and shares relative to "harder" scientific or system-level news. This finding anticipates a theme that recurs across the whole cluster: attention and credibility are decoupled, and virality tracks emotional resonance rather than epistemic quality. It also sets up the double-edged framing — gatekeeping fosters health literacy but simultaneously creates openings for disinformation — that the pandemic-era papers will treat as an urgent, no-longer-hypothetical risk.

## Mapping coordinated networks: from Italy outward

The empirical center of gravity is a family of studies using coordinated link-sharing behavior (CLSB) and related network-detection methods to map "nefarious" or contrarian actors. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] documents the MINE-FACTS project's content-agnostic CooRnet tool, tracing how an Italian covid-skeptic network grew out of pre-existing political clusters (League, Five Star Movement) and adapted tactics — link laundering, first-comment link placement, exploitation of religious groups — to evade platform moderation; critically, it demonstrates that CLSB detection surfaces problematic content at a substantially higher hit rate than routine fact-checking, making the case for behavioral over purely content-based detection. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this same Italian dataset to examine *who* gets amplified within these networks, identifying an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of alternative influencers whose posts are share-heavy rather than comment-heavy, and showing that legacy media and mainstream journalists remain paradoxically central to circulating covid-skeptic narratives via remediation of TV and newspaper statements — a hybrid-media-system dynamic that complicates any clean separation between "alternative" and "mainstream" as vectors of misinformation.

[[Song2025-yh]] internationalizes the CLSB approach, comparing pro- and anti-vaccine coordinated communities on Facebook in the UK and US. It complicates the assumption that coordination is inherently malicious: pro-vaccine CLSB in the US is large and institutionally grounded (NHS, CDC), while anti-vaccine CLSB is denser and more engaged in the UK, with content and framing shaped by distinct national political cultures (safety skepticism in the UK vs. individual-freedom rhetoric in the US). Together with [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]], this establishes coordination as a general infrastructural feature of health discourse — sometimes serving public health communication, sometimes subverting it — that must be read in context rather than assumed adversarial.

[[Efstratiou2026-ij]] shifts the network lens from Facebook to Twitter and from vaccine discourse specifically to the circulation of COVID-19 scientific papers, showing a comparable structural asymmetry: a small, non-automated coordinated network almost entirely amplifies contrarian credentialed experts, while pro-consensus superspreaders are more numerous, more credentialed, and better aligned with high-trust news outlets. Its finding that news coverage typically *follows* superspreader activity — rather than driving it — dovetails with Marino's remediation mechanism, suggesting a recurring pathway in which legacy/mainstream media launder or ratify claims that originate in coordinated or influencer-driven social media activity, regardless of national context or platform.

## Measuring the ecosystem: voids, exposure, and where it actually lands

[[Scalco2026-bd]] steps back from actor-level coordination to model the information ecosystem itself, operationalizing "information voids" as anomalous supply-demand imbalances across six European countries' vaccine discourse. Its key contribution — voids correlate with a documented drop in high-credibility content and a rise in misinformation share — offers an ecosystem-level mechanism that helps explain *when* the coordinated networks mapped by Giglietto, Marino, and Song find fertile ground: around major triggering events (EMA authorizations, the AstraZeneca suspension) when legitimate supply lags demand.

[[Lyons2026-ca]] then asks a more sobering empirical question about consequences at the individual level: how much low-credibility health content do people actually see, and who sees it? Using passive web tracking rather than platform-side network data, it finds exposure is rare in aggregate but sharply concentrated among older adults with poorer discernment and conspiracist worldviews — and, notably, that this exposure arrives via referrals from other low-credibility sites rather than from search or social media, cutting against the assumption (implicit in the CLSB literature) that platforms are the primary vector. This is a useful corrective to the network-centric papers: coordinated amplification on Facebook or Twitter may be necessary but not sufficient to explain who ultimately consumes dubious health content.

## From exposure to belief and outcome

The final move in the arc is causal: does any of this actually change minds or behavior? [[Adam2026-tz]] uses a panel-plus-web-tracking design in Germany/Switzerland to show that conspiracy belief formation is a genuine "marriage" of media exposure and predisposition — alternative media exposure produces contagion, mainstream debunking produces mitigation on average, but populists selectively engage with the former and avoid (or counterargue against) the latter, producing indirect reinforcement and backfire pathways. This paper supplies the individual-level psychological mechanism that complements the network-mapping papers' structural account of *how* problematic content circulates.

[[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] pushes furthest toward hard behavioral and epidemiological consequence, building a novel SIRVA compartmental model fit to county-level antivaccine tweet exposure, vaccination rates, cases, and deaths, and estimating via causal graphical modeling that antivaccine Twitter content caused roughly 14,000 vaccine refusals and at least 545 cases and 8 deaths in the US — a lower-bound but methodologically distinctive attempt to close the loop from platform speech to mortality, the endpoint implicitly gestured at but never quantified in the network-mapping and belief-formation studies.

## Synthesis

Read as a whole, this structure moves from Ducci's pre-pandemic diagnosis of engagement-driven gatekeeping, through an escalating set of tools for detecting coordination and ecosystem imbalance (Giglietto, Marino, Song, Efstratiou, Scalco), to individual-level accounts of who is actually exposed and why (Lyons, Adam), and finally to attempts at quantifying downstream harm (Bollenbacher). The throughline is a growing sophistication in disentangling *structure* (who coordinates, what gets amplified, when voids open) from *consequence* (who is exposed, what they come to believe, what they do), with the Italian CLSB tradition supplying much of the methodological backbone later adapted, generalized, and internationalized across platforms and countries.
