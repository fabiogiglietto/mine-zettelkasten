# Generative AI & Synthetic Influence Operations

## The Line of Argument

This cluster documents a pivot in the study of influence operations: from human-run troll farms and hand-crafted disinformation toward campaigns in which generative AI does the writing, the arguing, the image-making, and increasingly the coordinating. The notes trace three interlocking questions — how persuasive is generative AI compared to humans, how is it actually being deployed in the wild (gambling ads, propaganda networks, viral video farms), and how do audiences and institutions cope with an information environment where authenticity can no longer be assumed by default.

## How persuasive is AI, really?

A set of tightly controlled experiments establishes the empirical baseline. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] shows that frontier conversational AI out-argues not just laypeople but professional canvassers and elite competitive debaters, and that the advantage is driven by sheer information throughput — fact density — rather than charisma or personalization. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends this into live electoral contexts across four democracies, finding AI-voter dialogues shift candidate preference more than traditional political ads, again mainly through "central route" argumentation rather than psychological manipulation, though with a notable partisan asymmetry in factual accuracy when AI argues for right-leaning candidates. [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]] then interrogates the policy response: simply labeling a chatbot "AI" (the EU AI Act Article 50 approach) barely dents its persuasiveness, but disclosing its *persuasive intent* cuts influence roughly in half — suggesting that transparency regimes built around source-identity rather than purpose are addressing the wrong problem. Together these three papers reframe the "AI persuasion threat" away from deception and toward capability: even fully disclosed, honest AI argumentation is a powerful political tool, which has implications for how the rest of the cluster's deceptive-use cases should be read.

## Synthetic visual persuasion and the "zombie internet"

The empirical heart of the researcher's pivot is a pair of platform studies analyzing generative AI's role in coordinated commercial and political manipulation. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] dissects over 2,300 AI-generated images from coordinated Facebook gambling networks, showing that generative AI does not invent new persuasive logics so much as intensify and recombine existing ones — aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localization — through hyper-real, industrially scaled visual production, with posting volume exploding almost exactly at the launch of ChatGPT. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] widens the lens, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category that subsumes a pro-Putin propaganda network, the same gambling promotion ecosystem, and poorly moderated adult-content groups, arguing that what unites them is not shared content or motive but a shared structural exploitation of platform affordances and coordinated seeding — a condition the authors call the "Zombie Internet," where human and synthetic/bot activity become empirically indistinguishable and enforcement categories built around content falsity fail to catch them. [[Tang2026-gu]] supplies a production-side complement from a different context: Chinese "AI slop" creators who use generative tools plus matrix-account systems to convert virality itself into a managed, engineered output rather than an emergent accident — the labor-side mirror of the amplification dynamics Giglietto and Giada document from the platform side.

## Audience sensemaking under synthetic uncertainty

A second thread asks what happens on the receiving end. [[Choi2026-bz]] finds a "modality-congruent carryover effect": difficulty authenticating one deepfake shapes confidence and vulnerability toward subsequent fakes in the same format, implying that repeated synthetic-media exposure recalibrates discernment in format-specific ways. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] complicates alarmist assumptions about visual disinformation's unique power, showing AI-generated images do not systematically outperform text and that decontextualized *real* video — not synthetic imagery — is the more potent format when authentic footage is abundant, a finding that pushes back against generative-AI exceptionalism. [[Wack2026-bt]] extends this into a Global South, high-stakes political setting, showing in Kenya that a deepfake's persuasive damage is not fixed at production but negotiated collectively through surrounding comments and partisanship — credulous commentary deepens harm, skeptical commentary partially restores it. Read together, these three papers argue that the "danger" of synthetic media is less about the artifact itself and more about the social and cognitive context of its reception — a corrective to purely technical detection framings.

## Coordination at scale: swarms, agents, and their human ancestors

A third thread moves from single artifacts to coordinated systems. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] provides the pre-generative-AI baseline, an organizational history of the Internet Research Agency showing that even "low-tech" troll factories functioned as ordinary, labor-market-embedded media operations — a benchmark against which the newer agentic threat can be measured. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates what happens when that coordination is handed to LLM agents themselves, finding that networked generative agents spontaneously reproduce IRA-like coordination signatures — narrative convergence, synchronized amplification, rapid hashtag diffusion — with mere mutual awareness among agents nearly matching explicit centralized deliberation. [[Schroeder2026-im]] theorizes the endpoint of this trajectory, arguing that LLM-plus-multi-agent architectures constitute a qualitatively new "malicious AI swarm" threat capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning training data at a scale and adaptivity beyond anything IRA-style operations achieved, and arguing that voluntary platform compliance and symmetric "pro-social" countermeasures are structurally insufficient. [[Luceri2025-tr]] supplies the methodological counterpoint: existing coordinated-inauthentic-behavior detection, built for text platforms, does not transfer cleanly to video-first ecosystems like TikTok, underscoring a widening gap between the sophistication of emerging swarm/agentic threats and the tools available to detect them.

## Institutional and structural stakes

The final thread zooms out to systemic consequences. [[Emilio2026-ik]] synthesizes much of the above into a "Generative AI Paradox": as synthetic content saturates the environment across content, identity, interaction, and institutional layers, societies may rationally begin discounting *all* digital evidence, which paradoxically empowers bad-faith denial as much as fabrication does. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] traces this logic into financial regulation, documenting how deepfake-enabled fraud exploits jurisdictional fragmentation and platform liability shields, and arguing — in a manner structurally similar to Giada's "Zombie Internet" diagnosis — that gatekeepers and infrastructure, not individual victims, are the appropriate locus of intervention. [[Beacken2026-zb]] resists techno-deterministic narratives by showing that GenAI propaganda adoption varies substantially across democratically weakened states depending on local political and economic structure, cautioning against a single global narrative of AI-driven democratic collapse. Finally, [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s analysis of Grokipedia shows the same dynamics operating in the domain of encyclopedic knowledge itself: AI-generated content that is nominally derivative of a trusted source but systematically shifts sourcing toward lower-reliability and ideologically aligned material — a slow-motion, institutionally sanctioned version of the synthetic-reality erosion that Ferrara theorizes and that the gambling and Zombie Internet studies document in real time on social platforms.
