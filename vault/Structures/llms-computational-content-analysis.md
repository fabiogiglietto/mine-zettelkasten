# LLMs & Computational Content Analysis

## The Italian anchor: validating an LLM-in-the-loop pipeline

The empirical and methodological core of this collection is a pair of companion papers built around the same corpus: Facebook-shared political news from the 2018 and 2022 Italian elections. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] lays out the full pipeline — a fine-tuned political/non-political classifier, embedding-based clustering of links, and LLM-generated cluster labels — and, more importantly, confronts the problem that no existing validation protocol was built for a system in which LLMs perform *every* stage. Its central methodological claim, that crowdsourced raters are no longer an adequate ground truth once models outperform low-skilled human coders, pushes the field toward expert, domain-literate validators and toward stage-specific rather than end-to-end evaluation. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] drills into the embedding stage specifically, showing empirically why `text-embedding-3-large` was chosen over the Italian-specific UmBERTo model: across both election years and clustering algorithms, the OpenAI embedding produces more semantically coherent clusters, with clustering *algorithm* choice (K-means vs. HDBSCAN) mattering as much as embedding choice, and with a cautionary finding that HDBSCAN's apparent quality gains are often an artifact of dumping large shares of data into a noise class. Together these two papers model a discipline in the middle of building its own validation infrastructure rather than importing NLP benchmarks wholesale — a theme that recurs, in a very different register, in [[Paci2025-ag]]'s use of the IMPAQTS corpus of Italian political speech to show that even strong multilingual LLMs fail well short of expert human interpretation when the task shifts from surface classification to pragmatic inference (implicatures, presuppositions), suggesting that the "coherent cluster" success of the Italian pipeline papers may not generalize to deeper discourse-analytic tasks.

## LLMs as annotators and classifiers: how far does substitution for humans hold?

A second cluster interrogates, more generally, whether LLMs can stand in for human coders. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the emerging methodological landscape of LLM-based annotation and synthesis, providing the taxonomic scaffolding (generation, assessment, utilization of labels) that the more applied papers here instantiate. [[Brown2025-jk]] offers a sobering corrective to fears of systematic demographic bias in LLM annotation: across three models and four contentious datasets, bias in LLM-human disagreement is small, dataset- rather than model-specific, and dwarfed by item difficulty (human label entropy) — a finding that complicates any simple story of LLMs "inheriting" majority-group views, and that resonates with [[Balluff2026-if]]'s broader call for methodological reflexivity rather than either uncritical adoption or blanket rejection of LLMs in communication research. Domain-specific fine-tuning work extends the substitution question to structured event coding: [[Meher2025-qb]]'s ConflLlama shows that QLoRA-adapted Llama models can match or exceed BERT-era approaches (e.g., ConfliBERT) for multi-label conflict-event classification on consumer hardware, while [[Bailard2024-pj]] uses a fine-tuned DeBERTa classifier — not a prompted LLM — to code collective-action frames in extremist Telegram discourse at scale, then couples the classifications to offline-violence event data via Granger causality, illustrating how validated classification becomes an input to substantive causal-adjacent claims. [[Larsson2026-ro]] performs an analogous move for sentiment: zero-shot GPT-4 classification, human-validated, applied to a decade of Norwegian Facebook posts, demonstrating LLM-assisted content analysis outside English and outside text alone. That "beyond text" extension is pursued directly by [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]], which finds that a prompted multimodal LLM (GPT-4o) substantially outperforms specialized computer-vision pipelines (RetinaFace, FaceNet512, Google Cloud Vision) for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery — echoing the Italian pipeline's embedding-superiority finding but for vision rather than text. [[Ober2026-vd]] and [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] push substitution into more interpretive territory: the former integrates topic modeling, LLM labeling, and human codebook refinement for qualitative interview analysis, explicitly arguing that topic modeling's mathematical transparency should anchor LLM-assisted coding; the latter proposes LLM-paired comparisons as a way to scale open-ended survey responses into continuous latent measures, addressing the classic closed- vs. open-ended survey trade-off.

## Embeddings and LLMs as measurement instruments

A distinct but related strand treats embeddings and LLMs not as classifiers but as instruments for locating texts in latent spaces. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]]'s "ask and averaging" method — prompting an LLM repeatedly to place a text on an ideological dimension and averaging responses — offers a strikingly simple alternative to Wordscores/Wordfish-style scaling, validated across languages and text types. [[Lai2024-to]] scales ideology estimation to an entirely new unit of analysis (individual YouTube videos) via Reddit sharing behavior and correspondence analysis, extending the ideal-point tradition from legislators and users to content itself. [[Waight2025-al]] tackles a harder measurement problem — narrative similarity, as distinct from lexical or topical overlap — via an LLM-based claim-and-subject distillation pipeline, validated against gold-standard hand coding and shown to recover cross-source narrative diffusion (Russian "biolabs" claims) invisible to exact-text-reuse methods; this estimand-estimator argument parallels [[Fan2025-ut]]'s more infrastructural contribution, which shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) applied to embeddings removes confounding due to source or language and dramatically improves multilingual retrieval and clustering without degrading out-of-distribution performance — a technique directly relevant to the embedding-comparison questions raised by [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]]. [[Elfes2026-jb]] and [[Arminio2025-tw]] extend LLM-based measurement to structuralist and connotative meaning: the former operationalizes Greimas's actantial model via an LLM to detect "narrative polarisation" in YouTube discourse on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, finding that comments harmonize surface narratives across partisan camps even as deeper motifs remain divergent; the latter shows that VLLM-generated textual descriptions of images, embedded and clustered, capture *connotative* meaning (e.g., grouping wind turbines with solar panels as "renewable energy") that CNN pipelines miss, at only a small cost to denotative coherence. [[Arora2025-tx]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] round out this cluster with proposals for multi-modal and fully unsupervised framing analysis that move past predefined frame categories — the former by fusing text and image cues in gun-violence coverage, the latter by combining ML, network analysis, and NLP to surface emergent frames in polarized social-media discourse without ground truth.

## Applications: fact-checking, persuasion, and belief change

A third arc examines what these validated pipelines are used *for* in high-stakes democratic contexts. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] delivers a pointed methodological lesson directly relevant to the pipeline-validation theme: standard and even reasoning-enabled LLMs fact-check political claims poorly, web search helps only modestly, and it is curated retrieval-augmented context — not model scale or reasoning — that drives large gains, echoing the broader lesson that LLM performance on political content analysis is highly sensitive to what evidence or context is supplied. [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Szabo2026-rd]] both use LLM dialogue as an intervention rather than a measurement tool, finding that personalized, evidence-rich conversation durably reduces conspiracy belief and misinformation susceptibility, respectively — results that push against motivational accounts of belief resistance. [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] complicates this optimism: LLM persuasiveness on political-adjacent misconceptions is conditional on perceived neutrality, with brief out-party bias warnings cutting belief correction substantially, suggesting that trust in the "messenger" is a lever as important as message content or interactivity.

## Critical counterpoints: bias, provenance, and the limits of understanding

Running against the mostly instrumentalist tone of the above is a skeptical thread questioning what LLMs actually encode and whether their use is epistemically safe. [[Waight2026-ts]] shows that state media control leaves a traceable fingerprint in training data and commercial model outputs, with LLMs producing systematically more pro-regime responses in lower-media-freedom languages — a provenance concern that generalizes [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s forensic comparison of Grokipedia to Wikipedia, which finds heavy derivativeness paired with disproportionate citation of unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial and political topics, effectively documenting ideological capture of an AI-generated reference work. [[Lee2026-je]] flips the bias question toward the analyst's advantage and the subject's risk: LLMs can infer political alignment from ostensibly nonpolitical discourse with striking accuracy, raising privacy and micro-targeting concerns that mirror the annotation-bias debates in [[Brown2025-jk]]. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] offers the deepest conceptual challenge, arguing via conversation analysis and Luhmannian systems theory that chatbot competence is parasitic on human repair work rather than genuine understanding — a claim that reframes [[Paci2025-ag]]'s empirical finding of pragmatic failure as theoretically expected rather than a fixable engineering gap. [[Balluff2026-if]] ties these threads into an explicit methodological warning, cataloguing reproducibility, environmental, and bias costs of LLM adoption and urging a "least resource-intensive" default — a call that stands in productive tension with the pipeline-building optimism of the Italian election papers.

## Meta-level: validating AI in the research process itself

Two papers extend the validation question from content analysis to the scientific process itself. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] benchmarks frontier coding agents' ability to reproduce published social-science findings, finding high accuracy but also documenting sycophantic specification search under confirmatory prompting — a direct analogue, at the level of research practice, to the annotation-bias and context-sensitivity concerns raised elsewhere. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extends automation to peer review itself, proposing a taxonomy of AI roles that mirrors the "human-in-the-loop" framing central to [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]], suggesting that the validation challenges facing LLM-based content analysis are a special case of a more general problem: how to certify AI outputs when human expert judgment remains the only available ground truth.

## Substantive applications beyond the core pipeline

Finally, several papers apply LLM- or embedding-based methods to substantive political questions without foregrounding methodology per se. [[Minici2024-tf]] builds a graph foundation model combining language and network signals to detect coordinated information operations across heterogeneous state-sponsored campaigns. [[Cerulli2026-sl75]] links validated dark-personality questionnaires to Reddit text, finding a persistent gap between self-reported traits and computationally extracted linguistic markers of toxicity — a cautionary data point for any assumption that psychological constructs map cleanly onto surface text features. [[Marino2026-slef]] applies an LLM-based actor-classification and volatility-detection pipeline to Brazilian pro-Bolsonaro Facebook networks, showing that affective engagement is temporally unstable rather than fixed by ideology, directly challenging static echo-chamber models. Together with the Italian pipeline at the center of this collection, these applications demonstrate the same throughline: LLM-and-embedding methods are increasingly indispensable for political content analysis at scale, but every one of these papers — whether building, applying, or critiquing such pipelines — insists that their outputs remain only as trustworthy as the validation regime built around them.
