# Italian Political Communication (MINE Program)

## Foundations: TV, Twitter, and the Hybrid Media System

The MINE line begins from the premise that Italian political communication cannot be understood by isolating any single medium. [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] establishes this starting point empirically, tracing how audiences used Twitter as a "second screen" during 2013 election talk shows and finding that participation remained narrow, TV-agenda-bound, and dominated by opinion and interaction requests rather than genuine agenda-building. This early diagnosis — that networked publics interact with, but do not supplant, legacy broadcast logics — becomes the conceptual hinge for everything that follows: subsequent MINE papers keep returning to the question of how old and new media co-produce visibility, credibility, and partisan attention.

The 2018 and 2019 Italian elections mark the program's empirical core. [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]] shows that populist-aligned news sources (M5S, Lega) achieve higher Twitter "insularity" than mainstream outlets, and that insularity predicts distinctive Facebook engagement signatures — amplification via shares for echo-chambered sources, contestation via comments for cross-partisan ones — with M5S uniquely able to mobilize both dynamics strategically depending on valence. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] complements this by reframing disinformation research around *coordinated link sharing behavior* rather than content veracity, demonstrating across 2018 and 2019 that coordinated networks disproportionately spread problematic domains and cluster into either centralized or distributed structures depending on their political/non-political composition. Together these two papers define the program's signature move: pairing platform-trace data (Twitter attention, Facebook engagement, CrowdTangle-based coordination) with theoretically grounded constructs (insularity, coordination, parallelism) rather than treating "misinformation" as a fixed content category.

## Coordination, Manipulation, and Detection Infrastructure

A second cluster extends this coordination paradigm methodologically and temporally. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] operationalizes a *dynamic* detection workflow for the 2022 snap election, showing how iterative monitoring surfaces previously invisible actors — a hyperpartisan M5S network, a religious-clickbait operation, and a proselytizing Messenger-bot network — thereby exposing how static blocklists systematically underestimate manipulation. This concern with underestimation and infrastructural limits recurs in [[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]], which uses the Meta Content Library to show that Meta's political-content-reduction policy suppressed Italian parliamentarians' reach by 72% roughly ten months *before* its announced global rollout, while extremist accounts compensated through posting volume rather than per-post reach — a finding framed explicitly as evidence of platform transparency deficits even as it validates DSA-enabled data access as a genuine research advance.

Adjacent work broadens the "coordination" lens beyond Italy: [[Oprea2025-lf]] applies an Authenticity Matrix to Romanian Facebook pages during the 2024 EU election, finding hyperactive-user sharing on all major parties' pages and arguing that platform policy enforcement lags behind stated community standards; [[Slack1783507532-sl10]] extends the paid-advertising/coordination nexus to the 2021 German federal election. Read together, these papers trace an arc from Italy-specific detection methods toward a more general claim: coordinated inauthentic amplification is a structural feature of contemporary European campaigns, not an Italian idiosyncrasy, and its measurement is perpetually chasing a moving, adversarially-adapting target.

## From Detection to Interpretation: Computational Pipelines and Their Validation

A third strand turns inward, interrogating the computational tools the program itself relies on. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] benchmarks OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo model for clustering political news shared before the 2018 and 2022 elections, finding the general-purpose LLM embedding consistently more semantically coherent — a result that unsettles assumptions about the necessity of language-specific fine-tuning. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] builds directly on this by documenting the full "LLMs-in-the-loop" pipeline (classification, embedding, cluster labeling) used across the 2018/2022 datasets, and reflects critically on what validating such pipelines even means once crowdsourced annotation is no longer a reliable ground truth, since LLMs now often outperform low-skilled human coders. [[Paci2025-ag]] pushes this reflexivity further by testing whether LLMs can actually *understand* Italian political discourse rather than merely classify or cluster it, showing that even strong models fall well short of expert human interpretation of implicatures and presuppositions in the IMPAQTS corpus — a sobering methodological check on the optimism of the embedding and pipeline papers. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]], applied to the German 2021 campaign, shows the same LLM-superiority pattern in the visual domain (GPT-4o outperforming specialized face-recognition and person-counting models), suggesting the program's Italian-honed methodological insights about generalist multimodal models generalize across languages and modalities. Taken as a set, this cluster narrates the program's gradual pivot from bespoke NLP pipelines toward LLM-mediated analysis, accompanied by an increasingly self-aware discourse on what "validation" must mean under this new paradigm.

## Platform Governance, Data Access, and the Post-API Predicament

A recurring institutional concern is that all of the above depends on contingent, unstable platform access. [[Philipp2026-tl]] surveys how the EU Digital Services Act has prompted platforms to (re)establish public data portals and APIs, but shows that eligibility and scope vary unevenly, reopening only partial avenues for election research. [[Schulte2026-df]] operationalizes this concern empirically for the 2025 German election, arguing — based on parallel X and TikTok scraping — that the central bottleneck in electoral discourse research is not too few studies but the absence of reusable, standardized research infrastructure, compounded by weak correlations across textual, vocal, and facial modalities that demand genuinely multimodal tooling. [[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]] again bridges this concern with substantive findings, treating the Meta Content Library simultaneously as an object of critique (transparency deficits in undisclosed policy changes) and as evidence that DSA-mandated access, however imperfect, enables research previously impossible. These three papers collectively narrate the program's transition from the CrowdTangle era to the DSA era, tracking how each new access regime reshapes not just what can be studied but how confidently causal or descriptive claims can be made.

## Beyond Italy: Comparative Extensions of the Parallelism Paradigm

Several papers use methods forged on Italian data to test whether political-parallelism dynamics are Italy-specific or structural features of European media systems generally. [[Rossi2023-847d5a9f]] extends Meta's URL Shares Dataset analysis across France, Germany, and Italy, finding that untrustworthy-URL shares spike specifically in election years in Italy and Germany, and that older users disproportionately drive both exposure and sharing — a finding that generalizes the age-skew and electoral-cycle sensitivity long observed in Italian data. [[Balluff2026-bv]] pursues a structurally analogous but distinct phenomenon in Austria — covert government-advertising-for-coverage arrangements ("Inseratenaffäre") — using difference-in-differences designs to show visibility capture without corresponding favorability gains, effectively documenting a "pay positive to go negative" mechanism of media capture even within a nominally democratic-corporatist system, echoing MINE's broader interest in how partisan media ecosystems can be quietly instrumentalized. [[Iris2026-pg]] scales the visibility-bias question to five countries around the 2024 European Parliament election, finding disproportionate Radical Right coverage relative to polling and electoral outcomes across most cases — a cross-national validation of the disproportionate-attention dynamics MINE first identified within Italian populist coverage. This comparative arc suggests the program's Italian case studies were never merely local: they function as a laboratory for mechanisms — insularity, capture, coordinated amplification, visibility asymmetry — that recur across polarized-pluralist and even ostensibly consolidated democratic-corporatist systems.

## Theorizing the Field and Its Objects

Two more conceptual contributions sit above the empirical clusters, periodically reorienting the whole research line. [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]] is an early programmatic essay arguing that big data has transformed political communication along three axes — blurred organizational boundaries, micro-targeted advertising, and attention-amplification strategies — and that these three vectors interact to defeat straightforward regulatory and methodological responses; it effectively pre-announces the coordination-detection and platform-governance concerns that dominate the program's later empirical output. [[Mosca2026-yh]] revisits the "fake news" construct itself, proposing a Strategic Delegitimization and Selective Amplification (SDSA) model that treats fake-news accusations as rhetorical weapons in negative campaigning rather than as symptoms of misinformation per se — a reframing that resonates with the program's consistent skepticism toward content-based (versus behavior- or actor-based) disinformation frameworks first articulated in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]]. Finally, [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends the coordination and amplification apparatus into the pandemic domain, showing how an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster and legacy journalists jointly remediate expert statements into Covid-skeptic narratives on Italian Facebook — demonstrating that the insularity/amplification/remediation triad diagnosed in electoral contexts operates identically in a non-electoral crisis, underscoring that MINE's real object of study is not elections per se but the durable structural logics of Italian (and, increasingly, European) hybrid media ecosystems under conditions of platformized political communication.
