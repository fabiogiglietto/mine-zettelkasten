# Health Misinformation and Public Debate

## From Coordination to Cognition: Tracing the Argument

The papers filed here trace an arc from *how* problematic health content circulates to *why* it lands with particular audiences, and finally to *what* it does to behaviour and health outcomes. The MINE-FACTS lineage — [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]] — anchors the collection at the production/circulation end, while work like [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] and [[Lyons2026-ca]] pulls toward the reception and downstream-effects end. Read together, they compose an argument that misinformation is neither purely a supply problem (bad actors, coordinated pushes) nor purely a demand problem (predisposed audiences), but a structured interaction between the two.

## Coordination as the Underlying Infrastructure

[[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] establishes the foundational move: Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) is a *content-agnostic* signal that lets fact-checkers surface problematic material faster than manual review, precisely because nefarious actors leave behavioural fingerprints (first-comment link placement, image-macro cross-posting, AdSense-linked blog networks, "link laundering" through reputable outlets). The Italian covid-skeptic network it maps is not sui generis but an offshoot of pre-existing partisan clusters — a point that reframes health misinformation as a repurposing of political coordination infrastructure.

[[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this by asking *who* the coordinated networks amplify, identifying an "Intellectual Dark Web" of micro-celebrities whose statements are laundered through legacy TV and newspaper remediation. [[Song2025-yh]] then generalises the CLSB approach comparatively, showing that coordination is *ideologically agnostic*: pro-vaccine institutional actors (NHS, CDC) coordinate too, and coordination's political valence depends on national context — safety framings in the UK, freedom framings in the US. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] converges on a similar picture from Twitter/X: a small, dense, contrarian coordinated retweet network (96.4% contrarian) disproportionately amplifies a narrow set of credentialed anti-consensus experts, and news coverage tends to *follow* superspreader activity rather than lead it — inverting the classical agenda-setting arrow.

## The Legacy-Media Hinge

A striking cross-paper finding is that mainstream media are neither innocent bystanders nor pure debunkers. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] shows that AIN narratives ride on remediated TV clips and right-leaning newspaper pages; [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] demonstrates that UK newspapers polarise systematically in *which scientists* they cite (Great Barrington vs. John Snow signatories), constituting a "political use of science" even when the scientists themselves are mainstream. [[Adam2026-tz]] finds, via web-tracking in Germany and Switzerland, that even quality mainstream outlets circulated substantial conspiracy-*supporting* content during the pandemic — though on average their exposure had a mitigating effect. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] closes the loop by showing that high-trust outlets align with conformist superspreaders and low-trust outlets with contrarian ones, so the "mainstream" itself bifurcates along superspreader lines.

## Demand, Predispositions, and the Concentration of Exposure

If coordination explains supply, a second cluster of papers explains the *selective uptake* of that supply. [[Adam2026-tz]] operationalises Zaller's information-plus-predispositions model, showing populism and political mistrust drive both selective engagement with conspiracy-supporting alternative media and counterarguing/backfire when populists encounter mainstream debunking. [[Lyons2026-ca]] provides the demographic complement: exposure to low-credibility health content is *rare in aggregate but wildly concentrated* — the top 10% of users account for 77% of exposure, disproportionately older adults with poorer discernment and conspiracist worldviews, arriving via referrals from *other* low-credibility sites rather than search or social platforms. Together these two studies suggest a habitual, cross-domain, predisposition-driven consumption profile rather than incidental exposure — reframing "the misinformation problem" as one about a small, structurally embedded audience segment.

[[Scalco2026-bd]] adds a structural counterpart on the supply side: *information voids*, operationalised as anomalous negative gaps between demand and supply, correlate with reduced high-credibility content and elevated misinformation. Voids explain *when* predisposed audiences are most vulnerable — vaccine rollouts, EMA authorisations, the AstraZeneca suspension — providing a temporal mechanism to complement the demographic and psychological ones.

## Closing the Loop: From Speech to Bodies

The final analytical step is the leap from exposure to behaviour and epidemic outcomes. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] undertakes this via a SIRVA compartmental model fit to county-level tweet and vaccination data, estimating that antivaccine Twitter content caused ~14,000 refusals, ~545 additional cases, and ~8 additional deaths in a six-month window — a *lower bound* that nonetheless closes the causal chain from platform-level speech to disease burden. Read against [[Adam2026-tz]] and [[Lyons2026-ca]], this magnitude is consistent with a concentrated-effect story: small predisposed subpopulations, catalysed at moments of information void ([[Scalco2026-bd]]) by remediated AIN content ([[Marino2023-9137f448]]) amplified by contrarian coordinated networks ([[Efstratiou2026-ij]], [[Song2025-yh]], [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]).

## Points of Divergence and Open Questions

The papers disagree productively on several points. On *bots and automation*: [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] finds coordination is human-driven, not automated, complicating older "bot detection" framings that [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] already sought to displace with behavioural signals. On *symmetry*: [[Song2025-yh]] insists CLSB is ideologically neutral, whereas [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] find asymmetric contrarian dominance in their contexts — suggesting the symmetry claim may be a scope condition of platform and country. On *legitimate disagreement vs. misinformation*: [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] pointedly refuses to code Great Barrington signatories as misinformation-producers, a boundary-drawing exercise that most of the other papers sidestep by anchoring on IFCN-fact-checked URLs or NewsGuard scores. And on *interventions*: [[Lyons2026-ca]] reports null effects for a media literacy treatment, while [[Adam2026-tz]] finds mainstream debunking backfires among populists — collectively casting doubt on generic corrective strategies and pointing toward the structural, behavioural, and audience-segmented approaches that MINE-FACTS-descended work has pursued.
