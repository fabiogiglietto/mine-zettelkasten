# Problematic Health Information Online

## Mapping the Contours of an "Infodemic"

A first cluster of work in this collection asks a deceptively basic question: how do we even detect and measure the circulation of low-quality health information at scale? [[Scalco2026-bd]] operationalizes "information voids" as anomalous imbalances between public demand (Wikipedia views, Google Trends) and reliable supply (Facebook, Twitter, GDELT), showing that these voids are not just theoretical constructs but empirically linked to drops in credible content and spikes in misinformation around vaccine-rollout events. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] approaches the supply side from a gatekeeping angle, tracing how Google News Italia curates the health agenda that subsequently propagates on Facebook, and finding that engagement is driven less by source authority than by emotional topicality (celebrity health stories outperform "medical science" in comments and shares). Together these two studies suggest that the pathology of the health information ecosystem is structural: gatekeepers and algorithms reward affect over accuracy, and this imbalance becomes visible and quantifiable as demand-supply "voids." [[Lyons2026-ca]] complicates the picture from the demand side, using passive web-tracking to show that exposure to low-credibility health content is in fact rare in the aggregate but highly concentrated — a small, older, more conspiracist-minded slice of the population accounts for a disproportionate share of exposure, and this exposure travels through referral chains among low-credibility sites rather than through search or social media referral. Read alongside Scalco et al., this suggests the "infodemic" is less a diffuse contagion sweeping the population than a bounded ecosystem sustained by supply-side voids and a concentrated, habitual audience.

## The Machinery of Coordination and Amplification

A second thread investigates how problematic content is not merely encountered but actively organized and amplified through coordinated network behavior. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] documents the emergence of an Italian covid-skeptic coordinated network on Facebook, using the CooRnet methodology to show how such networks evolve tactically — laundering links through reputable outlets, hiding URLs in first comments, exploiting religious and community groups — to escape platform detection, and demonstrates that content-agnostic coordination signals can outperform manual fact-checking in surfacing problematic material, directly motivating the "speed-up fact-checking" framing of the topic. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this network-mapping approach by focusing on *who* gets amplified within these covid-skeptic networks, identifying an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of influencers whose content is disproportionately shared (rather than commented on) and showing that legacy media and journalists remain surprisingly central nodes, recycling and "remediating" expert soundbites from television into conspiracy-adjacent Facebook ecosystems. [[Song2025-yh]] broadens the comparative lens to the UK and US, showing that coordinated link-sharing behavior is not inherently malicious but bifurcates by both stance and national context: anti-vaccine coordination is denser and safety-focused in the UK but liberty-focused in the US, while pro-vaccine coordination — equally coordinated, but institutionally grounded — dominates in the American case. Read together, these three papers reframe "misinformation" not as an emergent property of individual belief but as an artifact of network structure, platform affordances, and political culture, with coordination cutting both ways depending on who wields it.

## From Exposure to Belief to Behavioral Harm

A third line of inquiry pushes past detection and diffusion to ask what difference exposure actually makes. [[Adam2026-tz]] combines panel surveys with behavioral web-tracking in Germany and Switzerland to show that conspiracy belief formation is a "marriage" of media exposure and prior predispositions: alternative media exposure produces contagion effects, mainstream debunking produces mitigation effects, but populists selectively engage with the former and avoid — or counter-argue against — the latter, revealing why simple exposure-based interventions often fail to move the needle for those most at risk. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] pushes the causal chain further still, coupling a novel SIRVA epidemic model with geolocated antivaccine tweets to estimate that Twitter-borne antivaccine content caused thousands of vaccine refusals and measurable excess COVID-19 cases and deaths — one of the few studies in this set to trace a quantitative line from online speech to offline mortality. [[Lieu2025-nl]], working in the adjacent domain of climate misinformation, offers a complementary methodological lesson: when experimentally crossing rhetorical fallacy type with substantive claim content, it is *what* is claimed (particularly attacks on climate solutions) rather than *how* it is argued that drives perceived credibility and political polarization — a finding with clear implications for how health misinformation taxonomies should be built and prioritized for debunking.

## Countermeasures: Debunking, Inoculation, and Conversational AI

The final arc turns to intervention. [[Costello2024-bg]] shows that even entrenched conspiracy beliefs can be durably reduced — by about 20%, sustained for months and generalizing beyond the focal belief — when an LLM engages believers in personalized, evidence-responsive dialogue, directly challenging motivational accounts that treat such beliefs as impervious to counterevidence. [[Szabo2026-rd]] approaches the same conversational-AI affordance prophylactically rather than curatively, showing that chatbot-delivered "conversational inoculation" builds resistance to future misinformation more effectively than static reading or writing exercises, particularly once individual baseline susceptibility is accounted for, and pinpointing trust-building and adaptability as the active ingredients. Read against the diagnostic and network papers above, these two studies suggest a shift in the field's center of gravity: having established how problematic health information is produced, coordinated, and unevenly consumed, the frontier task is now designing interventions — whether rapid fact-checking infrastructure per [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] or personalized AI dialogue per [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Szabo2026-rd]] — that can match the adaptive, coordinated, and audience-specific nature of the problem they are meant to solve.
