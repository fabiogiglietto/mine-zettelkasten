# Health Misinformation & Fact-Checking

## Mapping Coordinated Networks: From Italy's Covid-Skeptic Clusters to Platform-Wide Ecosystems

The empirical core of this topic traces a lineage of work using Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour (CLSB) and related network-detection methods to surface "nefarious" actors before fact-checkers can catch up manually. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] is the anchor text here: it documents the MINE-FACTS prototype built on the CooRnet package, showing how an Italian covid-skeptic network emerged as an offshoot of pre-existing political coordination (League, Five Star Movement) and adapted its tactics — link laundering, first-comment link-hiding, infiltration of religious groups — to evade moderation. Crucially, the tool's one-month field test with Facta.news demonstrated that content-agnostic, behavioural signals (rather than content classification) can roughly double the hit-rate of problematic content relative to routine fact-checking, operationalising the Gates-funded promise of *accelerating* verification rather than replacing it. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this same Italian dataset forward in time to ask *who* gets amplified within these networks, identifying an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of alternative influencers whose share-heavy engagement signature (low comments/shares ratio) marks hyperpartisan amplification, and showing that legacy media and mainstream journalists remain the load-bearing nodes that launder covid-skeptic narratives into wider circulation — a hybrid-media-system argument that complicates any clean separation of "misinformation actors" from "the mainstream." [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] supplies a structurally related but earlier-stage account of gatekeeping, showing that Google News Italia's algorithmic aggregation already primes which health topics reach Facebook, and that emotionally charged "popular" categories (celebrity health, healthcare malfunction) generate disproportionate engagement independent of source credibility — a pre-pandemic baseline against which the pandemic-era coordination studies can be read as an intensification.

[[Song2025-yh]] internationalises and complicates the CLSB paradigm, comparing UK and US Facebook vaccine discourse and showing that coordination itself is ideologically agnostic — pro-vaccine communities coordinate just as densely as anti-vaccine ones, differing mainly in content credibility and national political framing (safety concerns in the UK, individual-freedom rhetoric in the US). This reframes coordination detection as a necessary but not sufficient signal: the Gates/MUR-funded project line's premise that coordination maps onto "nefariousness" requires content and context-sensitive qualification. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] pushes the mapping agenda into the Twitter-news pathway, finding a predominantly contrarian coordinated network that amplifies a narrow set of credentialed anti-consensus experts, and tracing how superspreader activity on social media systematically precedes rather than follows news coverage — bridging the platform-mapping literature with inter-media agenda-setting concerns raised already in [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]]. [[Scalco2026-bd]] offers a complementary macro-diagnostic: rather than tracking actors, it operationalises "information voids" as measurable demand-supply imbalances across platforms and search, showing that such voids are empirically linked to lower-credibility content circulation — giving the network-mapping tradition a quantitative early-warning counterpart.

## From Correlation to Causal Impact: Does Exposure Matter?

A second cluster interrogates what these networks and exposures actually *do* to beliefs and behaviour, pushing back against assumptions baked into the mapping tradition. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] is the most causally ambitious: a SIRVA compartmental epidemic model fit to county-level tweet geolocation, vaccination, and case data estimates that antivaccine Twitter content caused on the order of 14,000 vaccine refusals and several hundred additional cases — a rare attempt to connect platform speech to offline epidemiological outcomes. [[Allen2024-av]] delivers a more deflationary, ecosystem-scale verdict: combining survey experiments with crowdsourced persuasion scores and exposure data across 13,000 URLs, it shows that fact-check-flagged misinformation, while more persuasive per view, reached so few people that unflagged "vaccine-skeptical" content from mainstream outlets had a 46-fold greater aggregate effect on hesitancy — directly complicating the Giglietto/Marino emphasis on coordinated "nefarious" actors by relocating harm toward ostensibly legitimate media. [[Lyons2026-ca]] and [[Adam2026-tz]] both refine the exposure side of this debate using tracking data: Lyons shows low-credibility health content exposure is rare in aggregate but concentrated among older, conspiracist-leaning, politically dubious-news-consuming users, and crucially that search engines and social media are *not* the main referral pathway (habitual low-credibility-to-low-credibility browsing is); Adam, using German/Swiss panel-plus-tracking data, shows conspiracy belief formation depends jointly on alternative-media contagion and mainstream-media mitigation effects, mediated by populist selective exposure and counterarguing — a Zaller-style synthesis that explains why mainstream media exposure can sometimes backfire. Together these four papers reorient the topic's causal imagination away from "bad actors spreading lies" toward differentiated exposure ecologies, user predispositions, and the surprisingly large role of credible-seeming mainstream content.

## Beyond Claim-Level Fact-Checking: Institutions, Emotion, and Narrative

A third strand examines fact-checking itself as a contested professional and rhetorical practice rather than a neutral technology. [[Farkas2026-lr]] analyses how European fact-checkers justify financial dependence on Meta, Alphabet, and ByteDance, finding a repertoire of differentiation and transcendence strategies that mirror classical journalistic "wall" rhetoric — revealing that the MINE-style tool-building project (as in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]) sits within an institutional field whose legitimacy is itself argued for, not given. [[Xue2025-bp]] complicates the normative self-image of fact-checking by showing that fact-check posts themselves are frequently emotionally charged, suggesting that the genre's claim to dispassionate correction is strategically compromised by its own need for engagement — a finding that resonates with [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]]'s and [[Marino2023-9137f448]]'s observations that emotional/popular framing drives engagement regardless of source. [[Ramos2026-qo]], written from inside the fact-checking organisation Maldita.es, makes the most direct methodological argument for moving past claim-by-claim debunking: using the 2024 Valencia floods conspiracy as a case, the authors propose narrative monitoring and argument-checking as necessary complements, treating disinformation as an evolving, emotionally resonant narrative rather than a set of discrete false claims — effectively proposing the next generation of the tool-building agenda first operationalised in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]].

## Inoculation and Logic-Checking as Pre-emptive Alternatives

Running parallel to detection and correction is a prebunking line asking whether resistance can be built before exposure. [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] demonstrates at striking scale (375k Instagram users) that a 19-second inoculation ad against emotional manipulation produces a durable 21-point accuracy gain and increased information-seeking, establishing ecological validity for inoculation outside the lab. [[Szabo2026-rd]] pushes this into conversational AI territory, showing that an LLM-chatbot-delivered "Conversational Inoculation" outperforms static reading/writing inoculation once individual baseline susceptibility is controlled, with trust-building and adaptability identified as key mechanisms — a natural technological extension of the MINE line's tool-building ethos from detection (CooRnet) toward intervention. [[Lieu2025-nl]] tests a cognate idea in climate misinformation, crossing content taxonomy (CARDS) with fallacy taxonomy (FLICC) and finding that content category, not logical form, drives perceived veracity and political polarization — a result that directly supports Ramos et al.'s argument-checking proposal by showing where rhetorical vulnerabilities concentrate (attacks on solutions and scientists).

## Synthesis: An Arc from Mapping to Mechanism to Intervention

Read together, these sixteen papers trace a coherent arc for the MINE sub-project: beginning with content-agnostic network mapping of coordinated health misinformation (Giglietto, Marino, Ducci, Song, Efstratiou, Scalco), the line of inquiry increasingly questions whether coordination and low-credibility labelling are the right diagnostic targets at all, given causal evidence that mainstream, unflagged content often matters more (Allen, Bollenbacher) and that exposure is unevenly concentrated and mediated by predispositions rather than simple contagion (Lyons, Adam). This pushes the field toward institutional reflexivity about fact-checking's own legitimacy and emotional register (Farkas, Xue) and toward narrative- and argument-level correction strategies (Ramos) that anticipate claim mutation rather than chasing it. Finally, the inoculation cluster (van der Linden, Szabo, Lieu) proposes shifting resources upstream — building resistance before exposure rather than correcting after — closing the loop between the project's founding ambition (speeding up fact-checking) and an emerging recognition that detection and correction alone cannot keep pace with the narrative and institutional dynamics of post-truth health discourse.
