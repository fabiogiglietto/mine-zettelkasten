# Generative AI and Media Manipulation

## Generative AI and Media Manipulation

### From artifact to ecosystem: reframing the object of study

The papers filed here converge on a single methodological pivot: the shift from asking "is this fake?" to asking "how does this system of production, circulation, and interpretation work?" [[Emilio2026-ik]] gives this shift its most systematic articulation, arguing that the real risk of generative AI is not isolated deepfakes but "synthetic realities" — layered stacks of content, identity, interaction, and institutional harm that produce an "epistemic tax" as societies rationally discount all digital evidence. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] operationalizes a parallel move empirically, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category that deliberately abandons truth/falsity as the defining criterion, showing how a pro-Putin propaganda network, a synthetic gambling campaign, and a poorly moderated adult-content network share the same structural playbook — affordance exploitation plus simulated coordination — despite radically different actors and motives. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] extends this ecosystem framing to the platform level itself, arguing that the "social media" paradigm is dissolving into algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private micro-communities, and AI-mediated dyadic communication, with synthetic content ("Shrimp Jesus") as one symptom of platforms decoupling from human-generated circulation. [[Swartz2026-zb]] pushes this further into political economy, contending that scams — increasingly AI-accelerated — are not peripheral but constitutive of platform capitalism, with anti-fraud infrastructure itself becoming a governance and surveillance mechanism.

### The gambling case study: a template for AI-scaled persuasion

[[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] offers the most granular empirical anchor for these ecosystem claims, documenting a 13,000%+ post-volume increase in coordinated Facebook gambling promotion coinciding with ChatGPT's launch, and building a typology of persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localization) that generative AI intensifies and recombines rather than replaces. This case exemplifies the broader argument, echoed in [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]]'s policy brief on deepfake financial fraud, that the crucial vulnerability lies not in the sophistication of individual fakes but in the "regulatory grey zone" between paid and organic content, and in the diffuse chain of platforms, telecoms, and financial intermediaries that make scam ecosystems scalable. Both papers argue that liability and governance must target amplification infrastructure and gatekeepers, not just content authenticity — a claim that resonates with [[Bosch2024-hj]]'s account of TikTok's sonic affordances as a platform-specific propaganda vector during the Ukraine invasion, and with [[Hollingshead2026-vx]]'s finding that generative AI is being used for racist "speculative worldbuilding" even on platforms where popular immigration discourse skews pro-immigrant.

### Coordinated and agentic manipulation: from botnets to swarms

A cluster of papers pushes past single-artifact deception toward multi-agent coordination as the next threat frontier. [[Schroeder2026-im]] theorizes "malicious AI swarms" — LLM-agentic systems capable of persistent, adaptive, cross-platform manufactured consensus — as a qualitative leap beyond earlier botnets like the IRA's, and [[Orlando2025-ul]] provides simulation evidence supporting this trajectory: networked LLM agents in a generative agent-based model spontaneously develop denser networks, synchronized amplification, and narrative convergence, with mere "teammate awareness" nearly matching explicit deliberation. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]], though thinly documented, gestures at a complementary temporal dynamic — that manipulation campaigns persist by opportunistically appropriating emerging events. Together these papers suggest disinformation studies are moving from analyzing static content to modeling emergent, self-organizing systems, a shift that also motivates [[Beacken2026-zb]]'s comparative call to examine how GenAI propaganda is shaped unevenly by the political-economic structures of specific "democratically weakened" states rather than following one universal trajectory.

### Persuasion research: mechanisms, limits, and the accuracy trade-off

A second major thread — more experimental and less alarmist — interrogates *how* and *how much* conversational AI actually persuades. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] establishes the foundational finding that post-training and prompting, not scale or personalization, drive persuasiveness, and that information density is the operative mechanism — but that the same levers trade off against factual accuracy. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] escalates the stakes by pitting AI against elite human debaters and professional canvassers, finding AI wins even after human coaching, and that constraining AI to human throughput eliminates its edge — confirming fact-density, not rapport, as the causal engine. [[Lin2025-xp]] corroborates this across four real elections, showing AI persuades chiefly through evidence rather than manipulation tactics, though right-leaning advocacy conditions produce systematically less accurate claims — an asymmetry that surfaces again in [[DeVerna2025-dl]]'s finding that search-enabled fact-checking LLMs disproportionately cite left-leaning credible sources. [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]] extend the persuasion-for-good angle: durable, personalized AI dialogue can shift entrenched conspiracy beliefs and skepticism on climate, vaccines, and inequality, with effects concentrated among the hardest-to-reach skeptics — complicating the assumption that manipulation and correction are technically distinct enterprises. [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] complicates this optimism further, showing that mere perception of political bias in the model — regardless of actual content — cuts persuasive power by up to 28%, implying that perceived neutrality is itself a persuasion lever alongside information density and interactivity.

### Deepfakes, modality, and situated interpretation

Running counter to techno-deterministic fears, several papers show that the *format* of synthetic deception matters less than assumed, and that audience interpretation is socially mediated rather than fixed at the point of production. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] finds AI-generated images and video do not universally outperform text as disinformation, with decontextualized "cheap" video only besting other modalities on highly salient, image-rich topics like the Ukraine war — suggesting low-tech recontextualization can rival generative AI's supposed advantage. [[Wack2026-bt]]'s Kenyan field experiment reinforces this social-construction thesis directly: a deepfake's persuasive damage depends heavily on accompanying comments, with credulous framing amplifying harm and skeptical framing restoring support, moderated by partisanship — deepfakes as "flexibly deceptive cues" rather than self-contained artifacts. [[Choi2026-bz]] adds a psychological mechanism, showing that difficulty authenticating one deepfake carries over, in a modality-congruent way, to affect confidence and susceptibility toward subsequent fakes of the same format.

### Trust, epistemology, and the changing status of "facts"

A more theoretical cluster asks what generative AI does to the underlying category of factuality and trust. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] proposes "emergent facts" as a new epistemic category for AI outputs that are plausible but unanchored in verified provenance, arguing traditional evidence-based, interpretative, and rule-based frameworks all fail to capture LLM-generated content. [[Suk2026-ai]] situates this within the longer arc of media-trust scholarship, calling for a multilevel framework analogous to trust research on mass and social media. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] offers a deflationary counterpoint from conversation analysis and Luhmannian systems theory: chatbots appear competent not because they understand but because they parasitically depend on users' repair work — a claim that quietly undercuts both hype about AI manipulation and hope about AI-driven correction, since neither presupposes genuine comprehension. [[Gilardi2026-hw]] tests audience-facing consequences of this ambiguity empirically, finding that readers cannot distinguish AI-generated from human-written news quality, and that disclosure boosts short-term engagement without shifting durable trust — a decoupling of message-level and source-level credibility. [[Dubey2026-bl]] complicates assumptions about who trusts AI-mediated information, finding that users with *higher* conspiracy belief paradoxically trust "balanced" news chatbots more than low-conspiracy users, challenging distrust-based models of conspiratorial cognition.

### Structural and infrastructural bias in the models themselves

Several papers turn manipulation analysis inward, toward the training and deployment pipeline as itself a site of political influence. [[Waight2026-ts]] demonstrates that state-controlled media is memorized into LLM training corpora and detectably shifts model outputs toward pro-regime valence when queried in low-media-freedom languages — a mechanism of institutional influence operating beneath the level of any deliberate "bias." [[Triedman2025-uy]] documents a more overt case: Grokipedia's derivative-but-rewritten relationship to Wikipedia, showing systematically higher rates of unreliable and blacklisted citations, especially on controversial and political topics, alongside a novel "auto-citogenesis" loop citing Grok/X posts as sources. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] rounds out this thread by analyzing how news media themselves frame LLMs with mentalistic-agentic language, shaping public epistemology of AI capability independent of any technical reality — the media system as another layer of the "synthetic reality" stack.

### Governance, labor, and countermeasures

Countermeasure research bifurcates between individual-level psychological interventions and institutional/infrastructural governance. On the individual side, [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] shows inoculation against emotional manipulation scales cheaply and durably via Instagram ads, while [[Szabo2026-rd]]'s "Conversational Inoculation" chatbot outperforms static reading/writing refutation by adapting to user attitudes — both extending classic inoculation theory into generative-AI-mediated delivery. On the institutional side, [[Goldberg2026-eb]] surveys AI-enabled possibilities for more deliberative digital public squares (bridging systems, collective dialogue tools, community moderation support) while cautioning that synthetic participation is democratically fraught. [[Gillespie2026-aa]] and [[Unknown2025-qj]] both interrogate red-teaming — the frontline safety practice meant to catch manipulative and harmful outputs before release — as a sociotechnical practice shaped by opaque value judgments, outsourced and often traumatic labor, and contested public-interest framing, drawing explicit parallels to the earlier history of commercial content moderation. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extends verification infrastructure to an adjacent domain, scientific peer review, showing agentic AI review tools can detect technical errors at scale — a rare case where AI is deployed as an epistemic corrective rather than a manipulation vector, though one that raises its own automation-governance questions echoed in the red-teaming taxonomy debates.

### Open threads

Underlying nearly all these papers is an unresolved tension between two pictures of generative AI's societal effect: an *escalation* narrative (swarms, synthetic realities, industrial-scale gambling promotion, state-laundered propaganda) and a *continuity* narrative (low-tech decontextualization rivaling deepfakes, persuasion driven by familiar fact-density rather than novel manipulation tactics, chatbot "competence" being parasitic rather than emergent). The [[UnknownUnknown-db]] survey of Claude users, oriented toward economic displacement rather than disinformation per se, is a reminder that the manipulation question sits inside a much larger reorganization of trust, labor, and epistemic authority that generative AI is producing — one the "IA Generativa e Media" course and this note's papers are only beginning to map systematically.
