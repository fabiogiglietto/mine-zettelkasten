# Information Disorder & Disinformation Narratives

## From "Fake News" to Narrative: Conceptual Foundations

The topic's deep background is a definitional struggle. [[Allcott2017-yz]] and [[Lazer2018-mm]] established the post-2016 baseline—fake news as fabricated content mimicking journalistic form—while flagging how thin the evidentiary base was. [[Freelon2020-yp]] argued for abandoning "fake news" altogether in favor of "disinformation," defined by deception, harm, and intent, a move [[Bennett2018-ys]] radicalized further by recasting the phenomenon as a systemic "disinformation order" rooted in institutional legitimacy crisis rather than isolated falsehoods. [[Chadwick2013-ns]] and [[Chadwick2015-ii]] supplied the structural vocabulary—the hybrid media system, political information cycles, media logics—that nearly every subsequent paper in this collection inherits, even when contesting it. [[Benkler2018-lw]] then empirically grounded the "disinformation order" thesis, showing an asymmetric, radicalized right-wing media ecosystem rather than technology or Russia as the primary driver of American epistemic dysfunction—a claim later echoed and extended by [[Tornberg2025-ir]], who finds radical-right populism (not populism or right-ideology alone) statistically predicts elite misinformation sharing across 26 countries.

A decisive conceptual pivot within the corpus is [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]]'s cybernetics-inspired argument that "fake news" is the invention of a liar: intent-centered, creator-focused typologies cannot capture how falsity actually circulates through chains of propagator judgment in a hybrid system. This propagation-centered view anticipates the collection's strongest throughline—the shift from classifying content to tracing narratives—developed most fully by [[Marwick2026-qd]], [[Sadler2025-vu]], and [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]], and is echoed in [[Dierickx2026-tw]]'s proposal of "emergent facts" to describe AI-generated content that resists classical evidentiary, interpretive, or institutional fact-categories altogether.

## Prevalence, Scale, and the "How Big Is This Really?" Debate

A persistent counter-current questions whether misinformation panic is proportionate to its actual reach. [[Allen2020-nj]] situates fake news as 0.15% of Americans' total media diet; [[Grinberg2019-ua]], [[Guess2019-ym]], and [[Guess2020-rr]] independently show extreme concentration—fake news exposure and sharing dominated by a tiny, older, highly conservative minority—a finding stabilized across platforms by [[Guess2021-ym]] and extended to Europe by [[Rossi2023-847d5a9f]], which tracks a divergence between rising *shares* of untrustworthy URLs and flat *views* in Germany, France, and Italy. [[Allcott2019-gn]] documents a relative post-2016 decline on Facebook even as Twitter's volume held steady, while [[Bergeron-Boutin2026-yh]] more recently finds concentrated but attitudinally inert exposure on Facebook/Instagram. [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Mosleh2024-op]] push this descriptive tradition toward platform comparison and diffusion-tree analysis, and [[Vincent_undated-re]] extends the monitoring logic into an explicitly regulatory register—TikTok's rising mis/disinformation share under EU Digital Services Act-adjacent measurement regimes.

This "it's smaller than you think" literature is directly challenged by two methodological interventions. [[Yang2023-cg]] shows that link-based measures massively undercount visual misinformation on Facebook (20%+ of political images), while [[Dahlke2026-sl34]] argues the entire "direct referrer" paradigm underestimates platforms' causal role by ignoring indirect, delayed exposure pathways—estimating effects 1.5 to 15× larger once embedded referral is modeled. [[Goel2025-iq]] delivers a parallel corrective at the narrative level: source-based reliability lists miss how *true* mainstream content is repurposed to support misleading narratives, a mechanism also central to [[Mosca2026-yh]]'s account of "fake news" as a rhetorical weapon and [[Yoo2026-ev]]'s finding that the term itself functions as an empty, weaponized label in hyperpartisan outlets.

## Diffusion Dynamics: Why and How Falsehoods Travel

Classic diffusion studies—[[Vosoughi2018-at]]'s finding that falsehood spreads farther, faster, and deeper than truth (driven by novelty, not bots), and [[Del-Vicario2016-uj]]'s percolation model of echo-chamber-bounded cascades—establish the mechanics that later narrative-centric work complicates. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] moves from correlation to a causally-modeled epidemiological link between antivaccine Twitter exposure and reduced vaccination (SIRVA model), while [[Altoe2026-wu]] operationalizes the temporal urgency problem computationally, building a streaming pipeline to predict narrative virality before fact-checks (median 4.5 days) can catch up to narrative peaks (median 1.5 days). [[Scalco2026-bd]] reframes the supply side of this dynamic through "information voids"—periods where demand outstrips credible supply—showing these voids are empirically linked to lower-credibility content and spikes around events like EMA vaccine authorizations.

## The Narrative Turn: PROMPT's Core Contribution

A cluster of recent papers—evidently central to the PROMPT project—explicitly theorizes disinformation as narrative rather than discrete claim. [[Marwick2026-qd]] shows disinformation functions as cross-platform, identity-affirming, cathartic *story*, signaled by catchphrases rather than propositions. [[Sadler2025-vu]] supplies the hermeneutic-realist philosophical apparatus (Ricoeur, Phelan) needed to evaluate such stories ethically and referentially without collapsing into true/false binaries. [[Prochaska2025-ef]] and [[Starbird2025-jj]] apply this lens empirically to US election rumors, showing that *how* a story is told—style, allusion, cueing—constitutes meaning as much as content, and that influencers often supply only evidence, relying on audiences to supply the interpretive frame ("call and response" rumoring). [[Prochaska2026-pr]] formalizes this into a "grammar" of election rumors (Actor–Object–Action, plus Scene/Setting and Remedies) recurring across 2020 and 2022. [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]] tests the narrative-exposure mechanism directly via survey, finding cumulative cross-outlet exposure to a narrative increases belief independent of single-claim exposure. [[Waight2025-al]] supplies the methodological infrastructure this agenda needs—an LLM-based pipeline for measuring true narrative similarity (not lexical or topical similarity) across languages, demonstrated on the Ukraine "biolabs" story. [[Ramos2026-qo]] and [[Rohrbach2026-rc]] bring this narrative framing into practice and journalism studies respectively: Maldita.es's "narrative monitoring and argument-checking" response to the Valencia floods conspiracy, and an inquiry into how journalistic strategies themselves can seed new conspiracy beliefs. [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE/t-CANE framework operationalizes cross-platform narrative migration computationally, identifying a tiny "bridge user" stratum that seeds most Truth Social-to-X narrative flows.

## Elites, Parties, and the Weaponization of Truth-Claims

A substantial strand examines political elites not as misinformation's targets but its agents and contested arbiters. [[Rodarte2026-dk]] shows Brazilian parliamentarians competing for "epistemic authority" during the Manaus COVID collapse via three distinct legitimation strategies. [[Gaber2022-bk]] theorizes "strategic lying" in Brexit/2019 UK campaigning as an intensification of PR spin where rebuttal paradoxically amplifies the lie. [[Osmundsen2021-et]] finds partisan animus, not ignorance, drives individual fake-news sharing; [[Tai2026-qk]] and [[Tornberg2025-ir]] extend this to institutional/ideological checks on elected officials and party-family effects respectively. [[Renault2025-uh]] uses Community Notes data to show Republicans are flagged 2.3× more than Democrats even under crowdsourced, bias-resistant evaluation—directly undercutting Musk/Zuckerberg's stated rationale for abandoning professional fact-checking, a policy shift also scrutinized by [[Holt2026-zq]] and [[Rogers2026-zz]], who track Meta's successive redefinition of "problematic content" (fake news → false news → misinformation → borderline) and its progressive depoliticization despite persistently right-skewed engagement. [[Van_Erkel2026-mk]] identifies a "hostile misinformation effect" (an analog to the hostile media effect) whereby partisans perceive their own side as disproportionately targeted by misinformation; [[Karlsson2026-hd]] and [[Berlinski2023-rj]] show that narratives *about* interference and fraud themselves degrade trust and polarize—interference discourse, not just interference itself, causes democratic harm. [[Rossini2026-jn]] and [[Rossini2026-mj]] trace Brazilian analogues, with belief in electoral misinformation eroding both tolerance and trust in results, moderated by messaging-app use.

## Platforms, Moderation, and the Infrastructure of Disorder

[[Donovan2025-ws]] offers the field's clearest historical account of "misinformation-at-scale" as a built-in feature, not a bug, of engagement-driven platforms, tracing platforms' retreat from behavioral moderation after 2021. [[Katzenbach2026-sl2e]] charts a parallel three-phase arc in election-policy development (protective 2018–20, civic-integrity 2020–22, rollback since 2022) across five platforms. [[Kuznetsova2026-hw]] extends this governance lens to climate misinformation, finding platforms substitute performative "soft" interventions and discursive responsibility-shifting for enforceable rules. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] and [[Thiele2025-ol]] attack the conceptual incoherence underlying all this moderation: existing "coordinated inauthentic behavior" definitions conflate inauthenticity with harm and resist generalizable, statistically grounded detection, a gap [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] fills empirically by showing structurally similar "Deceptive Information Operations" can be driven by propaganda, gambling, or lax moderation alike. [[Jaidka2026-sl34]] offers a structural-intervention counterpoint to individual-level fixes, showing that blocking elite visibility in X feeds reduces misinformation susceptibility without denting political engagement—implicitly validating Benkler et al.'s elite-centered diagnosis over purely cognitive accounts.

## Coordinated Behavior, Influence Operations, and State Propaganda

[[Starbird2019-qv]] and [[Keller2019-nk]] anchor this strand: disinformation as *collaborative work* performed by crowds (not just bots), and astroturfing detectable through coordination traces rather than bot-like individual signatures—South Korea's NIS campaign showing that even well-resourced coordination achieved limited measurable impact. [[Kansaon2025-id]], [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]], and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] extend coordination detection to WhatsApp in Brazil, Telegram in Russia/Belarus, and multilingual Twitter around China's 2022 protests respectively, showing dual-use infrastructures enabling both mobilization and repression. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT analysis of IRA employee CVs reframes troll farms as ordinary media-labor-market actors rather than exotic covert units, timed to Crimea rather than the 2016 US election. [[Gaw2025-ru]] theorizes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage; [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as a bot-assisted narrative-substitution tactic; [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] notes campaigns persist by appropriating emerging events. [[Graham2025-gp]] and [[Rogers2026-cy]] both center on propaganda's parasitic relationship to trust infrastructure—#IStandWithPutin exploiting verification affordances, and "post-truth spaces" mapped via network proximity to mainstream discourse on Moldovan Facebook.

## War, Geopolitics, and State-Aligned Narratives

The Russia-Ukraine war anchors several narrative-tracking studies: [[Waight2025-al]]'s biolabs case, [[Sadler2025-vu]]'s narratological reading of NATO-blame tweets, [[Bosch2024-hj]]'s discovery of sound as an under-studied TikTok propaganda vector, and [[Makeev2026-ma]]'s dual-audience model of Russian state propaganda (domestic legitimacy vs. foreign resolve-undermining). [[Hameleers2026-mc]] complicates assumptions about visual/AI disinformation's unique potency by showing decontextualized video only outperforms text for Ukraine specifically—where authentic footage is abundant—suggesting generative-AI alarm may be overstated relative to low-tech recontextualization.

## Platform Ecologies: TikTok, Instagram, Telegram, WhatsApp

Distinct platform affordances recur as explanatory variables. [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] finds TikTok's Canadian immigration discourse skews pro-immigration, contra Twitter-based priors, while sharing affordances symmetrically across ideological camps. [[Marwick2025-ov]] documents "ConspiracyTok" as populist, demographically diverse knowledge production under a "generous epistemology." [[Nangle2026-yo]] and [[Gardam2025-er]] examine Instagram's affective-visual softening of far-right and climate-denial content respectively, arguing visual-first methods are necessary where text-centric analysis fails. [[Righetti2025-slf9]] traces CitizenGO's centrally-coordinated, Change.org-mimicking cross-platform anti-gender network. [[Kansaon2025-id]] and [[Ventura2025-sw]] push the field past "feed-based" platform bias toward WhatsApp's encrypted, closed ecosystem in Brazil.

## Mainstreaming the Far Right and Conspiracy Culture

[[Rothut2026-or]] introduces "protest-facilitated mainstreaming," showing Querdenken's Telegram network became structurally embedded in far-right/conspiracist milieus via anti-elite framing bridges, complementing [[Rothut2026-wt]]'s 25-year scoping review arguing online/offline radicalization can no longer be meaningfully separated. [[Klinger2023-qu]] shows how fringe Identitarian content gets "laundered" through intermediary AfD-aligned networks into mainstream party communication without formal coordination. [[Tuters2026-vo]] theorizes the "Memeplex": conspiratorial alignment around the Great Reset emerges from platform-grammar-driven adjacency and modular reuse *before* coherent narrative forms—an infrastructural rather than belief-centered account that dovetails with [[Marwick2025-vx]]'s finding that "redpilling" is typically a gradual socialization process, not sudden conversion, with disinformation functioning as "evidence" legitimizing extremist self-image. [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Iris2026-pg]] document institutional-level mainstreaming: Sky News Australia's transformation into transnational right-wing network propaganda, and systematic radical-right overrepresentation in 2024 European election coverage across the political spectrum of outlets.

## Health Misinformation and the COVID Infodemic

[[Adam2026-tz]] models conspiracy belief formation as a "marriage" of media exposure and predispositions, finding both contagion (alternative media) and mitigation (mainstream media) effects, with populists selectively engaging/avoiding each. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces a largely human-driven (non-bot) coordinated network amplifying contrarian credentialed experts in COVID science discourse, with news coverage following rather than leading superspreader activity. [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] shows UK newspaper polarization in which scientists are cited, revealing political "use" of legitimate scientific disagreement rather than fringe misinformation per se. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] and [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] (on chemtrails and 15-minute cities) extend the infrastructure argument—antivaccine Twitter content and conspiratorial search-query framing both materially reshape what information users encounter, with Google's AI Overviews proving unevenly effective at debunking newer conspiracies.

## Generative AI: New Epistemics, New Threats

This emerging subcluster interrogates AI's effect on both production and verification of falsehood. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] and [[Dodds2026-df]] theorize, respectively, "emergent facts" as a new epistemic category and "AI hype" as a structuring journalistic infrastructure. [[Choi2026-bz]] identifies a "modality-congruent carryover effect" in deepfake authentication difficulty; [[Wack2026-bt]] shows deepfake impact in Kenya is socially negotiated through audience comments rather than fixed at production. [[Triedman2025-uy]] empirically audits Grokipedia against Wikipedia, finding heavy derivation but systematically more citations to blacklisted/unreliable sources, especially on controversial topics. [[Topinka2026-hb]] theorizes "slopaganda" as affective lure rather than belief-manipulating propaganda, with AI alignment itself optimized for "rhetorical attunement." [[Beacken2026-zb]] and [[Wang2025-zy]] situate generative AI propaganda and AI imaginaries comparatively across democratically weakened and culturally distinct national contexts. [[Paci2025-ag]] and [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] interrogate LLMs' own analytic capacities—pragmatic competence for implicit political discourse, and LLMs as collaborative codebook-refinement tools rather than classifiers—while [[Arora2025-tx]] extends automated framing analysis into the multimodal register.

## Interventions, Audiences, and Cross-National Variation

Closing the loop, [[Lewandowsky2012-vn]] remains the foundational psychological account of why corrections fail (continued influence effect) and what debiasing requires (alternative causal narratives, pre-exposure warnings). [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] demonstrates prebunking can scale cheaply and durably on Instagram, while [[Xue2025-bp]] interrogates fact-checking's own emotional strategies. [[Hourigan2026-oc]] and [[Aharoni2026-sl6f]] push toward audience-centered, "everyday" misinformation research—showing lay users define news and misinformation far more broadly (and differently across Brazil, India, UK, US) than scholarly topic-driven frameworks assume, a critique [[Humprecht2025-ml]] and [[Humprecht2020-gd]] generalize into a call for de-WEIRDing misinformation research, substantiated empirically by [[Nenno2025-xa]]'s 24-country news-values comparison and [[Heft2021-ky]]'s finding that transnational right-wing hyperlink networks still orient toward legacy mainstream media rather than forming insulated spheres. [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] and [[Balluff2026-ev]] round out the picture from marketing and entity-network perspectives, respectively showing influencer-source toxicity dynamics and surprising entity-level overlap between alternative and legacy media.
