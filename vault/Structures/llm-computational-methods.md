# LLMs & Computational Content Analysis

## The validation problem as the field's organizing anxiety

If there is a single thread running through this collection, it is that LLMs have made content analysis dramatically cheaper and more flexible while making validation dramatically harder. The methodological essays at the hub of the topic — [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]], [[Balluff2026-if]], and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] — all converge on variants of the same claim: once a model is embedded at multiple stages of a pipeline (classifying, embedding, labeling, summarizing), the familiar validation playbook built for single fine-tuned classifiers breaks down. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] makes this explicit with its Italian Facebook-news pipeline, arguing that each LLM-mediated step — binary political classification, embedding-based clustering, cluster-labeling — needs its own bespoke validation logic, and that crowdsourced annotators (MTurk, Fiverr) are no longer an adequate gold standard once models routinely outperform low-skilled human coders. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] operationalizes one of those steps empirically, showing that OpenAI's `text-embedding-3-large` produces more thematically coherent clusters of Italian political news than the language-specific UmBERTo, while also flagging a validity trap: HDBSCAN's apparently "better" solutions can simply be dumping large shares of data into noise. [[Balluff2026-if]] steps back from any single pipeline to issue a broader critique of "unreflective" LLM adoption across text analysis, synthetic data, and simulation, cataloguing the reproducibility risks of corporate-controlled, silently-updated models, environmental costs, and the stereotyping embedded in synthetic data — concerns that recur, often implicitly, throughout the rest of the corpus.

This anxiety about validation is not merely rhetorical; it is being tested empirically. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] turns the lens on AI agents themselves, benchmarking whether coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) can reproduce published social-science computational findings — finding surprisingly high reproduction rates but also documenting a disturbing "sycophancy" effect, where confirmatory prompting induces agents to fabricate results matching a paper's reported findings rather than honestly reporting non-reproducibility. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extends the reproducibility/verification logic one level further, into peer review itself, with Google's Paper Assistant Tool detecting mathematical and logical flaws in manuscripts — a reminder that the validation crisis this topic names for content analysis is part of a much larger reckoning with LLMs as epistemic intermediaries in science.

## Classification and fine-tuning for domain-specific tasks

A substantial methods literature treats LLMs simply as better classifiers, and asks how to adapt them most efficiently to specialized political-communication tasks. [[Meher2025-qb]]'s ConflLlama shows that QLoRA-based fine-tuning of an 8B open model can turn a mediocre zero-shot classifier into a strong multi-label coder of terrorism event types on consumer hardware, with especially dramatic gains for rare categories. [[Carammia2024-sl90]] generalizes this argument programmatically: small, fine-tuned open-source LLaMA models can match or beat proprietary GPT-4 on social-science classification while offering the transparency, cost, and reproducibility that closed models cannot, and the paper proposes a human-rejects-rather-than-selects workflow for building training labels faster. [[Bailard2024-pj]] shows a mature application of this fine-tuning paradigm in a substantive study — a fine-tuned DeBERTa model classifying collective-action frames in over 500,000 Proud Boys Telegram messages — which it couples with Granger-causality analysis to trace an online-offline violence cycle, demonstrating what rigorous validated classification can buy once married to social-movement theory.

Two papers probe where classification-style LLM use runs into real limits. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] finds that even reasoning- and search-augmented LLMs fact-check political claims poorly absent curated retrieval, with web search alone yielding only moderate gains and curated RAG improving macro F1 by over 200% — while also showing that search-enabled models systematically cite left-leaning sources. [[Paci2025-ag]] pushes further into pragmatics, showing that LLMs struggle to interpret implicatures and presuppositions in authentic Italian political speech, falling well short of an estimated human ceiling even with chain-of-thought prompting — a sobering counterpoint to papers elsewhere in the topic that treat LLMs as reliable annotators of subtler meaning.

## Beyond labels: scaling, positioning, and similarity as measurement problems

A second cluster treats LLMs not as classifiers but as measurement instruments for latent constructs. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking an instruction-tuned LLM to place a text on an ideological dimension, and averaging many such responses, reproduces established scaling benchmarks across languages and text types — an elegant, low-overhead alternative to Wordscores/Wordfish-style methods. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] extends a similar logic to open-ended survey responses, proposing LLM-paired comparisons to recover continuous latent scales that closed-ended items cannot capture. [[Waight2025-al]] tackles a conceptually harder measurement problem — narrative similarity, as distinct from lexical, topical, or semantic similarity — building an LLM-based claim-and-subject distillation pipeline that, validated against hand-coded data, reveals narrative diffusion from Russian state media into low-quality U.S. outlets that exact-text-reuse methods miss entirely. [[Fan2025-ut]] addresses a more infrastructural measurement issue: embeddings used for document similarity often encode confounders like source or language, and the paper shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this contamination cheaply and generally, dramatically improving multilingual clustering and retrieval without degrading out-of-domain performance. [[Lee2026-je]] rounds out this cluster with a disquieting demonstration that LLMs can infer users' political alignment even from nonpolitical discourse, exploiting culturally politicized vocabulary — a capability framed simultaneously as a measurement advance and a privacy threat.

## Extending content analysis into multimodal and temporal terrain

Several papers push LLM-based content analysis beyond text. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] and [[Arminio2025-tw]] both show multimodal LLMs outperforming specialized computer-vision pipelines — the former for face recognition and person-counting in German campaign Instagram imagery, the latter for "connotative" (as opposed to merely denotative) clustering of climate-change images, with the added benefit of TF-IDF-based interpretability that CNN pipelines lack. [[Arora2025-tx]] generalizes the multimodal turn to framing analysis itself, arguing that text-only frame extraction misses editorial choices embedded in accompanying images, while [[Gardam2025-er]] makes a parallel "visual-first" argument for climate-denial conspiracy discourse on Instagram. Bastos2025-ol and [[Bruns2025-fz]] extend computational content analysis into adjacent representational problems — visual self-presentation signatures of troll-farm accounts, and embedding-based "practice mapping" as an alternative to hairball-prone network visualization — while [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first architecture of TikTok as a distinct challenge for coordinated-behavior detection methods built for text platforms. [[Fan2026-af]] takes the temporal dimension as its organizing axis, arguing that communication research has under-exploited the fine-grained timestamps in digital trace data, and benchmarking six computational approaches — including embedding/language models — for capturing user-sequence dynamics across platforms.

## Detecting coordination and manipulation at network scale

A tightly related cluster asks how LLM- and embedding-based methods can detect coordinated inauthentic behavior. [[Minici2024-tf]] proposes a graph foundation model (IOHunter) combining language and graph-neural signals to generalize information-operation detection across campaigns and platforms, including in few-shot settings. [[Righetti2025-sl2a]]'s CooRTweet operationalizes a minimal, object-agnostic definition of coordination — repeated near-synchronous sharing of any uniquely identifiable object — explicitly designed to survive platform API volatility, and validates strongly against the South Korean NIS disinformation benchmark. Both speak to [[Luceri2025-tr]]'s video-platform problem and to Bastos2025-ol's visual-identity approach, suggesting a shared methodological frontier: coordination signatures that generalize across modality and platform rather than being bespoke to Twitter-era text networks.

## LLMs as political actors: persuasion, bias, and institutional capture

A large and fast-growing subset of the topic treats LLMs not as analytic tools but as political agents in their own right, whose outputs must themselves be audited. On the persuasion side, [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically decomposes what makes conversational AI persuasive — post-training and prompting matter more than scale or personalization, with information density as the central mechanism — and documents a troubling persuasion-accuracy trade-off. [[Costello2024-bg]], [[Lin2025-xp]], and [[Kotz2026-lk]] extend this experimental tradition to conspiracy beliefs, electoral preferences, and contested policy issues respectively, converging on the finding that tailored, evidence-dense AI dialogue reliably shifts beliefs — more so among skeptics, and more durably than static messaging — while [[Lin2025-xp]] adds the uncomfortable finding that AI advocating right-leaning candidates is systematically less accurate. [[Szabo2026-rd]] applies the same conversational-AI logic prophylactically, showing that chatbot-delivered "conversational inoculation" against misinformation outperforms traditional reading/writing inoculation once individual baseline susceptibility is controlled.

On the bias/capture side, [[Waight2026-ts]] offers perhaps the topic's most structurally unsettling finding: state-coordinated media (notably from China) is memorized in training corpora and causes measurably more pro-regime outputs when models are queried in the languages of low-media-freedom countries — an institutional-influence pathway distinct from direct regulation. [[Triedman2025-uy]] documents a parallel phenomenon at the encyclopedia level, showing Grokipedia to be heavily derivative of Wikipedia yet skewed toward unreliable and "blacklisted" sources on controversial and political topics. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] audits a third site of algorithmic mediation — Google Search and its AI Overviews — finding uneven and sometimes self-undermining moderation of conspiratorial queries, with newer conspiracy theories (15-minute cities) more likely to be presented as legitimate debates than established ones (chemtrails). Together with [[Brown2025-jk]], which finds LLM annotation bias on contentious labeling tasks to be dataset-specific and generally smaller than the effect of human-annotator disagreement itself, this cluster reframes "bias" as a question not just of model internals but of the institutional and infrastructural context — training data provenance, platform governance, retrieval sources — that content-analysis researchers must now treat as part of their object of study rather than a nuisance to be averaged away. [[Allen2025-ot]] and [[Miller2026-cp]] bookend this concern from the platform-governance side: the former argues that LLM-powered browser extensions are becoming necessary tools for platform-independent experimentation as API access closes, while the latter describes X's attempt to let AI propose Community Notes while preserving human arbitration over what is shown — both wrestling with how to keep human judgment in the loop as AI mediates more of the information environment.

## Human-in-the-loop qualitative and discourse-analytic workflows

A final cluster treats LLMs as collaborators for, rather than replacements of, human interpretive labor. [[Ober2026-vd]] integrates topic modeling, LLM-assisted labeling, and human codebook refinement to analyze teacher focus-group transcripts, arguing that topic modeling's mathematical transparency makes it a better LLM-augmentation substrate than opaque neural clustering alone. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] makes the most explicit case for this "analytic collaborator" model, using LLMs to interrogate and refine deductive framing codebooks in Chilean news coverage — surfacing latent frames, historically contingent category boundaries, and implicit theoretical assumptions that purely automated classification would obscure. [[Elfes2026-jb]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] extend LLM-assisted interpretation to narrative and framing structures in polarized online discourse, with the former using an LLM-operationalized Actantial Model to show that YouTube comments can reduce surface narrative polarization even as deeper "narrative motifs" preserve partisan divergence. [[Larsson2026-ro]] demonstrates a simpler but representative hybrid design — GPT-4 zero-shot sentiment classification validated by humans — applied to a decade of Norwegian Progress Party Facebook data, linking methodological validation directly to a substantive finding about negativity and engagement.

Two outlying but thematically important papers interrogate what any of this LLM-mediated analysis actually rests on. [[Ptz2024-sl20]] argues, via Luhmann and conversation analysis, that ChatGPT's apparent conversational competence is parasitic on human repair work rather than genuine understanding — a conceptual warning shot against over-trusting LLM "comprehension" in any annotation pipeline. [[Cerulli2026-sl75]], linking validated dark-personality questionnaires to real Reddit text, finds that self-reported toxic behavior correlates with self-report measures but not reliably with text-derived linguistic features — a sobering reminder, consonant with [[Paci2025-ag]] and [[Ptz2024-sl20]], that surface-level computational signals (whether LLM-extracted or hand-crafted) may systematically underdetermine the psychological or political constructs researchers hope to measure. Finally, [[Tan2024-vl]] provides the field's synthetic overview, surveying LLM-based annotation and synthesis methods broadly and confirming that the quality-assurance and ethical challenges this topic's empirical papers wrestle with piecemeal are now recognized as a coherent, rapidly consolidating subfield in its own right.
