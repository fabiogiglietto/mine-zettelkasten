# From \#StayWoke to “culture wars”: How social justice discourse is separately and synergistically politicized on twitter and YouTube

> Levin, S. H., Wang, R., Seo, J., Leong, Y. Y., Bennett, E., Yang, D., Yang, Z., Chen, M., Zhang, Y., & Suk, J. (2026). From \#StayWoke to “culture wars”: How social justice discourse is separately and synergistically politicized on twitter and YouTube. *Political Communication*, 1–25. https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2717428
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2717428)

## Summary

This paper traces how social justice discourse—epitomized by the trajectory from the #StayWoke hashtag toward broader "culture wars" framing—becomes politicized as it circulates across social media. Focusing on Twitter and YouTube, the authors argue that politicization is not confined to a single platform but unfolds through two distinct mechanisms: *separate* processes internal to each platform's environment, and *synergistic* processes that emerge from cross-platform interaction. By centering these cross-platform dynamics, the study positions the online transformation of movement discourse into polarized terms as a fundamentally multi-platform phenomenon in contemporary political communication.

## Key Contributions

- Advances understanding of politicization as a cross-platform rather than single-platform process.
- Introduces a conceptual framework distinguishing **separate** (within-platform) from **synergistic** (cross-platform) politicization dynamics.
- Uses the #StayWoke-to-"culture wars" trajectory as a concrete case for theorizing the online reframing of social justice movements.

## Methods

Analysis of social media discourse across both Twitter and YouTube, comparing within-platform and cross-platform patterns in how social justice framings evolve. (Specific methodological details are not available from the abstract alone.)

## Findings

- Social justice discourse originating in movements like #StayWoke is transformed and politicized as it circulates online.
- Discourse shifts from movement-oriented framings toward polarized "culture wars" framings.
- Both within-platform and cross-platform (synergistic) processes contribute to this politicization, with cross-platform interaction amplifying the reframing.
