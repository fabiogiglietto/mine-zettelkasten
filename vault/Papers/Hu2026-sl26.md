# Seeing, Reading, Hearing, Interpreting: Computational Multimodal Framing Analysis in Short-Form News Videos

> Hu, H. L., & Fu, K. (2026). Seeing, Reading, Hearing, Interpreting: Computational Multimodal Framing Analysis in Short-Form News Videos. *Computational Communication Research*. https://doi.org/10.5117/ccr2026.4.4.hu
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.5117/ccr2026.4.4.hu)

## Summary

This paper tackles a methodological blind spot in computational framing research: the text-centric bias of most video-understanding pipelines. Using 2,957 TikTok videos about the Russia-Ukraine war from CNN, CGTN Europe, and The Kyiv Independent, the authors benchmark single-modality (visual, audio, text) against multimodal LLM-assisted frame detection, evaluated against a manually coded gold standard across six frame categories. They argue that framing in short-form news video is distributed across visual, auditory, and textual channels, that multiple frames routinely co-occur within single clips as deliberate editorial choices, and that modality interactions (complementary, reinforcing, independent) are a central mechanism of multimodal framing. Their central practical finding is sobering: multimodal frameworks only modestly outperform text-only detection, and human judgment remains indispensable for abstract frames like morality.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a taxonomy of **modality interactions** — complementary, reinforcing, and independent — as a core analytic mechanism for multimodal framing.
- Provides a systematic **benchmark** comparing modality-specific and multimodal LLM pipelines against manual coding across six frame categories.
- Reports a detailed **human-in-the-loop validation protocol** (repeated prompting, confidence scoring, prompt/temperature sensitivity) that clarifies the limits of automated inference.
- Offers an **empirical cross-national mapping** of how US, Chinese, and Ukrainian outlets frame the war on TikTok from 2022 to October 2025.
- Extends Semetko and Valkenburg's five generic frames with a sixth Diplomatic/Political Leader frame.

## Methods

The authors collected 2,957 war-related TikTok videos and manually coded a 15% stratified sample (n=450, two coders, κ=0.69) as ground truth. They built parallel pipelines: VideoLLaMA3 for visual captions, a YAMNet/Whisper/Music2Emotion stack for audio, and Gemma3 for integrated multimodal inference. Detection was scored by F1, precision, and recall. Validation combined repeated prompting of GPT-4o and Gemma3 (labels accepted at ≥80% agreement over 10 iterations), Whisper confidence, caption-stability cosine similarity, and 12-variant prompt plus temperature sensitivity tests. Engagement and interaction-type analyses used weighted least squares regression with virality controls, posting-time splines, and outlet fixed effects, plus a weighted binomial GLM.

## Findings

- Multimodal achieved the highest overall F1 (.751, recall .93); audio-only (.739) and text-only (.716) were close behind, and visual-only lagged (.624) with poor precision (.48).
- Abstract frames were hardest: visual-only Morality scored just .12; even multimodal Morality reached only .54 despite being where gains were largest.
- Frame co-occurrence consistently predicted engagement (β=0.249, p<.001); a one-SD increase in combo commonness was associated with ~28% higher engagement.
- Outlets diverged strategically: CGTN emphasized Diplomatic/Political Leader frames, CNN prioritized Conflict and Responsibility, Kyiv Independent foregrounded Conflict and Human Interest.
- Of coded videos, 78% were complementary, 21% reinforcing, under 1% independent; multimodal detection performed best on complementary cases (.755) and worst on independent ones (.638).
- MLLMs over-predicted frames (M=3.27 vs. human M=2.66); dominance-weighted prompting raised precision to .88–.90 but suppressed recall.
- Video duration explained under 5% of co-occurrence variance, and interaction-type distribution stayed stable over the war.
