# A Networked Self: Identity, Community, and Culture on Social Network Sites

> Papacharissi, Z. (2011). A Networked Self: Identity, Community, and Culture on Social Network Sites.
>
> [View paper](http://viralmedia.pbworks.com/w/file/fetch/45052678/A%20Networked%20Self-Identity,%20Community%20and%20Culture%20on%20Social%20Network%20Sites%202011.pdf)

## Summary

This edited volume, organized by Zizi Papacharissi, brings together scholars from communication and media studies to examine how social network sites reshape identity, community, and culture in the early 21st century. Rather than treating identity as a stable, pre-given trait, the collection frames it as something performed and continually negotiated through the affordances of networked platforms. The volume's central conceptual move is to propose the "networked self" as a lens for understanding contemporary sociality — one in which selfhood, relationships, and cultural meaning are produced relationally across online and offline contexts rather than in isolation.

## Key Contributions

- Coins and elaborates the "networked self" as a framing concept for identity research in digital environments.
- Assembles an interdisciplinary set of essays spanning self-presentation, community, and cultural practice on social network sites.
- Serves as a foundational reference for later scholarship on social media, networked publics, and platform-mediated sociality.

## Methods

The book is an edited collection of scholarly essays rather than a single empirical study. Contributors draw on theoretical analysis and interdisciplinary perspectives from communication and media studies, synthesizing both conceptual arguments and existing empirical work on social network site use to build a shared framework around the networked self.

## Findings

- Self-presentation online is an ongoing, performative, and relational process, shaped jointly by platform design and imagined/actual audiences.
- Community on social network sites is organized through networked rather than spatially or socially bounded structures, blurring online/offline distinctions.
- Cultural expression and identity work are mutually constitutive in networked digital spaces — culture emerges from everyday practices of connection and display.

## Connections

This volume sits at the theoretical core of platform and identity research, anticipating and complementing later work on self-presentation and networked audiences such as [[Marwick2011-sl02]] and [[Marwick2026-ss]], as well as foundational network-society thinking like [[Castells2009-sle8]] and Boyd's work on networked publics ([[Boyd2026-op]]). It also connects to more recent empirical studies of platform community dynamics, including [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]], [[Farci2017-2a88c00e]], and [[Cabbuag2024-me]].
