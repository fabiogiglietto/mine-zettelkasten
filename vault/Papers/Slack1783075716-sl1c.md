# Beyond Artificial Intelligence: How Algorithms Communicate without Understanding

> Esposito, E. (2025). Beyond Artificial Intelligence: How Algorithms Communicate without Understanding. *Archivio istituzionale della ricerca (Alma Mater Studiorum Università di Bologna)*. https://doi.org/10.18716/pd.v2i1.11657
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.18716/pd.v2i1.11657)

## Summary

Esposito challenges the pervasive framing of contemporary machine learning systems — ChatGPT chief among them — as milestones toward machines that replicate human cognition. Her core argument is that these systems succeed not because they understand content, but because they produce outputs from which human users generate meaning and relevant information. The apparent "intelligence" of such systems is thus better understood as a *communicative* relation between users and machine outputs rather than as an internal cognitive achievement of the machine. The paper calls for a conceptual reorientation that treats these algorithms on their own terms, drawing on sociological and communication-theoretic traditions rather than cognitivist or computationalist ones.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes AI discourse away from the assumption that machines replicate or approximate human cognition.
- Advances a communication-theoretic account of how humans generate information *through* interaction with algorithms, rather than receiving it *from* them.
- Contributes to sociological debates on the meaning of "intelligence" in artificial systems and the mediating role of algorithms in human information practices.

## Methods

The paper is a conceptual and theoretical argument rather than an empirical study. It critiques prevailing interpretations of AI and uses contemporary large language models (notably ChatGPT) as illustrative cases to demonstrate that algorithmic usefulness stems from human interpretive work, not machine comprehension.

## Findings

- Algorithmic outputs derive their usefulness from human interpretive work rather than from any machine understanding of content.
- The "intelligence" attributed to these systems is more accurately described as a communicative or informational relation between users and outputs.
- The dominant AI framing misconstrues both what these algorithms do and how they interact with users.

## Connections

This paper sits within the broader current of social theory that questions how meaning and agency are attributed to AI systems, resonating with sociological accounts of algorithmic communication and datafied society such as [[Hepp2026-oi]]. Its critique of the "intelligence" imaginary connects to work interrogating how AI is discursively framed and understood, including [[Stanusch2026-ec]] and [[Gillespie2026-aa]]. Where those papers often address the rhetoric and infrastructures surrounding AI, Esposito's contribution is more foundational, targeting the conceptual register through which human-machine interaction is theorized in the first place.
