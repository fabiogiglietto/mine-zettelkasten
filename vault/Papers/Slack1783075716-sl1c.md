# Untitled

> Elena, E. (2025). Untitled. *Archivio istituzionale della ricerca (Alma Mater Studiorum Università di Bologna)*. https://doi.org/10.18716/pd.v2i1.11657
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.18716/pd.v2i1.11657)

## Summary

Esposito challenges the dominant framing of large language models like ChatGPT as milestones toward machine cognition, arguing that these systems do not replicate human thinking and do not understand the content they produce. Their success instead lies in generating outputs from which human users derive meaning and relevant information. The paper calls for a conceptual reorientation — away from "artificial intelligence" as cognitive replication and toward an account of human-machine interaction grounded in communication and information practices.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the debate on ChatGPT-like systems away from cognitivist and computationalist interpretations.
- Advances a communication-theoretic account in which algorithmic outputs become informative only through human interpretive uptake.
- Contributes to sociological critiques of AI discourse, questioning what "intelligence" means when attributed to machine learning systems.
- Positions users, not algorithms, as the site where meaning and information are generated.

## Methods

Conceptual and theoretical argumentation, drawing on sociological and communication theory to critique prevailing AI discourse. Contemporary generative systems, notably ChatGPT, serve as illustrative cases rather than empirical objects.

## Findings

- Machine learning systems produce usable outputs without understanding content; usefulness depends on human interpretive work.
- The "intelligence" ascribed to these systems is better analyzed as a communicative relation between users and machine outputs than as an internal cognitive property.
- The AI framing obscures how such systems actually mediate information practices.

## Connections

This paper provides a theoretical counterpoint to work that treats generative models as approximations of human capacities, connecting to conceptual and media-theoretic engagements with generative AI such as [[Manovich2026-ih]] and [[Hepp2026-oi]].
