# From the platform society to the AI society

> Törnberg, P., & Uitermark, J. (2026). From the platform society to the AI society. *Dialogues on Digital Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/29768640261487931
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/29768640261487931)

## Summary

Törnberg and Uitermark argue that generative AI, and large language models especially, is driving a tendential shift from the "platform society" toward an emerging "AI society." Rather than a clean break, they theorize this as a transformation that grows out of platform-era infrastructures, data, and political economies while partially rupturing them. Tracing the transition across three entangled domains — economic, epistemic, and political — they ask which concepts from critical platform studies should be carried forward and which must be revised to seed a dedicated critical AI studies. The overarching diagnosis is that where platform capitalism enclosed social interaction, AI capitalism moves to enclose the infrastructures of cognition itself.

## Key Contributions

- Theorizes generative AI as emerging from, yet partially rupturing with, the platform society, identifying three linked shifts: network-based to relational monopoly, predictive to generative knowing, and data politics to alignment politics.
- Introduces a critical vocabulary — **cognitive lock-in**, **research-as-a-service**, **synthetic social science**, the **infrastructuralization of theory**, **alignment politics**, AI-as-media-infrastructure, and **epistemic empire**.
- Proposes a methodological program for critical AI studies, including **alignment audits**, the **talkthrough** method (adapting the platform-studies "walkthrough"), and reflexive documentation standards.
- Outlines public-interest governance principles (transparency, pluralism, redress) reimagined for generative infrastructures, including calls for researcher-access architectures for LLMs.

## Methods

Theoretical and conceptual synthesis drawing on critical platform studies, critical data studies, STS, media studies, and emerging critical AI scholarship, framed through Foucauldian notions of epistemes and governmentality. The core device is a comparative framing contrasting the platform society and AI society across organizational form, monopoly mechanism, mode of extraction, epistemic mode, representation, and political mode (summarized in a stylized comparison table). The argument proceeds through illustrative examples — the GPT-4o deprecation backlash, ChatGPT shopping prompts, language-switching bias, Tufekci's interrogation of Grok — rather than new empirical data.

## Findings

- Generative AI reproduces some platform-era mechanisms (infrastructural lock-in, behavioral capture) but network effects no longer structure value, which instead flows from a dyadic user–system relationship and the accumulated history of their collaboration.
- Research conditions are shifting from "data-as-a-service" to "research-as-a-service," deepening scholars' dependence on opaque, proprietary, nondeterministic commercial systems.
- The Big Data "end of theory" is being reversed into an "end of the end of theory," with conceptual labor returning but entangled with models trained on the scholarly canon.
- Data doubles profiling real people are being supplanted by synthetic agents and "AI twins" that manufacture rather than sample publics, raising validation, representation, and democratic-legitimacy concerns.
- Alignment operates through at least four mechanisms — training-data composition, RL/fine-tuning, system prompts and orchestration layers, and content policies — each with distinct actors and governance stakes.
- Commercial incentives toward cognitive lock-in encourage "dark patterns" such as sycophancy and affective dependence as structural pathologies of the era.
- Regulation is uneven: the EU DSA provides researcher data access for platforms, but the AI Act offers no equivalent framework for independent scrutiny of LLMs.

## Connections

This paper offers a theoretical scaffold for much of the empirical and infrastructural work in the platform-governance and data-access literature; its concern with researcher access to opaque proprietary systems directly extends debates about platform data access and scrutiny found in [[Rieder2025-ju]] and [[Bruns2019-nr]].
