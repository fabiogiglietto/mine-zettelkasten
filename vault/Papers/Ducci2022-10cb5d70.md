# Media, Users And Health Online: The Propagation Dynamics Of Digital Information On Facebook And The Role Of The Online Gatekeeper Google News Italia

> Ducci, G., Artieri, G. B., Corsi, M., Giglietto, F., & Manattini, F. (2022). Media, Users And Health Online: The Propagation Dynamics Of Digital Information On Facebook And The Role Of The Online Gatekeeper Google News Italia. *UNICApress*.
>
> [View paper](https://ora.uniurb.it/handle/11576/2698369)

## Summary

This conference paper investigates how Google News Italia acts as an algorithmic gatekeeper for Italian-language health information, and how the news it surfaces subsequently circulates on Facebook. Drawing on a nine-month dataset (February–November 2019) of health-related items aggregated by Google News, combined with Facebook Graph API engagement metrics, the authors show a mismatch between what the aggregator elevates and what users actually engage with: while scientific and healthcare-system topics dominate the aggregated agenda, engagement — especially commenting — is disproportionately driven by emotionally charged categories such as celebrity health stories and reports of healthcare malfunctions. The paper reframes classical gatekeeping theory for a hybrid media ecosystem in which algorithmic aggregators and platform users jointly shape the public health agenda, arguing that this dynamic is a double-edged sword for health literacy and misinformation.

## Key Contributions

- Empirical mapping of Google News Italia as a health-information gatekeeper in the Italian ecosystem.
- A seven-category thematic coding scheme for health news, applied to the top 10% most-engaged articles.
- A comparison index (Ic) that distinguishes emotional (comment-heavy) from rational/technical (share-heavy) engagement patterns.
- Evidence that article topic, more than source identity, drives Facebook engagement with health news.
- Practical implications for health communicators about balancing scientific accuracy with emotional resonance to counter misinformation.

## Methods

Quantitative content analysis combined with platform metrics. Custom software queried Google News Italia RSS feeds every two hours across health, medicine, mental health, nutrition, and fitness categories, yielding 12,080 items. Facebook engagement (reactions, comments, shares) was collected via the Graph API for one week post-publication. After filtering to 9,456 items with engagement, the authors focused on the top 10% (n=960), which five coders manually classified into seven thematic categories (Krippendorff's α = 0.58). A comparison index Ic was constructed to gauge the balance of comments versus shares by category and source.

## Findings

- 78.4% of aggregated health items generated at least some Facebook interaction.
- Among high-engagement articles, medical science (18.5%) and healthcare system (18.4%) were most frequent; celebrity was least frequent (5.3%) but produced the highest average engagement (1,862), comments (1,158), and shares (1,174).
- Across all categories, shares outnumbered comments (57.7% vs. 42.3%), suggesting sharing is a lower-effort act than commenting.
- Technical categories showed a deficit of comments relative to shares; celebrity content showed the reverse — evidence of polarized engagement modes by topic.
- 308 distinct sources contributed, but the top 1% (ANSA, La Repubblica, Corriere della Sera) produced 17% of items; outlets displayed strong thematic specialization (e.g., Quotidiano Sanità on healthcare system, Vanity Fair on celebrity health).
- Within-source engagement variability suggests content, not source identity, is the primary engagement driver.
