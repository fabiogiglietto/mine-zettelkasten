# Degenerative AI: AI slop, brainrot, and the gimmick of generative culture

> Galip, I., & Lupinacci, L. (2026). Degenerative AI: AI slop, brainrot, and the gimmick of generative culture. *European Journal of Cultural Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494261468638
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494261468638)

## Summary

This short theoretical essay reframes the stigmatised categories of "AI slop" and "brainrot" — treating each as both a genre or style of (often automated) digital content and as an associated way of being and feeling. Drawing on Sianne Ngai's *Theory of the Gimmick*, Galip and Lupinacci argue that these outputs are "overrated devices" that paradoxically work "too little" (labour-saving tricks, deskilling) and "too hard" (strained algorithmic bids for attention). Positioned within cultural studies and critical media scholarship, the piece pushes back against sanitised, frictionless accounts of technology, arguing that slop and brainrot expose the contradictions of cultural production under "too late capitalism" — and, provocatively, may harbour critical potential as frictional, resistant forms.

## Key Contributions

- Extends Ngai's gimmick theory into the domain of generative-AI culture, offering a conceptual lens for interpreting slop and brainrot.
- Systematically maps competing public and academic discourses around slop and brainrot as content, experience, and mode of production.
- Reframes ostensibly degenerative, unproductive genres as legitimate objects of critical cultural inquiry and as potentially resistant "frictive forms."
- Contributes to debates on the "enshittification" of digital culture within a special issue on brainrot, AI slop, and internet degradation.

## Methods

A conceptual/theoretical essay rather than an empirical study — no datasets are generated or analysed. It applies Ngai's (2020) gimmick framework structured around three axes of capitalist life — **labour, value, and time** — and synthesises a wide range of journalism, public commentary, and emerging scholarship. Illustrative cases include viral "Italian Brainrot" phenomena (Tralalero Tralala, Ballerina Cappuccina), the AI microdrama "Fruit Love Island," and platform initiatives like Meta's Vibes and OpenAI's Sora.

## Findings

- **On labour:** slop is marketed as effortless and derided as craftless, making it the ultimate labour-saving gimmick that alienates creators from output — while still depending on outsourced low-wage labour and generating new corrective work (fixing botched outputs, prompt engineering).
- **On value:** slop is treated as flagrantly unworthy and "corrosively negative," yet is contingent on enormous financial and environmental costs; most slop fails platform monetisation thresholds, so profit comes instead from selling "how-to-slop" tutorials.
- **On time:** generative AI relies on decontextualisation and recursive compression/remixing of past human culture, and degrades when fed its own outputs — "Model Autophagy Disorder" — meaning the AI can itself be "brain rotted."
- **Brainrot's competing meanings:** an alleged effect of cognitive deterioration, but also a decompression-based, deliberately unproductive "genre of participation" and a form of "anaesthetic media" that relieves exhaustion.
- The experience of slop is defined by ambivalence — captivation and disgust, shock and fascination — capturing attention while corroding cognitive and aesthetic experience.

## Connections

This paper offers a critical-cultural counterpoint to more empirical and industry-facing accounts of synthetic media, and pairs well with work examining how platforms and audiences make sense of proliferating AI-generated content, such as [[Gillespie2026-aa]] and [[Baym2026-tr]]. Its critique of the discourse and imaginaries surrounding generative AI resonates with studies of how such systems are framed and legitimised, including [[Hepp2026-oi]] and [[Vertesi2026-lv]].
