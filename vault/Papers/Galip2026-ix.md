# Degenerative AI: AI slop, brainrot, and the gimmick of generative culture

> Galip, I., & Lupinacci, L. (2026). Degenerative AI: AI slop, brainrot, and the gimmick of generative culture. *European Journal of Cultural Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494261468638
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494261468638)

## Summary

This short theoretical essay reframes the stigmatised categories of "AI slop" and "brainrot" as objects worthy of serious critical cultural inquiry. Working through Sianne Ngai's *Theory of the Gimmick*, Galip and Lupinacci argue that these generative-AI outputs are "overrated devices" that appear to work both "too little" — as labour-saving tricks that deskill and alienate — and "too hard" — as strained algorithmic bids for attention. The authors treat slop and brainrot simultaneously as a genre or style of automated content and as associated ways of being and feeling. Their central provocation is that these ostensibly degenerative and unproductive forms expose the internal contradictions of cultural production under "too late capitalism," and may even carry critical potential as frictional, resistant forms against aesthetic sanitisation and enshittification.

## Key Contributions

- Extends Ngai's gimmick theory into the domain of generative-AI culture, providing a conceptual lens for interpreting slop and brainrot.
- Systematically maps competing public and scholarly discourses around slop and brainrot as content, experience, and mode of production.
- Reframes ostensibly degenerative, unproductive genres as legitimate objects for critical cultural inquiry and as potentially resistant "frictive forms."
- Contributes to debates on the "enshittification" of digital culture within a journal special issue on brainrot, AI slop, and internet degradation.

## Methods

A conceptual/theoretical essay rather than an empirical study — no datasets are generated or analysed. The authors organise the argument around Ngai's three axes of capitalist life (labour, value, and time), synthesising public commentary, journalism, and emerging scholarship on slop and brainrot. Illustrative examples include viral phenomena such as "Italian Brainrot" (Tralalero Tralala, Ballerina Cappuccina), the AI microdrama "Fruit Love Island," and platform initiatives like Meta's Vibes and OpenAI's Sora.

## Findings

- **Labour:** Slop is framed by tech firms as effortless and by critics as craftless — the ultimate labour-saving gimmick that alienates creators from output, yet it depends on outsourced low-wage labour and generates new corrective work (fixing botched outputs, prompt engineering).
- **Value:** Slop is treated as flagrantly unworthy and "corrosively negative" while being contingent on enormous financial and environmental costs; most slop fails to meet platform monetisation thresholds, with profit coming instead from selling "how-to-slop" tutorials.
- **Time:** Generative AI relies on decontextualisation and recursive compression of past human culture, and degenerates when fed its own outputs — "Model Autophagy Disorder" — meaning the AI can itself be "brain rotted."
- **Brainrot** carries competing meanings: an alleged effect of cognitive deterioration, yet also a deliberately unproductive "genre of participation" and a form of "anaesthetic media" that relieves exhaustion.
- The experience of slop is marked by ambivalence — captivation and disgust, shock and fascination — capturing attention while corroding cognitive and aesthetic experience.
