# The power of Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) for spreading Covid-19 problematic information on Facebook during a year of pandemic

> Marino, F. G. G. (2023). The power of Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) for spreading Covid-19 problematic information on Facebook during a year of pandemic. https://doi.org/10.1445/106772
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1445/106772)

## Summary

This paper investigates how Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) contributed to circulating problematic Covid-19 information on Italian Facebook during the pandemic's first year (March 2020–March 2021). Working from a dataset of 175,228 posts by previously identified covid-skeptic accounts, Giglietto and Marino extract mentioned public figures, cluster them into five categories, and analyze engagement signatures and content. They argue that an "Intellectual Dark Web" (IDW) subset of influencers generates disproportionately shareable content, and — crucially — that legacy media outlets and mainstream journalists remain central conduits, with AIN actors *remediating* expert statements from TV and newspapers to bolster covid-skeptic narratives while evading fact-checking.

## Key Contributions

- Empirical mapping of the Italian pandemic-era AIN, identifying an IDW subnetwork with a distinctive share-heavy engagement signature.
- Demonstrates the analytical utility of a comments/shares ratio (range -1 to 1) for distinguishing hyperpartisan amplification from contested debate.
- Extends Bolter and Grusin's remediation theory to alternative media, showing how legacy-media expert statements are recycled to legitimize problematic narratives.
- Contributes to inter-media agenda-setting literature by documenting co-production of visibility across AIN, alternative outlets, and legacy media.
- Provides a replicable mixed-methods pipeline: coordinated-behavior detection → NER → manual classification → k-means clustering → engagement metrics → stratified deductive content analysis.

## Methods

Facebook posts were collected via CrowdTangle from a pre-identified set of covid-skeptic Italian accounts flagged through coordinated link-sharing analysis. The authors extracted capitalized proper nouns across message, title, description, and image-text fields, selected the top 2,000 terms, and manually classified 254 last names into five non-mutually-exclusive roles (Alternative Influencers, Doctors/Scientists/Experts, Journalists, Other Public Figures, Politicians). After filtering apical public-role holders, 79 figures were grouped via k-means into five clusters. Engagement was measured with a comments/shares ratio metric, and a stratified sample of 150 posts (from the top 10% most-engaging) was subjected to deductive content analysis across clusters and ratio bands.

## Findings

- Five clusters emerged: Politicians, Other Public Figures, Journalists, Doctors/Scientists/Experts, and IDW Influencers.
- IDW Influencers showed the lowest comments/shares ratio (-0.28), indicating share-driven hyperpartisan amplification; Doctors/Scientists/Experts also skewed toward shares (-0.21).
- Journalists' posts had the highest ratio (+0.16), signaling more contested/commented content; Politicians posted most (9,879) but with moderate engagement.
- Despite modest volumes, IDW Influencers (1,649 posts) and Other Public Figures (1,958 posts) achieved high mean interactions (1,030 and 983 respectively).
- The most amplifying Facebook pages were mainstream-affiliated journalists (Porro, Paragone) and right-wing newspapers (*La Verità*, *Libero*); covid-skeptic micro-celebrities (Montanari, Messora) ranked lower.
- Content themes included: anti-immigration and anti-government framings (political clusters), judicial scandals (Other Public Figures), skeptical expert quotes from Zangrillo/Tarro (science cluster), and conspiracy narratives about microchips and global tyranny (IDW), frequently sourced from mainstream TV talk-show clips.
