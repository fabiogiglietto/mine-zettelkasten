# Community Notes undermoderate polarizing content by design creating risks in electoral processes

> Bouchaud, P., & Ramaciotti, P. (2026). Community Notes undermoderate polarizing content by design creating risks in electoral processes. *Science Advances*, *12*. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee6932
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee6932)

## Summary

This paper offers a large-scale empirical audit of X's Community Notes, arguing that its bridging-based selection mechanism — which promotes only notes that attract cross-partisan agreement along an inferred latent ideological dimension — systematically fails to moderate the very content most likely to cause democratic harm: polarizing political material. Drawing on the full corpus of 1.9 million notes and 135 million ratings through March 2025, cross-referenced with ideological scaling data from 13 countries, the authors show that undermoderation of divisive content is not an operational glitch but a structural feature of the algorithm's design, with direct implications for electoral integrity worldwide.

## Key Contributions

- First large-scale, cross-national empirical evaluation of Community Notes as a global moderation infrastructure.
- Reframes a widely praised "consensus-based" moderation approach as structurally biased against moderating polarizing content.
- Connects platform design choices in bridging algorithms to concrete risks for elections across diverse political systems.
- Distinguishes design-level from operational limitations, shifting the policy debate toward the governance of algorithmic moderation architectures.

## Methods

- Complete-corpus analysis of Community Notes: 1.9M notes and 135M ratings through March 2025.
- Integration with ideological scaling data across 13 countries to locate raters and notes in a latent political space.
- Comparative analysis of note visibility outcomes across national polarization contexts, isolating how the bridging criterion interacts with ideological structure.

## Findings

- Notes addressing highly polarizing content are markedly less likely to reach the cross-partisan agreement threshold needed for public display.
- The bridging algorithm's effectiveness is context-dependent: performance degrades in more ideologically polarized environments.
- Electoral and politically contested content falls disproportionately into the undermoderated category, precisely where accurate moderation matters most.
- Undermoderation is baked into the selection rule itself — not attributable to rater scarcity or bad-faith participation.
