# Defective, amplified, contested: Folk conceptualizations of AI slop

> Li, Z., & Banks, J. (2026). Defective, amplified, contested: Folk conceptualizations of AI slop. *arXiv [cs.CY]*.
>
> [View paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14231v1)

## Summary

This descriptive study examines how everyday social media users—rather than experts, technologists, or policymakers—conceptualize, experience, and contest "AI slop": the low-effort, mass-produced, derivative AI-generated content now saturating social feeds. Through inductive thematic analysis of 516 Reddit posts and comments across six slop-related subreddits, the authors argue that AI slop is best understood not as a fixed aesthetic category but as a relational and affective "fuzzy set"—defined by the gap between what algorithmic systems produce at scale and what audiences expect, resist, and sometimes embrace. The paper culminates in a user-grounded definition of AI slop as "audience-contested AI-generated content characterized by constitutional defects and enabled by platform logics," operationalized through a facet-theoretical mapping sentence to guide future research.

## Key Contributions

- One of the first empirical, user-centered accounts of folk conceptualizations of AI slop, complementing largely conceptual prior scholarship.
- A user-driven conceptual definition emphasizing audience contestation, constitutional defects, and platform logics.
- A facet-theoretical mapping sentence (contestation, AI-generated content, constitutional defects, platform logics) to scaffold systematic future research.
- Introduces the concept of the **"uncanny of provenance"**—disorientation arising not from how content looks but from the irresolvability of who made it—extending the uncanny valley.
- Articulates three analytic stances on slop (uncanny aesthetic/provenance space; evasion and masquerading; "fast food"/McDonaldized production) with design implications for labeling, moderation, and monetization.

## Methods

Inductive, descriptive thematic analysis following Braun and Clarke's six-step workflow, grounded in a constructionist stance and the first author's insider positionality within AI discussion communities. A corpus of N=516 posts and comments containing "AI slop" or variants was collected via the Python Reddit API Wrapper (two-channel: search endpoint with weekly segmentation plus scanning new feeds), validated with regex and deduplicated. Six subreddits were sampled across broad slop/social-impact discussion and visual-centric content-sharing communities. The time frame runs from January 2023 (post-ChatGPT) to October 2025 (post-Sora 2). Analysis combined textual and visual engagement, with a second author reviewing the thematic hierarchy for face validity and mutual exclusivity.

## Findings

- **Four themes emerged:** Constitutional defects (n=305), Algorithmic overreach (n=110), User resistance (n=103), and Entertainment outcomes (n=80).
- **Constitutional defects** span five subthemes: visual degradation, moral transgression, narrative inconsistency ("slop logic"), factual distortion, and provenance deficiencies (rejection based on nonhuman origin regardless of quality).
- **Algorithmic overreach** captures frustration at platforms via content saturation, platform decay ("enshittification"), automation over user autonomy, and engagement amplification (botting, "dead internet theory").
- **User resistance** includes assigning responsibility (creators, advertisers, tech leaders), reclaiming agency (reporting, filtering, quitting, petitions), and articulating societal risks (jobs, environment, vulnerable groups).
- **Entertainment outcomes** reveal hedonic enjoyment and guilty pleasure alongside eudaimonic *technostalgia*—longing for a pre-AI internet.
- Users develop folk taxonomies to distinguish slop from valued AI content, but these are hard to sustain as markers of human labor become reproducible at scale.
- Platform labeling and moderation, triggered mainly by deceptive hyper-realism and explicit rule violations, structurally fail to capture what users define as slop, shifting interpretive labor onto users.

## Connections

This paper's analysis of platform logics—monetization, automation, engagement amplification, and "enshittification"—connects to platform governance and infrastructure scholarship such as [[Helmond2026-ll]] and [[van-Dijck2018-up]], and its account of algorithmic visibility and audience experience resonates with work on the operation of the algorithm from the user's perspective in [[Bucher2017-qh]] and [[Cotter2019-js]]. Its treatment of AI-generated content's deceptive/hyper-realistic edge sits alongside generative-AI disinformation research including [[Hameleers2026-mc]], [[Hackenburg2025-dj]], and [[Goldberg2026-eb]], while its focus on folk/everyday interpretation echoes user-grounded approaches to online community understanding in [[Marwick2025-ov]].
