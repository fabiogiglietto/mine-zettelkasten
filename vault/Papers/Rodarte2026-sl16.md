# The Democratic Cost of Pursuing Virality: A Study into Parliamentary Communications

> Rodarte, A. K. (2026). The Democratic Cost of Pursuing Virality: A Study into Parliamentary Communications. *Political Communication*. https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2733316
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2733316)

## Summary

This mixed-methods study examines how Brazilian federal parliamentarians perceive and adapt to the engagement incentives of social media, and what those adaptations cost democracy. Combining 34 interviews with a computational analysis of over 4.5 million Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter posts from the 56th Legislature (2019–2022), Rodarte argues that platform logics penalize communication about legislative and constituency work while rewarding antagonistic, conflict-driven content aligned with news cycles. Because congressional content is systematically disadvantaged in reach, politicians face a perverse incentive to downplay the very work that enables public oversight — producing what the paper calls a "democratic cost." The study frames media logics not as static, coercive platform properties but as incentives internalized by political actors, and grounds its analysis in Brazil's candidate-centered system to move beyond the party-centric assumptions of Global North mediatization scholarship.

## Key Contributions

- Provides conceptual tools for studying parliamentary communication beyond the Global North, leveraging Brazil's candidate-centered system to depart from party-centric mediatization assumptions.
- Documents how algorithmic incentives become *internalized* as media logics in politicians' strategies, rather than treating media logics as fixed platform properties.
- Empirically links engagement dynamics to a "democratic cost" by showing accountability-relevant congressional content is systematically disadvantaged.
- Introduces and operationalizes *lacração* as a distinct antagonistic communicative style — a boundary-making mode not reducible to generic negativity.
- Offers a validated, cross-platform, dictionary-based computational pipeline and exploratory evidence on sequential virality pathways.

## Methods

The study follows a computational grounded theory approach. The qualitative arm comprises 34 semi-structured interviews with parliamentarians, staffers, public servants, and consultants (purposive sampling for ideological, seniority, and followership diversity; fieldwork in Brasília, 2023) analyzed via iterative inductive coding. The computational arm classifies posts as programmatic, parochial, or antagonistic using dictionary/regex methods validated in three steps (GPT-4 semantic annotation to expand dictionaries, comparison against the Portuguese LeIA sentiment model, and human intercoder content analysis). Engagement is modeled with negative binomial regressions on overdispersed count data, controlling for followers, posting volume, format, hashtags, and political bloc, with month fixed effects and standard errors clustered by politician. Ideology is captured through a tripartite classification combining legislative survey placements and roll-call government alignment, complemented by exploratory sequential/transition analysis of high-engagement post trajectories on Facebook.

## Findings

- Programmatic content declined ~1.9 points per year while parochial content rose ~2.3 points per year on Facebook; parochial content was more common among centrist/right-wing, government-aligned parties.
- On Facebook, programmatic posts had ~21% lower expected engagement and parochial posts ~47% lower; Instagram penalized congressional content most strongly, while on Twitter only parochial content was significantly penalized.
- Antagonistic content is rare (~2.7–4.1% across platforms) but rising; negative tone is far more common (~31–41%). Both concentrate among opposition and ideologically flanked parties.
- Antagonistic content and negative intensity both positively predict Facebook engagement, with predicted interactions rising from ~1,292 (low) to ~2,146 (high on both).
- High engagement is temporally clustered: a post following a high-engagement post is 79% more likely to also be high-engagement, and sustained sequences concentrate on negative/antagonistic content.
- Politicians appear to *overestimate* the prevalence of antagonistic communication, likely amplified by news coverage of conflict.

## Connections

This paper sits within a cluster of work on how platform incentives shape and reward political communication, particularly the visibility payoffs of negativity and antagonism — connecting to work on virality and engagement dynamics such as [[Brady2026-ln]] and to studies of platform amplification of political content like [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Bakshy2015-rn]]. Its focus on parliamentary and elite political communication complements other studies of politicians' platform behavior and strategic messaging in the register. The internalized-media-logic framing and the "democratic cost" argument offer a distinctive Global South, accountability-centered counterpoint to the more Northern, party-centric literatures these papers typically assume.
