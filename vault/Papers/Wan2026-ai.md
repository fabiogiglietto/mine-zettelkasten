# How does political efficacy condition clicks on politics? Understanding information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds over time

> Wan, J., Araujo, T., Helberger, N., & de Vreese, C. (2026). How does political efficacy condition clicks on politics? Understanding information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds over time. *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*, *31*. https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag022
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag022)

## Summary

This study examines how *political efficacy* — an individual's sense that they can understand and influence politics — shapes the way people select political information from algorithmically curated news feeds. Combining survey measures with real behavioral trace data from 186 Dutch users of Google Discover (a fully algorithmic feed), the authors distinguish between within-person changes over time and between-person differences in information-selection behavior. Their argument is that algorithmic exposure is not simply imposed on passive users: individual psychological dispositions like political efficacy help explain who clicks on politics, how much, and how diversely. The paper thereby reframes selective exposure in algorithmic environments as jointly conditioned by feed curation and user agency.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **political efficacy** as an explanatory construct for individual variation in political information selection within algorithmic feeds.
- Uses **real-world usage-history (trace) data** from Google Discover combined with survey measures, moving beyond self-reported or experimental exposure.
- Explicitly separates **within-person temporal dynamics** from **between-person differences** in algorithmic selection behavior.
- Measures selection across multiple dimensions, including the *proportion* of political content selected and *exposure diversity*.

## Methods

- Linked survey data with behavioral data from 186 Dutch individuals' actual usage histories on Google Discover.
- Analyzed within-person changes in information-selection behavior over time.
- Analyzed between-person differences in selection behavior conditioned by political efficacy.
- Operationalized selection along dimensions such as proportion of political information and exposure diversity.

## Findings

- Political efficacy helps explain individual differences in how users select political information from algorithmic feeds.
- Information-selection behavior on algorithmic feeds varies both within persons over time and between persons.
- Between-person differences in selecting political information are conditioned by users' levels of political efficacy.
