# How does political efficacy condition clicks on politics? Understanding information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds over time

> Wan, J., Araujo, T., Helberger, N., & de Vreese, C. (2026). How does political efficacy condition clicks on politics? Understanding information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds over time. *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*, *31*. https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag022
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag022)

## Summary

This paper examines how **political efficacy** — a person's sense that they can understand and influence politics — shapes the way individuals select political information from algorithmically curated news feeds. Combining survey measures with real usage-history (trace) data from 186 Dutch users of Google Discover, a fully algorithmic feed, the authors study information-selection behavior along dimensions such as the proportion of political content selected and exposure diversity. Crucially, they separate **within-person change over time** from **between-person differences**, arguing that political efficacy helps account for the latter. The work advances debates on selective exposure and algorithmic curation by grounding them in observed behavior rather than self-report or experimental stimuli.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **political efficacy** as a psychological construct for explaining individual variation in political information selection within algorithmic feeds.
- Uses **real-world trace data** from Google Discover paired with survey measures, moving beyond self-reported or experimental exposure.
- Analytically distinguishes **within-person temporal dynamics** from **between-person differences** in algorithmic selection behavior.

## Methods

- Combines survey data with behavioral usage histories from **186 Dutch individuals** on Google Discover, a fully algorithmically curated feed.
- Models **within-person changes** in information-selection behavior over time alongside **between-person differences** conditioned by political efficacy.
- Operationalizes selection across multiple dimensions, including the proportion of political information selected and exposure diversity.

## Findings

- Political efficacy helps explain individual differences in how users select political information from algorithmic feeds.
- Information-selection behavior varies both **within persons over time** and **between persons**.
- Between-person differences in selecting political information are **conditioned by users' levels of political efficacy**.
