# Understanding Engagement and Willingness to Speak Up in Social-Television: A Full-Season, Cross-Genre Analysis of TV Audience Participation on Twitter

> Giglietto, F., Artieri, G. B., Gemini, L., & Orefice, M. (2015). Understanding Engagement and Willingness to Speak Up in Social-Television: A Full-Season, Cross-Genre Analysis of TV Audience Participation on Twitter. *Elsevier BV*. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2611083
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2611083)

## Summary

This study examines how television audiences participate on Twitter across a complete 2012–2013 broadcast season of two Italian shows from contrasting genres: the talent competition *The X Factor* and the political talk show *Servizio Pubblico*. Working from a dataset of nearly 1.4 million tweets, the authors use a peak-detection algorithm to identify moments of heightened online engagement and link them back to the specific broadcast scenes that triggered them. Their central argument is that Twitter's engagement rhythm is tightly synchronized with television's format structure — demonstrating a prevailing framing effect of broadcast over online conversation — while the *forms* of audience expression differ systematically between entertainment and politics. Notably, political discussion tends to be more indirect, backed by information, and framed as jokes, whereas entertainment prompts more spontaneous original opinion and emotion.

## Key Contributions

- A hybrid methodology combining peak detection on original-tweet streams with scene-level content analysis, designed to avoid bias from the heavily skewed distribution of tweets.
- A rare full-season, cross-genre comparison of social TV participation spanning an entire broadcast season of two distinct formats.
- Operationalizes Luhmann's media selectors (surprise and suspense) as analytical tools for explaining genre-specific drivers of engagement.
- Extends and refines the AEIO content-form codebook to capture nuanced differences in willingness to speak up and forms of opinion expression.
- Empirically demonstrates television's framing effect on Twitter conversation, nuancing prior event-focused work that emphasized user agency and divergence.

## Methods

The authors collected a complete dataset of 1,383,414 tweets via the Twitter Firehose (Aug 2012 – June 2013) using the official hashtags #xf6 and #serviziopubblico. Data were segmented by air time into minute-by-minute time series of tweets, replies, retweets, and original tweets. A peak-detection algorithm identified 55 seasonal engagement peaks (16 for *The X Factor*, 39 for *Servizio Pubblico*), and the corresponding TV scenes were coded using a codebook grounded in Luhmann's media selector criteria (surprise, suspense) and show routine position. Finally, over 12,000 original tweets from eight sampled peaks were content-analyzed using an adapted AEIO matrix (information, opinion, opinion-as-joke, emotion, attention-seeking, interaction), with acceptable intercoder reliability throughout.

## Findings

- *The X Factor* audiences were far more active (≈62,500 tweets/episode) than *Servizio Pubblico* (≈16,900 tweets/episode).
- Talent-show engagement combined surprise (50% of peaks) and suspense (56%); the political show relied mainly on surprise (49%) with little suspense (5%).
- Original tweets were more frequent during entertainment (70%) than politics (63%), and when a peer-like figure (audience member or contestant) was on screen.
- Opinions were the most common tweet type overall (62%), but politics drew more jokes (25% vs 11%) and more opinion-plus-information tweets (10% vs 4%), while entertainment drew far more emotional tweets (33% vs 3%).
- For politics, pairing opinion with information was significantly more common in low-original-tweet peaks (13% vs 7%), suggesting a credibility-bolstering or risk-avoidance strategy.
- Explicit requests for change (interaction) were low (~11%) and only slightly lower for politics, challenging the assumption that political shows prompt more participatory demands.
