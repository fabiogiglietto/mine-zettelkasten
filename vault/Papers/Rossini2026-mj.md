# Disrupting democracy: how informational pathways and misinformation shape trust in election results

> Rossini, P., Mont'Alverne, C., & Kalogeropoulos, A. (2026). Disrupting democracy: how informational pathways and misinformation shape trust in election results. *Journal of Elections, Public Opinion and Parties*, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1080/17457289.2026.2701896
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/17457289.2026.2701896)

## Summary

This paper investigates how different informational pathways — mainstream news, social media, and messaging apps — together with belief in electoral misinformation shaped changes in Brazilian citizens' trust in the results of the 2022 presidential election. Drawing on an original three-wave panel survey administered after each voting round, the authors argue that professional journalism functions as a democratic stabilizer, bolstering electoral trust over time even among "losers" (Bolsonaro voters), whereas belief in electoral misinformation and having voted for the illiberal incumbent depress trust. The study's central theoretical move is to reframe misinformation not as isolated false content but as a strategic elite practice embedded in evolving digital media systems, with WhatsApp functioning as a "propaganda machine" among Bolsonaro supporters. It extends largely US-based scholarship to a Global South context marked by weak partisanship and compulsory voting.

## Key Contributions

- First multi-wave, over-time panel measurement of how informational pathways and electoral misinformation shape election-result trust in a Global South setting.
- Demonstrates the resilience-building role of mainstream journalism against elite disinformation, holding even among supporters of the losing illiberal incumbent.
- Disaggregates messaging-app effects by use type (news consumption vs. group discussion) and by network composition (strong-, weak-, and no-tie groups) rather than treating messaging apps monolithically.
- Advances a theoretical reframing of misinformation as strategic elite practice tied to democratic backsliding.
- Tests motivated-reasoning and winner-loser-gap theories in a multi-party system where vote choice signals rejection rather than partisan strength.

## Methods

Three-wave opt-in panel survey of Brazilian internet users (W1 n=1,600; W2 n=1,328; W3 n=1,034), recruited with quotas for age, gender, class, and region, fielded after each round of the 2022 election. The dependent variable was a single-item 4-point trust measure adapted from the World Values Survey. Independent variables captured frequency of mainstream, social media, and messaging news use; WhatsApp group talk with strong/weak/no ties; and a "misinformed score" (0–8) built from twelve statements (measured only in Wave 2). Random-effects within-between models separated within-person change over time from between-person effects, with Bonferroni correction across five focal hypotheses and a replication on the Bolsonaro-voter subset (n=492).

## Findings

- Belief in electoral misinformation was negatively associated with trust (β = −.157, p<.001).
- Voting for Bolsonaro strongly predicted lower trust (β = −.946), as did right-wing ideology (β = −.315).
- Mainstream news use positively predicted trust both between and within persons over time (β = .069), holding even among Bolsonaro voters.
- Social media news use had null effects; messaging news use showed a negative between-person effect (β = −.129) but no significant within-person effect in the full sample.
- Among Bolsonaro voters, messaging news use eroded trust over time (β = −.069), strong-tie WhatsApp discussion raised trust (β = .165), and stranger (no-tie) discussion lowered it (β = −.161).
- Weak-tie WhatsApp discussion weakly raised trust but did not survive Bonferroni correction; higher political interest raised trust generally but not among Bolsonaro voters.
