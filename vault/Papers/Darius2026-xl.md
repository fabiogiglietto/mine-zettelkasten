# Radical populist parties receive greater audience support on social media: a cross-platform monitoring of digital campaigning for the 2024 European Parliament election

> Darius, P., Drews, W., Neumeier, A., & Riedl, J. (2026). Radical populist parties receive greater audience support on social media: a cross-platform monitoring of digital campaigning for the 2024 European Parliament election. *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 1–14. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-08773-w
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-08773-w)

## Summary

This paper offers a large-scale, cross-platform, cross-national account of digital campaigning during the 2024 European Parliament election. Analyzing ~283,000 messages from 401 parties across all 27 EU member states on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter, and YouTube, the authors distinguish party *activity* (posting) from *audience support* (likes). Their central argument is that populist radical right (PRR) parties—and, to a lesser degree, far-left populist parties—receive disproportionately high online support, with TikTok the most asymmetric arena. Crucially, this advantage stems less from posting more than from communicative styles (emotional appeals, anti-elitism) and Eurosceptic positions that resonate with platform engagement dynamics. The paper thereby contributes empirical weight to the "equalisation" side of the normalisation-vs-equalisation debate, treating populism as both a thin-centered ideology and a communicative style.

## Key Contributions

- One of the first comprehensive **five-platform, 27-country, 401-party** analyses of party digital campaigning, extending prior single-platform/single-country work to TikTok and YouTube.
- Introduces and publicly releases the **EUDigiParty dataset** of party social media handles and party-level data.
- Triangulates PRR classification across three complementary operationalizations (EP group affiliation, EU&I expert issue positions, POPPA populist traits) for robustness.
- Advances the **normalisation-vs-equalisation** debate with evidence that platform affordances disproportionately amplify radical and populist actors.
- Provides cross-European support for the claim that populist communicative styles (emotion, anti-elitism) drive engagement, updated for the 2024 EP context.

## Methods

- Compiled the EUDigiParty dataset of 401 parties contesting the 2024 EP election, identifying main national accounts on five platforms.
- Collected ~283,000 messages (10 March–9 June 2024) via platform APIs and CrowdTangle, with TikTok supplemented by web scraping due to API problems.
- Two count-based dependent variables: party activity (number of posts) and support (number of likes).
- Operationalized the PRR family three ways: EP group affiliation (PfE, ECR, ESN vs. EPP reference), EU&I 2024 issue positions, and POPPA populist traits; controlled for European region and newcomer status (founded since 2019).
- Estimated **truncated negative binomial regressions** (justified by overdispersion and power-law-like distributions with DHARMa diagnostics), run per platform and per operationalization, reporting incidence rate ratios.

## Findings

- **Activity differences were modest**: mainly PfE parties posted more (Facebook +215%, TikTok +258%, YouTube +217%) and ECR more on TikTok (+180%); other PRR groups did not differ significantly from the EPP.
- **Support differences were large**: PfE dramatically outperformed the EPP in likes (TikTok +3121%, YouTube +2458%, Facebook +858%, Instagram +662%, X/Twitter +335%); ESN and ECR also showed strong advantages.
- The advantage extends to the left: **The Left** outperformed the EPP on YouTube (+417%), TikTok (+294%), and Instagram (+198%).
- **TikTok was the most asymmetric platform**, with strong support boosts for PfE, ECR, and ESN membership.
- **Eurosceptic positions** correlated with more support on TikTok, X/Twitter, and YouTube—operating through resonance rather than posting frequency.
- **Emotional appeals** predicted higher support (Facebook +27%, X/Twitter +70%) and **anti-elitism** predicted higher support (TikTok +65%, X/Twitter +63%, YouTube +75%).
- Northern and Western European parties showed lower activity and support relative to the Eastern baseline.

## Connections

This study sits within the political-communication literature on populist digital campaigning and platform affordances, and its focus on TikTok's amplification of radical and emotionally charged content aligns with related European work on far-right and populist online strategies such as [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]], [[Schulte2026-df]], and [[Grusauskaite2026-po]]. Its emphasis on engagement-driven asymmetry and the algorithmic rewarding of anti-elitist, emotional content connects to broader research on affective and moral virality (e.g. [[Brady2026-ln]]) and to the normalisation-vs-equalisation and polarization debates touched by [[Larsson2026-ro]] and [[Kalsnes2025-zb]].
