# Radical populist parties receive greater audience support on social media: a cross-platform monitoring of digital campaigning for the 2024 European Parliament election

> Darius, P., Drews, W., Neumeier, A., & Riedl, J. (2026). Radical populist parties receive greater audience support on social media: a cross-platform monitoring of digital campaigning for the 2024 European Parliament election. *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 1–14. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-08773-w
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-08773-w)

## Summary

This paper offers a large-scale, cross-platform, cross-national analysis of digital campaigning during the 2024 European Parliament election, covering 401 parties across all 27 EU member states on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter, and YouTube. The central argument is that populist radical right (PRR) parties—and, to a lesser degree, far-left populist parties—receive disproportionately high audience support online, and that this advantage stems not from posting more but from communicative styles (emotional appeals, anti-elitism) and Eurosceptic positions that resonate with platform engagement dynamics. TikTok emerges as the most asymmetric arena. The authors frame their contribution within the normalisation-vs-equalisation debate, arguing that platform affordances act as "equalizers" that amplify challenger and populist actors.

## Key Contributions

- One of the first comprehensive five-platform, 27-country analyses of party digital campaigning, extending prior single-platform/single-country work to TikTok and YouTube.
- Introduction and public release of the **EUDigiParty** dataset of party social media handles and party-level data.
- Methodological robustness via triangulation across three PRR operationalizations (EP group affiliation, EU&I issue positions, POPPA populist traits).
- Empirical advance on the normalisation-vs-equalisation debate, showing platform affordances disproportionately amplify radical and populist actors.
- Cross-European evidence that populist communicative styles (emotion, anti-elitism) drive engagement, updated for the 2024 EP context.

## Methods

The authors compiled the EUDigiParty dataset and collected ~283,000 messages between 10 March and 9 June 2024 via platform APIs (TikTok, YouTube, X/Twitter) and CrowdTangle (Facebook, Instagram), supplementing TikTok with web scraping. Two count-based dependent variables were used: party activity (number of posts) and party support (number of likes). PRR family membership was operationalized three ways—EP group affiliation (PfE, ECR, ESN vs. EPP reference), EU&I 2024 expert-coded issue positions, and POPPA populist traits—with controls for European region and newcomer status. Models were truncated negative binomial regressions (justified by overdispersion and power-law-like distributions), estimated separately per platform and per operationalization, reporting incidence rate ratios.

## Findings

- Activity differences were limited: mainly PfE parties posted significantly more (Facebook +215%, TikTok +258%, YouTube +217%) and ECR more on TikTok (+180%); other PRR groups did not differ significantly from EPP.
- Support differences were large: PfE received dramatically more likes than EPP (TikTok +3121%, YouTube +2458%, Facebook +858%, Instagram +662%, X/Twitter +335%); ESN and ECR also showed strong advantages.
- The far-left "The Left" group outperformed EPP on YouTube (+417%), TikTok (+294%), and Instagram (+198%), suggesting the mechanism spans populist actors broadly.
- TikTok was the most asymmetric platform (PfE +3121%, ECR +1251%, ESN +704%).
- More Eurosceptic positions were associated with more support on TikTok, X/Twitter, and YouTube—operating through resonance, not posting frequency.
- Expert-rated emotional appeals linked to higher support on Facebook (+27%) and X/Twitter (+70%); anti-elitism to higher support on TikTok (+65%), X/Twitter (+63%), and YouTube (+75%).
- Regional controls showed lower activity and support in Northern and Western Europe relative to the Eastern baseline.

## Connections

This paper contributes to a body of European electoral social media research and shares its 2024 EP election focus with [[Gattermann2025-yx]]. Its concern with populist and radical-right amplification connects to work on algorithmic dynamics and platform logics such as [[Tornberg2025-ir]], and its cross-platform TikTok campaigning approach resonates with studies like [[Larsson2026-ro]] (Larsson2025-ro) and [[Perez-Curiel2026-ld]].
