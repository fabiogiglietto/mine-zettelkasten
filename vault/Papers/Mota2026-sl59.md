# Weapon, wonder or both? Political framings of AI in global north and south media

> Mota, B. M. F. (2026). Weapon, wonder or both? Political framings of AI in global north and south media. *Information, Communication & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2026.2691775
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2026.2691775)

## Summary

This paper offers a large-scale comparative analysis of how mainstream news media in the Global North and Global South politically frame artificial intelligence. Drawing on 6,829 English-language articles from twelve newspapers across four continents (2010–2024), Mota argues that AI should be understood not simply as a technical artifact but as a *political infrastructure* reshaping institutions, governance, and power relations. The central finding is a systematic North–South divergence: Northern outlets frame AI through a risk- and threat-oriented, pessimistic lens, while Southern outlets lean optimistic and developmental. These asymmetries, the paper contends, reflect differing geopolitical and technological positions, with AI oscillating in public discourse between a "weapon" in a new technological cold war and a "wonder" for development.

## Key Contributions

- The first systematic comparative analysis of *political* framings of AI across Global North and South news media.
- Redirects attention from generic AI coverage toward AI as a site of political contestation involving corporate power, geopolitics, and digital governance.
- A large, openly shared empirical corpus (6,829 articles, 12 outlets, 15 years) processed through a reproducible mixed-method computational pipeline.
- An empirical periodisation of AI-politics coverage — incipient, crisis-politicisation, and exponential-acceleration phases — tied to identifiable catalytic events.
- Extends the concept of "risk entanglement" to a *state-platform* configuration, theorising how infrastructural dependence and regulatory pressure jointly reshape sovereignty.

## Methods

Comparative computational text analysis of articles retrieved from LexisNexis using keyword combinations pairing AI terms with election- and politics-related terms. The twelve newspapers span Europe, North America, Africa, and Asia/Oceania (e.g., NYT, The Guardian, China Daily, The Straits Times, Mail & Guardian, Times of India, Korea Herald). Topic modelling used LDA with Gibbs sampling (15 topics selected via triangulated metrics: Arun, Cao, Deveaud, Griffiths–Steyvers). Headline sentiment combined four lexicons (Bing, sentimentr, NRC, AFINN), with Mann–Whitney non-parametric testing validating North–South differences. Interpretation was structured around Hallin and Mancini's comparative media systems framework, supplemented by bigram and keyword-coherence analysis.

## Findings

- Three temporal phases, with inflection at the Cambridge Analytica scandal (2018) and the ChatGPT release (2022); article volume rose ~567% between 2022 and 2024.
- Fifteen frames emerged, dominated by Social Media and Tech Impact (16.03%), Technology and the Digital Economy (12.54%), and China–US Relations and Trade (11.41%).
- Geopolitical frames collectively account for 20.92% of coverage.
- Global North headlines skew negative (41.7% negative vs. 37.8% positive; AFINN −0.0113; The Guardian 68.15% negative).
- Global South headlines skew positive (51.5% positive vs. 22.7% negative; AFINN +0.102; China Daily 71.01% positive).
- Mann–Whitney test confirms a significant North–South sentiment difference (W=4,157,516; p<0.001).
- NRC analysis shows fear (17.85%) and trust (21.03%) coexisting in the North, while the South emphasizes anticipation (16.90%).
- Outlet-level variation is notable (e.g., digital-economy framing dominates in the Korea Herald and Toronto Star; geopolitics in The Straits Times).
- Post-2022 coverage expanded from present harms to existential/AGI risks amplified by elite open letters.
