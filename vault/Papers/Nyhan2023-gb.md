# Like-minded sources on Facebook are prevalent but not polarizing

> Nyhan, B., Settle, J., Thorson, E., Wojcieszak, M., Barberá, P., Chen, A. Y., Allcott, H., Brown, T., Crespo-Tenorio, A., Dimmery, D., Freelon, D., Gentzkow, M., González-Bailón, S., Guess, A. ## Summary

This paper combines population-scale behavioral measurement with a large preregistered field experiment to test the widespread claim that Facebook echo chambers drive political polarization. Using exposure data from the entire population of active US adult Facebook users during the 2020 election, the authors show that while a majority of content people see comes from politically like-minded sources, political and news content is only a small fraction of overall exposure and extreme echo chambers are relatively uncommon. In a three-month experiment with 23,377 consenting users, an algorithmic intervention that cut like-minded exposure by about a third meaningfully changed feed composition but produced precisely estimated null effects on eight preregistered attitudinal outcomes. The authors argue that social media content may reflect users' existing identities more than it shapes their views, undercutting the popular narrative that reducing congenial exposure is a remedy for polarization.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic, behavioral (non–self-report) measurement of like-minded exposure across the entire US adult Facebook population, using internal platform data.
- Rare large-scale causal evidence from a real-world field experiment on an operating platform on whether reducing like-minded exposure affects polarization.
- Demonstrates that algorithmic changes reshape feed composition without automatically increasing cross-cutting exposure or shifting attitudes.
- Precisely estimated null results with tight equivalence bounds, methodologically distinguishing genuine nulls from underpowered studies.
- Establishes a model of industry–academic collaboration with preregistration and academic control over analysis and publication (part of the 2020 Facebook and Instagram Election Study).

## Methods

The descriptive component classified sources as like-minded, cross-cutting, or neither for the full population of 231 million monthly active US adult users (Q3–Q4 2020), using an internal Facebook ideology classifier. The causal component was a preregistered, block-randomized multi-wave field experiment (24 September–23 December 2020) with 23,377 consenting users. The treatment downranked all content (not just political) from friends, Pages, and groups predicted to share the participant's political leaning. Analysis combined on-platform behavioral data with a five-wave survey (fieldwork by NORC), using OLS with HC2 standard errors, lasso-selected covariates, survey weights for population average treatment effects, false-discovery-rate adjustment, and equivalence-bounds tests.

## Findings

- The median user received 50.4% of content from like-minded sources versus 14.7% from cross-cutting sources; civic and news content were small shares of exposure (medians 6.9% and 6.7%).
- Extreme echo chambers were uncommon: only 20.6% of users got over 75% of exposures from like-minded sources, while 23.1% got less than 25%.
- The treatment cut like-minded exposure from 53.7% to 36.2% (0.77 s.d.), but the increase was mostly in "neither" content (25.6% to 35.9%) rather than cross-cutting content (20.7% to 27.9%).
- The intervention incidentally reduced exposure to uncivil content, slur words, and misinformation repeat offenders.
- Engagement rate with like-minded content actually rose even as total like-minded engagement fell.
- No measurable effects on eight preregistered attitudinal outcomes; seven of eight point estimates were within ±0.03 s.d., with effects of ±0.12 s.d. or larger ruled out, and no significant heterogeneity across 272 subgroup tests.

## Connections

This paper is a central node in the empirical literature challenging simple echo-chamber and filter-bubble accounts of polarization, extending [[Bakshy2015-rn]], [[Guess2021-ym]], and [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]], all Facebook-based studies from the same research program.
