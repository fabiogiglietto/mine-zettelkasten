# Answer engines and other communication partners

> Esposito, E. (2026). Answer engines and other communication partners. *Communication Theory*. https://doi.org/10.1093/ct/qtaf036
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/ct/qtaf036)

## Summary

Elena Esposito reframes large language models not as advanced artificial intelligence but as **communication partners** — "answer engines" that participate in social communication rather than think. Drawing on media theory and the sociology of communication, she argues that the dominant AI-centric and cognitive framings obscure the actual social role LLMs play. Instead of asking whether machines are intelligent, she suggests we should ask how they function as interlocutors in the management and circulation of information. Situating LLMs within a long genealogy of information-management technologies — archives, catalogs, libraries — the paper shows both the continuities with prior infrastructures and the distinctive communicative dynamics these systems introduce, and uses this reframing to clarify the ethical and operational challenges they pose.

## Key Contributions

- Offers a media-theoretical reframing of LLMs as **communication partners** rather than intelligent agents.
- Connects LLMs to a broader historical genealogy of information-management tools (archives, catalogs, libraries).
- Provides conceptual grounding for addressing the ethical and operational challenges of LLM use, derived from the communication lens rather than an AI-safety or cognitive lens.

## Methods

Conceptual and theoretical analysis grounded in media theory and the sociology of communication. The argument proceeds by comparatively situating LLMs alongside historical information-management technologies, extending Esposito's ongoing project of treating algorithmic systems as participants in communication rather than as artificial minds.

## Findings

- Interpreting LLMs as "answer engines" and communication partners reveals dimensions of their social impact that AI-centric framings render invisible.
- There are genuine continuities between LLMs and prior information infrastructures, but LLMs also introduce distinctive communicative dynamics that mark a departure from static archives or catalogs.
- Treating LLMs as interlocutors — rather than as intelligent agents — changes how we should understand the ethical and operational stakes of their deployment.

## Connections

This paper sits within a cluster of work on AI, social theory, and trust; its reframing of algorithmic systems as communicative participants provides a conceptual counterpoint to more empirical studies of how people relate to and rely on such systems. Among the papers under these topics, none directly share Esposito's media-theoretical treatment of LLMs as communication partners, so the connections here are thematic rather than tightly intellectual.
