# Answer engines and other communication partners

> Esposito, E. (2026). Answer engines and other communication partners. *Communication Theory*. https://doi.org/10.1093/ct/qtaf036
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/ct/qtaf036)

## Summary

Esposito argues that large language models are best understood not as artificial intelligences but as communication partners — "answer engines" that participate in social communication without thinking. Drawing on media theory and the sociology of communication, she contends that an AI- or cognition-centric framing obscures what is actually novel and consequential about LLMs. Situating them in a longer genealogy of information management technologies (archives, catalogs, libraries), the paper reframes both their continuities with prior media and the distinctive ethical and operational challenges they raise when treated as interlocutors.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes LLMs as communication partners rather than intelligent agents, shifting the analytic register from cognition to communication.
- Places LLMs within a genealogy of information management infrastructures, highlighting both continuity and rupture with archives, catalogs, and libraries.
- Provides conceptual scaffolding for identifying ethical and operational problems specific to LLM-mediated communication (e.g., accountability, reliability, interpretability of answers).
- Extends media-theoretical vocabulary — including the notion of the "answer engine" — as an alternative to dominant AI discourse.

## Methods

Conceptual and theoretical analysis rather than empirical study. Esposito draws on media theory and systems-theoretic sociology of communication, and proceeds through comparative reasoning that situates LLMs alongside earlier information management technologies to isolate what is genuinely new about their communicative role.

## Findings

- Treating LLMs as communication partners reveals social dynamics — trust, address, response, interpretation — that AI framings tend to overlook.
- LLMs continue the historical function of information management tools but reconfigure it: they generate situated answers rather than provide access to stored items.
- The shift from "finding" to "answering" changes users' epistemic posture toward mediated information and introduces new ethical liabilities.
- Framing LLMs as interlocutors rather than minds clarifies why familiar categories (intelligence, understanding, error) map poorly onto their outputs.

## Connections

This piece offers a media-theoretical counterpoint to more empirical or effects-oriented work on generative AI's societal role, complementing conceptual reflections on AI's cultural and epistemic status such as [[Manovich2026-ih]] and [[Hepp2026-oi]]. Its concern with LLMs as answering interlocutors resonates with studies of persuasion and belief change in dialogue with models, notably [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Hackenburg2025-dj]], and with work on chatbots as information sources like [[Dierickx2026-tw]].
