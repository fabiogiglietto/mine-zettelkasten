# The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure

> de León, E., Kristensen, J. B., Makhortykh, M., Mayerhöffer, E., & Adam, S. (2025). The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure. *SocArXiv*. https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1)

## Summary

This paper introduces **networked exposure** as a conceptual expansion of how scholars understand online political information exposure. The authors argue that prior research has focused too narrowly on *direct* encounters with overtly problematic content—misinformation, conspiracy theories, hyperpartisan outlets—while overlooking a different pathway: ordinary, seemingly neutral content such as mainstream news stories or Wikipedia articles can be circulated and politically "weaponized" within extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks. The central claim is that content acquires political meaning not only through its inherent character but through the networks that embed and circulate it. The paper is primarily a conceptual/theoretical contribution, reframing the study of exposure to attend to networked circulation rather than just audience-content encounters.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **networked exposure** as a distinct mode of political information exposure.
- Broadens the scope of exposure research beyond overtly problematic content to include the weaponization of mainstream and neutral material.
- Provides a conceptual framework for studying how ordinary content is politically repurposed through circulation within extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks.
- Offers a corrective to the field's prevailing emphasis on direct exposure to problematic content.

## Methods

Conceptual and theoretical development: the paper defines and elaborates the notion of networked exposure, situating it within existing literatures on online political communication, misinformation, and conspiracy content. Detailed empirical methodology is not recoverable from the abstract alone.

## Findings

- The paper's central analytical move is a reframing rather than an empirical result: exposure should be understood to include the networked circulation of ordinary content.
- Seemingly neutral content gains political significance through the partisan networks in which it is embedded and shared.
- Detailed empirical findings are not available from the abstract.

## Connections

This conceptual intervention connects to work on how coordinated networks circulate and repurpose content, including [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] and [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] on coordinated link sharing, and to studies of how conspiracy-aligned and extremist communities operate online such as [[Marwick2025-ov]] and [[Tuters2026-vo]]. The emphasis on reframing exposure beyond problematic content also speaks to broader debates about the actual prevalence and character of exposure addressed in [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Budak2024-ef]].
