# The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure

> de León, E., Kristensen, J. B., Makhortykh, M., Mayerhöffer, E., & Adam, S. (2025). The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure. *SocArXiv*. https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1)

## Summary

This paper introduces **networked exposure** as a conceptual expansion of how scholars understand online political information exposure. The authors argue that dominant research has focused too narrowly on *direct* encounters with overtly problematic content—misinformation, conspiracy theories, hyperpartisan outlets—while overlooking how ordinary content, such as mainstream news reports and Wikipedia articles, is circulated and weaponized within extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks. The central claim is that content acquires political meaning not solely from its inherent character but from the networks that circulate it. Networked exposure is offered as a corrective framework that shifts analytic attention from the audience-content encounter to the networked circulation that repurposes seemingly neutral material for political ends.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **networked exposure** as a distinct mode of political information exposure.
- Broadens exposure research beyond overtly problematic content to include the weaponization of mainstream and neutral material.
- Provides a conceptual framework for studying how content is politically repurposed through circulation in extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks.
- Reframes exposure as a property of networked circulation rather than only of the content itself.

## Methods

Primarily a conceptual/theoretical contribution: the paper develops and defines the notion of networked exposure, positioning it against prior direct-exposure paradigms. Full methodological detail is not available from the abstract alone; the core work is analytical reframing rather than empirical measurement.

## Findings

- Detailed empirical findings are not available from the abstract.
- The central analytical result is the reframing of exposure to include the networked circulation of ordinary content.
- Ordinary content (traditional news, Wikipedia) can be transformed into politically charged material through its embedding in partisan networks.
