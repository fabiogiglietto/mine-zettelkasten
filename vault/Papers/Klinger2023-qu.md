# From the fringes into mainstream politics: intermediary networks and movement-party coordination of a global anti-immigration campaign in Germany

> Klinger, U., Bennett, W. L., Knüpfer, C. B., Martini, F., & Zhang, X. (2023). From the fringes into mainstream politics: intermediary networks and movement-party coordination of a global anti-immigration campaign in Germany. *Information, Communication & Society*, *26*, 1890–1907. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2022.2050415
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2022.2050415)

## Summary

This paper traces how extremist anti-immigration ideas moved from a fringe movement into mainstream German politics via intermediary networks of aligned supporters and far-right media. Using the 2018 "Migrationspakt Stoppen" campaign against the UN Global Compact for Migration, the authors show how content originating with the small, state-surveilled Identitarian Movement was "laundered" of its extremist provenance as it diffused through low-level AfD activists and far-right news sites, ultimately enabling the AfD leadership to appropriate the campaign as its own official communication. The central argument is that indirect network bridging lets parties take up sensitive content while avoiding formal, visible coordination with extremist sources—mutual benefit emerges from a network structure rather than from an explicit alliance, which complicates both scholarly definitions of coordinated action and regulatory efforts against harmful speech.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces and empirically demonstrates **"source laundering"** via aligned intermediary networks as a mechanism for parties to appropriate extremist content without formal coordination.
- Distinguishes **intermediary networks** (strategic connectors bridging separated networks) from **peripheral networks** (snowball distributors), extending prior work on movement-to-society issue flows.
- Provides a multi-platform dynamic network methodology for tracing non-linear, staged content handoffs across media ecologies.
- Illuminates general challenges of regulating harmful content in hybrid media systems where no single actor is the core mobilizer.

## Methods

The study combines dynamic network analysis and time-series analysis of multi-platform content flows from 11 July–15 December 2018, tracking the terms "Migrationspakt" and "Migrationspakt Stoppen." Twitter data were gathered via Crimson Hexagon (506,079 tweets from 43,579 accounts, ~60% of relevant traffic), with daily random samples during the high-volume period. Accounts were classified by actor type (mass media, far-right news/information sites (RNIS), low- and high-level AfD, Identitarian Movement, and top-100 superspreaders) using manual and dictionary-based coding. YouTube data were collected via `tuber` (1,606 videos), far-right/mainstream media via Media Cloud and Newspaper3k scraping, and AfD party content directly. Bot detection used Botometer (threshold 0.75), with network modeling and ForceAtlas2 visualization in Gephi.

## Findings

- GCM activity was sporadic until Identitarian leader Martin Sellner's 16 September 2018 campaign video and petition; the @migrationspakt account became the most-retweeted node within a day.
- Superspreaders were only 0.27% of nodes but drove 21% of interactions, both amplifying and bridging otherwise separate networks; automated accounts were a small minority (5.9% of accounts, 3.4% of tweets).
- By early October the AfD launched a near-identical mirror campaign (switching the URL from .info to .de, reusing the "ticking clock" imagery and "replacement migration" framing), followed by a 1 November parliamentary petition exceeding 100,000 signatures.
- Far-right media (RNIS) played a dual role—first as intermediaries bridging extremists and the party, then as amplifiers—more evident in original tweets than retweets.
- The network grew to 23% of its final size after the October AfD campaign and nearly doubled after the November petition; mass media coverage spiked late but largely omitted the "Stoppen" frame, focusing instead on the CDU–CSU conflict.

## Connections

This paper extends the register's work on coordinated behavior by complicating what "coordination" means: its "source laundering" mechanism contrasts with approaches that detect formal coordinated inauthentic behavior through structural signals (e.g., [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]], [[Luceri2025-tr]]), since here mutual benefit arises from network structure rather than explicit alliance. It speaks directly to scholarship on the networked radical right and far-right media ecologies ([[Askanius2026-de]], [[Knupfer2025-vt]], [[Tuters2026-vo]]) and to studies of superspreaders and amplification dynamics in digital campaigns ([[Grinberg2019-ua]], [[Nizzoli2020-cf]]). Its focus on immigration framing and party appropriation also connects to work on anti-immigration messaging and electoral communication such as [[Tjaden2026-sl8e]].
