# Where&#x27;s the Evidence that Respondents Understand Your Survey Questions?

> Hinck, M., King, G., Nakamura, K., & Stewart, B. M. (2026). Where&#x27;s the Evidence that Respondents Understand Your Survey Questions?.
>

## Summary

This methodological essay challenges a foundational but usually unexamined assumption in survey research: that respondents interpret questions in the way researchers intend. The authors argue that this assumption is rarely backed by explicit evidence, creating an evidentiary gap that threatens the validity of survey-based inference. Rather than treating shared understanding as given, they contend that respondent comprehension is itself an empirical question. The paper calls for measuring and demonstrating comprehension to become a routine part of survey design, analysis, and reporting.

## Key Contributions

- Frames respondent comprehension as an underappreciated source of measurement error in surveys.
- Calls for a new methodological standard in which evidence of question understanding is explicitly demonstrated rather than assumed.
- Positions comprehension validation as a distinct research agenda within survey methodology.

## Methods

Based on the available abstract, the paper takes the form of a methodological and critical essay. ## Findings

- The paper's central observation is diagnostic: comprehension evidence is pervasively absent from standard survey practice.
- The unexamined assumption of shared understanding between researcher and respondent is treated as a validity threat rather than a benign simplification.
- No specific empirical results are reported in the abstract; the contribution is programmatic and normative.
