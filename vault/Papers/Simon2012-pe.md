# Generative AI and News Report 2026: From standalone tools to information infrastructure

> Simon, F., Nielsen, R. K., & Fletcher, R. (2012). Generative AI and News Report 2026: From standalone tools to information infrastructure.
>
> [View paper](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Generative%20AI%20and%20News%20Report%202026%3A%20From%20standalone%20tools%20to%20information%20infrastructure)

## Summary

This Reuters Institute report presents the third annual wave of a cross-national survey on public engagement with generative AI, covering nine high-income democracies (Argentina, Australia, Denmark, Finland, France, Japan, Norway, the UK, and USA). Its central argument is that generative AI has, within four years of ChatGPT's launch, transitioned from a standalone tool into pervasive **information infrastructure** — half of respondents now intentionally use AI weekly, while most also encounter it incidentally embedded in legacy platforms. Yet despite rapid adoption, public attitudes toward AI in news remain cautious, negative, and strikingly stable across three years, with people far more comfortable with human-made than AI-made journalism.

## Key Contributions

- Longitudinal, cross-national survey evidence (three waves, nine countries) tracking the mainstreaming of generative AI in high-income democracies.
- Introduces and applies the concept of **"incidental AI exposure"** (borrowed from news-use research) to describe AI embedded in legacy platforms.
- Documents generative AI's emergence as an **information intermediary** and a persistent **comfort gap** between human- and AI-made news.
- Provides a sector-by-sector map of public expectations about AI's impact via the ability-benevolence-integrity (ABI) trust framework.
- Supplies a benchmark measure of public AI literacy using the SAIL4ALL scale and relates it to use and trust.

## Methods

Online survey fielded by YouGov for the Reuters Institute (14 Aug–28 Sep 2026), ~2,000 respondents per country, using nationally representative quota sampling weighted to census/industry targets. As a non-probability design, differences of ±2pp are treated as non-meaningful. Trend analysis is restricted to the six countries present in all three waves. The study applies the **SAIL4ALL AI literacy scale** (52 true/false items, matrix-sampled) and the **ability-benevolence-integrity trust framework** to assess sectoral perceptions. The authors disclose AI assistance (ChatGPT 6, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.1) for analysis, copy-editing, and text suggestions under human review.

## Findings

- Weekly standalone AI use rose from 34% (2025) to 51% (2026); daily use nearly doubled year-on-year (7%→16%→28%).
- ChatGPT remains market leader (22%→34% weekly), with Gemini (11%→24%), Copilot (6%→11%), and Claude (1%→7%) growing; weekly users access ~two systems on average.
- Incidental exposure is widespread: 57% saw AI search summaries in the past week; only 20% saw AI nowhere; 49% noticed at least one AI feature on news sites/apps.
- 18% have access to paid AI — identical to the share with paid news access; AI agents remain niche (67% non-use weekly).
- Using AI for information grew from 11% (2024) to 38% (2026); AI news use grew from 3% to 9%.
- Among information users, trust in outputs is high (75% factual, 76% advice, 72% news), but **convenience (64%)** and interactivity (56%), not reliability (21%), drive use.
- The public is optimistic about AI use by scientists, healthcare professionals, and AI companies, but pessimistic about governments, social media, news media, and especially politicians; honesty is the weakest-rated dimension everywhere.
- The comfort gap is large and sticky: only 12% are comfortable with fully AI-made news (19% with oversight) versus 67% for human-made; people expect AI news to be cheaper (+34pp) but less transparent (-10pp) and less trustworthy (-17pp).
- The sharpest attitudinal divide over AI's impact is between intense daily users (optimistic) and never-users (pessimistic) — larger than gaps by age, politics, or literacy.

## Connections

This report sits within the Reuters Institute's broader program on digital news consumption and AI, directly complementing [[Fletcher2026-lv]] on public perceptions of AI in the information environment.
