# Exploring the Zombie Internet: Anatomy of Three Deceptive Information Operations on Facebook

> Giada, M., Fabio, G., Anwesha, C., Massimo, T., & Samuel, O. (2026). Exploring the Zombie Internet: Anatomy of Three Deceptive Information Operations on Facebook. *MEDIASCAPES JOURNAL*.
>
> [View paper](https://ora.uniurb.it/handle/11576/2779031)

## Summary

This paper proposes **Deceptive Information Operations (DIOs)** as an analytical framework that transcends conventional false-news taxonomies. Rather than centring misinformation, it defines manipulative activity by the convergence of three dimensions: deceptive intent in content or identity, strategic exploitation of platform affordances, and (real or simulated) multi-account coordination. Through an automated detection workflow on Facebook, the authors examine three structurally similar but motivationally distinct cases — a state-aligned pro-Putin propaganda network, a coordinated online gambling promotion campaign, and a network of poorly moderated groups distributing adult content and fraudulent links. The central argument is that despite differences in actor, content, and intent, all three share a common underlying structural logic that constitutes a "Zombie Internet" condition, exposing systemic vulnerabilities in platform enforcement frameworks that focus too narrowly on content inauthenticity.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces and empirically grounds the **DIO** framework, synthesising deception theory, strategic information operations, and Meta's Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour (CIB) policy.
- Demonstrates empirically that operations differing in actor, content, and motivation (epistemic, economic, social-normative harm) share a common structural logic, making motivational distinctions analytically secondary.
- Identifies gaps in platform governance frameworks (CIB, Coordinated Social Harm) that address content inauthenticity rather than the structural conditions enabling manipulation at scale.
- Presents an automated, cyclical detection workflow (VeraAI Alert System) usable by researchers and civil society.
- Among the first studies to employ the **Meta Content Library** at scale, documenting both its potential and limitations (e.g., post download caps).
- Operationalises the "Zombie Internet" as a systemic ecosystem condition tied to platform-level systemic risks recognised by EU regulators.

## Methods

- **Detection:** VeraAI Alert System, a CrowdTangle-based dynamic tracking workflow monitoring coordinated sharing among problematic accounts.
- **Seeding:** 1,225 Pages/groups that coordinatedly shared at least four URLs from a corpus of 36,091 fact-checker-flagged web pages (2017–2022), with iterative network expansion.
- **Coordination detection:** the CooRTweet tool, identifying synchronous sharing via identical URLs, image/text similarity, and temporal clustering.
- **Sampling:** purposive selection of three cases from 17 detected networks for typological and mechanism diversity.
- **Data:** account metadata plus the 500 most-viewed posts per network via the Meta Content Library (content Oct 2023–Aug 2024; tracked Nov 2024–Jan 2025).
- **Analysis:** inductive category development via constructivist grounded theory across three theoretically motivated dimensions (identity management, behavioural coordination, content manipulation); 30% cross-coding.

## Findings

- **Pro-Putin network** (27 groups): coordinated naming, synchronised name changes before geopolitical events, and synchronised posting (an identical image across 10 groups within three minutes; a Zelenskyy meme exceeding 1.1M views); blends benign content with propaganda and mixes coordinated seeding with genuine engagement.
- **Gambling network** (260 groups, 30K–600K members): almost entirely synthetic — AI-generated visuals, bot-inflated comments (a post with 169.1K views and 44.1K comments but only 7 reactions), brandjacking (Orion Stars, Juwa); 368 of the top 500 posts removed, implying a maximise-exposure-before-takedown strategy.
- **Unmoderated groups** (222 groups): exploited weak moderation, renaming accounts (one 13 times), disguising adult content and fraudulent links behind innocuous blurbs, and using "listening to" status posts linking to empty Musician/Band pages to evade CIB detection; a false Coca-Cola-contains-alcohol claim reached 3.5M views.
- Across all cases: concealment of intent behind benign content, coordinated sharing to game algorithmic ranking and simulate organic popularity, and deployment of synthetic/AI material.
- The three cases diverge in engagement mode (seeding + real engagement; near-total synthetic; inadvertent amplification), producing distinct harm types.
- Reporting to Meta yielded acknowledgement but limited action, consistent with enforcement categories that fail to recognise such operations as harmful.
