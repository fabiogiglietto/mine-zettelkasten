# Exploring the Zombie Internet: Anatomy of Three Deceptive Information Operations on Facebook

> Giada, M., Fabio, G., Anwesha, C., Massimo, T., & Samuel, O. (2026). Exploring the Zombie Internet: Anatomy of Three Deceptive Information Operations on Facebook. *MEDIASCAPES JOURNAL*.
>
> [View paper](https://ora.uniurb.it/handle/11576/2779031)

## Summary

This paper proposes **Deceptive Information Operations (DIOs)** as a unifying analytical framework for studying manipulative activity on social media that goes beyond narrow misinformation or "fake news" taxonomies. Rather than relying on falsity of content, DIOs are defined by the convergence of three dimensions: deceptive intent, strategic exploitation of platform affordances, and coordinated (real or simulated) multi-account participation. The authors ground the concept empirically by tracing three structurally distinct but mechanically similar Facebook operations — a pro-Putin propaganda network, a coordinated gambling-promotion network, and a cluster of poorly moderated public groups distributing adult content and fraud links — arguing that these disparate cases reveal a shared underlying logic they term the "Zombie Internet," in which engagement-driven ranking and inconsistent moderation blur the line between authentic and inauthentic activity.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces and empirically substantiates DIOs as a synthesis of Deception Theory, Strategic Information Operations, and Meta's Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour (CIB) policy category.
- Demonstrates empirically that operations differing in actor, content, and motive nonetheless share a common structural logic, making motivational distinctions analytically secondary.
- Identifies specific gaps in platform governance frameworks (CIB, coordinated sharing) that focus too narrowly on content inauthenticity, missing structural enabling conditions.
- Showcases a scalable, cyclical automated detection workflow (VeraAI Alert System) usable by researchers and civil society.
- One of the first large-scale scholarly uses of the Meta Content Library, documenting both its analytical value and practical limitations.
- Operationalizes the "Zombie Internet" as a systemic ecosystem condition, linking it to EU-recognized platform systemic risk categories.

## Methods

The authors used the vera.ai/VeraAI Alert System, a CrowdTangle-derived workflow, to monitor coordinated sharing among a seed list of 1,225 Facebook Pages/groups previously linked to sharing fact-checker-flagged URLs (2017–2022). Coordination was detected via the CooRTweet tool (synchronous URL sharing, content similarity, temporal clustering), producing 17 candidate networks from which three cases were purposively sampled for typological and mechanism diversity. Data (account metadata plus top 500 posts per network, Oct 2023–Aug 2024) were drawn from the Meta Content Library. Analysis combined constructivist grounded theory with three theory-driven dimensions (identity management, behavioral coordination, content manipulation) and inductive coding, cross-validated by dual coding of 30% of the data.

## Findings

- The pro-Putin network (27 groups) used synchronized name changes and near-simultaneous cross-group posting, blending polarizing propaganda with benign content and mixing coordinated seeding with genuine engagement.
- The gambling network (260 groups) relied almost entirely on synthetic activity — AI-generated visuals, bot-inflated comments, brandjacking — with high post-removal rates suggesting a maximize-exposure-before-takedown strategy.
- The unmoderated-groups network (222 groups) exploited weak moderation via frequent renaming, disguised adult/fraud content behind innocuous text, and false claims that reached millions of views.
- All three cases relied on concealment of intent, coordinated sharing to game algorithmic ranking, and synthetic/AI-generated material, despite differing engagement modes and harm types.
- Reporting the findings to Meta produced acknowledgement but little substantive enforcement action, illustrating that current CIB-style frameworks fail to recognize such operations as harmful.
