# Understanding Coordinated and Inauthentic Link Sharing Behavior on Facebook in the Run-up to 2018 General Election and 2019 European Election in Italy

> Giglietto, F., Righetti, N., & Marino, G. (2019). Understanding Coordinated and Inauthentic Link Sharing Behavior on Facebook in the Run-up to 2018 General Election and 2019 European Election in Italy. *Center for Open Science*. https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/3jteh
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/3jteh)

## Summary

This paper tackles the conceptual vagueness of Facebook's "coordinated inauthentic behavior" by grounding the term in prior scholarship on *coordination* and *authenticity* and translating it into an operationalizable empirical framework. Studying two Italian electoral periods — the 2018 general election and the 2019 European election — the authors introduce the notion of **coordinated link sharing behavior (CLSB)**: networks of pages, groups, and profiles that share identical political news stories within very short time windows. They demonstrate that such networks can be detected at scale from engagement data, and that in the Italian case these coordinated actors disproportionately amplified far-right and anti-immigration content while relying on sources already blacklisted by fact-checkers.

## Key Contributions

- A literature-grounded, operationalizable definition of "coordinated inauthentic behavior," making Facebook's loose platform-policy language researchable.
- Introduction of the concept and detection method of **coordinated link sharing behavior** based on shared URLs within short time windows.
- An original methodological combination of CrowdTangle API data with curated datasets of Italian political news.
- Empirical evidence of coordinated disinformation networks across two distinct Italian electoral contexts.
- A link between detected coordination and real-world propaganda effects and fact-checker-flagged sources.

## Methods

The authors begin with a conceptual analysis to derive their definition, distinguishing *coordination* (detectable via synchronized sharing) from *inauthenticity* (assessed via self-presentation). Empirically, they draw Facebook engagement data from the CrowdTangle API and cross-reference it against two curated datasets of Italian political news from the 2018 and 2019 campaign periods. Entities sharing the same news stories within very short time spans are flagged as coordinated; qualitative inspection of their profiles then assesses authenticity (e.g., entertainment pages sharing political content). A comparative analysis contrasts engagement for coordinated-network news against other news, and shared sources are checked against fact-checker blacklists.

## Findings

- 10 coordinated networks (28 entities) detected in the 2018 general election period.
- 50 coordinated networks (143 entities) detected in the 2019 European election period.
- Some coordinated entities were overtly political; others presented as entertainment venues despite sharing political content — warranting the "inauthentic" label.
- News shared by coordinated networks received higher Facebook engagement than other news in the dataset.
- Much shared content amplified anti-immigration and far-right propaganda.
- Several outlets circulated by these networks had already been blacklisted by fact-checkers.

## Connections

This is a foundational statement of the coordinated link sharing behavior research program, directly extended and refined in later methodological and empirical work such as [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]], as well as the Italian electoral studies [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]].
