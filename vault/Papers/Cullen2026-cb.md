# Seeing like an API: Platform-mediated research and the politics of access

> Cullen, Z. N., Ellison, N. B., & Pasquetto, I. V. (2026). Seeing like an API: Platform-mediated research and the politics of access. *Big Data & Society*, *13*. https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517261461463
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517261461463)

## Summary

This paper examines the experiences of academic researchers who relied on Meta's CrowdTangle (CT) API to access and analyse social media platform data prior to the tool's discontinuation. Drawing on interviews with 20 academic users, the authors develop the conceptual framework of "Seeing like an API" — a deliberate echo of James Scott's "Seeing Like a State" — to argue that APIs are not neutral pipes delivering data but active intermediaries that shape the epistemic conditions of research. The paper contends that CrowdTangle's technical design, governance rules, and the knowledge networks surrounding it jointly determined what researchers could observe, ask, and conclude about platform activity, thereby foregrounding the politics of access in platform-dependent scholarship.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the **"Seeing like an API"** framework for theorizing how infrastructural intermediaries govern knowledge production in social media research.
- Provides empirical, interview-based insight into researchers' lived experiences with CrowdTangle before its sunsetting.
- Advances critical scholarship on the politics of data access and the precarity of platform dependence in academic work.

## Methods

Qualitative interview study with 20 academic users of the CrowdTangle API. From analysis of participants' accounts, the authors inductively develop the "Seeing like an API" framework, attending specifically to three interacting dimensions: the API's technical design, its governance rules, and the platform-mediated knowledge networks that surrounded its use.

## Findings

- Researchers encountered significant challenges in accessing, interpreting, and assessing data obtained via the CT API.
- Participants adapted their research practices to work around the API's constraints and limitations.
- The API's design and governance actively shaped the scope of observable platform activity and constrained the conclusions researchers could legitimately draw.
- The eventual sunsetting of CrowdTangle exposed the deep power asymmetries and precarity inherent in platform-dependent research.

## Connections

This paper's critical, infrastructural reading of APIs as epistemic gatekeepers connects to broader platform-governance critiques such as [[Gillespie2010-sla2]], [[van-Dijck2018-up]], and [[Gillespie2022-jx]], which theorize platforms as governing intermediaries rather than neutral conduits. Its empirical focus on CrowdTangle as a research tool and its discontinuation relates closely to work on researcher data access and the aftermath of API shutdowns, including [[Freelon2018-ao]], [[Bruns2019-nr]], and [[Tonneau2025-bv]].
