# Seeing like an API: Platform-mediated research and the politics of access

> Cullen, Z. N., Ellison, N. B., & Pasquetto, I. V. (2026). Seeing like an API: Platform-mediated research and the politics of access. *Big Data & Society*, *13*. https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517261461463
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517261461463)

## Summary

This paper examines the constraints that academic researchers encountered while using Meta's CrowdTangle (CT) API to study social media platform data, prior to the tool's discontinuation. Drawing on interviews with 20 academic users, the authors develop the conceptual framework of "Seeing like an API" — an explicit evocation of Scott's *Seeing Like a State* — to theorize how an API's technical design, governance rules, and platform-mediated knowledge networks jointly structure what researchers can observe, investigate, and conclude. Rather than treating APIs as neutral conduits of data, the paper argues they are active mediators that shape the epistemic boundaries of platform research, and it foregrounds the politics of access and power asymmetries inherent in platform-dependent scholarship.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the **"Seeing like an API"** framework for understanding how infrastructural intermediaries govern knowledge production in social media research.
- Provides empirical, interview-based insight into researcher experiences with CrowdTangle before its sunsetting.
- Advances critical scholarship on the politics of data access and platform dependence in academic research.

## Methods

- Qualitative interview study with 20 academic users of the CrowdTangle API.
- Inductive development of a conceptual framework derived from analysis of researchers' lived experiences.
- Analysis focused on three intertwined dimensions: technical design, governance rules, and platform-mediated knowledge networks, and how each affected research practice.

## Findings

- Researchers faced substantial challenges in accessing, interpreting, and assessing the reliability of data obtained through the CT API.
- Participants adapted their practices to work within the API's constraints, reshaping their questions to fit what the tool allowed.
- The API's design and governance shaped the scope of observable platform activity and, in turn, the conclusions researchers could legitimately draw.
- The discontinuation of tools like CrowdTangle exposed the precarity and power asymmetries built into platform-dependent research.
