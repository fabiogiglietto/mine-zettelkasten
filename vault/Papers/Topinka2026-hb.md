# Feed them slop: Alignment, attunement, and the affective lure of slopaganda

> Topinka, R. (2026). Feed them slop: Alignment, attunement, and the affective lure of slopaganda. *European Journal of Cultural Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494261468632
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494261468632)

## Summary

Topinka theorises **slopaganda** (also "agitslop") — AI-generated political content, exemplified by the "Trump Gaza" video and the "Kirkinator" meme videos, that is obviously fake yet affectively real. The core argument is that slopaganda does not work like deepfakes, which lure viewers into mistaking synthetic content for reality; instead it operates as an affective *lure* that hooks fickle attention (including hostile attention) by attuning to the moods and affordances of social media feeds. Drawing on rhetorical theory and affect theory, Topinka contends that AI models — through training, labelling, and alignment via RLHF — are optimised for rhetorical attunement, functioning as "ideological attunement machines" that fold into and intensify existing ideological and technical formations rather than disrupting them. Even when incoherent, slop remains political because to circulate it must conform to the regimes of legibility and plausibility conditioned by digital culture.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes AI slop from an error or excess into an **ambient rhetoric** highly attuned to dominant norms and technical incentives.
- Introduces the concept of AI models as **"ideological attunement machines,"** extending critique beyond the standard "bias in the training data" argument to alignment and fine-tuning practices.
- Distinguishes **deepfakes** (which trade on mistaken reality) from **slopaganda** (obviously fake but affectively real).
- Bridges rhetorical theory (ambient rhetoric, affective lures) with the political economy of AI (RLHF, alignment, foundation models as platforms).
- Offers an analytical method attuned to how affective lures coordinate ideology even when their political destinations are unpredictable.

## Methods

A conceptual and theoretical essay in cultural studies (no empirical datasets). Topinka performs close rhetorical and cultural readings of specific artefacts — the "Trump Gaza" video, DHS/White House slop posts, the "Kirkinator" Agartha videos, AI Blackface content, and an Iran-produced "Lego Trump" video — synthesising Rickert's *ambient rhetoric*, Sampson's affective *lure*, Massumi's affect/emotion distinction, Noordenbos and Tuters' *ambient propaganda*, and Meyer's *platform realism*, alongside critical accounts of AI industry practices (alignment, RLHF, labelling).

## Findings

- The "Trump Gaza" video began as an LA studio's joke testing AI generation speed and spread via Mel Gibson to Trump — showing slop propagates through shitpost/engagement logics rather than deliberate propaganda design.
- Slop's targets are disproportionately women and racial others (immigrants, Black protestors, Black lawmakers), giving form to racist and colonial fantasies.
- Training and labelling encode rhetorical attunements: image datasets were labelled overwhelmingly by workers from "WEIRD" countries; structured platform descriptions (e.g. Shopify) bias training data toward consumer expectations.
- AI alignment traces to Bostrom's paperclip scenario and relies on hyperexploited RLHF workers, mostly in the Global South, who attune outputs to human expectations.
- AI Blackface slop revives minstrelsy tropes and the "erotic life of racism," synthesising pre-existing racist fantasies into nostalgic new outputs.
- The "Kirkinator" videos coordinate affect via esoteric Nazi "Agartha" imagery while refusing coherent claims, which explains their appeal even to left-wing meme accounts — and such ambient archives have framed real-world violence (e.g. "for Agartha" at a Jakarta mosque attack).
