# Networked intimacy. Intimacy and friendship among Italian Facebook users

> [!note] Corrected
> - Correction (2016-07-19): [10.1080/1369118x.2016.1211636](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2016.1211636)

> Farci, M., Rossi, L., Artieri, G. B., & Giglietto, F. (2017). Networked intimacy. Intimacy and friendship among Italian Facebook users. *Information, Communication & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2016.1203970
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2016.1203970)

## Summary

This paper advances the concept of **networked intimacy** to describe how intimacy on Facebook is achieved not through private self-disclosure but through *public, collaborative practices* that depend on the cooperation of one's friendship network. Drawing on qualitative interviews with 120 Italian Facebook users, the authors argue that intimate relationships are negotiated in semi-public symbolic spaces where meaning is deliberately layered so that only intended audiences can decode it. The paper positions itself against narcissistic readings of social media (Turkle, Lasch, Bauman), reframing public intimacy as a relational, community-oriented resource for friendship maintenance and social inclusion.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes **networked intimacy** as a theoretical framework synthesizing and extending prior work on Facebook self-disclosure.
- Identifies and operationalizes five collaborative disclosure strategies, notably adding **Tagging** and **Liking** to previously discussed categories.
- Broadens the geographic scope of SNS intimacy research beyond predominantly Anglo-American samples by providing evidence from the Italian context.
- Reframes public intimacy as relational and community-oriented rather than narcissistic self-promotion.
- Bridges sociological theories of pure relationships and friendship (Giddens, Jamieson, Pahl) with platform studies and affordance theory.

## Methods

A qualitative study based on 118 semi-structured interviews (plus 2 excluded pilots) with Italian Facebook users conducted in 2013, part of a national multi-year PRIN-funded project directed by Giovanni Boccia Artieri across four Italian research units. Recruitment used guided snowball sampling drawn from participants' own Facebook contact lists, stratified by age (13–54) and gender to match Italian Facebook penetration. Interviews averaged 45 minutes, were conducted in participants' homes while they browsed their own profiles, and were analyzed using Strauss and Corbin's Grounded Theory (open, axial, selective coding in Dedoose), moving from roughly 400 initial codes to broader conceptual categories.

## Findings

- Participants' central concern was managing visibility and self-presentation while avoiding undesired disclosure consequences.
- **Showing rather than telling** — visual cues and images carry layered meanings legible only to intended audiences.
- **Sharing implicit content** — quotes, lyrics, and cryptic posts encode messages for select friends (related to but distinct from social steganography).
- **Tagging** publicly marks exclusive relational events and directs content to specific individuals while keeping the post public.
- **Expectation of mutual understanding** — a tacit relational competence where close friends recognize targeted content without explicit cues.
- **Liking** serves as a low-cost signal of relational proximity, mutual recognition, and "thinking of you."
- These practices create ephemeral, semi-public symbolic spaces where intimate audiences are continually reconstituted.
- Notably, participants did not spontaneously associate "intimacy" with Facebook but described fitting practices once prompted.

## Connections

The paper's reliance on boyd's and Marwick's concepts of networked publics, context collapse, and audience management situates it directly alongside [[Boyd2026-op]] and [[Marwick2011-sl02]], whose frameworks of self-presentation under imagined audiences it extends into the domain of intimacy. Its concern with how platform affordances shape visibility and disclosure resonates with [[Bucher2017-qh]]'s work on affordances. Given the Italian Facebook sampling and the broader PRIN project, it connects to the same research program behind [[Giglietto2015-320951c4]] and [[Giglietto2017-4375de2f]].
