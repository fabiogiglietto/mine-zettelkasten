# Untrustworthy sources on Facebook and Instagram in 2020: Concentrated exposure but no attitudinal effects

## Summary

This paper leverages platform-wide data from Facebook and Instagram covering all active US adult users to answer two questions that misinformation research has struggled to address at scale: how much content from untrustworthy sources (repeat publishers of misinformation) do people actually encounter, and does that exposure change their attitudes? The authors find that average exposure in 2020 was low, but that it was heavily concentrated among a small minority of users. Crucially, experimentally reducing exposure produced no detectable attitudinal effects. The study reframes debates about misinformation's reach by separating average exposure from concentrated exposure and by supplying rare causal evidence on downstream attitudinal impact.

## Key Contributions

- Provides large-scale, platform-wide evidence on the actual frequency of exposure to untrustworthy content, filling a gap left by prior work reliant on limited or non-representative samples.
- Distinguishes analytically between low average exposure and highly concentrated exposure among a subset of users.
- Delivers causal (null) evidence on the attitudinal effects of reducing exposure, informing debates about misinformation's real-world influence.

## Methods

- Analysis of 2020 platform-wide data covering all active US adult users across Facebook and Instagram.
- Measurement of exposure to content from Pages, groups, and web domains (Facebook) and public accounts (Instagram) identified as repeat publishers of misinformation.
- An experimental/interventional design that reduced users' exposure to untrustworthy sources in order to estimate resulting changes in attitudes.

## Findings

- The average user saw relatively little untrustworthy-source content in their feed.
- Exposure was concentrated among a limited subset of users rather than evenly distributed across the population.
- Reducing exposure to untrustworthy sources produced no measurable attitudinal changes.

## Connections

This paper sits at the intersection of platform-scale measurement and the meta-science of misinformation research, and its emphasis on concentrated exposure and null attitudinal effects speaks directly to critical assessments of misinformation's overstated reach found in work like [[Budak2024-ef]] and [[Allen2025-ot]]. Its reliance on privileged platform-wide data connects it to ongoing debates over data access and the reproducibility of such collaborations, themes addressed in [[Freelon2024-sc]] and [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]]. The concentration-of-exposure result also resonates with structural accounts of how misinformation circulates among small user segments, as explored in [[Pierri2025-hm]].
