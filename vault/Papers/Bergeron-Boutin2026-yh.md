# Untrustworthy sources on Facebook and Instagram in 2020: Concentrated exposure but no attitudinal effects

## Summary

This paper leverages platform-wide data from Facebook and Instagram covering all active US adults in 2020 to measure how much content from untrustworthy sources — repeat publishers of misinformation — users actually encountered, and whether that exposure mattered for their attitudes. The authors report a two-part finding that recurs across recent misinformation research: exposure is low on average but highly concentrated among a small minority of users, and experimentally reducing that exposure produced no detectable attitudinal effects. The work argues that concerns about the pervasive attitudinal influence of misinformation on major platforms may be overstated, at least at the scale and during the period studied.

## Key Contributions

- Provides large-scale, platform-wide evidence on the frequency of exposure to untrustworthy content, moving beyond the limited or non-representative samples common in prior work.
- Distinguishes clearly between *average* exposure and *concentrated* exposure, showing the two tell very different stories.
- Adds causal, null evidence on the attitudinal effects of reducing exposure, directly informing debates about misinformation's real-world impact.

## Methods

- Analysis of 2020 platform-wide data covering all active US adult users on Facebook and Instagram.
- Measurement of exposure to content from Pages, groups, and web domains (Facebook) and public accounts (Instagram) that repeatedly publish misinformation.
- An interventional/experimental component reducing exposure to untrustworthy sources to assess resulting attitudinal changes.

## Findings

- Average users saw relatively little content from untrustworthy sources in their feeds.
- Exposure was highly concentrated among a small subset of users rather than evenly distributed.
- Reducing exposure produced no measurable change in user attitudes.

## Connections

This is part of the collaborative US 2020 Facebook/Instagram election research program, closely tied to work on the platforms' recommendation systems and the concentration of exposure such as [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Bakshy2015-rn]]. Its "concentrated exposure but limited effects" conclusion aligns with broader reassessments of misinformation's reach and impact, including [[Budak2024-ef]] and [[Allcott2025-jb]], and speaks to the meta-scientific debate over how misinformation harms are measured, as raised in [[Bak-Coleman2025-pm]].
