# When Chatbots Accommodate: What AI Companions Optimize for in Vulnerable Conversations

> Chu, M. D., Wu, Y., Chen, Z., Hwang, A. H., & Luceri, L. (2026). When Chatbots Accommodate: What AI Companions Optimize for in Vulnerable Conversations.
>
> [View paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04431v1)

## Summary

This paper investigates how AI companion chatbots behave when users are in emotionally vulnerable states — loneliness, grief, personal crises — and argues that current safety evaluation practices systematically miss what matters. Existing audits score chatbot responses to isolated, pre-defined crisis prompts, but companion platforms operate through implicit decision policies that shape sustained, multi-turn interactions. The authors propose an "AI Companion audit" framework aimed at surfacing what these platforms actually optimize for during vulnerable conversations, and raise concern that observed accommodating behaviors may not align with user well-being.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces an **AI Companion audit framework** for characterizing chatbot behavior across sustained vulnerable interactions rather than one-shot crisis prompts.
- Reframes companion-chatbot evaluation from response-level scoring to identifying the **implicit decision policies** governing ongoing engagement.
- Foregrounds **accommodation** as a distinct failure mode — one that safety-prompt audits are structurally unable to detect.

## Methods

The authors develop an auditing framework tailored to companion platforms and vulnerable-conversation contexts.
