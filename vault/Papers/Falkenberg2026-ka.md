# Toward an integrated and globally equitable understanding of political polarization

Toward an integrated and globally equitable understanding of political polarization. *Nature communications*.

> [View paper](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Toward%20an%20integrated%20and%20globally%20equitable%20understanding%20of%20political%20polarization)

## Summary

This perspective piece, authored by a large multidisciplinary and multinational team, argues that the study of political polarization suffers from two structural weaknesses: it is fragmented across disciplines, and its empirical evidence base is geographically skewed toward a handful of (mostly Western, especially US) contexts. The authors contend that this narrow focus limits the generalizability of polarization findings and produces a globally inequitable understanding of the phenomenon. They call for an integrated research agenda that spans network science, political science, computational social science, communication, and psychology, and that deliberately incorporates diverse political, cultural, and media contexts worldwide.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes an agenda for integrating polarization research across disciplinary boundaries.
- Advocates for global equity in polarization scholarship by broadening geographic and cultural scope beyond dominant Western/US cases.
- Frames a collaborative, cross-national approach to studying political polarization.

## Methods

A perspective/commentary-style synthesis rather than a new empirical study. It draws on the collective expertise of its many authors to review and critique the current landscape of polarization scholarship, diagnosing its disciplinary silos and geographic imbalances.

## Findings

- Polarization scholarship remains siloed across disciplines, hindering cumulative understanding.
- Empirical attention is disproportionately concentrated on a few countries, leaving many global contexts understudied.
- A more integrated and globally representative research design is needed to capture how polarization dynamics vary across societies.

## Connections

This agenda-setting critique connects to empirical polarization studies that could benefit from cross-national broadening, such as work on affective polarization and partisan dynamics like [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] and [[Green2025-ap]], and to research on social-media exposure and ideological sorting such as [[Bakshy2015-rn]]. Its concern with cross-context generalizability also resonates with comparative and cross-national analyses of the information environment like [[Humprecht2025-ml]].
