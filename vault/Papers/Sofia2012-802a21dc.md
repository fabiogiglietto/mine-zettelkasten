# Moving towards a socially-driven internet architectural design

> Sofia, R., Mendes, P., Damásio, M. J., Henriques, S., Giglietto, F., Giambitto, E., & Bogliolo, A. (2012). Moving towards a socially-driven internet architectural design. *ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review*. https://doi.org/10.1145/2317307.2317316
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/2317307.2317316)

## Summary

This position paper argues that the next generation of Internet architecture should be designed from an explicitly interdisciplinary standpoint, blending social capital theory with the technical concerns of computer networking. Its central premise is that Internet users have evolved from passive consumers to **prosumers** — simultaneously producing and consuming content and network resources — and increasingly to **network prosumers**, a role embodied in User-centric Networks (UCNs) that pushes user empowerment down into the lower OSI layers. The authors diagnose the current Internet supply chain as structurally *asymmetric*, with certain stages profiting from traffic growth while others stagnate for lack of price signals, and they call for a symmetric redesign that accommodates prosumer activity. They propose operationalizing social capital properties — trust, influence, reach, and engagement — into concrete networking metrics, while critically revisiting how centrality measures are (mis)applied across the social and technical domains.

## Key Contributions

- Articulates a vision and set of design guidelines for a **socially-driven Internet architecture** merging social capital theory with network design.
- Extends the prosumer concept into the network layer through the **"network prosumer"** notion, grounded in User-centric Networks.
- Proposes a **symmetric Internet supply-chain model** that explicitly includes prosumers and UCNs as stages, contrasted against the conventional asymmetric chain.
- Provides a **comparative mapping** between social-capital centrality concepts and their networking counterparts, identifying specific misalignments to resolve.
- Identifies **reach, engagement, and influence** as the properties most actionable for incorporation into Internet design, proposing candidate networking metrics for each.

## Methods

A conceptual, interdisciplinary position paper drawing on computer networking, social sciences, communication studies, and economics. The authors conduct a literature review spanning Future Internet projects (GENI, EIFFEL, SocialNets, ULOOP) and social capital theorists (Bourdieu, Coleman, Putnam, Burt). Analytically, they use a 7-stage Internet supply-chain model (Pigliapoco and Bogliolo) to diagnose asymmetry, offer a comparative analysis of degree, betweenness, and closeness centrality across social and networking contexts (summarized in a comparison table), and sketch a socially-driven design cycle linking social media, open datasets, social behavior models, and new media/business practices.

## Findings

- Vertical integration by operators conflicts with the modular TCP/IP stack, constrains innovation, and misaligns costs with pricing models.
- Supply-chain stages 2, 3, 5, and 7 benefited from traffic growth, while stages 1, 4, and 6 stagnated due to weak price signals.
- User-centric Networks rest on four properties — resource sharing, cooperation, trust, and self-organization — pushing prosumer behavior into lower OSI layers.
- Networking and social-capital interpretations of centrality diverge: high-betweenness nodes act as inter-cluster "bridgers" socially but are treated as mere relays technically; high-closeness nodes imply better diffusion socially yet can become bottlenecks in networks.
- The "bridger" role and the transitivity of strong ties are poorly represented in current pervasive networking models.
- Prosumer activity exhibits power-law properties, evidencing the coexistence of consumer and prosumer models.

## Connections

This paper sits apart from most of the empirical, platform-analytic work in these topics, but it shares intellectual lineage with the political-economy critique of platform infrastructures and user labor that underpins work like [[van-Dijck2018-up]]. Its concern with how centrality and social-capital metrics translate into networked influence resonates loosely with computational treatments of influence and visibility such as [[Luceri2025-tr]], though the present paper's architectural, infrastructure-layer framing is largely unique among the related set.
