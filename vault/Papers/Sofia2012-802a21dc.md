# Moving towards a socially-driven internet architectural design

> Sofia, R., Mendes, P., Damásio, M. J., Henriques, S., Giglietto, F., Giambitto, E., & Bogliolo, A. (2012). Moving towards a socially-driven internet architectural design. *ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review*. https://doi.org/10.1145/2317307.2317316
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/2317307.2317316)

## Summary

This position paper argues that Internet architecture should be redesigned through an interdisciplinary lens that fuses social capital theory with networking design. The authors trace the user's evolution from passive consumer to prosumer, and further to "network prosumer" — a role enacted through User-centric Networks (UCNs) where end-users contribute resources at lower OSI layers. They diagnose the current Internet supply chain as structurally asymmetric, with weak price signals starving several stages of investment, and propose a symmetric alternative that explicitly incorporates prosumers and UCNs. Central to the proposal is operationalizing social capital properties — trust, influence, reach, and engagement — as networking metrics, while reconciling divergent meanings of centrality across the two disciplines.

## Key Contributions

- A design vision for a socially-driven Internet architecture merging social capital theory with networking.
- Extension of the prosumer concept into the network layer via the "network prosumer" grounded in UCNs.
- A symmetric Internet supply-chain model that includes prosumers and UCNs as explicit stages.
- A comparative mapping between social capital and networking notions of centrality, exposing specific misalignments.
- Identification of reach, engagement, and influence as the most actionable social properties for networking metrics, with candidate operationalizations.

## Methods

Conceptual position paper combining literature review across Future Internet projects (GENI, EIFFEL, SocialNets, ULOOP) and social capital theorists (Bourdieu, Coleman, Putnam, Burt). The argument is structured around a 7-stage supply-chain model of the Internet and a comparative table contrasting centrality measures (degree, betweenness, closeness) in social capital modeling versus networking. The authors sketch a socially-driven design cycle linking social media, open datasets, behavioral models, and new business practices.

## Findings

- Vertical integration by operators clashes with the modular TCP/IP stack and misaligns costs with price models.
- Supply-chain stages 2, 3, 5, and 7 have benefited from traffic growth; stages 1, 4, and 6 have stagnated due to weak price signals.
- UCNs rest on four properties: resource sharing, cooperation, trust, and self-organization, pushing prosumer behavior downward in the stack.
- Centrality is interpreted incompatibly across fields: high-betweenness nodes are social "bridgers" but treated as mere relays in networking; high-closeness nodes imply social diffusion but can be networking bottlenecks.
- The bridger role and the transitivity of strong ties are under-represented in pervasive networking models.
- Prosumer activity exhibits power-law properties, evidencing coexistence of consumer and prosumer regimes.
