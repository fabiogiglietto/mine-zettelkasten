# The Hybrid Media System: Politics and Power

> Chadwick, A. T. (2014). The Hybrid Media System: Politics and Power. *Choice Reviews Online*. https://doi.org/10.5860/choice.51-6974
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.5860/choice.51-6974)

## Summary

Chadwick's work-in-progress paper introduces the **hybrid media system** as a framework for understanding contemporary political communication, arguing that old and new media are not competing dichotomies but interdependent forces bound by evolving power relations of adaptation and mutual reliance. Rejecting essentialist "old vs. new" thinking, the paper develops an *ontology of hybridity* and calls on scholars to empirically map where the distinction between media types actually matters. Centered on news production and drawing chiefly on British and American evidence, it advances the concept of the **political information cycle** to replace the elite-controlled "news cycle," and uses WikiLeaks as an extended case of hybridity and interdependence in news construction.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the **hybrid media system** as a foundational framework for political communication research.
- Advances the **political information cycle** as a reconceptualization of the traditional, elite-dominated news cycle.
- Imports an **ontology of hybridity** into media studies, including the distinction between *diluted* and *particulate* hybridity, and *relations of exteriority*.
- Applies the notion of **news-making assemblages** (after DeLanda) to political news as loosely coupled, interdependent actors who plug in and out in real time.
- Offers a detailed hybridity-based analysis of WikiLeaks as a multi-form media actor interdependent with legacy journalism.
- Argues methodologically for combining rich narrative case studies with quantitative tracing of who does the decisive "work."

## Methods

Primarily interdisciplinary theory-building, synthesizing conceptions of hybridity from comparative politics (hybrid regimes), governance and regulation studies, organizational sociology, cultural and postcolonial studies, and science and technology studies (actor-network theory). Empirical illustration comes from detailed narrative case studies with real-time observation: a close five-day logging of press, broadcast, and online material during the 2010 UK "bullygate" scandal and the first live televised UK prime ministerial debates, plus a WikiLeaks case study drawing on insider accounts, journalist testimony, and secondary reporting. Comparative evidence is drawn from Britain and the United States.

## Findings

- In the "bullygate" case, bloggers and Twitter activists engaged in cross-platform, real-time contestation that helped discredit newspaper revelations — the political information cycle in action.
- During the televised leaders' debates, a satirical `#nickcleggsfault` campaign trended globally and helped collapse a *Daily Telegraph* "scoop," showing online actors contesting press coverage.
- Political information cycles involve more numerous, diverse actors and complex temporalities — fragments can lie dormant then re-erupt — loosening but not eliminating elite control.
- WikiLeaks built a sophisticated sociotechnical infrastructure (encryption, Tor, distributed redundant servers) yet remained chaotic, financially precarious, and reliant on individuals like Assange.
- WikiLeaks evolved from a pure submission platform toward acting like a professional news producer (e.g., the edited *Collateral Murder* film) and ultimately toward collaboration with elite newspapers.
- Legacy news organizations provided legal expertise, legitimacy, government-bargaining experience, and verification, while WikiLeaks provided publishing infrastructure and network resilience — illustrating mutual interdependence.
- Accounts diverge on power: the NYT framed WikiLeaks as merely "a source," while the *Guardian* treated it as a collaborative partner; the reality was interdependent compromise.
- The Anonymous "Operation Payback"/"Avenge Assange" hacktivist campaigns functioned as a mobilized show of network support within the system.

## Connections

This paper is a foundational statement of the hybrid media system concept that recurs in this author's later work on political communication power [[Chadwick2015-ii]], and it shares the platform-as-infrastructure and platform-power lens developed in [[van-Dijck2018-up]] and [[Gillespie2022-jx]]. Its argument about a distributed, multi-actor information ecology and the erosion of elite gatekeeping also speaks to work on networked propaganda and asymmetric media systems [[Benkler2018-lw]] and to broader accounts of digitally networked political action [[Bennett2018-ys]].
