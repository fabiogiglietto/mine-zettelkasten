# Re-generating hate

> Crichton, F., & Torres, G. (2026). Re-generating hate. *Platforms & Society*, *3*. https://doi.org/10.1177/29768624261449726
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/29768624261449726)

## Summary

This short piece examines how generative AI tools are becoming embedded within far-right propaganda ecosystems rather than remaining external to them. Using the 2023 Dublin riots and the 2024 Southport unrest as case studies, Crichton and Torres trace what they call a "re-generative pipeline": a process in which extremist imagery harvested from Telegram and X is converted into text prompts and then regenerated through commercial image-generation models. Their central argument is that these AI systems do not reliably filter out hateful content. Instead, they strip away its most explicit markers while preserving and recirculating its underlying messaging — effectively laundering extremist imagery into a more palatable, widely shareable form. The paper thus interrogates the limits of content moderation and model safeguards when faced with extremist reappropriation.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the concept of a **re-generative pipeline** for understanding how extremist content moves through and is reshaped by generative AI systems.
- Provides empirical illustration through two recent European unrest events of how generative AI intersects with far-right propaganda.
- Challenges the assumption that commercial AI content safeguards neutralize hate, arguing instead that they reshape and recirculate it.

## Methods

A qualitative case study design focused on the 2023 Dublin riots and the 2024 Southport unrest. The authors conducted a media experiment that collected extremist images from Telegram and X, then applied a pipeline approach: converting the collected images into text prompts and re-creating them with commercial image-generation models to observe how the content was transformed.

## Findings

- AI image models often strip away the most explicit markers of hateful content while retaining its substance.
- The re-generation process can effectively launder or sanitize extremist imagery, making it more palatable for wider circulation.
- Model safeguards reshape hateful content rather than eliminating it, allowing its messaging to persist.
