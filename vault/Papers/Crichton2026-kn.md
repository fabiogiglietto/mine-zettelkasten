# Re-generating hate

> Crichton, F., & Torres, G. (2026). Re-generating hate. *Platforms & Society*, *3*. https://doi.org/10.1177/29768624261449726
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/29768624261449726)

## Summary

This short piece examines how generative AI tools are being folded into far-right propaganda ecosystems rather than sitting outside them. Using the 2023 Dublin riots and the 2024 Southport unrest as case studies, Crichton and Torres trace what they call a "re-generative pipeline": extremist imagery harvested from Telegram and X is converted into text prompts and then reconstituted through commercial image-generation models. Their central argument is that these AI systems do not reliably filter out hateful content. Instead, they tend to sanitize its most explicit visual markers while preserving — and thereby recirculating — its underlying extremist messaging, effectively laundering hate into a more palatable, shareable form.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the concept of a **"re-generative pipeline"** for understanding how extremist content moves through and is reshaped by generative AI systems.
- Provides empirical illustration through two recent European unrest events (Dublin 2023, Southport 2024) of how generative AI intersects with far-right propaganda.
- Challenges the assumption that commercial AI content safeguards neutralize hate, arguing instead that they reshape and recirculate it.

## Methods

Qualitative case study of two unrest events, paired with a media experiment. The authors collected extremist images circulating on Telegram and X, converted those images into descriptive text prompts, and then regenerated comparable imagery using commercial image-generation models — allowing them to observe what the models strip out, retain, or transform in the process.

## Findings

- AI image models often remove the most explicit markers of hateful content while retaining its substance and intent.
- The re-generation process can launder or sanitize extremist imagery, making it more palatable and thus more circulable in mainstream spaces.
- Generative AI is becoming embedded *within* far-right propaganda workflows rather than remaining external to them.
