# Misinformation beyond traditional feeds: Evidence from a WhatsApp deactivation experiment in Brazil

> Ventura, T., Majumdar, R., Nagler, J., & Tucker, J. A. (2025). Misinformation beyond traditional feeds: Evidence from a WhatsApp deactivation experiment in Brazil. *J. Polit.*. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4457400
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4457400)

## Summary

This paper reports a field experiment in Brazil that uses a **WhatsApp deactivation design** to causally estimate how the encrypted messaging platform shapes users' political knowledge, beliefs, and exposure to misinformation. Conducted in the context of Brazilian elections, the study argues that WhatsApp occupies a distinct position in political information ecosystems compared to feed-based social media like Facebook and Twitter. By inducing participants to stop using the platform and comparing treated and control users, the authors extend the deactivation-experiment literature into the domain of private messaging, and into a key Global South context where WhatsApp is central to political communication.

## Key Contributions

- Provides experimental **causal evidence** on the political effects of a major encrypted messaging platform, a literature previously dominated by studies of Facebook and Twitter.
- Expands the empirical scope of misinformation research beyond public-facing feeds to **private, non-feed-based platforms**.
- Delivers evidence from **Brazil**, a politically significant non-U.S. context where WhatsApp is deeply embedded in electoral communication.

## Methods

- Field experiment using a **WhatsApp deactivation design**: participants were induced to stop using the platform for a defined period.
- Conducted in Brazil around an electoral period, comparing treated (deactivated) and control users.
- Outcomes measured include political knowledge, political beliefs, and exposure to misinformation.

## Findings

- The study reports causal effects of WhatsApp deactivation on users' information environments and political attitudes in Brazil (specific magnitudes not recoverable from the abstract alone).
- Suggests that understanding misinformation requires attention to private messaging platforms, not just traditional public social media feeds.

## Connections

This paper sits squarely in the lineage of social media deactivation experiments; it is a methodological and substantive cousin to work using Facebook/Instagram deactivation and large-scale platform-effects studies such as [[Allcott2025-jb]], [[Allcott2019-gn]], and [[Guess2023-ur]], while pushing that paradigm toward encrypted messaging and the Global South. Its focus on WhatsApp and Brazilian political communication connects it to other work on misinformation in Brazilian and messaging-app contexts, including [[Rossini2026-jn]] and [[Rossini2026-mj]].
