# From mass media and social media to AI: A multilevel framework for understanding trust in generative AI

> Suk, J., & Zhang, Y. (2026). From mass media and social media to AI: A multilevel framework for understanding trust in generative AI. *Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media*, 1–24. https://doi.org/10.1080/08838151.2026.2694048
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/08838151.2026.2694048)

## Summary

This conceptual paper proposes a multilevel framework for studying public trust in generative AI, arguing that the emerging AI trust literature should build on decades of communication research on trust in mass media and social media. The authors treat generative AI as the next site in a longer trajectory of trust in information and communication technologies, and synthesize prior media-trust scholarship to identify the levels of analysis — from broad institutional and technological trust down to more granular interactions — that empirical work on generative AI should address. Rather than presenting new data, the paper offers a theoretical scaffold and research agenda.

## Key Contributions

- Articulates a **multilevel framework** for conceptualizing trust in generative AI.
- Bridges communication scholarship on **media and platform trust** with emerging questions about **AI trust**.
- Positions generative AI within a historical trajectory alongside mass media and social media as objects of public trust.
- Sets out a research agenda to guide future empirical inquiry into AI trust.

## Methods

Conceptual and theoretical synthesis. The authors review prior literature on trust in mass media and social media, extract recurring analytic levels and mechanisms, and translate them into a framework applicable to generative AI.

## Findings

- The paper is theoretical; no empirical findings are reported.
- It argues that trust in generative AI is **distinct but continuous** with trust in earlier communication technologies.
- It contends that single-level accounts (e.g., trust in "AI" as a monolith) are inadequate and must be disaggregated across multiple levels.
- Lessons from the trajectory of media trust research — including its shift from institutional to platform-level and interpersonal dynamics — can pre-empt analogous confusions in AI trust research.

## Connections

This framework paper sits upstream of empirical work probing how audiences perceive and evaluate generative AI outputs and journalism, such as studies on AI-generated news credibility and disclosure effects like [[Hameleers2026-mc]] and [[Dierickx2026-tw]], and audience-facing analyses like [[Choi2026-bz]] and [[Schroeder2026-im]]. It also complements institution-level accounts of how newsrooms and media systems are integrating generative AI, e.g. [[Hepp2026-oi]] and [[Gottfried2026-ww]], which supply the organizational level a multilevel trust framework would need to accommodate.
