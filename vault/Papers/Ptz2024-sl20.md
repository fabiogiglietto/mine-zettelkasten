# Performance without understanding: How ChatGPT relies on humans to repair conversational trouble

> Pütz, O., & Esposito, E. (2024). Performance without understanding: How ChatGPT relies on humans to repair conversational trouble. *Discourse & Communication*. https://doi.org/10.1177/17504813241271492
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/17504813241271492)

## Summary

Pütz and Esposito argue that ChatGPT's apparent conversational competence is a case of *performance without understanding*: the chatbot generates contextually appropriate text while remaining incapable of grasping what a conversation is about. Drawing on Luhmann's systems theory, ethnomethodology, and conversation analysis, they show that LLMs function as communication partners only *parasitically* — leaning on the intelligence sedimented in their training data and, crucially, on the ongoing interpretive and repair work of their human interlocutors. The upshot is that improving human-chatbot interaction is not a problem of building better machine cognition but of cultivating a new communicative competence in users.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the "do LLMs understand?" debate by separating text *generation* from *understanding* and locating chatbot competence at the level of communication rather than cognition.
- Introduces conversation-analytic repair (especially Schegloff's third position repair) as a diagnostic instrument for probing chatbot capabilities.
- Demonstrates empirically, through edited-turn experiments with ChatGPT (GPT-3.5), that successful repair is accidental and surface-driven.
- Advances a *social* rather than computational remedy: developing user-side communicative competence and a critical stance toward chatbot output.

## Methods

The paper combines theoretical argument with qualitative analysis of dialog excerpts. Luhmann's concept of communication and Garfinkel/Schegloff's tools for analyzing trouble and repair are used to interpret three case studies with ChatGPT: an ambiguous-reference case involving similar-sounding names, a violin/bottle scenario probed through multiple edited variants of a third position repair, and a "hallucination" case around the primality of 9791. The systematic editing of user turns functions as a lightweight experimental manipulation.

## Findings

- Faced with referential ambiguity, ChatGPT picks the statistically likelier reading and proceeds without initiating repair.
- In the violin/bottle case, response quality depends heavily on the exact wording of the user's repair turn; some variants produce coherent reconstructions, others (e.g., Version 5) fail — showing that apparent repair is accidental.
- The chatbot tends to recycle wording from the user's most recent turn rather than re-evaluating earlier dialog associations.
- On the primality question, the chatbot flips between correct and incorrect answers depending on the pragmatic slant of the user's follow-up, indicating no stable knowledge state or "conviction."
- Users detect plausible-sounding nonsense mainly when they already suspect it may occur, underscoring the need for critical monitoring.
