# From polls to persistence: Examining coordinated political discourse outside the Philippine election period on Facebook

> Fenis, M. R. B., Agonos, M. J. M., & Figueroa, P. D. (2027). From polls to persistence: Examining coordinated political discourse outside the Philippine election period on Facebook. *Asian Politics & Policy*, *19*. https://doi.org/10.1111/aspp.70113
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/aspp.70113)

## Summary

This paper examines how coordinated political behavior on Facebook endures *between* Philippine election cycles, focusing on the first two years of the Marcos Jr. administration (June 2022–May 2024). Against the common framing of influence operations as episodic, campaign-driven tactics, the authors argue that coordinated behavior constitutes a persistent, structural infrastructure — a socio-cultural assemblage in which platform affordances, algorithmic systems, coordinated actors, and political narratives interact to sustain personality-driven partisanship. Rather than treating Facebook as an autonomous engine of polarization, they advance a relational account in which six platform affordances are activated contingently through their embedding in the Philippines' dynastic and clientelistic political culture. Coordinated actors, they find, prioritize an "illusion of presence" and manufactured consensus over genuine virality.

## Key Contributions

- Extends the study of Philippine influence operations beyond elections, showing digital manipulation as a permanent feature of political communication.
- Integrates affordance theory with a Deleuzo-Guattarian socio-cultural assemblage framework, theorizing affordances as relational, contingent potentials rather than fixed platform properties.
- Challenges technologically determinist accounts of platform-driven polarization, situating it within interactions among platform features, coordinated actors, and political culture.
- Positions cross-posting as an empirical, observable marker of sustained coordinated activity and manufactured consensus.
- Connects digital coordination mechanisms to the reproduction of dynastic, clientelistic, personality-driven politics.

## Methods

A case study within a larger Southeast Asian influence-operations project. The authors used Google Trends to identify three salient discourses (elite political contest, foreign policy, the 2022 elections), which seeded personality-centered keyword strings (e.g., "uniteam [bongbong marcos]", "martial law"). They applied social network analysis to publicly available Facebook pages (excluding groups), detecting coordination through content duplication, temporal synchronization, and network structure rather than a fixed cross-post threshold. A three-person coding team then conducted qualitative content analysis of content type, post elements, engagement, and manifested affordances, framed by Kakavand's six affordances and assemblage theory (Deleuze and Guattari, DeLanda, Hopkins).

## Findings

- SNA identified 14 communities across 1,387 posts containing 356 unique messages.
- Average delay between cross-posting accounts was just 61.71 seconds — far faster than organic behavior — with coordinated pages ranging from 2 to 30.
- Despite tight coordination, engagement was low, suggesting scalability is weaponized to project structural presence rather than trigger virality.
- Connectivity hardened factional partisanship: distinct opposition clusters (around Leni Robredo and Kiko Pangilinan, sustained via the Angat Buhay Foundation) versus fluid, multi-node pro-administration coalitions.
- Persistence enabled strategic archiving — posts from as early as 2021 remained accessible, with cross-posting typically within 5 days using identical captions, hashtags, and formatting.
- Nearly half of high-centrality pages were deleted, renamed, or made unavailable between 2022 and 2023, enabling page recycling/rebranding to evade moderation while preserving follower bases.
- Searchability was operationalized via standardized hashtags (#PBBM, #BBM, #UniTeam) and charged labels ("dilawan", "oligarkiya", "komunista"), including "oppositional searchability" that injects counter-narratives into pro-administration keyword spaces.
- Identifiability varied: state and government pages projected high institutional branding, while advocacy accounts used generic avatars and fake news-outlet names to mimic grassroots legitimacy.

## Connections

This work extends the coordinated-inauthentic-behavior literature on detection via cross-posting and temporal synchronization — methods central to [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], and [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] — into the understudied non-election period. Its relational, assemblage-based critique of technological determinism speaks directly to debates about whether platforms autonomously produce polarization raised in [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]], [[Guess2023-ur]], and [[Nyhan2023-gb]], and complements work on affective and partisan polarization such as [[Iyengar2019-jj]] and [[Osmundsen2021-et]]. Its Philippine focus connects it to other regionally grounded studies of coordinated political discourse, including [[Gaw2025-ru]] and [[Pante2025-pq]].
