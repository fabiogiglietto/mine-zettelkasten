# Toward a transnational information ecology on the right? Hyperlink networking among right-wing digital news sites in Europe and the United States

> Heft, A., Knüpfer, C., Reinhardt, S., & Mayerhöffer, E. (2021). Toward a transnational information ecology on the right? Hyperlink networking among right-wing digital news sites in Europe and the United States. *The International Journal of Press/Politics*, *26*, 484–504. https://doi.org/10.1177/1940161220963670
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1940161220963670)

## Summary

This paper examines whether right-wing alternative news sites (RNS) constitute interconnected national and transnational news ecologies through hyperlinking. Analyzing hyperlink data harvested from 65 RNS across six Western democracies (Austria, Germany, US, UK, Denmark, Sweden) over three months in 2018, the authors study both direct links between RNS and the external reference points these sites share. They find that RNS do build interlinked right-wing ecologies both domestically and across borders, that U.S. sites function as a central transatlantic hub, and—counterintuitively—that legacy "mainstream" media are the most important transnationally shared reference points. This last finding qualifies the common assumption that such sites form insulated echo chambers, suggesting instead that they seek connection to the broader information environment.

## Key Contributions

- Provides an empirical, cross-national mapping of transnational hyperlink networks among RNS in six Western democracies, moving beyond single-country studies.
- Introduces the **Transnational Reference Score (TRS)**, combining a domain's prevalence among RNS with its spread across country collections to identify transnationally shared reference points.
- Qualifies fears of insulated right-wing counter-publics by showing RNS link heavily to mainstream legacy media.
- Demonstrates the central hub role of the U.S. right-wing news ecology in transatlantic information flows, framing the U.S. as a possible developmental trajectory for European ecologies.
- Advances a hybrid conceptualization of hyperlinking that blends professional journalistic logic (citational/credibility links) with political/movement logic (force multiplication, identity building).

## Methods

The authors performed hyperlink network analysis on article content from 65 RNS selected for institutionalization as news media, self-positioning as "alternative" correctives, right-wing ideology, and national scope. Countries were chosen as a strong test case via three geographically/culturally close pairs (Germany–Austria, US–UK, Denmark–Sweden), varying in the maturity of right-wing news infrastructure. Article content (102,379 articles, July–October 2018) was collected via Media Cloud; hyperlinks were extracted with `rvest` (~12.5 million links, cleaned to 725,450 substantive links). Transnationality was operationalized two ways: horizontal cross-border RNS-to-RNS links (via the External–Internal index) and the TRS for shared external references. A manual content analysis classified linked actors by type, scope, and country (Krippendorff's alpha 0.79 for country, 0.67 for actor type).

## Findings

- Of 725,450 total links, 23,806 connected sampled RNS, but only 830 crossed borders—most RNS-to-RNS linking is domestic.
- U.S. sites are by far the most active linkers (average outdegree 647.3), followed by German (149.3), Danish (71.7), and Swedish (66.9); Austrian (18.1) and UK (2.7) sites are far less engaged.
- U.S. RNS form the densest, most homogeneously interconnected domestic cluster and are the only cluster addressed by RNS from all other countries—the network's main integrating hub.
- Linking patterns differ sharply: U.S., Swedish, and German RNS link predominantly domestically; Danish RNS link almost exclusively transnationally (95%); UK sites link only externally.
- Transnational links follow geographic/cultural proximity (Austrian↔German; Danish→Swedish and U.S.).
- Among top-100 transnationally shared reference points, legacy media dominate (nytimes.com, theguardian.com, reuters.com, dailymail.co.uk); Trump's Twitter ranked 8th; only four sampled RNS (all U.S.) appear.
- Across 288 high-TRS domains, ~75% are media/journalists, ~16% economic actors, under 5% each political and societal.

## Connections

This paper's core finding that right-wing sites link heavily outward to mainstream media speaks directly to debates over echo chambers and cross-cutting exposure explored in [[Bakshy2015-rn]], [[Barbera2015-fw]], [[Flaxman2016-lm]], and [[Guess2021-ym]].
