# Coordinated inauthentic behaviour on Facebook? A typology of manufactured attention

> Rogers, R., & Righetti, N. (2025). Coordinated inauthentic behaviour on Facebook? A typology of manufactured attention. *Platforms & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/29768624251369784
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/29768624251369784)

## Summary

Rogers and Righetti empirically map coordinated link-sharing on Facebook between October 2023 and July 2024, arguing that the standard framing of Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour (CIB) as deceptive influence operations obscures a far broader ecosystem of "manufactured attention." Using CooRnet-based detection, they identify 19 highly coordinated communities spanning influence operations, mainstream media outlets, political meme networks, gambling and cyber-scam advertisers, and hijacked public groups. The central provocation is that coordination signatures alone cannot distinguish authentic from inauthentic actors — a Czech influence operation and German tabloids look identical at the network level — and that Meta's progressive narrowing of what counts as CIB has effectively legitimised most of this coordinated attention manufacture.

## Key Contributions

- An empirically-grounded six-part typology of highly coordinated link-sharers on Facebook that moves beyond the disinformation frame.
- The concept of **manufactured attention** as an analytic umbrella covering both authentic and inauthentic coordination.
- An **authenticity continuum** organised by actor identification and disclosure of ties to a master source or campaign.
- A methodological argument that content-agnostic coordination detection must be paired with actor and content analysis.
- A critical policy-discursive analysis tracing how Meta's CIB definition narrowed from "false amplifiers" (2017) to "adversarial threat actors" (2022+).

## Methods

Detection of near-synchronous co-sharing (same URL within a 60-second window, more frequently than 99% of accounts) via CooRnet through the CrowdTangle API, seeded from the Global Map of Coordinated Accounts 2023 and fact-checker-rated problematic URLs. An iterative pipeline collected up to 100 overperforming posts every six hours from 304 Pages and 2,095 Groups, yielding a network of 743 nodes, 16,670 edges, and 6,200 unique URLs. Louvain community detection produced 19 coordinated communities, then characterised qualitatively (actors, content) and quantitatively (centralisation of coordination) using Chaffee's concept explication framework. A parallel discursive analysis traced Meta's evolving CIB policy language.

## Findings

- Six actor-based types emerged: influence operations, media outlets, political meme activists, gambling ad networks, cyber-scam networks, and hijacked large public groups.
- A Czech influence operation and outlets like FOCUS and TAG24 produced near-identical full-scale, evenly distributed coordination signatures.
- Political meme networks varied: Brazilian, Ukrainian and Slovakian cases were core-driven; the pro-AMLO Mexican network resembled distributed "participatory propaganda."
- Gambling communities were among the largest coordinated clusters, appearing in both core-driven and distributed variants.
- AfD-adjacent fan pages shared administrators, sometimes linked to right-wing groups run by AfD politicians.
- The U.S. Daily Wire case exemplifies identified-but-undisclosed coordination — Pages amplifying a single source without disclosing affiliation.
- Meta's terminology drifted from "fake news" (2016) → "false amplifiers" (2017) → "CIB" (2018) → "adversarial threat actors" (2022+), progressively shrinking the enforcement surface.
