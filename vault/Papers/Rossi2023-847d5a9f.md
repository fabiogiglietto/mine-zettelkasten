# Cracking Open the European Newsfeed

> Rossi, L., Giglietto, F., & Marino, G. (2023). Cracking Open the European Newsfeed. *Journal of Quantitative Description: Digital Media*. https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020)

## Summary

This paper extends Guess et al.'s (2021) US-based study of news quality on Facebook to the European context, analyzing the prevalence, exposure, and sharing of trustworthy and untrustworthy news URLs in Germany, France, and Italy from 2019 to 2022. Drawing on Meta's privacy-protected URL Shares Dataset combined with NewsGuard domain trustworthiness ratings, the authors document a steady decline in total URLs shared, a rising share of untrustworthy URLs (notably in electoral years in Germany and Italy), but relatively stable proportions of *Views* from untrustworthy sources — suggesting platform-level dampening of exposure. The study confirms that older users disproportionately drive both exposure to and sharing of untrustworthy content, while also offering a pointed methodological critique of the datasets it relies on.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic quantitative description of trustworthy vs. untrustworthy news circulation on Facebook across three major European countries using Meta's URL Shares Dataset.
- Temporal (through 2022) and geographic (EU) extension of Guess et al. (2021), enabling within-Europe cross-national comparison.
- Methodological critique of the URL Shares Dataset (the 100-share threshold, US-skewed coverage) and of using NewsGuard ratings longitudinally, with explicit rating-stability checks.
- Documentation of age-based patterns of misinformation engagement in European contexts, extending US findings on older users' outsized role.
- Discussion of implications for upcoming EU Digital Services Act transparency and platform data-access obligations.

## Methods

The authors replicate and adapt Guess et al.'s (2021) publicly available code to European data, analyzing Meta's URL Shares Dataset v10.1 (Jan 2017 – Oct 2022) filtered by `top_country` for Germany, France, Italy, and the US. Domains are classified as Trustworthy (NewsGuard score ≥60) or Untrustworthy (<60) using an October 2022 snapshot of 8,506 rated domains applied retrospectively; historical NewsGuard data confirm that only ~1–3% of domains changed category over 2019–2022. NewsGuard coverage is benchmarked against alternative lists (Lasser-Rupp, Media Bias/Fact Check, Grinberg et al. 2019). Views and Shares are disaggregated across six age cohorts, with confidence intervals calculated to account for differential-privacy noise (σ=14 for shares, σ=2,228 for views).

## Findings

- Total URL volume declined steadily from 2019 to 2022; France consistently has the highest volume, followed by Italy, with Germany at roughly half or less (consistent with lower German Facebook news adoption).
- Untrustworthy URL shares were similar (~13–14%) across the three countries in 2019 but diverged by 2022 (France 11%, Germany ~21%, Italy 22%), with peaks coinciding with national election years.
- Absolute Views and Shares of untrustworthy URLs peaked in 2020 before declining in line with the overall URL decline.
- The percentage of *Views* from untrustworthy sources stayed stable or slightly declined despite rising untrustworthy URL shares, indicating platform-level dampening of exposure.
- Sharing of untrustworthy content skews older than exposure: 65+ dominates in France (28%), 55–64 in Germany (36%), and 45–54 in Italy (33%); users under 34 play a marginal role.
- US data show a stronger correlation between NewsGuard untrustworthy-domain share and dataset URLs than European data, motivating the EU-only comparative focus.

## Connections

This paper is explicitly a European replication and extension of US-centric quantitative work on platform information quality, and shares methodological lineage with foundational misinformation-prevalence studies such as [[Guess2021-ym]], [[Grinberg2019-ua]], and [[Allen2020-nj]]. Its concern with cross-national variation in disinformation resilience connects to comparative frameworks in [[Humprecht2020-gd]] and [[Humprecht2025-ml]], while its reliance on Meta's URL Shares Dataset relates to debates about platform data access and exposure measurement in [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Budak2024-ef]].
