# Cracking Open the European Newsfeed

> Rossi, L., Giglietto, F., & Marino, G. (2023). Cracking Open the European Newsfeed. *Journal of Quantitative Description: Digital Media*. https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020)

## Summary

This paper extends Guess et al.'s (2021) US analysis of news quality on Facebook to three major European democracies — Germany, France, and Italy — over the 2019–2022 period. Using Meta's privacy-protected URL Shares Dataset combined with NewsGuard domain ratings, the authors document declining overall URL volumes, rising shares of untrustworthy URLs in electoral years (particularly Germany and Italy), but strikingly stable proportions of *Views* from untrustworthy sources. They also confirm in a European context that older users disproportionately drive both exposure to and sharing of low-quality news, while under-34s play a marginal role. Alongside these substantive findings, the paper offers a pointed methodological critique of the dataset itself — notably the 100-share inclusion threshold and the US-skewed coverage of trustworthiness ratings — arguing that direct US–EU comparisons are unreliable.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic cross-national quantitative description of trustworthy vs. untrustworthy news circulation on Facebook in Germany, France, and Italy using Meta's URL Shares Dataset.
- Temporal (through 2022) and geographic extension of Guess et al. (2021).
- Methodological critique of the URL Shares Dataset (100-share threshold, differential privacy noise) and of longitudinal use of NewsGuard ratings, including stability checks showing only ~1–3% category churn over four years.
- European evidence for the "older users share more untrustworthy content" pattern previously documented in the US.
- Discussion of implications for platform research access under the forthcoming EU Digital Services Act.

## Methods

- Replication and extension of Guess et al. (2021), adapting Aslett's (2022) public code to European data.
- Meta URL Shares Dataset v10.1 (Jan 2017 – Oct 2022), filtered by `top_country` for DE, FR, IT, and US.
- NewsGuard October 2022 snapshot (8,506 rated domains): Trustworthy ≥ 60, Untrustworthy < 60, applied retrospectively.
- Cross-validation of NewsGuard coverage against the Lasser-Rupp list, Media Bias/Fact Check, and Grinberg et al. (2019).
- Confidence intervals computed to accommodate differential privacy noise (σ=14 shares, σ=2,228 views).
- Disaggregation of Views and Shares across six age cohorts (18–24 through 65+).

## Findings

- France has the highest URL volume, then Italy; Germany's is roughly half or less, consistent with lower Facebook news use in Germany (17% vs. Italy's 45%, Reuters 2022).
- Untrustworthy URL shares converged around 13–14% in 2019 but diverged sharply by 2022 (France ~11%, Germany ~21%, Italy 22%), with peaks aligning to national election years.
- Absolute Views and Shares of untrustworthy URLs peaked in 2020, then declined in line with the overall URL decline.
- Percentage of *Views* from untrustworthy sources remained stable or slightly declined despite rising shares of untrustworthy URLs — suggesting platform-level dampening of exposure.
- Share of *Shares* to untrustworthy domains rose substantially in Germany (22% in 2021) and Italy (26% in 2022).
- Sharing skews older than exposure: 65+ dominates in France (28%), 55–64 in Germany (36%), 45–54 in Italy (33%).
- US data show a much tighter coupling between NewsGuard's untrustworthy-domain share and observed untrustworthy URLs than European data, motivating the EU-only comparative frame.
