# Multimodal narratives of climate denial: A novel, visual-first methodology for analysing conspiracy theory discourse on Instagram

> Gardam, C., Riedlinger, M., Angus, D., & Tan, X. Y. (. (2025). Multimodal narratives of climate denial: A novel, visual-first methodology for analysing conspiracy theory discourse on Instagram. *Discourse, Context & Media*, *68*, 100946. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcm.2025.100946
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcm.2025.100946)

## Summary

This paper proposes a **visual-first methodology** for analysing multimodal climate denial and conspiracy theory discourse on Instagram. Its central argument is that established, text-centric approaches to discourse analysis are ill-suited to image-driven platforms, where visual elements carry the primary communicative load. Rather than treating images as supplementary to text, the authors invert the analytical priority: visual rhetoric is foregrounded, with captions, hashtags, and platform context integrated as interpretive support. The methodology is positioned as a contribution to multimodal discourse analysis and to the growing body of scholarship on how conspiracy narratives circulate through visual social media.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a novel **visual-first methodological framework** for studying multimodal discourse on image-driven platforms.
- Extends discourse analytic tooling for examining climate denial and conspiracy theory communication specifically.
- Offers a **replicable framework** transferable to other domains of visual conspiracy discourse beyond climate.

## Methods

The paper is primarily methodological: it develops and articulates a visual-first framework and demonstrates it through application to Instagram content on climate denial and conspiracy theories. The approach combines close visual analysis with attention to accompanying captions, hashtags, and platform-specific context, thereby reading images as the organising unit of the discourse rather than as illustration of text.

## Findings

- Instagram climate denial discourse relies heavily on **visual rhetorical strategies** that text-first methods systematically underrepresent.
- Multimodal narrative structures are central to how climate conspiracy theories are **constructed and circulated** on the platform.
- Integrating visual, textual, and contextual cues surfaces denial narrative strategies that would otherwise be missed.

## Connections

This paper's focus on how conspiracy and denial narratives are constructed and circulate connects it to broader work on conspiracy theory discourse and communities such as [[Grusauskaite2026-po]] and [[Askanius2026-de]], and to research on misinformation resistance and climate/science denial exemplified by [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]]. Its methodological emphasis on visual and multimodal analysis of platform content is a distinctive contribution, complementing rather than duplicating the largely text-oriented monitoring approaches elsewhere in this collection.
