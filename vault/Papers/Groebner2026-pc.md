# From national outlets to transnational audiences: far-right hyperpartisan news sources on YouTube

> Groebner, K. (2026). From national outlets to transnational audiences: far-right hyperpartisan news sources on YouTube. *Media International Australia*. https://doi.org/10.1177/1329878x261474201
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1329878x261474201)

## Summary

This article asks whether the far-right information environment on YouTube has become genuinely transnational, and answers with a resounding yes. Groebner examines four English-speaking hyperpartisan news channels — Sky News Australia, Fox News (US), Rebel News (Canada) and GB News (UK) — treating them as nodes in a broader far-right movement whose electoral actors are merely the "tip of the iceberg." Drawing on social movement theory, network propaganda scholarship, and work on the far right on YouTube, the paper marshals a whole-lifespan dataset of over 114,000 videos and 51 million comments to demonstrate a highly integrated, cross-national audience bound by shared ideological pillars and common enemies. The United States, and Fox News in particular, sits at the centre of this network, exerting outsized gravitational pull on comparable outlets abroad.

## Key Contributions

- Provides rare, large-scale empirical evidence for a *transnational far-right audience* at the audience (rather than elite or hyperlink) level.
- Introduces a novel data-gathering method using YouTube's Search API on a per-day, per-channel basis to bypass the 20,000-video playlist cap and build a historically representative, whole-lifespan sample — with an accompanying validation procedure.
- Extends analysis beyond the well-studied "Alternative Influence Network" to traditional-style hyperpartisan news outlets on YouTube.
- Names concrete transnationalisation strategies: covering another country's politics, constructing common enemies, and featuring internationally recognisable figures.
- Integrates commenter-overlap tracking, co-commenting network analysis (E-I and Gini-Simpson indices), and BERTopic topic modelling into one mixed-methods design.

## Methods

The author assembled metadata from 114,025 videos (Fox News largest at 43,037), 103,545 with captions, and 51,449,494 top-level comments spanning each channel's founding through 31 December 2024. Method validity was checked via a binary logistic regression predicting a video's inclusion from views, likes, comments and duration, plus rolling-average plots to detect temporal decay. Audience integration was measured using commenters as a proxy: a lifetime directed overlap network, monthly pairwise overlap plots, and a co-commenting network of each channel's top 1,000 videos (edges filtered to >200 shared commenters), with Louvain community detection in Gephi. Content was analysed with BERTopic (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings, UMAP, HDBSCAN), yielding 175 topics (coherence 0.61, diversity 0.60).

## Findings

- Strong lifetime commenter overlap: 56.22% of Sky News Australia commenters also commented on Fox News; Fox was a major overlap target for Rebel News (44%) and GB News (38%).
- Fox News is the largest and most domestic channel with limited outward overlap, yet functions as the central overlap target for the others.
- Sky News Australia emerges as a central transnational hub connecting to all channels, having aggressively targeted US far-right content to win a large US audience.
- Overlap spikes within 2–3 months of the launches of Sky News Australia (2019) and GB News (2021), indicating a pre-existing transnational audience that new channels could immediately capture.
- Cross-channel activity tracks engagement: mean comments per account rise from 3 (one channel) to 97.4 (all four), and average activity span from 155 to 1,687 days.
- The co-commenting network (2,743 nodes, 121,703 edges) shows communities organised by both upload period and topic; transphobia, coverage of foreign politics, and shared figures (Tommy Robinson, Piers Morgan, Douglas Murray) act as transnational bridges.
- Topic modelling confirms a shared far-right agenda: nativism/anti-immigration, authoritarianism, populism, Biden criticism, pro-Trump content, transphobia and royal-family coverage.

## Connections

This audience-level account of far-right transnationalisation complements platform-affordance and recommendation-driven analyses of far-right and hyperpartisan content circulation such as [[Rieder2026-pp]] and [[Bruns2026-yv]], and speaks to broader work on partisan information ecosystems and coordinated far-right activity like [[Starbird2025-jj]] and [[Falkenberg2026-ka]]. Its methodological focus on commenter overlap and co-commenting networks as a measure of shared communities relates to network and audience-mapping approaches such as [[Kulichkina2025-sl09]], while its emphasis on shared enemies and collective identity connects to studies of populist and polarising discourse including [[Gerbaudo2026-fo]] and [[Tornberg2025-ir]].
