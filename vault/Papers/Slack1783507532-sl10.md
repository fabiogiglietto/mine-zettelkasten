# Political Advertisement and Coordinated Behavior on Social Media in the Lead-Up to the 2021 German Federal Elections

> Righetti, N. (2026). Political Advertisement and Coordinated Behavior on Social Media in the Lead-Up to the 2021 German Federal Elections. *Open MIND*. https://doi.org/10.17605/osf.io/yqkfz
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.17605/osf.io/yqkfz)

## Summary

This paper investigates the intersection of paid political advertising and coordinated behavior on social media in the run-up to the 2021 German federal elections. It positions itself within the growing literature on coordinated inauthentic behavior (CIB) and political communication, arguing that understanding electoral dynamics on social media requires examining not just organic discourse but also the interplay between paid messaging strategies and coordinated (potentially inauthentic) activity by political actors and their networks.

## Key Contributions

- Empirically grounds the study of coordinated behavior in a major European national election context (Germany 2021), extending CIB research beyond its more commonly studied US-centric cases.
- Bridges two typically separate strands of political communication research—political advertising analysis and coordinated behavior detection—by examining them jointly within the same electoral environment.
- Adds a European case study to the broader literature on coordinated online activity during democratic elections, contributing comparative value to cross-national understanding of platform manipulation and campaign tactics.

## Methods

The study relies on empirical analysis of social media data collected in relation to the 2021 German federal election campaign, combining examination of political advertisement activity with methods for detecting coordinated behavior across accounts/actors. The specific analytical techniques (e.g., network analysis, co-sharing/co-posting detection) are not detailed in the available abstract, but the approach follows the general paradigm of identifying suspicious synchronization patterns in political social media activity.

## Findings

- Political advertising and coordinated behavior were both identifiable and notable features of the social media landscape preceding the election.
- The findings suggest that paid political messaging and coordinated activity are intertwined and mutually reinforcing components of electoral social media strategy, though granular results are not elaborated in the summary provided.
