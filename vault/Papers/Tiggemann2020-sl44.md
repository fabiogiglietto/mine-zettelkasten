# Social media is not real: The effect of ‘Instagram vs reality’ images on women’s social comparison and body image

> Tiggemann, M., & Anderberg, I. (2020). Social media is not real: The effect of ‘Instagram vs reality’ images on women’s social comparison and body image. *New Media & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444819888720
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444819888720)

## Summary

This experimental study provides the first direct test of the 'Instagram vs reality' trend — a social media activism practice in which the same woman is shown side-by-side in an idealized, staged pose and a more natural, candid one. Grounded in sociocultural and social comparison theory, the authors test whether these posts can counteract the well-documented body-image harms of idealized Instagram content. Across three conditions (ideal images alone, realistic images alone, and the paired format), viewing the paired and realistic images lowered state body dissatisfaction and, crucially, neutralized the detrimental effect of appearance comparison that was present for idealized images. The authors conclude that such posts function as a brief, protective form of social media literacy, at least in the short term.

## Key Contributions

- First explicit experimental evaluation of the 'Instagram vs reality' trend on women's body image.
- Strong experimental control: the same women in the same settings are used across all conditions, isolating image type from confounds of person, clothing, or setting.
- Demonstrates that realistic and paired imagery neutralizes the harmful moderating role of appearance comparison, refining theory on how such interventions work.
- Conceptualizes 'Instagram vs reality' posts as a potential brief social media literacy intervention.
- Critically notes a limitation of the movement itself — all images featured thin, attractive women, raising concerns about relevance to larger-bodied women and possible anti-fat framing.

## Methods

Randomized between-subjects experiment with pre- and post-exposure measurement. 305 US women aged 18–30 (300 after attention-check exclusions; mean age 25.34, mean BMI 26.98) were recruited via TurkPrime/Amazon Mechanical Turk and assigned to one of three conditions. Stimuli comprised 15 images per set, drawn from public Instagram profiles tagged #instagramversusreality and #instagramversusreallife; ideal and real conditions used the separated halves of the same paired images, displayed in a simulated iPhone/Instagram frame for 5 seconds each. Measures included VAS for state body dissatisfaction (weight, appearance) and body appreciation, the 3-item State Appearance Comparison Scale, a Perceived Realism Scale with thinness/attractiveness ratings as manipulation checks, and an open-ended item on perceived message. Analyses used repeated-measures ANOVA, ANCOVA with orthogonal planned comparisons, hierarchical regression for comparison × image-type interactions, and thematic/chi-square analysis of open responses.

## Findings

- Body dissatisfaction rose after viewing ideal images but fell after real and paired images; the ideal vs (real + paired) comparison was significant (p = .031), with no significant difference between real and paired.
- Body appreciation changes were in the predicted direction but non-significant (interaction p = .213).
- Manipulation checks confirmed real and paired images were rated more realistic, and the women in them less thin and attractive.
- State appearance comparison did not differ significantly across conditions, but overall predicted greater body dissatisfaction and lower appreciation.
- Crucially, appearance comparison worsened body image *only* for ideal images (r = .424 dissatisfaction, −.324 appreciation), not for real or paired — image type moderated the comparison effect, likely by shifting comparisons from upward to lateral or downward.
- 95.1% of women recognized a message in paired images (vs 81.6% real, 61.6% ideal); the dominant paired theme was 'social media is not real' (80.4%).
- The paired format offered no clear additional benefit over viewing the realistic image alone.
