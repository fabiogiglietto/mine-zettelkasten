# The Memeplex: Platform grammars and conspiratorial alignment around the Great Reset

> Tuters, M. (2026). The Memeplex: Platform grammars and conspiratorial alignment around the Great Reset. *New Media & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448261446931
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448261446931)

## Summary

This article reconceptualizes conspiracism as an infrastructural phenomenon shaped by platform grammars rather than a matter of belief, misinformation, or coherent narrative. Taking the World Economic Forum's "Great Reset" as its central case, Tuters argues that conspiratorial alignment forms through patterned circulation—repeated adjacency, symbolic compression, and modular reuse—rather than through shared doctrine. Crucially, conspiratorial alignment *precedes* narrative coherence: patterned co-occurrence stabilizes before fully articulated conspiracy stories emerge. Drawing on a 3.7-million-tweet corpus and cross-platform triangulation, the paper shows how the WEF's own institutional and foresight-oriented language is recoded into a durable "grammar of distrust," and it extends Hofstadter's "paranoid style" into a "high-tech paranoid style" fitted to platform-mediated technocratic governance.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces three analytic concepts: **conspiricization** (institutional outputs reframed as evidence of elite coordination), **modular conspiracism** (recombination of fragments into portable templates of distrust), and the **Memeplex** (the media ecology binding grievances through repetition and adjacency).
- Develops the **"high-tech paranoid style,"** updating Hofstadter for conditions of platform scale, opacity, and abstraction, and in conversation with Jameson's "high-tech paranoia" and cognitive mapping.
- Offers a platform-native, infrastructural methodology that shifts attention from belief toward circulation, format, and metadata.
- Reframes conspiracy as an ongoing *process* (conspiricization) analogous to platformization or datafication.
- Recasts the "globalist" imaginary as a vernacular misrecognition of the platform grammars organizing digital political life.

## Methods

The study centers on co-hashtag network analysis of a 3.7-million-tweet corpus containing #TheGreatReset, #Agenda2030, or #DigitalID (Twitter Academic API v2, 2015–2022), with pre- and post-pandemic comparison. Networks were visualized in Gephi with ForceAtlas2 and read interpretively as "spatializations." A "world-in-a-word" heuristic treats a single hashtag as an entry point into the issue-world it condenses, inverting traditional multi-query issue mapping. Findings are triangulated across 1,614 anti-WEF TikTok posts, 9,165 YouTube comments beneath a Russell Brand video, and hyperlink/metadata analysis of the Wide Awake Media counter-map site, alongside qualitative reading of remix genres, infographics, bestselling books, and influencer rhetoric.

## Findings

- Between 2018 and 2022, fintech- and blockchain-oriented clusters shifted from central frames to peripheral residues, while control, surveillance, and depopulation tropes moved to the center.
- By 2022 the networks showed dense convergence of pandemic-governance and biopolitical anxiety anchored by #Agenda2030, #covid19, #WEF, #BuildBackBetter, #NWO, and #CBDC—aligning finance, biometrics, and surveillance into a shared antagonistic orbit.
- A small conspiracist enclave (#NWO, #Agenda21, #depopulation, #BillGates) existed at the margins pre-pandemic and later became central, complicating the view that conspiracies emerge purely from misreading neutral policy.
- The slogan "You'll own nothing and be happy" traveled via repetition-with-variation across platforms, placards, and electoral rhetoric, accumulating force through reuse rather than explanation.
- The same modular alignments recur across Twitter/X, TikTok, counter-maps, and commercial publishing, indicating a cross-media logic rather than a platform-specific artifact.
- Alignment consolidated *before* coherent narratives stabilized.
