# The Memeplex: Platform grammars and conspiratorial alignment around the Great Reset

> Tuters, M. (2026). The Memeplex: Platform grammars and conspiratorial alignment around the Great Reset. *New Media & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448261446931
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448261446931)

## Summary

This article reconceives contemporary conspiracism as an *infrastructural* phenomenon produced by platform grammars rather than a matter of belief, misinformation, or coherent narrative. Using the World Economic Forum's "Great Reset" as its central case, Tuters analyzes how institutional policy language — the WEF's own foresight-oriented self-description — gets recoded into formations of suspicion through patterned circulation: repeated adjacency, symbolic compression, and modular reuse. Drawing on a 3.7-million-tweet corpus and cross-platform triangulation, the paper argues that conspiratorial *alignment* precedes narrative coherence, and introduces three analytic concepts (conspiricization, modular conspiracism, and the Memeplex) alongside a "high-tech paranoid style" updating Hofstadter for platform-mediated technocratic governance.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **conspiricization** (institutional outputs reframed as evidence of elite coordination), **modular conspiracism** (recombination of fragments into portable templates of distrust), and the **Memeplex** (the overarching media ecology binding grievances through repetition and adjacency).
- Develops the **"high-tech paranoid style,"** updating Hofstadter's paranoid style for conditions of platform-mediated scale, opacity, and abstraction, in conversation with Jameson's "high-tech paranoia" and cognitive mapping.
- Offers a platform-native, infrastructural methodology that shifts analytic attention from belief and misinformation to circulation, format, and metadata.
- Reframes conspiracy as an ongoing *process* (analogous to platformization or datafication) and recasts the "globalist" imaginary as a vernacular misrecognition of the platform grammars organizing digital political life.

## Methods

- Co-hashtag network analysis of a 3.7-million-tweet corpus (#TheGreatReset, #Agenda2030, #DigitalID) via the Twitter Academic API v2, 2015–2022, with pre-/post-pandemic temporal comparison.
- Network visualization in Gephi (ForceAtlas2), read interpretively as "spatializations."
- A **"world-in-a-word"** heuristic zooming into a single hashtag as an entry point to the issue-world it condenses, inverting traditional multi-query issue mapping.
- Cross-platform triangulation: 1,614 anti-WEF TikTok posts, 9,165 YouTube comments beneath a Russell Brand video, and hyperlink-scraped issue-network metadata from the Wide Awake Media site.
- Qualitative analysis of vernacular remix genres, counter-mapping infographics, bestselling anti-WEF books, and influencer/political rhetoric.

## Findings

- Between 2018 and 2022 the #DigitalID and #Agenda2030 issue spaces reconfigured: fintech/blockchain clusters moved from central frames to peripheral residues, while control, surveillance, and depopulation tropes moved to the center.
- By 2022 the networks showed dense convergence of pandemic-governance and biopolitical anxiety (#Agenda2030, #covid19, #WEF, #BuildBackBetter, #NWO, #CBDC), aligning finance, biometrics, and surveillance into a shared antagonistic orbit.
- A small conspiracist enclave (#NWO, #Agenda21, #depopulation, #BillGates) already existed at the pre-pandemic margins and later became central — complicating the idea that conspiracies emerge purely from misreading neutral policy.
- The slogan "You'll own nothing and be happy" (from Ida Auken's 2016 WEF article) traveled via repetition-with-variation, accumulating force through reuse rather than explanation.
- The same modular alignments recur across Twitter/X, "ConspiracyTok," Wide Awake Media counter-maps, and commercial publishing — a cross-media logic rather than a platform-specific artifact.
- Alignment consolidated *before* coherent narratives stabilized: patterned adjacency preceded storytelling.

## Connections

This paper's infrastructural account of conspiratorial alignment through circulation and adjacency resonates with work on alternative epistemic communities and conspiracy cultures such as [[Grusauskaite2026-po]] and [[Askanius2026-de]], and with broader studies of platform governance and affordances like [[Gillespie2010-sla2]] and [[van-Dijck2018-up]]. Its concern with memetic reuse and symbolic compression connects to meme-focused scholarship including [[Farci2017-2a88c00e]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]], while its reframing of misinformation away from belief contrasts productively with correction- and exposure-centered accounts such as [[Nyhan2023-gb]] and [[Starbird2019-qv]].
