# The Multiple Nuances of Online Firestorms: The Case of a Pro-Vietnam Attack on the Facebook Digital Embassy of China in Italy Amidst the Pandemic

> Righetti, N. (2024). The Multiple Nuances of Online Firestorms: The Case of a Pro-Vietnam Attack on the Facebook Digital Embassy of China in Italy Amidst the Pandemic. *CINECA IRIS Institutional Research information system (University of Urbino)*. https://doi.org/10.13136/isr.v15i1.850
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.13136/isr.v15i1.850)

## Summary

This paper reconceptualizes online firestorms — sudden, large-scale outpourings of negative online attention — as heterogeneous phenomena that can spring from very different sociological and techno-communicative processes even when they look superficially alike. Using a case study of a pro-Vietnam firestorm that targeted the official Facebook "digital embassy" of China in Italy during the COVID-19 pandemic, the author argues that firestorms are not only marketing-related reputational crises (the dominant framing) but can also constitute forms of coordinated political digital protest. The contribution is primarily conceptual: broadening how scholars interpret collective online behavior by attending to its underlying drivers rather than its surface appearance.

## Key Contributions

- Expands the prevailing marketing/reputational-crisis account of online firestorms to include political digital protest as a legitimate driver.
- Foregrounds the multiple sociological and techno-communicative processes that can produce apparently similar collective online behaviors.
- Offers an empirical case study linking geopolitical tension (China–Vietnam–Italy) to online collective action during the pandemic.

## Methods

A single-case study of a specific firestorm — a pro-Vietnam attack on the Facebook page serving as China's "digital embassy" in Italy during COVID-19. The analysis focuses on the collective online behavior visible on Facebook, though fuller methodological detail is not available from the abstract.

## Findings

- The examined firestorm operated as a coordinated political digital protest, not a conventional marketing reputational crisis.
- Seemingly uniform firestorms can mask fundamentally distinct underlying dynamics, warranting a more nuanced, process-oriented interpretation.
- Geopolitical grievances can be channeled into cross-national online collective action against state-run social media presences.
