# The Multiple Nuances of Online Firestorms: The Case of a Pro-Vietnam Attack on the Facebook Digital Embassy of China in Italy Amidst the Pandemic

> Nicola, R. (2024). The Multiple Nuances of Online Firestorms: The Case of a Pro-Vietnam Attack on the Facebook Digital Embassy of China in Italy Amidst the Pandemic. *CINECA IRIS Institutional Research information system (University of Urbino)*. https://doi.org/10.13136/isr.v15i1.850
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.13136/isr.v15i1.850)

## Summary

This paper revisits the concept of the "online firestorm" — typically theorized in marketing and crisis-communication research as a sudden wave of negative collective online criticism damaging a brand or organization's reputation — and argues that this framing is too narrow. Through a case study of a pro-Vietnam attack on the Facebook "digital embassy" of China in Italy during the COVID-19 pandemic, Righetti shows that a firestorm can also function as a form of coordinated political digital protest, driven by geopolitical grievances rather than consumer or reputational concerns. The paper's central claim is that superficially similar bursts of online collective outrage can emerge from fundamentally different sociological and techno-communicative processes, and that treating "firestorm" as a single uniform category obscures this diversity.

## Key Contributions

- Expands the dominant marketing/reputational-crisis framing of firestorms to include political digital protest as an alternative underlying dynamic.
- Demonstrates that online collective behaviors bearing similar surface features can stem from distinct sociological and technical processes.
- Offers an empirical case tying together geopolitical tensions (China–Vietnam–Italy) with pandemic-era online mobilization on Facebook.
- Advances a more nuanced, pluralistic conceptual vocabulary for studying online collective action beyond crisis-management concerns.

## Methods

The paper is a qualitative case study analyzing a specific firestorm event: coordinated critical commentary and collective action directed at the Facebook page representing China's "digital embassy" in Italy, occurring amid the COVID-19 pandemic and linked to Vietnam-related grievances. The analysis focuses on the online collective behavior observable on this Facebook page, situating the event within broader debates on digital protest and firestorm dynamics (further granular methodological detail is not specified in the available abstract).

## Findings

- The examined firestorm operated as a coordinated political digital protest rather than a conventional marketing-driven reputational crisis.
- The case illustrates that firestorm-like phenomena can be politically motivated and geopolitically embedded, rather than solely triggered by corporate or brand-related grievances.
